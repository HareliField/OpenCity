# The savings club

[What the street remembers](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Bank of the Coast.** A savings club that meets on the bank steps has enough money for exactly one member to take a long-delayed trip.

## Alec Nielsen

AH-R0471 · Age 42 · Home help · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a tune my father tapped instead of naming.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Alec by a small Thursday habit. Alec knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I want the ticket home before my father forgets the songs we sing together.”

**What Alec wants.** To visit the father while they still share the song.

**Family.** [Sabine Ito](../03/11-the-widow-s-razor.md#ah-r0409) is Alec’s cousin. Alec is free to hang up first. Sabine makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The widow's razor*, Sabine says, “I drove the widow here and never asked why the box stayed in her lap.”

**People in this story.** [Aubrey Ahmed](#ah-r0478). They price tickets going in opposite directions for two family reunions.

**Personality.** Alec has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Alec's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Alec has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Alec takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The club draws the name of someone who cannot travel. Alec lets the winner describe where they actually want the money to go.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Alec moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If the joke lands badly, Alec seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Kai Ibrahim

AH-R0472 · Age 70 · Retired caretaker · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember winning a raffle for a coat that didn't fit.” People around Lighthouse Cove know Kai by a small Wednesday habit. Kai takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I've won the draw twice and deferred it twice. This year the person I was visiting has moved here.”

**What Kai wants.** To spend a deferred holiday with the relative now nearby.

**Family.** [Noel Ford](../10/15-the-last-light-at-the-harbor.md#ah-r1498) is Kai’s younger relative by marriage. Kai chooses the familiar route. Noel can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The last light at the harbor*, Noel says, “I am the electrician who suggested timers. The owners asked me to leave the switches where human hands can get to them.”

**People in this story.** [Vera Price](#ah-r0480) (club treasurer). A deferred holiday and an unused refund may create a second journey.

**Personality.** Kai listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Kai has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with a gray streak running back from the forehead. Kai raises one eyebrow long before saying yes during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A donor asks for the club's name in return for a ticket. Kai asks the members to hear the terms before deciding on a name.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Kai waits at the lit stop with them and checks whether the next service is one they can actually board.
- When a second witness disagrees, Kai keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Sasha Perez

AH-R0473 · Age 53 · Boarding-house receptionist · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first guest spending the evening on the doorstep.” On Sunday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Sasha usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I can contribute time in a spare room on the route, which our rules don't count.”

**What Sasha wants.** To make a spare room count as a real contribution.

**Family.** [Vincent Armas](../08/01-reopening-at-six.md#ah-r1054) is Sasha’s cousin raised in the same household. Sasha brings the paper and a pen. Vincent decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Reopening at six*, Vincent says, “I used to pay the old owner's daughter to sit with me when the place emptied.”

**People in this story.** [Kit Evans](#ah-r0475). One member can give a room even as the other needs some of the savings back.

**Personality.** Sasha talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Sasha's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a striped cotton shirt and a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. Sasha has a round face with a pronounced chin, with brown hair pinned away from the temples. Sasha draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The club draws the name of someone who cannot travel. Sasha lets the winner describe where they actually want the money to go.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Sasha stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Sasha says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Mia Sloan

AH-R0474 · Age 46 · School dinner worker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a magazine holiday family with nobody washing dishes.” Sunday often includes a stop at the downtown fountain, where Mia keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “The holiday photograph on my fridge is of somebody else's holiday. I want one ordinary breakfast picture of my own.”

**What Mia wants.** To take one unposed breakfast photograph on holiday.

**Family.** [Alan Ko](../05/14-the-split-visit.md#ah-r0736) is Mia’s cousin. Mia calls about good news first. Alan is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The split visit*, Alan says, “I am the son who books the visits from overseas.”

**People in this story.** [Ravi Foster](#ah-r0477). A holiday photograph doesn't need a sponsor's name across the front.

**Personality.** Mia prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Mia is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a small enamel bird on the lapel, Mia folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A donor asks for the club's name in return for a ticket. Mia asks the members to hear the terms before deciding on a name.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Mia asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Mia clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Kit Evans

AH-R0475 · Age 38 · Recently laid-off line worker · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being paid my last week's wages in coins.” Thursday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Kit likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I deposited a week's overtime just before getting laid off. I need to ask for it back in front of friends.”

**What Kit wants.** To recover enough savings to bridge the lost wage.

**Family.** [Rae Stein](../07/14-the-carer-s-afternoon.md#ah-r1033) is Kit’s younger cousin. Kit remembers the birthdays. Rae asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The carer's afternoon*, Rae says, “I cover the hour between school and the evening shift.”

**People in this story.** [Sasha Perez](#ah-r0473). One member can give a room even as the other needs some of the savings back.

**Personality.** Kit never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Kit can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Kit is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket, Kit touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The club draws the name of someone who cannot travel. Kit lets the winner describe where they actually want the money to go.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Kit brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Kit may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Denise Barker

AH-R0476 · Age 32 · Instrument repairer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember engraving a sign somebody could feel with one finger.” People around Lighthouse Cove know Denise by a small Monday habit. Denise takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I used to draw the winning number with my mother. She can't see the slips now so I embossed them.”

**What Denise wants.** To let the mother identify the winning slip herself.

**Family.** [Erin Roche](../08/10-the-first-delivery-fee.md#ah-r1149) is Denise’s half-sibling. Denise offers the first invitation. Erin gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The first delivery fee*, Erin says, “I work the deli counter. The first paid rider wants the tips I used to divide among the cooks.”

**People in this story.** [Cory Mann](#ah-r0479). The family redesigning the draw slips records the sound of the old savings jar.

**Personality.** Denise often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Denise have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Denise has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Denise raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- A donor asks for the club's name in return for a ticket. Denise asks the members to hear the terms before deciding on a name.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Denise writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Denise rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Ravi Foster

AH-R0477 · Age 49 · Estate assistant · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a dinner where the host bought every vacant chair.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Ravi likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “My millionaire employer offered the balance on condition we name the club after her. I asked for train vouchers instead.”

**What Ravi wants.** To secure travel help without renaming the club.

**Family.** [Aubrey Carver](../06/01-the-upstairs-lease.md#ah-r0752) is Ravi’s stepsibling. Ravi puts the kettle on. Aubrey chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The upstairs lease*, Aubrey says, “I offered to move in with my sister until she showed me where three children would sleep.”

**People in this story.** [Mia Sloan](#ah-r0474). A holiday photograph doesn't need a sponsor's name across the front.

**Personality.** Ravi thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Ravi sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Ravi has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, with a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Ravi listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The club draws the name of someone who cannot travel. Ravi lets the winner describe where they actually want the money to go.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Ravi offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Ravi asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Aubrey Ahmed

AH-R0478 · Age 47 · Nursing aide · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember receiving a return ticket with no fixed date.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Aubrey by a small Tuesday habit. Aubrey can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I would rather buy a return for my sister than a departure for myself.”

**What Aubrey wants.** To buy a return trip for the sister.

**Family.** [Mateo Adair](../05/14-the-split-visit.md#ah-r0739) is Aubrey’s older cousin. Aubrey chooses an ordinary story to tell. Mateo asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The split visit*, Mateo says, “I took the spare half-hour to walk to the corner alone.”

**People in this story.** [Alec Nielsen](#ah-r0471). They price tickets going in opposite directions for two family reunions.

**Personality.** Aubrey gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Aubrey privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Aubrey is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Usually dressed in a narrow-striped sweater with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip, Aubrey presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A donor asks for the club's name in return for a ticket. Aubrey asks the members to hear the terms before deciding on a name.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Aubrey suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Aubrey offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Vera Price

AH-R0480 · Age 54 · Savings club treasurer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first interest payment smaller than its postage stamp.” Friday often includes a stop at Lighthouse Cove, where Vera takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I'm the bookkeeper. I found the money for a second ticket in a refund none of us claimed.”

**What Vera wants.** To recover the unused refund for a second ticket.

**Family.** [Jesse Starr](../03/15-the-tide-clock.md#ah-r0442) is Vera’s half-sibling. Vera asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Jesse remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The tide clock*, Jesse says, “I gave the employee a warning before I checked the dial.”

**People in this story.** [Kai Ibrahim](#ah-r0472) (savings club member). A deferred holiday and an unused refund may create a second journey.

**Personality.** Vera feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Vera has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, Vera listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A donor asks for the club's name in return for a ticket. Vera asks the members to hear the terms before deciding on a name.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Vera keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Vera lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.
