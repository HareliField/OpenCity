## Orla March

AH-R0581 · Age 72 · Retired domestic worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a hand taking mine in an ambulance queue.” On Monday, near Breakwater Way, Orla stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I iron for the young man opposite because he held my hand when the ambulance took my wife.”

**What Orla wants.** To finish a clean shirt for the bereaved neighbor.

**Family.** [Chris Valdez](../05/11-the-free-fringe.md#ah-r0702) is Orla’s first cousin once removed. Orla offers the first invitation. Chris gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The free fringe*, Chris says, “I timed the conversation like a normal trim.”

**People in this story.** [Jorge Nash](#ah-r0582) (owner of the pressed shirt). A grateful customer tries to pay the neighbor who irons the stranger's shirt.

**Personality.** Orla has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Orla is easy to recognize by a long silver braid and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Orla draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A staff supervisor demands the stranger's laundry back. Orla checks with the shirt's owner before handing it over to anyone else.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Orla finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- When somebody trades on a title, Orla asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.
