## Eden Diaz

**Life in the city.** “I still remember watching beach performers from under the rail.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Eden by a small Thursday habit. Eden pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I always thought she was diving. From my wheelchair she's clearly reaching for somebody.”

**What Eden wants.** To show the sculpted hand from wheelchair height.

**Family.** [Billie Morrow](../02/14-saturday-keys.md#ah-r0283) is Eden’s second cousin. Eden gets to change the plan. Billie offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *Saturday keys*, Billie says, “I returned the ring inside a soup tin to keep it dry.”

**People in this story.** [Lara Ng](#ah-r0527). A lower view of the mural makes the possible second figure easier to see.

**Personality.** Eden uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Eden well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Eden is easy to recognize by short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a moss-green cardigan with a small enamel bird on the lapel, Eden presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor wants the painted figure made easier to recognize. Eden asks the original model which part has been misunderstood.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Eden offers an ordinary errand that lets the distressed person leave without being followed by an audience.
- After a family quarrel, Eden brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Jody Gao

AH-R0526 · Age 56 · Running coach · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sharing a dressing after a relay race.” On Thursday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Jody pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I recognize the scar on her painted calf. My old teammate got it in the same meet I lost.”

**What Jody wants.** To identify the teammate who shared the old injury.

**Family.** [Ismael Wu](../09/13-the-school-photograph-that-travels.md#ah-r1330) is Jody’s cousin raised in the same household. Jody leaves the first voice message. Ismael calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The school photograph that travels*, Ismael says, “I paid postage for the last copy. It is the first letter the girl's father will open in his new home.”

**People in this story.** [Ava Boyd](#ah-r0530). They propose a new panel beside the original painted scar.

**Personality.** Jody enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Jody is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. Jody has one cheek that creases before the other, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Jody takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The old model asks to choose the viewing angle. Jody walks the observers to the angle the model chose.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Jody asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Jody keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.
