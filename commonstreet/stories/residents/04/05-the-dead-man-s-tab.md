# The dead man's tab

[What the street remembers](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Ferris Deli.** A Ferris Deli tab survives the death of a regular who did time for fraud. Three people offer to settle it.

[Benjamin Fox](#ah-r0491) · [Marc Ward](#ah-r0492) · [Kai Day](#ah-r0493) · [Karen Pierce](#ah-r0494) · [Faye Daniels](#ah-r0495) · [Eli Brady](#ah-r0496) · [Hector Clay](#ah-r0497) · [Carla Lam](#ah-r0498) · [Ava Nichols](#ah-r0499) · [Dominic Alvarez](#ah-r0500)

## Benjamin Fox

AH-R0491 · Age 42 · Kitchen designer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember lunch paid for by somebody my father distrusted.” On Friday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Benjamin looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “He conned my dad and paid for my lunch. Both facts belong on the same page.”

**What Benjamin wants.** To keep the lunch gift and the fraud on the same record.

**Family.** [Levi Boyd](../06/08-summer-staff.md#ah-r0825) is Benjamin’s stepsibling. Benjamin keeps the photographs. Levi knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Summer staff*, Levi says, “I have money for one retention bonus. I'm asking every worker what a useful final week would look like.”

**People in this story.** [Kai Day](#ah-r0493). They can honor lunches that were paid for while disagreeing about the man who bought them.

**Personality.** Benjamin feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Benjamin is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket, Benjamin draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A late customer's daughter objects to the memorial payment. Benjamin gives the daughter a private chance to say what is upsetting about the payment.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Benjamin finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Benjamin lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Marc Ward

AH-R0492 · Age 49 · Former prisoner and warehouse packer · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember returning from prison to a kitchen with unfamiliar cups.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the bank steps, where Marc takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I shared his flat after prison. The tab includes meals for men whose first paycheck hadn't come.”

**What Marc wants.** To recognize the meals bought for new arrivals after prison.

**Family.** [Terry Acosta](../03/09-the-upstairs-piano.md#ah-r0385) is Marc’s half-sibling. Marc sets the time to leave. Terry picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The upstairs piano*, Terry says, “My father scratched the piano name off after the creditors came.”

**People in this story.** [Faye Daniels](#ah-r0495). They discuss who gets to direct the memorial money after shared prison housing ends.

**Personality.** Marc treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Marc has a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape, a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. When somebody else has the floor, Marc gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- The deli is asked to publish the whole tab. Marc asks who owns the personal information on the tab before any of it leaves the counter.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Marc moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Marc changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Kai Day

AH-R0493 · Age 36 · Street drug dealer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an envelope of cash smelling of oranges.” On Friday, near the blocks below Foundry Lofts, Kai checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I sold him pills. I won't call the debt friendship just because he died.”

**What Kai wants.** To separate grief from what remains unpaid.

**Family.** [Elin Watts](../01/13-the-borrowed-bass.md#ah-r0129) is Kai’s cousin. Kai brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Elin makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The borrowed bass*, Elin says, “He thinks giving up the audition proves he is decent.”

**People in this story.** [Benjamin Fox](#ah-r0491). They can honor lunches that were paid for while disagreeing about the man who bought them.

**Personality.** Kai is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Kai is concerned.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Kai has one cheek that creases before the other, with auburn hair cut square at the nape. Kai squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A late customer's daughter objects to the memorial payment. Kai gives the daughter a private chance to say what is upsetting about the payment.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Kai stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- When someone invents an answer, Kai asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

**What Karen wants.** To date the letter from the price of lunch.

**Appearance.** Karen is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a rust-colored canvas coat with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Karen listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Karen offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Faye Daniels

AH-R0495 · Age 65 · Shop debtor and driver · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember giving up a seat for a daughter nobody recognized.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Faye picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I want the deli to give the money to the daughter everyone speaks for.”

**What Faye wants.** To let the daughter choose where memorial money goes.

**Family.** [Joy Johannsen](../10/09-a-house-divided-kindly.md#ah-r1433) is Faye’s younger relative by marriage. Faye brings the paper and a pen. Joy decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *A house divided kindly*, Joy says, “I am the daughter afraid this garden scheme means Mum is getting confused.”

**People in this story.** [Marc Ward](#ah-r0492). They discuss who gets to direct the memorial money after shared prison housing ends. Also in the story is [Ava Nichols](#ah-r0499) (wake cook).

**Personality.** Faye enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Faye is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Faye is easy to recognize by two white streaks in otherwise dark hair and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Faye raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A late customer's daughter objects to the memorial payment. Faye gives the daughter a private chance to say what is upsetting about the payment.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Faye stays where the child can see the shop door and asks the neighboring counter to help find the adult expected there.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Faye keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Eli Brady

AH-R0496 · Age 27 · Sign maker · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember forging a family birthday card before I could spell.” On Saturday, near Juniper Street, Eli knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I forged my father's signature once. Seeing the real one in the book made me own up.”

**What Eli wants.** To own up to the borrowed signature at home.

**Family.** [Bess Lai](../02/07-the-margin-reader.md#ah-r0211) is Eli’s older relative by marriage. Eli can always ask for five minutes alone. Bess asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The margin reader*, Bess says, “I wrote the first objection when I was seventeen.”

**People in this story.** [Dominic Alvarez](#ah-r0500). A real signature matters to both the former forger and the person making a limited repayment.

**Personality.** Eli is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Eli has a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. When somebody else has the floor, Eli counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- The deli is asked to publish the whole tab. Eli asks who owns the personal information on the tab before any of it leaves the counter.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Eli takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Eli helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Hector Clay

AH-R0497 · Age 55 · Repair shop owner · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember hanging a new lock after a stranger slept in our shop.” Saturday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Hector stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “He charged me rent for a room he didn't own. I came to the reading because I wanted to hear someone say so.”

**What Hector wants.** To tell the family which tenancy was fraudulent.

**Family.** [Peyton Waters](../09/08-the-other-side-of-the-cove.md#ah-r1273) is Hector’s stepsibling. Hector remembers the birthdays. Peyton asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The other side of the cove*, Peyton says, “I brought the cleaner a newspaper every summer.”

**People in this story.** [Ava Nichols](#ah-r0499). The cook hears from the customer who rented a room the man never owned.

**Personality.** Hector finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Hector is easy to recognize by a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Hector raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A late customer's daughter objects to the memorial payment. Hector gives the daughter a private chance to say what is upsetting about the payment.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Hector asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Hector waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

Asked about *Two left slippers*, Denise says, “I ticked footwear present on the form. I have added a chair where people can actually try the pair.”

**Appearance.** Carla has black hair twisted into a small knot, a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. When somebody else has the floor, Carla looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Carla asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Carla listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first soup I sold without tasting.” People around Juniper Street know Ava by a small Sunday habit. Ava knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and one cuff folded back twice. Ava has fine brows over wide-set eyes, with a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. Ava looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

## Dominic Alvarez

AH-R0500 · Age 64 · Retired legal secretary · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember noticing my late husband's old account had never been closed.” Friday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Dominic stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I'm his old victim's sister. I offered to cover only the meals that went to people still alive.”

**What Dominic wants.** To pay only for the meals still doing somebody good.

**Family.** [Melle Juric](../01/07-an-unfinished-atlas.md#ah-r0068) is Dominic’s cousin’s child. Dominic calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Melle leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *An unfinished atlas*, Melle says, “My father remembers the coffee seller's face but loses names halfway through a sentence.”

**People in this story.** [Eli Brady](#ah-r0496). A real signature matters to both the former forger and the person making a limited repayment.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Dominic takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a rust-colored canvas coat and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Dominic has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with a neat white crop. Dominic presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The deli is asked to publish the whole tab. Dominic asks who owns the personal information on the tab before any of it leaves the counter.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Dominic tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Dominic asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.
