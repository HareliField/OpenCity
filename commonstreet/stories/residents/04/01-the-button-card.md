## Tali Moreno

AH-R0451 · Age 42 · Uniform fitter · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a returned blazer that still had bus tickets in it.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Tali notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I recognized the button from my first school blazer and came back with its twin.”

**What Tali wants.** To return the twin button to the original blazer.

**Family.** [Sera Noor](../01/10-the-thirteenth-doughnut.md#ah-r0096) is Tali’s cousin raised in the same household. Tali brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Sera makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The thirteenth doughnut*, Sera says, “Our night crew never attends the monthly breakfast meeting.”

**People in this story.** [Audrey Hughes](#ah-r0458). Common work buttons let the card keep giving after the valuable ones go.

**Personality.** Tali is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Tali has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a charcoal zip cardigan with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. In conversation Tali counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A child takes the most valuable button. Tali lets the child show what the button is for before calling it too valuable.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Tali waits at the lit stop with them and checks whether the next service is one they can actually board.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Tali helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Bruno Gao

AH-R0452 · Age 53 · Family carer · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sewing a coin into my father's lapel for luck.” On Sunday, near Juniper Street, Bruno knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I've been sewing pockets shut since my mother began losing coins. I want her to choose her own fastening.”

**What Bruno wants.** To let the father choose a fastening he can use.

**Family.** [Peter Patel](../02/06-a-red-mitten.md#ah-r0206) is Bruno’s cousin. Bruno calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Peter leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A red mitten*, Peter says, “It fit my prosthetic thumb. For five minutes I remembered the weight of holding hands with a child.”

**People in this story.** [Carmen Ali](#ah-r0454). They distinguish the helpful sewn coin from the anchor someone wants to reclaim.

**Personality.** Bruno finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Bruno has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for a center part and straight chestnut hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a boxy denim jacket with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Bruno rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The donor recognizes the uniform it came from. Bruno asks the owner which garment the donor wants remembered.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Bruno offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Bruno waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Benjamin Lewis

AH-R0453 · Age 31 · Costume bead dealer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a costume star made from cracked buttons.” On Thursday, near Lighthouse Cove, Benjamin takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I collect glass buttons but gave away the best one because the boy needed a moon for his costume.”

**What Benjamin wants.** To finish the child's moon costume before the show.

**Family.** [Rain Daniels](../05/06-two-left-slippers.md#ah-r0655) is Benjamin’s older relative by marriage. Benjamin remembers the birthdays. Rain asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *Two left slippers*, Rain says, “I embroider names inside slippers. I'm going to ask first who can bend far enough to read them.”

**People in this story.** [Elizabeth Osei](#ah-r0459). A child's costume moon survives beside an older worker's maternity buttons.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Benjamin has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cream pullover with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Benjamin counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A child takes the most valuable button. Benjamin lets the child show what the button is for before calling it too valuable.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Benjamin writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Benjamin listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Carmen Ali

AH-R0454 · Age 62 · Port receptionist · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember washing an anchor button until the gold wore through.” Sunday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Carmen likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I found a brass anchor from a uniform my sister swore she'd burned.”

**What Carmen wants.** To ask the sister what happened to the anchor uniform.

**Family.** [Joanna Ellis](../02/02-five-missing-lunches.md#ah-r0169) is Carmen’s stepsibling. Carmen asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Joanna chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Five missing lunches*, Joanna says, “The dents on those lids came from our lunchtime domino games.”

**People in this story.** [Bruno Gao](#ah-r0452). They distinguish the helpful sewn coin from the anchor someone wants to reclaim.

**Personality.** Carmen is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Carmen loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Carmen is easy to recognize by a long silver braid and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Carmen grins on one side while the eyes stay serious when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The donor recognizes the uniform it came from. Carmen asks the owner which garment the donor wants remembered.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Carmen asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- If somebody calls for applause, Carmen gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Milo Rizzo

AH-R0455 · Age 39 · Outerwear buyer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember stepping out of my first self-bought winter coat.” People around Tideline Market know Milo by a small Thursday habit. Milo picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “The yellow button came from the first coat I ever bought without asking permission.”

**What Milo wants.** To repair the first self-bought coat with the same yellow.

**Family.** [Nadine Lam](../09/06-a-doctor-moves-away.md#ah-r1257) is Milo’s cousin raised in the same household. Milo puts the kettle on. Nadine chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *A doctor moves away*, Nadine says, “I am the parent's carer in the other city. I want the daughter to come home as family before she comes home as a doctor.”

**People in this story.** [Lola Jeong](#ah-r0460). The yellow winter-coat button acquires a new history in the costume box.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Milo takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Milo has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Milo squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child takes the most valuable button. Milo lets the child show what the button is for before calling it too valuable.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Milo helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Milo asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Nelson Gallagher

AH-R0456 · Age 52 · Dry-cleaning attendant · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember reading cufflinks as if they were tiny portraits.” Sunday often includes a stop at Paper & Pine Books, where Nelson starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I left cufflinks on the card and came back to find someone had paired them with shoelaces.”

**What Nelson wants.** To return the cufflinks to somebody who will wear them.

**Family.** [Vera Gallagher](../06/07-reading-the-payslip.md#ah-r0811) is Nelson’s half-sibling. Nelson chooses an ordinary story to tell. Vera asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Reading the payslip*, Vera says, “I learned to read novels at fifty. Numbers with brackets still make me sweat.”

**People in this story.** [Celia Lai](#ah-r0457). They put the largest easy fastening where the person who needs it can actually reach. Also in the story is [Lola Jeong](#ah-r0460) (button collector).

**Personality.** Nelson can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Nelson has a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a mustard canvas overshirt. It usually goes with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. When somebody else has the floor, Nelson listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- The donor recognizes the uniform it came from. Nelson asks the owner which garment the donor wants remembered.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Nelson asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Nelson asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Celia Lai

AH-R0457 · Age 57 · Part-time receptionist · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first clasp I could close without help.” People around the market by Market Flats know Celia by a small Friday habit. Celia often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I need an enormous button I can close with one useful thumb.”

**What Celia wants.** To fasten a coat one-handed.

**Family.** [Cecil Hassan](../05/11-the-free-fringe.md#ah-r0706) is Celia’s stepsibling. Celia checks the date twice. Cecil brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The free fringe*, Cecil says, “I used to dye my fringe to hide a scar. I came to have the scar shown for the first time.”

**People in this story.** [Nelson Gallagher](#ah-r0456). They put the largest easy fastening where the person who needs it can actually reach.

**Personality.** Celia can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Celia can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Celia has a long plait tucked inside the collar, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Celia folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A child takes the most valuable button. Celia lets the child show what the button is for before calling it too valuable.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Celia asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Celia suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Audrey Hughes

AH-R0458 · Age 37 · Warehouse loader · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember work shirts handed down from a cousin half my size.” On Wednesday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Audrey usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I kept taking the plain black ones for work shirts. Today I bought a whole packet to leave.”

**What Audrey wants.** To replenish the common black buttons.

**Family.** [Sam Sharp](../05/15-a-boat-for-sale-twice.md#ah-r0746) is Audrey’s stepsibling. Audrey brings a spare shopping bag. Sam chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A boat for sale twice*, Sam says, “I used to polish that boat for a season pass.”

**People in this story.** [Tali Moreno](#ah-r0451). Common work buttons let the card keep giving after the valuable ones go.

**Personality.** Audrey carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Audrey has close-cropped dark curls, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Audrey grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- The donor recognizes the uniform it came from. Audrey asks the owner which garment the donor wants remembered.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Audrey keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Audrey offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Elizabeth Osei

AH-R0459 · Age 68 · Retired maternity orderly · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an old nurse adjusting her own swollen cuff.” People around the market by Market Flats know Elizabeth by a small Sunday habit. Elizabeth often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I can identify the old maternity uniforms by their flat-backed buttons. I wore one through both winters.”

**What Elizabeth wants.** To recognize the old maternity workers beyond their uniforms.

**Family.** [Sam Fox](../08/06-two-names-on-the-chart.md#ah-r1107) is Elizabeth’s younger cousin. Elizabeth asks before bringing somebody along. Sam can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Two names on the chart*, Sam says, “I copied her old hospital number onto a holiday card once.”

**People in this story.** [Benjamin Lewis](#ah-r0453). A child's costume moon survives beside an older worker's maternity buttons.

**Personality.** Elizabeth has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Elizabeth has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Look for tightly curled gray hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Elizabeth looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- A child takes the most valuable button. Elizabeth lets the child show what the button is for before calling it too valuable.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Elizabeth takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Elizabeth says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Lola Jeong

AH-R0460 · Age 29 · Community costume assistant · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the lucky charm sewn into our practice curtain.” Thursday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Lola picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I came to return a button my wife thought unlucky. It won somebody else the costume prize.”

**What Lola wants.** To explain the returned lucky button to its next wearer.

**Family.** [Percy Andrade](../02/08-low-water-music.md#ah-r0222) is Lola’s older cousin. Lola can always ask for five minutes alone. Percy asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Low water music*, Percy says, “I scraped my knee there and have called it a much ruder name since.”

**People in this story.** [Milo Rizzo](#ah-r0455). The yellow winter-coat button acquires a new history in the costume box. Also in the story is [Nelson Gallagher](#ah-r0456) (button donor).

**Personality.** Lola treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Lola lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Lola is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Lola gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The donor recognizes the uniform it came from. Lola asks the owner which garment the donor wants remembered.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Lola lets the child choose whether to stand by the door or sit beside the window while another adult gets help.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Lola says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.
