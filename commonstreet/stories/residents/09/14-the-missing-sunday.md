## Avery Williams

AH-R1331 · Age 74 · Retired salesman · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember watching a whole Sunday match too quietly.” Saturday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Avery likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I know when my pills are due. I want somebody who will argue about the football on a Sunday.”

**What Avery wants.** To get one unhurried football argument back into Sundays.

**Family.** [Magdalena Macrae](../06/03-money-after-marriage.md#ah-r0771) is Avery’s second cousin. Avery decides what stays off the paper. Magdalena brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Money after marriage*, Magdalena says, “I founded it but my ex brought every first customer.”

**People in this story.** [Cora Adair](#ah-r1332) (daughter). They renegotiate the difference between managing Sunday's tasks and spending Sunday together.

**Personality.** Avery is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Avery loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Avery is easy to recognize by a neat white crop and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip, Avery gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The widower asks for a Sunday with no appointments. Avery keeps necessary care covered and leaves the social part of Sunday open.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Avery repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- If somebody calls for applause, Avery gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Cora Adair

AH-R1332 · Age 46 · Family care planner · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember finishing a lovely plan and leaving nobody at the table.” Sunday often includes a stop at Lighthouse Cove, where Cora takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I arranged every important task. My father asked what hour I'd set aside to waste with him.”

**What Cora wants.** To set aside actual time to waste together.

**Family.** [William Nielsen](../05/12-level-with-the-window.md#ah-r0717) is Cora’s half-sibling. Cora knows which ones can be shared. William keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Level with the window*, William says, “I put up a score on match days. The passengers started holding up their predictions on the return journey.”

**People in this story.** [Avery Williams](#ah-r1331) (father). They renegotiate the difference between managing Sunday's tasks and spending Sunday together.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Cora takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Cora has short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a charcoal zip cardigan. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Cora leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- One volunteer cannot make the match. Cora gives the widower a timely message and lets him decide whether he wants another visitor.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Cora sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Cora asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Vera Baines

AH-R1333 · Age 55 · Sunday home visitor · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember singing at a job nobody expected to hear me enjoy.” People around Maré Coffee know Vera by a small Tuesday habit. Vera likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I have the Sunday key and normally clean silently. He invited me to bring my dreadful singing voice.”

**What Vera wants.** To bring music into a previously silent visit.

**Family.** [Inez Park](../06/04-the-saturday-swap.md#ah-r0785) is Vera’s stepsibling. Vera gets to change the plan. Inez offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The Saturday swap*, Inez says, “I took everyone's unwanted Saturdays last year.”

**People in this story.** [Robin Poole](#ah-r1338). Two visitors who do not need to watch the score can stay audible from the kitchen.

**Personality.** Vera can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Vera is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Vera starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The widower asks for a Sunday with no appointments. Vera keeps necessary care covered and leaves the social part of Sunday open.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Vera lets the child choose whether to stand by the door or sit beside the window while another adult gets help.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Vera asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Jody Clay

AH-R1334 · Age 41 · Divorced engineer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember telling my spouse the time I actually had for Sunday.” On Thursday, near Juniper Street, Jody knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I love his company but I also need Sundays for my own marriage. I'm joining once a month on a fixed date.”

**What Jody wants.** To offer a sustainable monthly Sunday.

**Family.** [Jo March](../03/11-the-widow-s-razor.md#ah-r0404) is Jody’s parent’s cousin. Jody can always ask for five minutes alone. Jo asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The widow's razor*, Jo says, “I was forbidden to cut girls' hair here. That woman lent me the razor for my first paying customer.”

**People in this story.** [Quinn Park](#ah-r1336). A sustainable family date gives the social worker a better measure of companionship than a timed task.

**Personality.** Jody can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Jody can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Jody has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Jody folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- One volunteer cannot make the match. Jody gives the widower a timely message and lets him decide whether he wants another visitor.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Jody checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Jody suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Toby Hsu

**Life in the city.** “I still remember putting the wrong club scarf in my father's shopping.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Toby picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I live next door and didn't know he followed our team. I have two scarves and an ordinary invitation.”

**What Toby wants.** To watch a match with the neighbor.

**Family.** [Devin Ames](../05/10-midnight-cinnamon.md#ah-r0697) is Toby’s first cousin once removed. Toby calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Devin leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Midnight cinnamon*, Devin says, “I brought the wrong sack and almost lost the account.”

**People in this story.** [Ruth Spencer](#ah-r1339). An old scarf and a working away-match radio let two neighbors bring the club home.

**Personality.** Toby carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Toby is easy to recognize by a neat white crop and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it, Toby looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The widower asks for a Sunday with no appointments. Toby keeps necessary care covered and leaves the social part of Sunday open.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Toby suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Toby offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.
