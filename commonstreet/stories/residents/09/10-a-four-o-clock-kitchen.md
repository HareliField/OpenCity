# A four o'clock kitchen

[The price of being known](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Night Shift Donuts.** A Night Shift cook starts preparing breakfast four hours early for port crews whose schedule changed. Her own teenagers never see her at breakfast anymore.

## Zara Barclay

AH-R1291 · Age 43 · Bakery cook · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember finishing restaurant prep before my own child's alarm.” People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Zara by a small Monday habit. Zara checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I love feeding strangers. I don't want my children to think that's easier than feeding them.”

**What Zara wants.** To find one weekly breakfast at home with the teenagers.

**Family.** [Orla Till](../01/09-the-porch-light.md#ah-r0084) is Zara’s second cousin. Zara can always ask for five minutes alone. Orla asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The porch light*, Orla says, “I used the lamp to know exactly how far I had left to walk.”

**People in this story.** [Anita Porter](#ah-r1293) (daughter). The cook and the oldest child finally talk about who has been making breakfast at home.

**Personality.** Zara has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for an unlined tan jacket and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Zara has a round face with a pronounced chin, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Zara leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A port buyer asks the cook to start still earlier. Zara checks what the earlier start would do to the cook's family breakfast.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Zara brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Zara says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember going to the quay hungry after missing the first bus.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Orlando likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I work the port shift and the first warm meal makes me less short-tempered with my kid after work.”

**What Orlando wants.** To keep an affordable warm meal before work.

**Family.** [Jo Grant](../07/04-a-civilian-apology.md#ah-r0931) is Orlando’s second cousin. Orlando decides what stays off the paper. Jo brings the paper and a pen. **People in this story.** [Julia Cao](#ah-r1299). A port employee's need for breakfast and a union subsidy get considered alongside the cook's time.

**Appearance.** Orlando is easy to recognize by a long plait tucked inside the collar and a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Orlando gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Orlando says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Anita Porter

AH-R1293 · Age 16 · Secondary school pupil · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember forgetting my own breakfast while preparing my sister's.” Thursday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Anita prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I am the cook's older daughter. I can run breakfast, but I'm tired of adults praising how little help I need.”

**What Anita wants.** To stop carrying the whole weekday breakfast alone.

**Family.** [Franklin Cole](../02/14-saturday-keys.md#ah-r0286) is Anita’s older cousin. In one winter of double shifts family news traveled in notes tucked under the bread bin. Some notes solved practical problems. The favorites never asked anybody to do anything. Anita sometimes writes for the plain pleasure of being answered. Asked about *Saturday keys*, Franklin says, “I missed lunch waiting for the knock. I want a window of time I can rely on without sitting in my coat.”

**People in this story.** [Zara Barclay](#ah-r1291) (mother). The cook and the oldest child finally talk about who has been making breakfast at home.

**Personality.** Anita is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Anita is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Anita has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. Outside school, the familiar outfit is a plum-colored cardigan with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. In conversation Anita touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A port buyer asks the cook to start still earlier. Anita checks what the earlier start would do to the cook's family breakfast.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Anita asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Anita agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

In conversation Victor draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

**In their own words.** “I was asking for cheaper coffee before I learned the cook was paying someone to wake her youngest.”

**Family.** [Penelope Jeong](../06/02-nine-unpaid-hours.md#ah-r0767) is Francis’s cousin. A relative always brought two cakes, the good one and the one that had collapsed. People at home learned to ask for the collapsed one first so the baker could sit down. Francis thinks that counted as good manners. Asked about *Nine unpaid hours*, Penelope says, “I am the boss's daughter. The lads asked for my signature and didn't ask me to defend him.”

**Personality.** Francis is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Francis has short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Francis draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- The cook's teenager wants an ordinary breakfast together. Francis moves practical work away from the table for the time that the teenager asked for.
