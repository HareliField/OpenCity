# Window seven

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**St. Mary Hospital.** A patient in St. Mary Hospital asks to see the sunrise from window seven one last time. A delivery cage, a curtain rail and two institutional timetables stand in the way.

## Anton Dove

AH-R0051 · Age 47 · Ward porter · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Anton knows which ward windows fog first and which patients would rather see streetlights than the sea. His eldest boy thinks he pushes beds for a living. Both descriptions are accurate, which pleases him. Wednesday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Anton usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “Window seven is the first place I saw the sea after I moved here. I timed the empty corridor with my watch and asked the patient what counted as sunrise to her.”

**What Anton wants.** To work out a clear route to window seven before sunrise.

**Family.** [Bruna Morse](../06/02-nine-unpaid-hours.md#ah-r0764) is Anton’s half-sibling. Anton asks before bringing somebody along. Bruna can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Nine unpaid hours*, Bruna says, “I ran payroll. I warned management on Wednesday and told the crews on Friday.”

**People in this story.** [Lois Fell](#ah-r0059) (ward colleague). The porter knows this window. The chaplain is discovering how much of the morning that knowledge can hold.

**Personality.** Anton takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Anton can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Anton has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Anton listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A relative wants the sunrise streamed to a phone. Anton asks the patient whether they want a camera in this particular morning.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Anton suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Anton turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Nessa Wren

AH-R0052 · Age 42 · Palliative-care nurse · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Nessa used to promise impossible comforts to people she loved. In nursing school she learned precise language. Her hard days are the ones when precision starts sounding to a family like indifference. On Thursday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Nessa looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I promised her we'd try. I kept saying try because of the time I promised my father a trip he never got to make. This time I told the family exactly when to arrive.”

**What Nessa wants.** To let the patient decide how far to go on the morning.

**Family.** [Marc Ramos](../02/14-saturday-keys.md#ah-r0287) is Nessa’s cousin’s child. Nessa makes room at the table. Marc brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Saturday keys*, Marc says, “I delivered the missing keys on my skates. It's the first time a grown-up thanked me for using the pavement.”

**People in this story.** [Cass Pennell](#ah-r0057). They ask the patient what colors and timing she would want her family to remember.

**Personality.** Nessa admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Nessa learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Nessa has short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Nessa grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- A different patient needs the same porter. Nessa checks which part can wait and makes sure the other patient hears about the delay.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Nessa gets the people at the next doorway away from the smoke and asks a bystander to make the call.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Nessa describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Mika Drew

AH-R0053 · Age 36 · Hospital curtain fitter · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Mika charges hospital jobs by the rail and domestic jobs by the hour. A squeak at night feels very different in those two accounts. Mika is collecting real complaints before bidding for the next ward contract. Wednesday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Mika checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “The rail squealed when I moved it and woke three people. I returned after supper to quiet the other rails too. A day's work can have a different ending from the one on the invoice.”

**What Mika wants.** To quiet the rails on the adjoining beds before the next night shift.

**Family.** [Greta Hassan](../09/09-the-returned-daughter.md#ah-r1287) is Mika’s second cousin. Mika saves a place. Greta chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The returned daughter*, Greta says, “I am the half sister born later. Today's visit is the first time anyone explained the family photograph with a cut edge.”

**People in this story.** [Bea Rial](#ah-r0056). The last obstacles between the bed and the view are a sticky rail and a pane with an old handprint.

**Personality.** Mika has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Mika is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Mika presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative wants the sunrise streamed to a phone. Mika asks the patient whether they want a camera in this particular morning.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Mika tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If accused of favoritism, Mika explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Gita Moss

AH-R0054 · Age 57 · Flower seller · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Gita learned to wait outside a room until someone made eye contact after walking into a family quarrel carrying congratulations. Her whole flower business depends on reading a doorway in time. Thursday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Gita asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I delivered yellow roses to the wrong bed here once. The woman asked to keep them. Today I stood back until someone told me whose view I might be blocking.”

**What Gita wants.** To bring flowers to the bedside without taking anybody's view.

**Family.** [Cian Khan](../05/01-salt-in-the-pockets.md#ah-r0610) is Gita’s younger relative by marriage. Gita picks the meeting place. Cian sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Salt in the pockets*, Cian says, “I wanted a scientific name for every crystal until the dockers started giving me better names.”

**People in this story.** [Anwar Fen](#ah-r0058). Flowers and toast have to reach the same bedside without getting in anybody's sight line.

**Personality.** Gita reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Gita trusts most.

**Appearance.** Gita is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Gita squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A different patient needs the same porter. Gita checks which part can wait and makes sure the other patient hears about the delay.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Gita walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- If told to back off, Gita does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Remy Stowe

AH-R0055 · Age 35 · Hospital scheduler · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Remy once shuffled appointments to free a consultant's afternoon and left six patients waiting in gowns. He now writes the name of the person giving up time beside the name of the person gaining it. Wednesday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Remy stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “Those eight minutes belonged to a patient waiting for a scan. I phoned him before moving the slot. He asked if we could send him a picture of what he'd bought.”

**What Remy wants.** To get the waiting scan patient an answer about the changed time.

**Family.** [Elise Kapoor](../03/04-the-spare-spectacles.md#ah-r0338) is Remy’s stepsibling. Remy keeps the photographs. Elise knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The spare spectacles*, Elise says, “I drew the scene instead of spelling it. The witness moved the desk six inches on paper.”

**People in this story.** [Ilan Peretz](#ah-r0060). They are the two people who can get the displaced scan patient his picture and his new time.

**Personality.** Remy has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Remy's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Remy has auburn hair cut square at the nape, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. When somebody else has the floor, Remy turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A relative wants the sunrise streamed to a phone. Remy asks the patient whether they want a camera in this particular morning.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Remy stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- If the joke lands badly, Remy seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Bea Rial

AH-R0056 · Age 49 · Window cleaner · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Bea has cleaned most city landmarks without ever being invited to an opening. She likes the ward because somebody always notices when the sea suddenly becomes clearer. Saturday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Bea asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “You can see an old handprint when the sun catches that pane. My daughter left it on a visit years ago. I cleaned the rest and asked the patient whether she minded the hand.”

**What Bea wants.** To leave or remove the little handprint according to the patient's wishes.

**Family.** [Denise Ho](../06/12-the-late-crossing.md#ah-r0863) is Bea’s half-sibling. Bea makes the call. Denise is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The late crossing*, Denise says, “I lost my license and kept quiet. I contribute by watching the driver's children for the round trip.”

**People in this story.** [Mika Drew](#ah-r0053). The last obstacles between the bed and the view are a sticky rail and a pane with an old handprint.

**Personality.** Bea listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Bea has one cheek that creases before the other, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Bea draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A different patient needs the same porter. Bea checks which part can wait and makes sure the other patient hears about the delay.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Bea offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- When a second witness disagrees, Bea keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Cass Pennell

AH-R0057 · Age 39 · Respiratory therapist · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Cass keeps a collection of patient descriptions of breath, from tight shirts to small rooms. Those ordinary comparisons have done more for her bedside manner than any of her examination answers. Thursday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Cass asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “She used her breath to name the colors instead of answering my questions. I wrote them down in order for her granddaughter. It's the only clinical note I've ever copied in orange pencil.”

**What Cass wants.** To pass the sequence of colors to the granddaughter in the patient's own words.

**Family.** [Zachary Teo](../05/02-the-crane-shadow.md#ah-r0614) is Cass’s cousin. Cass leaves the first voice message. Zachary calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The crane shadow*, Zachary says, “I drew the wrong crane in my design portfolio.”

**People in this story.** [Nessa Wren](#ah-r0052). They ask the patient what colors and timing she would want her family to remember.

**Personality.** Cass talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Cass's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Cass has thick, tightly coiled hair, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a little coil of soft measuring tape. When somebody else has the floor, Cass folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A relative wants the sunrise streamed to a phone. Cass asks the patient whether they want a camera in this particular morning.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Cass asks the person already using it whether they can comfortably stand, rather than presuming who needs it most.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Cass says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Anwar Fen

AH-R0058 · Age 61 · Cafeteria cook · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Anwar makes twelve kinds of toast from the same tin loaf. It is the job he kept after the hotel laid off cooks with better certificates. He has no ambition to serve important people unless they are hungry. Tuesday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Anwar looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I brought breakfast in a cup because her hands shook. She wanted to smell toast. I burned a corner the way my mother used to and let it cool on the sill.”

**What Anwar wants.** To make a hospital breakfast smell recognizably like the patient's kitchen.

**Family.** [Briar Mann](../02/01-hem-by-hem.md#ah-r0151) is Anwar’s cousin. Anwar chooses the familiar route. Briar can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Hem by hem*, Briar says, “I moved the seam that once hid a pregnancy.”

**People in this story.** [Gita Moss](#ah-r0054). Flowers and toast have to reach the same bedside without getting in anybody's sight line.

**Personality.** Anwar prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. Anwar has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, with gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears. Anwar grins on one side while the eyes stay serious during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A different patient needs the same porter. Anwar checks which part can wait and makes sure the other patient hears about the delay.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Anwar accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Anwar clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Lois Fell

AH-R0059 · Age 55 · Hospital chaplain · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Lois trained to fill silences on radio before she became a chaplain. At the hospital the hardest part of the job has been learning to let somebody else's unsteady voice finish a sentence. Thursday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Lois pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I had a reading ready. At the window she asked me to tell her the names of the ships. I didn't know any and had to let the porter be the expert.”

**What Lois wants.** To learn the names of the ships in time for the next good morning.

**Family.** [Cory Mann](../04/03-the-savings-club.md#ah-r0479) is Lois’s cousin. Lois brings the paper and a pen. Cory decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The savings club*, Cory says, “I broke the jar years ago and replaced it with one from my wedding.”

**People in this story.** [Anton Dove](#ah-r0051) (ward colleague). The porter knows this window. The chaplain is discovering how much of the morning that knowledge can hold.

**Personality.** Lois never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Lois can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Lois is easy to recognize by a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Lois touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative wants the sunrise streamed to a phone. Lois asks the patient whether they want a camera in this particular morning.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Lois asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Lois may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Ilan Peretz

AH-R0060 · Age 34 · Patient's liaison and former patient · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Ilan kept counting windows from his treatment bed to remind himself there was a street outside. The liaison badge lets him offer people company in a waiting room he used to know by its ceiling. People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Ilan by a small Friday habit. Ilan pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I took the picture for the man whose scan moved. My own scans always made me feel as if the city had carried on without me. I printed him one with the ward reflected in the glass.”

**What Ilan wants.** To put a printed sunrise photograph into the waiting patient's hand.

**Family.** [Dora Iyer](../08/11-the-hair-after-illness.md#ah-r1158) is Ilan’s older relative by marriage. Ilan calls about good news first. Dora is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The hair after illness*, Dora says, “I had the same change years ago. I kept my advice in my pocket until she asked.”

**People in this story.** [Remy Stowe](#ah-r0055). They are the two people who can get the displaced scan patient his picture and his new time.

**Personality.** Ilan often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Ilan have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Ilan has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Ilan gives a little nod before asking a hard question during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A different patient needs the same porter. Ilan checks which part can wait and makes sure the other patient hears about the delay.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Ilan takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Ilan rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.
