# The porch light

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The western residential streets.** On a western residential street a porch lamp has burned every night for thirty years. It goes dark the week the owner puts the house up for sale.

## Abel Weaver

AH-R0081 · Age 73 · Widowed homeowner · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Abel wired the porch on their first wedding anniversary because he had forgotten to buy a gift. His wife never told the neighbors that part. Losing the house without telling somebody the whole story worries him more than moving. Friday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Abel buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “My wife left the lamp on for the last nurse walking home from St. Mary. People think it was for me. I'll sell the house if the buyer lets me take the lamp to her sister.”

**What Abel wants.** To deliver the working lamp to his sister-in-law.

**Family.** [Winifred Reese](../05/01-salt-in-the-pockets.md#ah-r0602) is Abel’s cousin’s child. The family has a birthday plate with a shallow crack. Whoever borrows it is responsible for returning it in person, which has salvaged more than one bad goodbye. Abel insists on washing it by hand. Asked about *Salt in the pockets*, Winifred says, “My mother used to shake out our coats over a blue plate and read the weather in the grains.”

**People in this story.** [Orla Till](#ah-r0084) (neighbor who works nights). The night route and the porch lamp turn out to have been part of the same marriage without the nurse ever knowing it.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Abel takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Abel is easy to recognize by soft white hair brushed sideways and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Abel gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers extra for the porch lamp. Abel asks the owner whether the lamp is even part of the sale before discussing the offer.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Abel asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Abel asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Ruth Eade

AH-R0082 · Age 38 · Estate agent · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Ruth grew up in a house staged for sale every spring. She knows how exhausted a family can become trying to look as if it has never used its own kitchen. She has started scheduling viewings around one untouched family meal. Tuesday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Ruth stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I described it as original hardware. When the owner started to cry I crossed the phrase out. I still need the commission to cover my own rent, and I'll earn it with a different sentence.”

**What Ruth wants.** To sell the house with terms the owner can sign freely.

**Family.** [Ariel Powell](../10/14-the-last-home-visit.md#ah-r1481) is Ruth’s parent’s cousin. Ruth checks the date twice. Ariel brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The last home visit*, Ariel says, “I brought sweet tea for years. My oldest client said she'd waited for my last day to admit she liked it bitter.”

**People in this story.** [Verity Long](#ah-r0085). The prospective buyer and agent must decide what a lodger means for the old owner's conditions.

**Personality.** Ruth can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Ruth has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Ruth counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A shift worker turns up after the porch goes dark. Ruth keeps the worker company at the gate and lets the homeowner explain the dark.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Ruth brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Ruth asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Lev Adair

AH-R0083 · Age 54 · Electrician · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Lev tested signs for a shop chain until it replaced his team. He opened his repair business with one bulb over the door and kept a private count of drivers who slowed down on the first evening. Monday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Lev looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “The switch worked. He'd unscrewed the bulb to see who would notice. I recognized the trick because I once did it to my shop sign. I left a spare on his kitchen table.”

**What Lev wants.** To leave a working spare without exposing the owner's private test.

**Family.** [Logan Calder](../02/02-five-missing-lunches.md#ah-r0167) is Lev’s second cousin. Lev brings a spare shopping bag. Logan chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Five missing lunches*, Logan says, “I changed the roster to save wages. Now I eat alone next to five imaginary people.”

**People in this story.** [Nabil Reed](#ah-r0087). They need to open the porch post without destroying the dates hidden behind the wiring.

**Personality.** Lev can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Lev can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Lev is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a moss-green cardigan with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand, Lev looks toward the door whenever a name is called when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers extra for the porch lamp. Lev asks the owner whether the lamp is even part of the sale before discussing the offer.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Lev asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Lev suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Orla Till

AH-R0084 · Age 49 · Overnight nurse · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Orla measures the night shift in the distance between places where somebody knows she's walking home. She never thought the porch owner knew about her. Asking for the pedometer back would be easier than telling him what the light meant. Sunday often includes a stop at Juniper Street, where Orla knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I used the lamp to know exactly how far I had left to walk. I'm giving him my old pedometer with the count from the gate to my door still taped on its back.”

**What Orla wants.** To let the owner know how far his lamp carried a stranger.

**Family.** [Zara Barclay](../09/10-a-four-o-clock-kitchen.md#ah-r1291) is Orla’s second cousin. Orla asks before bringing somebody along. Zara can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A four o'clock kitchen*, Zara says, “I love feeding strangers. I don't want my children to think that's easier than feeding them.”

**People in this story.** [Abel Weaver](#ah-r0081) (porch owner). The night route and the porch lamp turn out to have been part of the same marriage without the nurse ever knowing it.

**Personality.** Orla carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Out of work clothes, look for a boxy denim jacket and a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. Orla has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Orla looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A shift worker turns up after the porch goes dark. Orla keeps the worker company at the gate and lets the homeowner explain the dark.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Orla writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Orla offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Verity Long

AH-R0085 · Age 41 · Newly divorced school secretary · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Verity became a school secretary because records showed her exactly which families needed another reminder before a deadline. In her own divorce she has been much worse at asking people for time. On Tuesday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Verity knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I can afford the house only with a lodger. The owner asked if that meant strangers all the time. I told him a stranger is how my children got the aunt who raised them.”

**What Verity wants.** To make a fair room offer to a prospective lodger.

**Family.** [Ruth Farah](../04/14-laundry-for-a-stranger.md#ah-r0589) is Verity’s cousin raised in the same household. Verity makes room at the table. Ruth brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Laundry for a stranger*, Ruth says, “I built her an iron rest from a broken tile.”

**People in this story.** [Ruth Eade](#ah-r0082). The prospective buyer and agent must decide what a lodger means for the old owner's conditions.

**Personality.** Verity has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Verity is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Verity gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers extra for the porch lamp. Verity asks the owner whether the lamp is even part of the sale before discussing the offer.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Verity agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Verity says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Gus Lark

AH-R0086 · Age 64 · Hardware merchant · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Gus can remember the size of a light fitting from the way its owner cups both hands. The old ledger interests him because it records his shop under three successive names and never once misspells his. On Tuesday, near Studio Boulevard, Gus waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I sold the owner two bulbs every winter. He always wrote my name in a little book beside the price. I'd like to borrow the book for the shop's fiftieth birthday.”

**What Gus wants.** To display the bulb ledger alongside a photograph of the old shop sign.

**Family.** [Reuben Sawyer](../03/03-the-widow-s-dividend.md#ah-r0330) is Gus’s cousin. Gus saves a place. Reuben chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The widow's dividend*, Reuben says, “I want one share for my grandson so somebody younger will keep asking how the rent is set.”

**People in this story.** [Della Keys](#ah-r0090). The hardware ledger gives the committee a less costly way to commemorate the lamp than buying it.

**Personality.** Gus treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Gus lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Gus has tightly curled gray hair, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. When somebody else has the floor, Gus squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- A shift worker turns up after the porch goes dark. Gus keeps the worker company at the gate and lets the homeowner explain the dark.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Gus separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Gus says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Nabil Reed

AH-R0087 · Age 48 · Porch carpenter · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Nabil used to hide dates on roof timbers to prove he'd had a part in a house. Since his daughter started asking about his jobs he has preferred marks that future owners can actually find. People around Ferris Deli know Nabil by a small Saturday habit. Nabil asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “We rebuilt the post around that live lamp during a blackout anniversary. I can take it down without losing the pencil dates inside. I want the daughters there when I open it.”

**What Nabil wants.** To save the pencil dates when dismantling the porch post.

**Family.** [Lou Diaz](../08/07-a-prize-nobody-entered.md#ah-r1115) is Nabil’s cousin raised in the same household. Nabil picks the meeting place. Lou sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *A prize nobody entered*, Lou says, “I promised the prize money to a printer before the author answered.”

**People in this story.** [Lev Adair](#ah-r0083). They need to open the porch post without destroying the dates hidden behind the wiring.

**Personality.** Nabil is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Nabil is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Nabil is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip, Nabil starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers extra for the porch lamp. Nabil asks the owner whether the lamp is even part of the sale before discussing the offer.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Nabil asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Nabil agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## June Wicks

AH-R0088 · Age 16 · Newspaper carrier · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** June is saving paper-route wages for a secondhand keyboard. She stopped to look at the dark porch on a morning when lateness would cost her the whole route's tip. Thursday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where June usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I thought the dark porch meant he'd died. I left six papers to make sure people would stop. Now he wants me to come in for coffee and explain the headlines to him.”

**What June wants.** To read the headlines with the man behind the dark porch.

**Family.** [Eden Sun](../02/04-the-second-dog.md#ah-r0181) is June’s older cousin. June keeps the photographs. Eden knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The second dog*, Eden says, “I adopted him after he jumped into my van. I never noticed the other family's number under his tag.”

**People in this story.** [Ari Pennrose](#ah-r0089). One has the morning papers and the other the night-light figures. Together they can reconstruct how long the porch stayed dark.

**Personality.** June tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far June will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. June has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with a center part and straight chestnut hair. June starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A shift worker turns up after the porch goes dark. June keeps the worker company at the gate and lets the homeowner explain the dark.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. June waits at the lit stop with them and checks whether the next service is one they can actually board.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, June repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Ari Pennrose

AH-R0089 · Age 36 · Energy auditor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Ari's job rewards the number of wasted kilowatts identified in a district. An elderly nurse kept asking where his figures accounted for safe journeys home. He has started walking the blocks he used to measure from the office. On Sunday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Ari looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I used that lit window in a slide about waste. The night nurse was in the audience and stayed behind. I've asked her to record what the walk sounds like in darkness.”

**What Ari wants.** To measure the usefulness of the light on an actual night walk.

**Family.** [Alex Cotton](../05/15-a-boat-for-sale-twice.md#ah-r0749) is Ari’s half-sibling. Ari makes the call. Alex is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A boat for sale twice*, Alex says, “I want the name painted over. It's my mother's name and she wants it back for her own dinghy.”

**People in this story.** [June Wicks](#ah-r0088). One has the morning papers and the other the night-light figures. Together they can reconstruct how long the porch stayed dark.

**Personality.** Ari is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Ari asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Ari has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Ari listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers extra for the porch lamp. Ari asks the owner whether the lamp is even part of the sale before discussing the offer.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Ari finds out what the destination actually looks like to the traveler and gives directions in those terms.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Ari accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Della Keys

AH-R0090 · Age 58 · Neighborhood association treasurer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Della is very good at raising money for things a committee can own. Paying for a journey whose only result is seeing a sister again feels uncomfortably difficult to photograph for the annual report. On Friday, near Lighthouse Cove, Della takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “We offered to buy the lamp as a landmark. He reminded me his wife's sister is still alive. I changed the vote to a travel allowance so he can deliver it himself.”

**What Della wants.** To pay for the owner's visit to his wife's sister.

**Family.** [Ariel Pinto](../08/14-the-new-language-at-dinner.md#ah-r1185) is Della’s half-sibling. Della leaves the first voice message. Ariel calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The new language at dinner*, Ariel says, “I translated our first visit and cried in the lift because he sounded exactly like my late father.”

**People in this story.** [Gus Lark](#ah-r0086). The hardware ledger gives the committee a less costly way to commemorate the lamp than buying it.

**Personality.** Della is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Della has a blunt cut just below the ears, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. When somebody else has the floor, Della rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A shift worker turns up after the porch goes dark. Della keeps the worker company at the gate and lets the homeowner explain the dark.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Della asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Della thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.
