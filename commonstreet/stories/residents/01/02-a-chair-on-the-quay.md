# A chair on the quay

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Port of Asphalt.** A wooden kitchen chair turns up beside the rail sidings at the Port of Asphalt. A retiring crane operator recognizes the height marks cut into its back.

## Owen Vale

AH-R0011 · Age 65 · Retiring crane operator · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Owen learned the crane in a cab still marked with his predecessor's cigarette burns. He made a point of leaving it clean for the next worker and now feels embarrassed by how much he wants his own marks preserved on the chair. People around Paper & Pine Books know Owen by a small Sunday habit. Owen starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “My dad marked my height on that chair after every voyage. When my parents split, we thought it went to the dump. I want one last picture of it hanging above the quay, which my supervisor thinks is a terrible idea.”

**What Owen wants.** To bring the chair home with a picture his granddaughter can keep.

**Family.** [Una Bell](../10/06-two-birthdays-in-one-ward.md#ah-r1409) is Owen’s younger cousin. Owen keeps the photographs. Una knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Two birthdays in one ward*, Una says, “I work the kitchen. The boy asked me to save one slice for the cleaner who comes when everybody else has gone.”

**People in this story.** [Ivor Lake](#ah-r0013). One wants the family to recognize a childhood chair. The other has to decide which repairs would erase its history. Also in the story is [Fen Ellis](#ah-r0017) (childhood friend).

**Personality.** Owen is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Owen is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Owen has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a neat white crop. Owen leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Two families claim the kitchen chair. Owen asks each claimant what they remember about the seat before the marks are shown.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Owen checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Owen agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Bettine Cross

AH-R0012 · Age 37 · Stevedore · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Bettine goes to sea to visit cousins when holidays permit, so she understands the weight an ordinary object gains after months afloat. The crew member who first entrusted her with the chair worries her more than the people claiming it on shore. People around Tideline Market know Bettine by a small Friday habit. Bettine picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I carried it off a ship because a woman in the crew asked if anyone here repaired cane seats. Now half the port has a claim on it. I still owe that woman an answer.”

**What Bettine wants.** To give the seafarer who handed over the chair a straight answer.

**Family.** [Tristan Collins](../10/06-two-birthdays-in-one-ward.md#ah-r1405) is Bettine’s half-sibling. Bettine makes the call. Tristan is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Two birthdays in one ward*, Tristan says, “I am the parent afraid there won't be another birthday.”

**People in this story.** [Willa True](#ah-r0016). They disagree about moving the chair indoors before the ship's crew gets an answer.

**Personality.** Bettine tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Bettine will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Bettine has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Bettine holds eye contact until the other person has finished during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A farewell photographer asks everyone to move aside. Bettine checks that the granddaughter can see the chair before people start arranging the adults.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Bettine draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Bettine repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Ivor Lake

AH-R0013 · Age 54 · Furniture restorer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Ivor charges by the finished repair because disability makes his hours unpredictable. Families sometimes mistake the quiet pace of his workshop for leisure. He has been trying to get his apprentice to quote without embarrassment. Tuesday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Ivor asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “The lower marks run down instead of up. Somebody measured a child on their knees. I offered to mend the seat and leave the pencil lines exactly where the family put them.”

**What Ivor wants.** To make the cane seat sound enough for an adult without disturbing the marks.

**Family.** [Javier Lai](../10/08-no-lifeguard-at-dusk.md#ah-r1423) is Ivor’s cousin raised in the same household. Ivor leaves the first voice message. Javier calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *No lifeguard at dusk*, Javier says, “I own the cart beside the steps. I can sit for company after the shutters come down, without promising to be a lifeguard.”

**People in this story.** [Owen Vale](#ah-r0011). One wants the family to recognize a childhood chair. The other has to decide which repairs would erase its history.

**Personality.** Ivor is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Ivor asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Ivor has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a mustard canvas overshirt. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Ivor leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- Two families claim the kitchen chair. Ivor asks each claimant what they remember about the seat before the marks are shown.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Ivor asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Ivor accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Tamsin Leung

AH-R0014 · Age 42 · Rail clerk · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** After having her first child Tamsin expressed milk in a locked rail office. She has never forgotten the humiliation of asking for a key to a room nobody was using. She makes a fuss about chairs because a chair once mattered that much. Wednesday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Tamsin knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I put the chair in lost property under freight fittings. That's where I used to sit on breaks during my pregnancy. I'd like the old break room reopened before we give it away.”

**What Tamsin wants.** To restore a break-room seat for the next worker returning from leave.

**Family.** [Cian Ingram](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0977) is Tamsin’s stepsibling. Tamsin chooses the familiar route. Cian can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Cian says, “I brought takeaway chips to the public bench.”

**People in this story.** [Fen Ellis](#ah-r0017). A chalk gull lets them test two very different ideas about who used to sit here.

**Personality.** Tamsin is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Tamsin is easy to recognize by a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Tamsin touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A farewell photographer asks everyone to move aside. Tamsin checks that the granddaughter can see the chair before people start arranging the adults.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Tamsin offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Tamsin thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Mateo Serrat

AH-R0015 · Age 59 · Seafarers' cook · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** Mateo packed his kitchen into one fish crate when his first shore lease ended. He can cook a week's dinners with the contents of that crate and can still describe the flat by counting the steps to the landing. On Wednesday, near the bank steps, Mateo takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I remember carrying that chair up four flights to my first shore flat. I'm sending the family the curry recipe I had to stand on it to reach.”

**What Mateo wants.** To cook the old shore-flat supper for both sides of the family.

**Family.** [Briar Poole](../06/05-the-first-straight-paycheck.md#ah-r0792) is Mateo’s younger cousin. Mateo brings the paper and a pen. Briar decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The first straight paycheck*, Briar says, “I bought from him regularly. Losing his number matters less than losing the only person who asked if I'd eaten.”

**People in this story.** [Leon Armas](#ah-r0018). They have undertaken to get a retirement meal and a granddaughter to the same side of the port gate.

**Personality.** Mateo loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Mateo remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Mateo has a thin scattering of white hair over the crown, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a black overshirt with pearl buttons. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Mateo leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- Two families claim the kitchen chair. Mateo asks each claimant what they remember about the seat before the marks are shown.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Mateo sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- When invited to tell the story later, Mateo includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Willa True

AH-R0016 · Age 49 · Night watchkeeper · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** A night in lost property taught Willa that the people most comfortable claiming a thing often know least about it. She lets quieter owners describe one private detail before she brings anything out from behind the door. People around the approach to Seabright Court know Willa by a small Sunday habit. Willa likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “Somebody once took my mother's effects from a loading bay marked free. I moved the chair indoors and made the first person claiming it tell me what was under the seat.”

**What Willa wants.** To establish ownership from a memory somebody could never learn from a photograph.

**Family.** [Anita Gill](../04/15-the-engine-lullaby.md#ah-r0597) is Willa’s stepsibling. Willa calls about good news first. Anita is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The engine lullaby*, Anita says, “I recorded it with my wedding tape by mistake.”

**People in this story.** [Bettine Cross](#ah-r0012). They disagree about moving the chair indoors before the ship's crew gets an answer.

**Personality.** Willa has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a pale blue work shirt and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Willa has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Willa gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A farewell photographer asks everyone to move aside. Willa checks that the granddaughter can see the chair before people start arranging the adults.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Willa asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When somebody trades on a title, Willa asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Fen Ellis

AH-R0017 · Age 45 · Sign painter · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Fen drew birds as a child because the family moved too often to keep pets. He signed his first paid shopfront with a tiny gull where only someone opening the shutter could see it. Friday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Fen notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “The chalk gull underneath is mine. I used to draw birds under things at friends' houses. This is the first time one of them has flown back.”

**What Fen wants.** To find one of the other childhood gulls before an old house is sold.

**Family.** [Hollis Osei](../07/09-a-tenant-who-stays.md#ah-r0984) is Fen’s older relative by marriage. Fen remembers the birthdays. Hollis asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A tenant who stays*, Hollis says, “I would share the big room for a season. Nobody asked because they thought an old person always wants silence.”

**People in this story.** [Tamsin Leung](#ah-r0014). A chalk gull lets them test two very different ideas about who used to sit here. Also in the story is [Owen Vale](#ah-r0011) (childhood friend).

**Personality.** Fen finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Fen is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Fen looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two families claim the kitchen chair. Fen asks each claimant what they remember about the seat before the marks are shown.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Fen helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- When thanked for a shared success, Fen points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Leon Armas

AH-R0018 · Age 56 · Union delegate · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Leon has given fifteen retirement speeches, all of them wrong about one thing. Now he takes the person concerned for tea first and asks which story they would hate to hear repeated at the microphone. On Thursday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Leon can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “Everybody wants a retirement speech. He wants his granddaughter admitted past the gate for ten minutes. I spent the morning finding a quiet place on the public side for the picture.”

**What Leon wants.** To get the granddaughter a place at the farewell within reach of her grandfather.

**Family.** [Briar Patel](../04/14-laundry-for-a-stranger.md#ah-r0586) is Leon’s second cousin. Leon offers the first invitation. Briar gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *Laundry for a stranger*, Briar says, “My son needs a good shirt for court. I asked the volunteer if the stranger might lend it.”

**People in this story.** [Mateo Serrat](#ah-r0015). They have undertaken to get a retirement meal and a granddaughter to the same side of the port gate.

**Personality.** Leon keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Leon wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Leon is easy to recognize by a high puff tied with plain cord and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole, Leon presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A farewell photographer asks everyone to move aside. Leon checks that the granddaughter can see the chair before people start arranging the adults.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Leon sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Leon offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Merit Shaw

AH-R0019 · Age 40 · Freight forwarder · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Merit's first forwarding job was sending home a dead sailor's boots. She learned that a parcel can arrive before a family is ready to open it. Her quotations include a space for the recipient to choose a date. On Thursday, near Juniper Street, Merit knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I offered to pay to ship it to his sister. Then I learned they haven't spoken in eleven years. I gave him the quote in a sealed envelope he can throw away.”

**What Merit wants.** To leave the sister free to decline both the chair and the reconciliation.

**Family.** [Adriana Arnett](../02/04-the-second-dog.md#ah-r0188) is Merit’s half-sibling. Merit puts the kettle on. Adriana chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The second dog*, Adriana says, “I have the delivery photo of both dogs in the same yard.”

**People in this story.** [Nell Oram](#ah-r0020). The shipping quote and the recorded recollections are the only things the absent sister can receive without taking ownership.

**Personality.** Merit always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Merit is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Merit has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. In conversation Merit turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- Two families claim the kitchen chair. Merit asks each claimant what they remember about the seat before the marks are shown.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Merit asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Merit suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Nell Oram

AH-R0020 · Age 31 · Oral historian · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Nell has a splendid memory for voices and a terrible memory for places. She asks interviewees to walk while they talk. It makes the recordings windy and prevents her inventing a street somebody never walked. Tuesday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Nell picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “He told me three versions of who cut the first mark. I asked his permission to record all three. Families ought to be allowed to change their minds on tape.”

**What Nell wants.** To let people listen to the conflicting accounts with their speakers' permission.

**Family.** [Terry Sullivan](../05/10-midnight-cinnamon.md#ah-r0693) is Nell’s cousin raised in the same household. Nell chooses an ordinary story to tell. Terry asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Midnight cinnamon*, Terry says, “I thought it was my grandmother's spice bread.”

**People in this story.** [Merit Shaw](#ah-r0019). The shipping quote and the recorded recollections are the only things the absent sister can receive without taking ownership.

**Personality.** Nell has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Nell knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Nell has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with brown hair pinned away from the temples. Nell presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A farewell photographer asks everyone to move aside. Nell checks that the granddaughter can see the chair before people start arranging the adults.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Nell repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Nell asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.
