# The empty berth

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**South Shore Harbor.** One of South Shore Harbor's four boats is temporarily lifted out for repairs. A rectangle of clean water makes the marina's private waiting list a subject of public curiosity.

## Sylvia Moros

AH-R0141 · Age 61 · Marina owner and property millionaire · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Sylvia learned the marina business selling the cheapest berth from a folding table. She lives extremely comfortably now and can still identify the four engines by sound from her kitchen. Her daughter suspects she loves the sounds more than the assets. People around the brick passage off the shop row know Sylvia by a small Sunday habit. Sylvia takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I inherited the smallest boat and bought the water around it in installments. The empty berth would earn more than my first house cost. I'd like my daughter to tell me what she thinks it's worth in summer afternoons.”

**What Sylvia wants.** To ask the family what a Sunday berth means before taking a high offer.

**Family.** [Yvette Hill](../10/13-one-last-dress-rehearsal.md#ah-r1473) is Sylvia’s second cousin. Sylvia chooses the familiar route. Yvette can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *One last dress rehearsal*, Yvette says, “I paid the lights for the extra hour. I want my own child's group to hear how little that really cost.”

**People in this story.** [Fenn Roche](#ah-r0144). A broker's imaginary vacancy has to be answered by the woman who really owns the four berths.

**Personality.** Sylvia reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Sylvia trusts most.

**Appearance.** Sylvia has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. Back on the street, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. In conversation Sylvia gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A broker offers cash to keep the dock empty. Sylvia finds out who was promised the empty dock time before discussing a price.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Sylvia asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- If told to back off, Sylvia does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Ivo Carden

AH-R0142 · Age 48 · Boat mechanic · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Ivo makes bent timbers the slow way when the result warrants it. His grandfather taught using short silences instead of instructions. Ivo worries that his impatient apprentice might mistake one of those silences for dismissal. Saturday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Ivo likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “The boat needs a plank my grandfather steamed into shape forty years ago. I could finish by Tuesday with plywood. I've asked the owner for a week and a chance to teach an apprentice the bend.”

**What Ivo wants.** To teach the apprentice to bend the replacement plank.

**Family.** [Anthony Clement](../08/01-reopening-at-six.md#ah-r1056) is Ivo’s older cousin. Ivo brings the paper and a pen. Anthony decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Reopening at six*, Anthony says, “I thought my pension meant timing didn't matter.”

**People in this story.** [Robin Kiln](#ah-r0149) (sailing student). The student can tell the mechanic what a beginner's foot does on the slippery step.

**Personality.** Ivo has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Ivo's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a long navy raincoat and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Ivo has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with brown hair pinned away from the temples. Ivo listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A junior rower says the wet step feels unsafe. Ivo stops the lesson there and asks the rower to show the troublesome patch from a stable spot.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Ivo offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- If the joke lands badly, Ivo seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Nell Zane

AH-R0143 · Age 35 · Municipal water sampler · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Nell became a municipal sampler after her family stopped swimming near a private slip. She keeps a private inventory of the best city views that you can only see while doing a poorly paid public job. On Thursday, near Ferris Deli, Nell asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “The best sample is right in the private berth. I have been reaching around a hull for three years. I asked for permanent access and brought the owners the first clear jar to look at.”

**What Nell wants.** To get dependable sample access even after the boat comes back.

**Family.** [Benedict Holt](../07/14-the-carer-s-afternoon.md#ah-r1040) is Nell’s cousin raised in the same household. Nell calls about good news first. Benedict is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The carer's afternoon*, Benedict says, “I always ask what she did with her time. This Wednesday I put the kettle on and let her offer the first word.”

**People in this story.** [Esme Valejo](#ah-r0148). They inspect the same private water from the very different viewpoints of safety and public access.

**Personality.** Nell listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Nell has a loose dark braid, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, Nell counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A broker offers cash to keep the dock empty. Nell finds out who was promised the empty dock time before discussing a price.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Nell asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- When a second witness disagrees, Nell keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Fenn Roche

AH-R0144 · Age 40 · Yacht broker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Fenn has sold boats to people who never once left the marina in them. He is very good at selling the feeling of a Saturday that doesn't yet exist. A buyer with new cushions and nowhere to put them makes the trick too literal for comfort. Thursday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Fenn usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I've been selling an imaginary fifth berth in people's daydreams. One client has already bought cushions. I owe her a phone call before she buys the boat.”

**What Fenn wants.** To tell the buyer the truth about the nonexistent vacancy.

**Family.** [Jules Wong](../06/06-home-with-stairs.md#ah-r0803) is Fenn’s cousin. Fenn remembers the birthdays. Jules asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *Home with stairs*, Jules says, “I priced the missing rail. My family did the original job and used the cheapest possible ending.”

**People in this story.** [Sylvia Moros](#ah-r0141). A broker's imaginary vacancy has to be answered by the woman who really owns the four berths.

**Personality.** Fenn talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Fenn's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a watch on a cracked leather band. Fenn has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with short black hair swept back. Fenn looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A junior rower says the wet step feels unsafe. Fenn stops the lesson there and asks the rower to show the troublesome patch from a stable spot.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Fenn asks each person to say what they believe is actually owed before anybody proposes a payment.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Fenn says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Agnes Osei

AH-R0145 · Age 42 · Rowing coach · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Agnes coaches children whose families assume rowing is too expensive. The first lesson is how to stand on a wet dock without looking as if you're scared. She admits to the children that sometimes she is scared too. Tuesday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Agnes checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “My juniors need a place to learn how to step off a dock as much as they need water. I asked for Sunday mornings until the repair is finished. I'll sign for every scratch on the timber.”

**What Agnes wants.** To give the juniors supervised time on the empty dock.

**Family.** [Alex Scott](../04/10-closing-soup.md#ah-r0545) is Agnes’s cousin. Agnes offers the first invitation. Alex gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *Closing soup*, Alex says, “I put a rind in that was meant for my own dinner.”

**People in this story.** [Rafe Costa](#ah-r0147). A coach asking to borrow the docks meets somebody who remembers when nobody needed to ask.

**Personality.** Agnes prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a dark green knitted vest and a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. Agnes has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with a blunt cut just below the ears. Agnes raises one eyebrow long before saying yes during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A broker offers cash to keep the dock empty. Agnes finds out who was promised the empty dock time before discussing a price.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Agnes asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Agnes clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Fern Gillen

AH-R0146 · Age 29 · Boat cleaner · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Fern cleans below decks where the expensive conversations above arrive as muffled footsteps. Taking an uncle to hear the shrimp promises the pleasure of introducing him to a sound the paying guests all miss. On Saturday, near the blocks below Foundry Lofts, Fern checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I know the four boats by the noises their owners leave behind. The empty slot is the first place I've heard the little shrimp click. I'm bringing my hard-of-hearing uncle down on a quiet morning.”

**What Fern wants.** To bring the uncle to hear the marina during its quietest hour.

**Family.** [Mika Black](../04/06-the-waiting-room-plant.md#ah-r0506) is Fern’s cousin. Fern puts the kettle on. Mika chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The waiting-room plant*, Mika says, “I mistook its red tie for an allergy marker.”

**People in this story.** [Petra Noel](#ah-r0150). They are arranging two very different little family outings during the same week of repair.

**Personality.** Fern never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Fern can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Fern has black hair twisted into a small knot, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Fern counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A junior rower says the wet step feels unsafe. Fern stops the lesson there and asks the rower to show the troublesome patch from a stable spot.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Fern starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Fern may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Rafe Costa

AH-R0147 · Age 57 · Shrimp seller · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Rafe left commercial fishing when his back stopped tolerating nets. Selling shrimp close enough to smell the water is the compromise that stuck. He has been teaching a great-niece to tie the smallest knots first. On Saturday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Rafe usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “My father tied up here before there was a gate. I don't want the whole dock back. I want to show his great-granddaughter where to set a bucket so it doesn't tip.”

**What Rafe wants.** To show the next generation where their family worked.

**Family.** [Amanda Hart](../03/09-the-upstairs-piano.md#ah-r0383) is Rafe’s half-sibling. Rafe chooses an ordinary story to tell. Amanda asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The upstairs piano*, Amanda says, “I live above the landing. I offered my breakfast hour for a concert while we figure it out.”

**People in this story.** [Agnes Osei](#ah-r0145). A coach asking to borrow the docks meets somebody who remembers when nobody needed to ask.

**Personality.** Rafe often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Rafe have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Rafe has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. In conversation Rafe presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- A broker offers cash to keep the dock empty. Rafe finds out who was promised the empty dock time before discussing a price.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Rafe asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Rafe rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Esme Valejo

AH-R0148 · Age 38 · Marine insurer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** Esme's first week insuring watercraft was spent in a dry office pricing hurricanes. The first claim she handled involved a bruised knee two feet from shore. She wants her next apprentice to begin with the step. People around the steps at Pier Beach know Esme by a small Monday habit. Esme watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I put a price on a storm that might come once in fifty years. I missed the slippery step people use every morning. I'm coming back in deck shoes with the junior rowers.”

**What Esme wants.** To repair the slippery step and revise the inspection notes.

**Family.** [Lucia Webb](../04/04-station-steps-in-rain.md#ah-r0487) is Esme’s stepsibling. Esme checks the date twice. Lucia brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Station steps in rain*, Lucia says, “I came to interpret for one family and ended up translating an argument about a wet dog.”

**People in this story.** [Nell Zane](#ah-r0143). They inspect the same private water from the very different viewpoints of safety and public access.

**Personality.** Esme thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Esme sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Esme has close-cropped dark curls, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Esme squares up pieces of paper while thinking. Esme changes into well-worn deck shoes before walking the pontoons.

**Under pressure**

- A junior rower says the wet step feels unsafe. Esme stops the lesson there and asks the rower to show the troublesome patch from a stable spot.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Esme offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Esme asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Robin Kiln

AH-R0149 · Age 19 · Sailing student · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Robin got the sailing place on a very good essay about the sea and had to borrow nearly everything required to reach it. Offering the next pupil a shelf of shoes is a way to improve the award without disparaging the people who made it. People around Maré Coffee know Robin by a small Monday habit. Robin likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “My scholarship covers lessons and nothing you wear to them. I came in borrowed shoes and fell on that step. I want a shelf where the next beginner can borrow a pair that fits.”

**What Robin wants.** To stock a shelf of properly fitting loan shoes.

**Family.** [Jody Baker](../02/01-hem-by-hem.md#ah-r0157) is Robin’s first cousin once removed. Robin brings a spare shopping bag. Jody chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Hem by hem*, Jody says, “I thought the small pocket held a ring. It held bus money in case she wanted to leave.”

**People in this story.** [Ivo Carden](#ah-r0142) (dockside mentor). The student can tell the mechanic what a beginner's foot does on the slippery step.

**Personality.** Robin gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Robin privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Robin is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a charcoal zip cardigan with one cuff folded back twice, Robin rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A broker offers cash to keep the dock empty. Robin finds out who was promised the empty dock time before discussing a price.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Robin keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Robin offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Petra Noel

AH-R0150 · Age 54 · Harbor pilot · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Petra navigates large vessels for people who assume watching the horizon is a purely professional habit. Guiding a boat into one precise spot so her mother can see an old front porch will be her smallest and most exacting pilotage job. People around the downtown fountain know Petra by a small Thursday habit. Petra keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “From offshore you can see straight through the gap to the porch where I grew up. I asked the mechanic for the exact hour the hull goes back so I can take my mother out to see it once.”

**What Petra wants.** To get her mother onto the water while the porch is visible.

**Family.** [Valentina Carey](../05/09-an-address-in-pencil.md#ah-r0686) is Petra’s second cousin. Somebody once took a family child's drawing to be framed and trimmed away the signature. The trimming hurt worse than spoiling the picture. At home they still ask where the signature goes. Petra checks whether its maker actually wants credit. Asked about *An address in pencil*, Valentina says, “I didn't know we had two numbering systems before the viaduct opened.”

**People in this story.** [Fern Gillen](#ah-r0146). They are arranging two very different little family outings during the same week of repair.

**Personality.** Petra uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Petra well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Petra has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. Back on the street, the familiar outfit is a narrow-striped sweater with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation Petra folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A junior rower says the wet step feels unsafe. Petra stops the lesson there and asks the rower to show the troublesome patch from a stable spot.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Petra asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- After a family quarrel, Petra brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.
