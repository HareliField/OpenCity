# Ten fingers

**Verve Nail Studio.** Verve Nail Studio's oldest regular asks ten people to choose one color each for her birthday appointment and forbids anyone to coordinate.

## Suki Mossman

AH-R0101 · Age 47 · Nail technician · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Suki learned nails on her grandmother's hands, knuckles bent from long hospital shifts. She offers her wrist for clients to lean on and lets them decide when to take their own hand back. People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Suki by a small Saturday habit. Suki prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I knew her husband by the hand he held out for her on our front step. This is her first birthday without him. I saved the untidiest finger for the shade he bought her by mistake.”

**What Suki wants.** To keep the birthday appointment a celebration the client directs.

**Family.** [Layla March](../07/13-a-dance-for-two-fathers.md#ah-r1029) is Suki’s second cousin. Suki offers the first invitation. Layla gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *A dance for two fathers*, Layla says, “I am the other father who didn't get invited.”

**People in this story.** [Fay March](#ah-r0102) (birthday customer). The birthday woman has trusted these hands with every difficult appointment since her husband died.

**Personality.** Suki never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Suki can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Suki has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. After the last appointment, the familiar outfit is a plum-colored cardigan with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. In conversation Suki looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- A birthday guest starts rearranging other people's colors. Suki asks the birthday client whether she wants the colors moved.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Suki asks where they stood and what they could hear, leaving the rest open instead of filling it with guesses.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Suki may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Fay March

AH-R0102 · Age 78 · Retired seamstress · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Fay sewed wedding dresses for women who had opinions and mothers who had more. She trained herself to hear the quietest voice in the fitting room. Now that family speaks softly around her she would rather they raise a cheerful racket. People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Fay by a small Monday habit. Fay knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I am the birthday girl. Everybody has taken to speaking softly around me. I asked for ten colors because I wanted to hear them quarrel over something cheerful.”

**What Fay wants.** To hear her friends argue and laugh over the ten colors.

**Family.** [Ivy Perreault](../02/04-the-second-dog.md#ah-r0183) is Fay’s younger relative by marriage. Fay puts the kettle on. Ivy chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The second dog*, Ivy says, “I took the first report and wrote brown. The little girl gave me nine different browns to choose from.”

**People in this story.** [Suki Mossman](#ah-r0101) (longtime nail technician). The birthday woman has trusted these hands with every difficult appointment since her husband died.

**Personality.** Fay often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Fay have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Fay has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for silver temples and a dark fringe. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. In conversation Fay looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- The birthday woman asks for five minutes by herself. Fay lets the room empty and puts the next invitation in her hands.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Fay writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Fay rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Imri Black

AH-R0103 · Age 23 · Salon apprentice · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Imri has superb steady hands and still has trouble telling a customer that an idea won't work. Clear varnish over a gold thread is an argument made with the hands before having to make it aloud. Thursday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Imri asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I chose clear and got laughed at until I showed them the gold thread underneath. It came off my first uniform. I'd like her to see I'm ready for a chair of my own.”

**What Imri wants.** To win an independent chair with a design the birthday client likes.

**Family.** [Harley Hooper](../03/15-the-tide-clock.md#ah-r0446) is Imri’s cousin. Imri chooses an ordinary story to tell. Harley asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The tide clock*, Harley says, “I want two clocks on the wall so workers with ferry connections can actually make them.”

**People in this story.** [Pari Zed](#ah-r0104). Clear varnish and a wrongly mixed red give them an unexpected argument about who gets to name a color.

**Personality.** Imri thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Imri sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** After the last appointment, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. Imri has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Imri presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A birthday guest starts rearranging other people's colors. Imri asks the birthday client whether she wants the colors moved.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Imri asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Imri asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Pari Zed

AH-R0104 · Age 39 · Color mixer · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** Pari mixes shade samples by the salon window because half the colors fashionable under the lamp look dead outdoors. An odd red taught her to let a client name what went wrong before trying to repair it. Sunday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Pari picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I made the wrong red three summers ago. She wore it to her anniversary dinner anyway. I'm bringing the original sample and letting her name it.”

**What Pari wants.** To let the customer give the difficult red its permanent salon name.

**Family.** [Teresa Ellis](../05/01-salt-in-the-pockets.md#ah-r0605) is Pari’s second cousin. Pari checks the date twice. Teresa brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Salt in the pockets*, Teresa says, “I wanted to return every coat to the right worker.”

**People in this story.** [Imri Black](#ah-r0103). Clear varnish and a wrongly mixed red give them an unexpected argument about who gets to name a color.

**Personality.** Pari gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Pari privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. Pari has a long face and high cheekbones, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Pari starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The birthday woman asks for five minutes by herself. Pari lets the room empty and puts the next invitation in her hands.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Pari writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Pari offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Greta Falk

AH-R0105 · Age 34 · Wedding planner · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** Greta has put on more elaborate weddings than she can afford for herself. Her sisters have seen her change a table plan during dessert. Letting them surprise her about clothes is a considerable gamble. Thursday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Greta checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I was halfway through arranging the colors into a gradient when she caught me. My own wedding is in two months. I'm letting my sisters pick their dresses.”

**What Greta wants.** To get through her own wedding without arranging everyone's clothes.

**Family.** [Denise Zhou](../08/06-two-names-on-the-chart.md#ah-r1103) is Greta’s older cousin. Greta brings a spare shopping bag. Denise chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Two names on the chart*, Denise says, “I built the paper index and recognized my teenage handwriting.”

**People in this story.** [Remi Ash](#ah-r0107). One wants an elegant arrangement. The other wants the birthday guest to enjoy a wonderfully rude shade name.

**Personality.** Greta uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Greta well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Greta has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Look for a long plait tucked inside the collar. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Greta folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A birthday guest starts rearranging other people's colors. Greta asks the birthday client whether she wants the colors moved.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Greta moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- After a family quarrel, Greta brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Lark Kane

AH-R0106 · Age 31 · Deaf dance teacher · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Lark ran a children's dance class with the music turned off so the pupils would watch each other breathe. Parents called it silence. The children remember how noisy ten pairs of socks could be on a clean floor. Thursday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Lark knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I picked a green that flashes under stage light because I watch hands for a living. I've invited her to stand in the front row and conduct the warmup with those fingers.”

**What Lark wants.** To give the birthday client control of the warmup at one dance class.

**Family.** [Beatrice Su](../08/05-the-boy-on-the-corner.md#ah-r1092) is Lark’s older cousin. Lark asks before bringing somebody along. Beatrice can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The boy on the corner*, Beatrice says, “I used to buy from the boy's dad. I'm offering childcare and he has good reason to check on me first.”

**People in this story.** [Liv Oren](#ah-r0109). Conducting with ten bright fingers gives the photographer a way of taking the picture without posing the hands.

**Personality.** Lark feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Lark is easy to recognize by a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf, Lark gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The birthday woman asks for five minutes by herself. Lark lets the room empty and puts the next invitation in her hands.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Lark asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Lark lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Remi Ash

AH-R0107 · Age 29 · Paint shop clerk · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Remi learned color names in a trade catalogue and swear words from the customers misreading them. Sending the birthday woman into helpless laughter was the first sales demonstration that made the whole shop sit down. Sunday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Remi buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “Our yellow has a vulgar stock name. My grandmother hated being told what words she could say. I whispered it to the birthday girl and she made me say it louder.”

**What Remi wants.** To let the woman enjoy the yellow's outrageous name in company.

**Family.** [Salma Hayward](../07/12-the-crash-anniversary.md#ah-r1017) is Remi’s older relative by marriage. Remi makes room at the table. Salma brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The crash anniversary*, Salma says, “I lost a brother somewhere else. I sent one note to the family and left my phone number off it.”

**People in this story.** [Greta Falk](#ah-r0105). One wants an elegant arrangement. The other wants the birthday guest to enjoy a wonderfully rude shade name.

**Personality.** Remi treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Remi has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, a straight nose and deep-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Remi gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- A birthday guest starts rearranging other people's colors. Remi asks the birthday client whether she wants the colors moved.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Remi checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Remi changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Galen Howe

AH-R0108 · Age 52 · Barber · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Galen's father had one barber chair and a cash tin that also paid the household bills. Losing the corner lease hurt more than he has admitted to the salon next door. Lending the first chair is a manageable beginning. People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Galen by a small Thursday habit. Galen checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “We used to compete for the same corner lease. I chose the blue that is still on my shopfront. I'll lend her the folding chair my first customers sat in for the group picture.”

**What Galen wants.** To bring the original barber chair over for the birthday portrait.

**Family.** [Jean Shen](../05/01-salt-in-the-pockets.md#ah-r0604) is Galen’s cousin raised in the same household. Galen saves a place. Jean chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *Salt in the pockets*, Jean says, “I took a photograph because it looked like our street under the one snowfall I remember.”

**People in this story.** [Marta Ely](#ah-r0110). The borrowed barber chair goes into a room whose rent will have to be negotiated tomorrow.

**Personality.** Galen is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Galen is concerned.

**Appearance.** After the last appointment, look for a washed violet jersey and one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. Galen has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a blunt cut just below the ears. Galen touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The birthday woman asks for five minutes by herself. Galen lets the room empty and puts the next invitation in her hands.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Galen asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- When someone invents an answer, Galen asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Liv Oren

AH-R0109 · Age 36 · Bridal photographer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Liv learned photography at events where bridesmaids spent hours arranging pictures the guests had already taken. She has developed a liking for hands that are carrying, pouring, buttoning or busy enough to forget her. People around the steps at Pier Beach know Liv by a small Saturday habit. Liv watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I kept trying to pose the hands until she grabbed the kettle. That's the picture I'm giving her. You can see all ten colors and exactly who still pours the tea.”

**What Liv wants.** To deliver the candid kettle picture with the clients' agreement.

**Family.** [Frankie Armas](../07/13-a-dance-for-two-fathers.md#ah-r1030) is Liv’s younger cousin. After a relative came home with a walker, everyone offered errands and nobody remembered the threshold. The two of them still measure doors before offering a room to anyone. Liv notices the physical difficulty first. Asked about *A dance for two fathers*, Frankie says, “I used to think my family was embarrassing.”

**People in this story.** [Lark Kane](#ah-r0106). Conducting with ten bright fingers gives the photographer a way of taking the picture without posing the hands.

**Personality.** Liv loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Liv is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a washed violet jersey with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Liv leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A birthday guest starts rearranging other people's colors. Liv asks the birthday client whether she wants the colors moved.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Liv lets the child choose whether to stand by the door or sit beside the window while another adult gets help.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Liv offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Marta Ely

AH-R0110 · Age 59 · Salon landlord · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Marta owns the salon walls after a long career maintaining other people's properties. She still sees a repair bill behind every pretty display. The birthday client is unusually good at making her see the display as well. Sunday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Marta checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I came to talk about the rent and found myself holding a tiny bottle of mauve. I came back the next morning with the figures. She deserved one evening without them.”

**What Marta wants.** To bring the rent figures back on a day that belongs to business.

**Family.** [Terry March](../09/12-the-driving-lesson-fund.md#ah-r1318) is Marta’s younger relative by marriage. Marta keeps the photographs. Terry knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The driving lesson fund*, Terry says, “I have a wealthy dad. The teacher asked me how many free practice hours he gave me before I called my pass hard-earned.”

**People in this story.** [Galen Howe](#ah-r0108). The borrowed barber chair goes into a room whose rent will have to be negotiated tomorrow.

**Personality.** Marta enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Marta is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Marta has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for silver temples and a dark fringe. After the last appointment, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Marta steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- The birthday woman asks for five minutes by herself. Marta lets the room empty and puts the next invitation in her hands.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Marta keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Marta keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.
