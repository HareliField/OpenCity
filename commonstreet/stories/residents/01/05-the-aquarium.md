# The aquarium

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The brick passage off the shop row.** A landlord empties a ground-floor flat off the brick passage. The tenant has gone to stay with family. A hundred-litre aquarium with two living fish remains on the pavement.

## Denny Ames

AH-R0041 · Age 32 · Street-level drug dealer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Denny started selling to cover a brother's missed share of rent and kept selling after that emergency ended. He likes being the person everyone can find. Handing over the aquarium key means admitting that some uses of being needed are better than others. On Sunday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Denny prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I used to feed those fish while the tenant did dialysis. Everybody in the passage knows how I pay rent. Nobody knew I had his spare key until I gave it to the housing worker.”

**What Denny wants.** To keep the fish alive and reopen contact with the displaced tenant.

**Family.** [Marina Carvalho](../02/14-saturday-keys.md#ah-r0289) is Denny’s older cousin. Denny makes room at the table. Marina brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Saturday keys*, Marina says, “I keep an emergency key list in my hymn book.”

**People in this story.** [Bina Marsh](#ah-r0045). A surrendered spare key gives the housing worker a way to call a displaced man from beside his own fish.

**Personality.** Denny treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Denny lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Denny has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Denny rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A reporter asks which neighbor held the aquarium key. Denny asks whether the tenant wants the key holder named before saying anything to the reporter.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Denny asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Denny says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Harriet Sloan

AH-R0042 · Age 44 · Property manager · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Harriet's first building flooded while she was on holiday. Ever since, she tries to close every open task before going home. She is capable of real generosity once she notices who is being hurried out of the picture. Wednesday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Harriet often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I wrote abandoned on the inventory because the flat was empty. My son would have asked where the fish were supposed to go. I bought them a secondhand tank with my inspection fee.”

**What Harriet wants.** To replace the lost tank and correct the word on the inventory.

**Family.** [Amos Tate](../03/12-one-unposted-detour.md#ah-r0413) is Harriet’s half-sibling. Harriet saves a place. Amos chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *One unposted detour*, Amos says, “I lost a day's ice. I want the contractor to pay the stallholders who lost sales too.”

**People in this story.** [Ariel Kaye](#ah-r0047). The manager's abandonment inventory and the advocate's pavement measurements tell different stories about the same home.

**Personality.** Harriet is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Harriet is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Harriet has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a washed violet jersey with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Harriet draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- The tenant calls while everyone is arguing over the tank. Harriet quietens the group long enough for the tenant to hear the tank and decide what should happen next.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Harriet takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Harriet agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Cyrus Cole

AH-R0043 · Age 38 · Aquarium hobbyist and payroll clerk · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Cyrus took up fishkeeping during a miserable year of night payroll. The little routines gave him one piece of a day he could predict. He will rearrange a whole week's plans for an animal that needs food dropped on a particular side. On Friday, near Studio Boulevard, Cyrus waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “One fish has a crooked jaw. It needs its food dropped on the left. I took the difficult one, then learned separating the pair made both stop eating.”

**What Cyrus wants.** To bring the two fish back into the same working tank.

**Family.** [Aubrey Farah](../04/14-laundry-for-a-stranger.md#ah-r0584) is Cyrus’s older relative by marriage. Cyrus picks the meeting place. Aubrey sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Laundry for a stranger*, Aubrey says, “I accused my carer of mixing up the bags. I am taking the apology to her home on foot.”

**People in this story.** [Mo Gill](#ah-r0048). They try the shop's spare pump on the two fish whose feeding habits only one of them really knows.

**Personality.** Cyrus tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Cyrus will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Cyrus has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Cyrus leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- A reporter asks which neighbor held the aquarium key. Cyrus asks whether the tenant wants the key holder named before saying anything to the reporter.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Cyrus puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Cyrus repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Pat Doran

AH-R0044 · Age 41 · Former burglar and glazier · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Pat learned glazing while serving a sentence. Customers sometimes ask for lurid stories when all he wants is to explain the price of glass. His daughter trusts the nights he calls from a bus stop more than the nights he promises to be home early. People around Ferris Deli know Pat by a small Monday habit. Pat asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I chipped a pane carrying the tank out. Once I'd have disappeared before anybody saw. I spent the evening cutting a replacement and missed the last bus to my daughter's.”

**What Pat wants.** To replace the chipped pane before the tank is filled again.

**Family.** [Marina Liu](../07/14-the-carer-s-afternoon.md#ah-r1036) is Pat’s half-sibling. Pat keeps the photographs. Marina knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The carer's afternoon*, Marina says, “I said I'd pay for respite and then lost my biggest account.”

**People in this story.** [Tessa Abreu](#ah-r0049) (probation officer). They finish a probation conversation with a heavy corner of glass between their hands.

**Personality.** Pat is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Pat asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Pat has a loose dark braid, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. When somebody else has the floor, Pat starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- The tenant calls while everyone is arguing over the tank. Pat quietens the group long enough for the tenant to hear the tank and decide what should happen next.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Pat checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Pat accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Bina Marsh

AH-R0045 · Age 33 · Housing outreach worker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** Bina left a landlord's office with a useful set of phone numbers and an uncomfortable knowledge of how quickly a spreadsheet can turn a home into vacant stock. She carries a spare charger so residents can make their own calls. Saturday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Bina notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “He kept telling me the filter was humming wrong. It took me twenty minutes to understand he was asking whether his home would still be there tomorrow. I rang him from beside the glass.”

**What Bina wants.** To get the tenant a direct answer about returning home.

**Family.** [Aidan Henry](../08/07-a-prize-nobody-entered.md#ah-r1113) is Bina’s older cousin. Bina makes the call. Aidan is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A prize nobody entered*, Aidan says, “I sat on the jury and recognized our family's kitchen in the scene.”

**People in this story.** [Denny Ames](#ah-r0041). A surrendered spare key gives the housing worker a way to call a displaced man from beside his own fish.

**Personality.** Bina is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Bina is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Bina draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A reporter asks which neighbor held the aquarium key. Bina asks whether the tenant wants the key holder named before saying anything to the reporter.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Bina draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Bina thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Miro Quade

AH-R0046 · Age 30 · Grocery stocker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Miro works split shifts so his boy can stay at one school. Their walk by the aquarium takes six minutes. On a week when other things have changed without warning, both of them rely more than they admit on those six minutes. On Wednesday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Miro usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “Those orange fish were the first things my boy named. I brought a bucket and promised him they would be fine. Now I have a child who expects daily news and two fish who don't know my name.”

**What Miro wants.** To keep the boy updated even if the fish have to go elsewhere.

**Family.** [Marco Lam](../10/09-a-house-divided-kindly.md#ah-r1437) is Miro’s parent’s cousin. Miro leaves the first voice message. Marco calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A house divided kindly*, Marco says, “I want a paved path for my walking frame. The other gardener's favorite flowers are growing exactly where it should go.”

**People in this story.** [Leni Crow](#ah-r0050). One owes a child news of the fish. The other owes the tenant a chance to hear a song before anyone else does.

**Personality.** Miro loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Miro remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a boxy denim jacket and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Miro has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Miro presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The tenant calls while everyone is arguing over the tank. Miro quietens the group long enough for the tenant to hear the tank and decide what should happen next.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Miro accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- When invited to tell the story later, Miro includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Ariel Kaye

AH-R0047 · Age 48 · Tenant advocate · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Ariel's family once moved with their possessions spread across three neighbors' kitchens. Every inventory she makes now starts by asking what somebody needs on their first night and only then considers what it costs to replace. On Thursday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Ariel likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “The housing photographs kept cropping the tank out. I measured the bit of pavement the family suddenly occupied. I want that measurement in the appeal beside the flat's floor area.”

**What Ariel wants.** To get the pavement inventory admitted to the tenant's appeal.

**Family.** [Desmond Calloway](../09/12-the-driving-lesson-fund.md#ah-r1313) is Ariel’s half-sibling. Ariel chooses the familiar route. Desmond can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The driving lesson fund*, Desmond says, “I can drive but never had a chance to sit the test.”

**People in this story.** [Harriet Sloan](#ah-r0042). The manager's abandonment inventory and the advocate's pavement measurements tell different stories about the same home.

**Personality.** Ariel has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Ariel has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Ariel starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A reporter asks which neighbor held the aquarium key. Ariel asks whether the tenant wants the key holder named before saying anything to the reporter.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Ariel tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When somebody trades on a title, Ariel asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Mo Gill

AH-R0048 · Age 53 · Pet supply wholesaler · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Mo's warehouse is profitable because almost everything moves. One shelf for damaged, usable goods troubles him more than a large donation because it means paying attention every week to who might need them. People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Mo by a small Friday habit. Mo pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I gave them an old pump marked display only. My warehouse is full of things nobody can sell and somebody could use. I want my partners to let the outreach worker choose a shelf.”

**What Mo wants.** To open a shelf of usable damaged stock to the outreach worker.

**Family.** [Grey Black](../10/03-the-gift-nobody-wants.md#ah-r1374) is Mo’s half-sibling. Mo brings the paper and a pen. Grey decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The gift nobody wants*, Grey says, “I rent the shop below. The family's generosity could decide where my family eats next year.”

**People in this story.** [Cyrus Cole](#ah-r0043). They try the shop's spare pump on the two fish whose feeding habits only one of them really knows.

**Personality.** Mo finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Mo has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Mo draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- The tenant calls while everyone is arguing over the tank. Mo quietens the group long enough for the tenant to hear the tank and decide what should happen next.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Mo offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- When thanked for a shared success, Mo points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Tessa Abreu

AH-R0049 · Age 37 · Probation officer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Tessa can finish a form in a moving bus. She is trying to learn when a form completed that efficiently leaves out the only reason the visit mattered. People around the steps at Pier Beach know Tessa by a small Sunday habit. Tessa watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “My appointment with the glazier ended up on a pavement with our sleeves wet. He was late because he was holding up one corner. That's the sentence I put in his file.”

**What Tessa wants.** To make sure the glazier's missed minutes are understood in context.

**Family.** [Gerard Ito](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0971) is Tessa’s half-sibling. Tessa calls about good news first. Gerard is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Gerard says, “I suggested pretending we'd already eaten. My mum turned the menu over and told the children the truth.”

**People in this story.** [Pat Doran](#ah-r0044) (supervised client). They finish a probation conversation with a heavy corner of glass between their hands.

**Personality.** Tessa keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Tessa wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Tessa has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for a center part and straight chestnut hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Tessa rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A reporter asks which neighbor held the aquarium key. Tessa asks whether the tenant wants the key holder named before saying anything to the reporter.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Tessa takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Tessa offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Leni Crow

AH-R0050 · Age 26 · Spoken-word host · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Leni first took a microphone to tell jokes about the laundromat where she waited for her mum. She finds private pain terribly easy to turn into a public performance and is learning to ask whose applause she wants. Friday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Leni checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I wanted to turn the fish into a song about dispossession. The tenant asked if I knew anything about fish. I shelved the song and learned how to test the water.”

**What Leni wants.** To earn the tenant's trust before asking to perform the aquarium story.

**Family.** [Dario Moreau](../04/12-the-ladder-crossing.md#ah-r0564) is Leni’s parent’s cousin. Leni remembers the birthdays. Dario asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The ladder crossing*, Dario says, “I measured the crossing too narrowly. Two people carrying an ordinary thing showed me the error.”

**People in this story.** [Miro Quade](#ah-r0046). One owes a child news of the fish. The other owes the tenant a chance to hear a song before anyone else does.

**Personality.** Leni always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Leni is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Leni has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, an old plum windbreaker. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, Leni gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- The tenant calls while everyone is arguing over the tank. Leni quietens the group long enough for the tenant to hear the tank and decide what should happen next.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Leni offers an ordinary errand that lets the distressed person leave without being followed by an audience.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Leni suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.
