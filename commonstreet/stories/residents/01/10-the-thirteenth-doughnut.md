# The thirteenth doughnut

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Night Shift Donuts.** Night Shift Donuts sells a dozen in a box designed for thirteen. Someone starts leaving a thirteenth doughnut for the person who opens the shop next door.

## Joss Farin

AH-R0091 · Age 43 · Overnight baker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Joss's husband can taste lemon in a room where nobody else smells it. Their marriage runs on such very local bits of expert knowledge. He wants the bakery to know its late customers half as well. Wednesday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Joss likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I made the first one with lemon because my husband hated lemon and wouldn't eat it on the way. Now people leave me notes telling me what their night tasted like. I want to bake one of those notes.”

**What Joss wants.** To turn one customer's description of a hard night into a breakfast flavor.

**Family.** [Bianca Breen](../09/02-the-worker-who-couldn-t-read-the-joke.md#ah-r1215) is Joss’s stepsibling. Joss puts the kettle on. Bianca chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The worker who couldn't read the joke*, Bianca says, “I run the break room. I won't make the wronged worker stand up at a meeting unless he wants to.”

**People in this story.** [Drew Milo](#ah-r0093). A lemon doughnut passed between their shifts before either knew why the other was awake. Also in the story is [Sera Noor](#ah-r0096) (night-crew organizer).

**Personality.** Joss keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Joss wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Joss has close-cropped dark curls, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a black overshirt with pearl buttons. It usually goes with one cuff folded back twice. When somebody else has the floor, Joss folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A customer tries to sell the spare doughnut story to a brand. Joss asks the baker and the intended recipient for their separate consent.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Joss asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Joss offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Lane Clegg

AH-R0092 · Age 39 · Diabetic pastry critic · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Lane reviews pastries by keeping separate notes on first bite and last bite. Portion sizes took on new meaning after the diabetes diagnosis. Sharing a cake is part pleasure, part calculation, and entirely Lane's business. Monday often includes a stop at the steps at Pier Beach, where Lane watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I cut the gift into four and gave three pieces away. Somebody wrote that I disliked it. I came back with the knife my grandmother used for celebrations and explained what a little can mean.”

**What Lane wants.** To introduce a tasting portion customers can easily divide.

**Family.** [Miles Sato](../09/07-school-after-sixty.md#ah-r1266) is Lane’s older relative by marriage. Lane chooses an ordinary story to tell. Miles asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *School after sixty*, Miles says, “I brought the fountain pen I saved for finishing school.”

**People in this story.** [Jon Solis](#ah-r0095). They compare two ways of sharing a pastry without treating a small portion as a refusal.

**Personality.** Lane always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Lane is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Lane has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a straight nose and deep-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. When somebody else has the floor, Lane touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A late worker finds somebody has eaten the saved breakfast. Lane finds out which shift the breakfast was for before offering a replacement.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Lane tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Lane suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Drew Milo

AH-R0093 · Age 51 · Night security guard · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Drew found the guard work after a restaurant closure and is tired of people describing the empty night as restful. He knows how much company a lighted bakery can provide somebody walking a long round. Monday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Drew knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I got the first free one and ate it sitting on the loading step. I'd just learned my son was moving out. I've paid for the next man's breakfast every Friday since.”

**What Drew wants.** To keep the Friday breakfast paid ahead for the next tired worker.

**Family.** [Terry Morse](../07/01-the-uniform-money.md#ah-r0903) is Drew’s second cousin. Drew checks the date twice. Terry brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The uniform money*, Terry says, “I thought my daughter was being vain about the right shade of skirt until I remembered my first day here.”

**People in this story.** [Joss Farin](#ah-r0091). A lemon doughnut passed between their shifts before either knew why the other was awake.

**Personality.** Drew has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Drew knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Drew is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Drew looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A customer tries to sell the spare doughnut story to a brand. Drew asks the baker and the intended recipient for their separate consent.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Drew asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Drew asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Teri Okafor

AH-R0094 · Age 34 · Box designer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Teri still has the envelope containing the first rejection from a packaging firm. She'd printed sample boxes that wasted a strip of card. What an accountant calls waste has often been the part people remember. Tuesday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Teri checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “The thirteenth slot was a cutting mistake I was ashamed of. I'm taking the old die to the design school with a box full of people's notes and a copy of the rejected invoice.”

**What Teri wants.** To show the cutting mistake to students together with the actual box.

**Family.** [Owen Green](../08/08-a-summer-marriage.md#ah-r1124) is Teri’s cousin. Teri brings a spare shopping bag. Owen chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A summer marriage*, Owen says, “I am their oldest friend. My speech is mostly about the groom's ex and I'm rewriting every line.”

**People in this story.** [Nora Reiss](#ah-r0100). The packaging error cannot become a bank forecast until someone decides whether the thirteenth slot is for sale.

**Personality.** Teri enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Teri is easy to recognize by a long plait tucked inside the collar and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Teri grins on one side while the eyes stay serious when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A late worker finds somebody has eaten the saved breakfast. Teri finds out which shift the breakfast was for before offering a replacement.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Teri stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Teri puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Jon Solis

AH-R0095 · Age 27 · Dishwasher and new father · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Jon learned to sleep between dish shifts and the baby's first feeds. He can carry a box level on a bus without thinking. He has yet to make it home with icing that looks the way it did in the shop. On Friday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Jon knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I kept the lemon one in a bag all day for my wife. The sugar stuck to the paper. She liked peeling it off better than eating the cake. I asked for the next one unglazed.”

**What Jon wants.** To bring his wife a breakfast she can eat straight off the wrapper.

**Family.** [Iva Hayes](../08/07-a-prize-nobody-entered.md#ah-r1117) is Jon’s second cousin. Jon asks before bringing somebody along. Iva can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A prize nobody entered*, Iva says, “I translated two pages as a favor. My name is on the cover and the author's wishes aren't.”

**People in this story.** [Lane Clegg](#ah-r0092). They compare two ways of sharing a pastry without treating a small portion as a refusal.

**Personality.** Jon has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Jon can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Jon has a loose dark braid, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Jon rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A customer tries to sell the spare doughnut story to a brand. Jon asks the baker and the intended recipient for their separate consent.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Jon tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- If a friend borrows money, Jon agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Sera Noor

AH-R0096 · Age 45 · Union organizer · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Sera spent years recording decisions people never saw because they were working while the meeting took place. She now tries to place a vote somewhere floury or noisy enough to prove workers actually came. Monday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Sera knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “Our night crew never attends the monthly breakfast meeting. I put a question under each doughnut and collected answers after midnight. We moved the vote to shift change.”

**What Sera wants.** To hold the vote at an hour the night crew can attend.

**Family.** [Tali Moreno](../04/01-the-button-card.md#ah-r0451) is Sera’s cousin raised in the same household. Sera makes room at the table. Tali brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The button card*, Tali says, “I recognized the button from my first school blazer and came back with its twin.”

**People in this story.** [Dina Locke](#ah-r0099). The breakfast vote now has to reach the worker who shows up after everybody else leaves. Also in the story is [Joss Farin](#ah-r0091) (breakfast supplier).

**Personality.** Sera takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Sera can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Sera is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a charcoal zip cardigan with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges, Sera gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A late worker finds somebody has eaten the saved breakfast. Sera finds out which shift the breakfast was for before offering a replacement.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Sera moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Sera turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Vic Cho

AH-R0097 · Age 37 · Radio producer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Vic collects sounds at a volume that lets the people producing them hear each other. Some of the best recordings in the cupboard have no permission form. They will stay in the cupboard. Wednesday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Vic checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I recorded the crunch for a midnight program and accidentally caught two men making up in the background. I asked them which part I could keep. They chose the silence after it.”

**What Vic wants.** To give the recorded couple a final choice about the broadcast.

**Family.** [Franklin Sun](../04/12-the-ladder-crossing.md#ah-r0563) is Vic’s second cousin. Vic saves a place. Franklin chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The ladder crossing*, Franklin says, “I saw the ladder's shadow stretch across my old playground and asked for a photograph.”

**People in this story.** [Eli Carr](#ah-r0098). A night broadcast and two music tickets link the bakery's sounds to an audience beyond the block.

**Personality.** Vic admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Vic learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. Vic has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, with a loose dark braid. Vic folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A customer tries to sell the spare doughnut story to a brand. Vic asks the baker and the intended recipient for their separate consent.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Vic asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Vic describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Eli Carr

AH-R0098 · Age 40 · Flour delivery driver · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Eli inherited a flour route and spent six months telling friends it was temporary. He finally stopped apologizing for it after hearing his own band's music pouring through the door of the bakery he was supplying. Sunday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Eli stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “My father's flour route paid for my first drum kit. I can tell who is on the oven by the smell on the loading step. I'm leaving the baker a pair of tickets under the thirteenth slot.”

**What Eli wants.** To bring the baker to a show paid for by the flour route.

**Family.** [Ramona Penn](../06/02-nine-unpaid-hours.md#ah-r0765) is Eli’s second cousin. Eli picks the meeting place. Ramona sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Nine unpaid hours*, Ramona says, “I left the contractor over favoritism. They still owe me two goodbye shifts and a borrowed radio.”

**People in this story.** [Vic Cho](#ah-r0097). A night broadcast and two music tickets link the bakery's sounds to an audience beyond the block.

**Personality.** Eli has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Eli is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Eli counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A late worker finds somebody has eaten the saved breakfast. Eli finds out which shift the breakfast was for before offering a replacement.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Eli tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If accused of favoritism, Eli explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Dina Locke

AH-R0099 · Age 56 · Street sweeper · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Dina sweeps four blocks before dawn and can tell who works late by the things they throw away. Being recognized by a bakery employee without her cart startled her more than being recognized with it. People around the market by Market Flats know Dina by a small Thursday habit. Dina often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I get there after every good thing is gone. One morning they wrote my shift time on the lid. I want to learn the name of the person who knew it without asking.”

**What Dina wants.** To thank the person who held breakfast until the street was swept.

**Family.** [Ruben Ali](../09/03-the-million-dollar-flat.md#ah-r1230) is Dina’s younger cousin. Dina keeps the photographs. Ruben knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The million-dollar flat*, Ruben says, “I came for a property feature. She introduced me to the fitter who can make the bath usable for another ten years.”

**People in this story.** [Sera Noor](#ah-r0096). The breakfast vote now has to reach the worker who shows up after everybody else leaves.

**Personality.** Dina reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Dina trusts most.

**Appearance.** Dina has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for close-cropped dark curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an old plum windbreaker with a small enamel bird on the lapel. In conversation Dina presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- A customer tries to sell the spare doughnut story to a brand. Dina asks the baker and the intended recipient for their separate consent.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Dina sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- If told to back off, Dina does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Nora Reiss

AH-R0100 · Age 42 · Small business lender · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Nora once refused credit to a café that fed late workers for free. The café closed and the workers' lateness rose across the street. She is still working out what a lender should have seen in that sequence. Sunday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Nora prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “The bank wants a sales forecast for the spare slot. I've sat through three dawns trying to find a number for it. I'm lending against the dozen and leaving the extra one out.”

**What Nora wants.** To get the loan approved on what a dozen actually earns.

**Family.** [Geraldine Quintero](../03/04-the-spare-spectacles.md#ah-r0337) is Nora’s parent’s cousin. Nora makes the call. Geraldine is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The spare spectacles*, Geraldine says, “I got my old glasses back and finally read the note I found in their case.”

**People in this story.** [Teri Okafor](#ah-r0094). The packaging error cannot become a bank forecast until someone decides whether the thirteenth slot is for sale.

**Personality.** Nora has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Nora's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Nora has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with short copper curls. Nora steadies a cup with both hands while listening during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A late worker finds somebody has eaten the saved breakfast. Nora finds out which shift the breakfast was for before offering a replacement.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Nora looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- If the joke lands badly, Nora seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.
