# Three minutes at the bridge

[When the lights go out](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Coast Ridge Road.** A group of Coast Ridge commuters realizes a three-minute difference in shift start times would let them share rides for an entire winter.

## Patricia Keane

AH-R1461 · Age 40 · School-age child's parent and sign fitter · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember holding my boy's hand until the school bell actually rang.” On Wednesday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Patricia can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I need those three minutes to get my youngest through the school gate with a hug.”

**What Patricia wants.** To keep the morning school hug.

**Family.** [Mara Bly](../07/02-the-absent-signature.md#ah-r0917) is Patricia’s stepsibling. Patricia can always ask for five minutes alone. Mara asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The absent signature*, Mara says, “I want to speak to my aunt without mentioning the berth once.”

**People in this story.** [Alexei Zhang](#ah-r1462). Sharing the first supplier visit protects a parent's last minutes at the school gate.

**Personality.** Patricia feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Patricia is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole, Patricia listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A shift changes by exactly three minutes. Patricia checks the school pickup and shift clocks together before changing the route.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Patricia asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Patricia lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Alexei Zhang

AH-R1462 · Age 49 · Delivery coordinator · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember letting another worker receive a difficult supplier.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Alexei by a small Thursday habit. Alexei knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I can start earlier if someone else receives the first delivery. I've been pretending to be indispensable there for years.”

**What Alexei wants.** To share the first supplier visit.

**Family.** [Frances Jimenez](../05/06-two-left-slippers.md#ah-r0656) is Alexei’s younger cousin. Alexei is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Frances calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Two left slippers*, Frances says, “I kept the wrong slipper as a joke until someone at the bus stop offered me its mate.”

**People in this story.** [Patricia Keane](#ah-r1461). Sharing the first supplier visit protects a parent's last minutes at the school gate. Also in the story is [Sylvia Kang](#ah-r1469) (payroll colleague).

**Personality.** Alexei treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Alexei has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a train token tied to a zipper pull. In conversation Alexei touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A carpool passenger needs an unplanned Friday stop. Alexei asks the pharmacy passenger and the other riders which stop works for them.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Alexei offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Alexei changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Kit Curtis

**Life in the city.** “I still remember calculating a week's wages in taxi receipts.” Friday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Kit stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I have been paying taxi fares with credit. I showed the crew my actual monthly statement before the vote.”

**What Kit wants.** To get through winter without further credit fares.

**Family.** [Gale Levin](../02/11-one-sleeve-short.md#ah-r0256) is Kit’s stepsibling. Kit brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Gale makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *One sleeve short*, Gale says, “I paid for a matching cuff lining on condition the customer never learns my name.”

**People in this story.** [Sylvia Kang](#ah-r1469). The worker living on costly taxis takes an actual statement to the person checking the clock rounding.

**Personality.** Kit loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Kit has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. In conversation Kit counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A carpool passenger needs an unplanned Friday stop. Kit asks the pharmacy passenger and the other riders which stop works for them.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Kit draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Kit offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

AH-R1466 · Age 47 · Early shift bookkeeper · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember spending one shared journey quietly after a family illness.” On Wednesday, near the market by Market Flats, Santiago often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I used to travel in complete silence. I asked the others to let me have one quiet morning a week.”

**What Santiago wants.** To claim one quiet ride each week.

**Family.** [Nadia Black](../04/11-the-fitting-mirror.md#ah-r0556) is Santiago’s cousin. Santiago sets the time to leave. Nadia picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The fitting mirror*, Nadia says, “I am tired of brides apologizing to the glass.”

**Appearance.** Santiago has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. In conversation Santiago looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**
