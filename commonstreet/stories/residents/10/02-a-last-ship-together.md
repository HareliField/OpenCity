# A last ship together

[When the lights go out](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Port of Asphalt.** Two lifelong dock workers will retire a week apart. Their supervisors can offer them one final shared unloading shift or separate paid farewell days.

## Mika Williams

AH-R1361 · Age 64 · Retiring stevedore · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being given the first cup of tea on a shift with strangers.” Friday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Mika pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I started beside a stranger and want one final day's work beside my friend even if we talk about nothing important.”

**What Mika wants.** To work a final ordinary shift beside a friend.

**Family.** [Zachary Silva](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1240) is Mika’s half-sibling. Mika sets the time to leave. Zachary picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Zachary says, “I was off duty and brought the letter from my neighbor to the desk.”

**People in this story.** [Ariel Sawyer](#ah-r1362) (longtime workmate). The two retiring workers have earned both a shared shift and a few private minutes at its end.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Mika is easy to recognize by iron-gray waves and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Mika looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The two old workers are offered separate farewell days. Mika asks each worker separately what kind of last day they want.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Mika finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Mika listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Ariel Sawyer

AH-R1362 · Age 66 · Retiring crane worker · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember shutting a crane cab door after a hard goodbye.” On Thursday, near Breakwater Way, Ariel stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I had planned to sit in the cab alone and cry. I need him to know a shared shift still allows one closed door.”

**What Ariel wants.** To arrange one private goodbye inside the crane cab.

**Family.** [Hollis Fong](../03/13-the-empty-drum-stool.md#ah-r0424) is Ariel’s younger relative by marriage. Ariel brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Hollis checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The empty drum stool*, Hollis says, “I only play on kitchen pots. The stool was higher than our worktop, so I stood up.”

**People in this story.** [Mika Williams](#ah-r1361) (longtime workmate). The two retiring workers have earned both a shared shift and a few private minutes at its end.

**Personality.** Ariel is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Ariel loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a plum-colored cardigan and a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. Ariel has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with a long silver braid. Ariel listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A younger worker is asked to perform the final lift. Ariel lets the trainee carry out the job they are qualified for and keeps space for the retirees to watch.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Ariel moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If somebody calls for applause, Ariel gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Stanley Hull

AH-R1363 · Age 51 · Dock foreman · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember using my paid hour to cover a colleague's daughter's birthday.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Stanley notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I am the foreman who cannot offer both options. I have put my own paid hour into the pot.”

**What Stanley wants.** To offer an hour that lets both retirees choose.

**Family.** [Tess Walker](../06/04-the-saturday-swap.md#ah-r0784) is Stanley’s half-sibling. Stanley asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Tess remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The Saturday swap*, Tess says, “I want to swap so I can meet the sister my father only mentioned in his will.”

**People in this story.** [Clyde Schmidt](#ah-r1367). The foreman's hour gives the younger hand a real chance to work beside the departing crew.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Stanley takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Stanley is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a cropped bottle-green coat with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Stanley draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The two old workers are offered separate farewell days. Stanley asks each worker separately what kind of last day they want.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Stanley asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Stanley asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Vincent Trent

AH-R1364 · Age 35 · Freight rigger · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my trainers watching the first load I handled without them.” Sunday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Vincent prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I used to be their apprentice. I want them to stand back and see a lift go right without either of them directing it.”

**What Vincent wants.** To show the two mentors a lift their trainee can handle.

**Family.** [Amanda Roy](../08/10-the-first-delivery-fee.md#ah-r1148) is Vincent’s older cousin. Vincent asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Amanda remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The first delivery fee*, Amanda says, “I used to do those drops while my baby slept in the sling.”

**People in this story.** [Maria Jackson](#ah-r1369). A parent's request to see the ship lets an apprentice demonstrate a clean lift where the family can see it.

**Personality.** Vincent can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Vincent has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a washed violet jersey with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Vincent rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A younger worker is asked to perform the final lift. Vincent lets the trainee carry out the job they are qualified for and keeps space for the retirees to watch.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Vincent helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Vincent asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Jamie Hamilton

AH-R1365 · Age 47 · Industrial engraver · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember spelling a nickname the way a man actually said it.” People around Maré Coffee know Jamie by a small Tuesday habit. Jamie likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I make the retirement plaques. One misspelling on the old time sheets has become a nickname they both want on the brass.”

**What Jamie wants.** To put the familiar spellings on the plaques.

**Family.** [Parker Valdez](../04/12-the-ladder-crossing.md#ah-r0569) is Jamie’s stepsibling. Jamie is free to hang up first. Parker makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The ladder crossing*, Parker says, “I was late for an interview. I described the crossing when they asked how I handle delays.”

**People in this story.** [Marcel Easton](#ah-r1370). The person making the plaque brings it to the room where the kettle and the crew actually are.

**Personality.** Jamie can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Jamie can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Jamie has short black hair swept back, one cheek that creases before the other, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, Jamie turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- The two old workers are offered separate farewell days. Jamie asks each worker separately what kind of last day they want.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Jamie stays where the child can see the shop door and asks the neighboring counter to help find the adult expected there.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Jamie suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Joel Arendt

AH-R1366 · Age 64 · Retired canteen worker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember packing lunches an hour before anyone else stirred.” On Tuesday, near Harbour Bakery, Joel buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I spent forty years packing those two lunches. My partner asked what retirement will change for me.”

**What Joel wants.** To give the retiring worker's partner a real voice about home life.

**Family.** [Ely Kern](../01/08-a-tide-at-noon.md#ah-r0075) is Joel’s younger cousin. Joel chooses whether to use it. Ely saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *A tide at noon*, Ely says, “I sent guests down in polished shoes. One guest took hers off and stayed for hours.”

**People in this story.** [Lionel Wu](#ah-r1368). Packed lunches and one last scorecard complicate the story that only two people are retiring.

**Personality.** Joel carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a charcoal zip cardigan and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Joel has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, with a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. Joel steadies a cup with both hands while listening during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A younger worker is asked to perform the final lift. Joel lets the trainee carry out the job they are qualified for and keeps space for the retirees to watch.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Joel brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Joel offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Clyde Schmidt

AH-R1367 · Age 30 · New dock loader · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember walking into a port office under somebody else's lucky number.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Clyde by a small Wednesday habit. Clyde starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I am the younger hand who gets the vacancy. I am proud of it and have promised them their old table whenever they visit.”

**What Clyde wants.** To keep visiting rights at the old table.

**Family.** [Jasmine Miranda](../05/09-an-address-in-pencil.md#ah-r0689) is Clyde’s second cousin. Clyde is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Jasmine calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *An address in pencil*, Jasmine says, “I wanted the house number on a necklace so my mother can tell a driver where she lives.”

**People in this story.** [Stanley Hull](#ah-r1363). The foreman's hour gives the younger hand a real chance to work beside the departing crew.

**Personality.** Clyde has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Clyde has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. When somebody else has the floor, Clyde gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- The two old workers are offered separate farewell days. Clyde asks each worker separately what kind of last day they want.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Clyde finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Clyde says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Lionel Wu

AH-R1368 · Age 44 · Rail checker · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember scoring a domino match on the back of a payslip.” People around the downtown fountain know Lionel by a small Thursday habit. Lionel keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I beat them at dominoes as a teenager. I want one last game with both clocks still ticking on company time.”

**What Lionel wants.** To play the last scored game before clocking out.

**Family.** [Lydia Ames](../02/05-the-pawned-trumpet.md#ah-r0197) is Lionel’s stepsibling. A relative let everybody believe a favorite tune was a folk song. Only later did the family find the melody in a book with a relative's real name under it. Lionel wants the person who made something to have a chance to claim it. Asked about *The pawned trumpet*, Lydia says, “I sold the trumpet with a practice mute. The mute is still on my shelf and so is the child's name.”

**People in this story.** [Joel Arendt](#ah-r1366). Packed lunches and one last scorecard complicate the story that only two people are retiring.

**Personality.** Lionel treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Lionel lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Lionel has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a straight nose and deep-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Lionel touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A younger worker is asked to perform the final lift. Lionel lets the trainee carry out the job they are qualified for and keeps space for the retirees to watch.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Lionel asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Lionel says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Maria Jackson

AH-R1369 · Age 38 · Public port tour guide · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember seeing two cranes in the space between a fence and my father's hat.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Maria by a small Thursday habit. Maria watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I watched the ship berth from the road as a child. The two workers promised my daughter she can see it too.”

**What Maria wants.** To bring the daughter to a safe public view of the berth.

**Family.** [Rosemary Heller](../08/05-the-boy-on-the-corner.md#ah-r1098) is Maria’s second cousin. Maria chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Rosemary brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The boy on the corner*, Rosemary says, “I rent the shop. I cleared a table for homework and asked the oldest brother to respect what happens at it.”

**People in this story.** [Vincent Trent](#ah-r1364). A parent's request to see the ship lets an apprentice demonstrate a clean lift where the family can see it.

**Personality.** Maria is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Maria is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Maria is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a cropped bottle-green coat with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand, Maria leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The two old workers are offered separate farewell days. Maria asks each worker separately what kind of last day they want.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Maria puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Maria agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Marcel Easton

AH-R1370 · Age 54 · Harbor kettle attendant · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember hearing a kettle above the sound of a stopped ship.” On Monday, near Maré Coffee, Marcel likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I thought goodbye would sound like a whistle. The last thing they wanted to hear was the kettle boiling in the break room.”

**What Marcel wants.** To put the kettle on for the last shared break.

**Family.** [Valentina Berry](../04/12-the-ladder-crossing.md#ah-r0562) is Marcel’s cousin raised in the same household. Marcel brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Valentina checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The ladder crossing*, Valentina says, “I held up a bus and lost the driver's goodwill.”

**People in this story.** [Jamie Hamilton](#ah-r1365). The person making the plaque brings it to the room where the kettle and the crew actually are.

**Personality.** Marcel tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Marcel will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Marcel has close-cropped dark curls, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a plum-colored cardigan. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Marcel starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A younger worker is asked to perform the final lift. Marcel lets the trainee carry out the job they are qualified for and keeps space for the retirees to watch.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Marcel finds out what the destination actually looks like to the traveler and gives directions in those terms.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Marcel repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.
