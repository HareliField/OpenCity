## Lara Carey

AH-R1451 · Age 62 · Recently widowed care coordinator · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember seeing my first grown-up skirt reflected in three panels.” On Friday, near Juniper Street, Lara knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I want it for the mornings when I can't imagine another version of myself getting dressed.”

**What Lara wants.** To see a next chapter in the familiar mirror.

**Family.** [Wynn Kapoor](../06/02-nine-unpaid-hours.md#ah-r0768) is Lara’s cousin’s child. Lara can always ask for five minutes alone. Wynn asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Nine unpaid hours*, Wynn says, “I missed child support because the transfer didn't come.”

**People in this story.** [Gregory Lang](#ah-r1452). A would-be mirror buyer and its owner each have a personal history the other cannot put a price on. Also in the story is [Laurence Williams](#ah-r1454) (brother-in-law).

**Personality.** Lara takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Lara can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Lara has closely cropped salt-and-pepper hair, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Lara holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The widow asks to buy the fitting mirror. Lara asks the tailor and widow what access others would lose if the mirror moved.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Lara tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Lara turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Gregory Lang

AH-R1452 · Age 60 · Tailor and mirror owner · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember carrying a large mirror up to a first-floor shop.” People around the market by Market Flats know Gregory by a small Monday habit. Gregory often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I own the mirror and have seen a hundred other lives in it. I don't want to price them.”

**What Gregory wants.** To preserve access for other longtime clients.

**Family.** [Adela Griffin](../03/06-the-hospital-radio.md#ah-r0352) is Gregory’s cousin. Gregory sets the time to leave. Adela picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The hospital radio*, Adela says, “I never learned the stations' names. I memorized how far the dial turned from the crack.”

**People in this story.** [Lara Carey](#ah-r1451). A would-be mirror buyer and its owner each have a personal history the other cannot put a price on.

**Personality.** Gregory admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Gregory learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Gregory has soft white hair brushed sideways, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. When somebody else has the floor, Gregory squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- Another customer asks for a last fitting in front of it. Gregory sets aside an ordinary fitting time before making plans for the sale.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Gregory moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Gregory describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

**Under pressure**

- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Hollis offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- If accused of favoritism, Hollis explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Laurence Williams

AH-R1454 · Age 66 · Late husband's brother and plumber · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember frightening a widow with my own brother's profile.” Thursday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Laurence likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I am the late husband's brother. We look alike and I hate accidentally frightening her in reflections.”

**What Laurence wants.** To warn the widow before the resemblance startles her.

**Family.** [Jo Briggs](../03/12-one-unposted-detour.md#ah-r0418) is Laurence’s first cousin once removed. A family supper used to end the moment somebody mentioned money. They invented a second meal, eaten standing at the sink, where that was the only thing anybody was allowed to discuss. Laurence keeps the household arithmetic honest. Asked about *One unposted detour*, Jo says, “I drove the crew car. The person we made wait was my mum, who hasn't waited for me in years.”

**People in this story.** [Reuben Marsh](#ah-r1460). A cautious family resemblance and an annual coat fitting both belong among the mirror's continuing uses. Also in the story is [Lara Carey](#ah-r1451) (widow of a sibling).

**Personality.** Laurence reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Laurence trusts most.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Laurence has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with two white streaks in otherwise dark hair. Laurence looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Another customer asks for a last fitting in front of it. Laurence sets aside an ordinary fitting time before making plans for the sale.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Laurence asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- If told to back off, Laurence does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Layla Colón

AH-R1455 · Age 55 · Mirror restorer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember losing a ring while emptying a flooded studio.” On Wednesday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Layla takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I polished the silvering after the shop flooded. A small gray patch hides the dreadful corner where I lost my wedding band.”

**What Layla wants.** To keep the gray repair visible.

**Family.** [Una Curtis](../09/07-school-after-sixty.md#ah-r1265) is Layla’s stepsibling. Layla gets to change the plan. Una offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *School after sixty*, Una says, “I teach at the university. This is my first attempt to learn bookkeeping and I am the slowest in class.”

**People in this story.** [Kai Berry](#ah-r1458). Morning cleaning gives the restorer a chance to explain the gray patch to the widow in private.

**Personality.** Layla has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Layla's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Layla is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a moss-green cardigan with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand, Layla presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The widow asks to buy the fitting mirror. Layla asks the tailor and widow what access others would lose if the mirror moved.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Layla offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If the joke lands badly, Layla seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cutting replacement glass for an irreplaceable frame.” On Thursday, near Paper & Pine Books, Malcolm starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I want to make her a new mirror in the same shape. She explained that shape was the least important part.”

**What Malcolm wants.** To offer a new mirror without promising the same history.

**Family.** [Bianca Spencer](../06/12-the-late-crossing.md#ah-r0867) is Malcolm’s second cousin. Malcolm is free to hang up first. Bianca makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The late crossing*, Bianca says, “I can pay toward fuel but can't say it in front of my kids.”

**People in this story.** [Seth Massey](#ah-r1459). A sample of new glass offers a familiar apartment reflection without pretending to be the original mirror.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a black overshirt with pearl buttons and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Malcolm has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Malcolm starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Another customer asks for a last fitting in front of it. Malcolm sets aside an ordinary fitting time before making plans for the sale.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Malcolm gets the people at the next doorway away from the smoke and asks a bystander to make the call.
- When a second witness disagrees, Malcolm keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Kai Berry

AH-R1458 · Age 44 · Shop cleaner · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cleaning a mirror that still held its dawn mist.” On Sunday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Kai usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I clean the mirrors on quiet mornings. I offered the widow those mornings before we decide who takes the glass.”

**What Kai wants.** To open quiet mornings for private visits.

**Family.** [Edward Roy](../07/07-the-first-language.md#ah-r0965) is Kai’s stepsibling. Kai is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Edward calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The first language*, Edward says, “I once changed my name to make school easier.”

**People in this story.** [Layla Colón](#ah-r1455). Morning cleaning gives the restorer a chance to explain the gray patch to the widow in private.

**Appearance.** Kai is easy to recognize by a center part and straight chestnut hair and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with one cuff folded back twice, Kai gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Another customer asks for a last fitting in front of it. Kai sets aside an ordinary fitting time before making plans for the sale.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Kai asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Kai clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The other side of the cove*, Yusuf says, “I am the cleaner who came after her. A plaque can't promise working wages and I want both issues on the agenda.”

**People in this story.** [Laurence Williams](#ah-r1454). A cautious family resemblance and an annual coat fitting both belong among the mirror's continuing uses.

**Personality.** Reuben often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Reuben have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Reuben has a high puff tied with plain cord, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, an olive canvas jacket. It usually goes with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. When somebody else has the floor, Reuben steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- Another customer asks for a last fitting in front of it. Reuben sets aside an ordinary fitting time before making plans for the sale.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Reuben offers to cover one concrete expense and leaves the friend room to say the expense isn't the real problem.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Reuben rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.
