# The last light at the harbor

[When the lights go out](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**South Shore Harbor.** The four South Shore boats put out their cabin lights one at a time at the end of an ordinary working Sunday. People on shore realize each extinguished light means someone has arrived home.

## Ashley West

AH-R1491 · Age 38 · Night marina porter · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember walking home with the water lights still on my sleeve.” Sunday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Ashley usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I always leave my lamp longest because nobody is waiting in my flat. Tonight somebody asked me to walk back together.”

**What Ashley wants.** To share one walk home after the last light.

**Family.** [Harley Cao](../02/03-the-sandwich-board.md#ah-r0171) is Ashley’s stepsibling. Ashley knows which ones can be shared. Harley keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The sandwich board*, Harley says, “My mum wrote that last price for the kids from the court.”

**People in this story.** [Micah Hassan](#ah-r1497) (marina manager). The night porter and the marina manager compare who still needs company after the office closes.

**Personality.** Ashley keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Ashley wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Ashley is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf, Ashley draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A cabin light stays on after its usual time. Ashley checks in through an ordinary contact before treating the late light as an emergency.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Ashley suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Ashley offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Samuel Mehta

AH-R1492 · Age 54 · Liveaboard resident and printer · Home — a boat at South Shore Harbor

**Life in the city.** “I still remember rewiring the cabin where my grown daughter learned to duck.” On Friday, near Tideline Market, Samuel picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I turn ours off from below because my hands no longer reach the switch. My daughter moved it before she left town.”

**What Samuel wants.** To keep the cabin switch within reach.

**Family.** [Jude Wick](../01/04-the-wrong-bicycle.md#ah-r0035) is Samuel’s half-sibling. Samuel calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Jude leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The wrong bicycle*, Jude says, “Both wheels were built in my shop. I spoke for the wheel I remembered, and the child corrected me.”

**People in this story.** [Noel Ford](#ah-r1498). The resident and electrician agree that an accessible switch can still belong in human hands.

**Personality.** Samuel always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Samuel is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Samuel is easy to recognize by short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Samuel raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative wants to walk home along the docks in silence. Samuel walks alongside without making the relative fill the silence.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Samuel offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Samuel suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember photographing the owner locking a very small gate.” People around Maré Coffee know Adrian by a small Monday habit. Adrian likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I have been photographing glamorous boats. The owner asked me to photograph the little walk from deck to door instead.”

**What Adrian wants.** To photograph the unglamorous trip home.

**Family.** [Mateo Katz](../07/06-the-empty-chair-on-nights.md#ah-r0960) is Adrian’s second cousin. Adrian chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Mateo brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The empty chair on nights*, Mateo says, “I want one quiet breakfast with her outside work.”

**People in this story.** [Joel Sun](#ah-r1495). A photographer learns which ordinary walk once belonged to the resident watching a former boat from shore.

**Appearance.** Adrian has a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape, one cheek that creases before the other, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. When somebody else has the floor, Adrian listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A cabin light stays on after its usual time. Adrian checks in through an ordinary contact before treating the late light as an emergency.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Adrian gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Adrian asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Albert Wilson

AH-R1494 · Age 47 · Hospital night nurse · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember starting a ward shift as a last cabin went dark.” On Friday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Albert prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I work nights at the hospital. Four cabin lights going out means it's time to put on my own work shoes.”

**What Albert wants.** To begin the night rounds after seeing the harbor settle.

**Family.** [Darcy Hardy](../04/07-a-borrowed-name.md#ah-r0511) is Albert’s half-sibling. On moving day a family cupboard wouldn't fit up the stairs. Their household spent the night dining off drawers stacked on the floor. There is still one drawer nobody wants to part with. Albert keeps a careful list of what can actually fit through a door. Asked about *A borrowed name*, Darcy says, “I gave the character a limp like mine. We got it in different places and we laugh differently about it.”

**People in this story.** [Nico Lin](#ah-r1499). A hospital shift and a waiting spouse use the same set of lights to mark opposite ends of the day.

**Personality.** Albert enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Out of work clothes, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a narrow brass watch set a little fast. Albert has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Albert holds eye contact until the other person has finished during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A relative wants to walk home along the docks in silence. Albert walks alongside without making the relative fill the silence.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Albert asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Albert puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Joel Sun

AH-R1495 · Age 51 · Former liveaboard and bus driver · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember lying awake on a boat after signing divorce papers.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Joel by a small Saturday habit. Joel can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I lived on the smallest boat after my divorce and still count its light even though another family sleeps there.”

**What Joel wants.** To recognize the new family living on the old boat.

**Family.** [Jules Drake](../02/10-the-bitter-orange.md#ah-r0244) is Joel’s younger relative by marriage. Joel can always ask for five minutes alone. Jules asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The bitter orange*, Jules says, “I still have her loose change in a jar. It buys exactly one sandwich at the old price.”

**People in this story.** [Adrian Keller](#ah-r1493). A photographer learns which ordinary walk once belonged to the resident watching a former boat from shore.

**Personality.** Joel has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Joel can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Joel has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Joel steadies a cup with both hands while listening during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A cabin light stays on after its usual time. Joel checks in through an ordinary contact before treating the late light as an emergency.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Joel asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- If a friend borrows money, Joel agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

**Appearance.** Ruben has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Look for short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. **Under pressure**

- If someone else wins the opportunity, Ruben turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Micah Hassan

**Appearance.** Micah has a high puff tied with plain cord, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, a plum-colored cardigan. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Micah presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

## Noel Ford

AH-R1498 · Age 40 · Dock electrician · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember fitting a switch somebody could comfortably reach with wet hands.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Noel by a small Saturday habit. Noel knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**Family.** [Kai Ibrahim](../04/03-the-savings-club.md#ah-r0472) is Noel’s older relative by marriage. Noel can stop the walk at any corner. Kai chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The savings club*, Kai says, “I've won the draw twice and deferred it twice.”

**People in this story.** [Samuel Mehta](#ah-r1492). The resident and electrician agree that an accessible switch can still belong in human hands.

**Personality.** Noel has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Noel is easy to recognize by a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Noel looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative wants to walk home along the docks in silence. Noel walks alongside without making the relative fill the silence.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Noel lets the child choose whether to stand by the door or sit beside the window while another adult gets help.
- If accused of favoritism, Noel explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.
