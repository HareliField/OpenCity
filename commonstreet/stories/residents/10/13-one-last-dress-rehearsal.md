## Alvin Jeong

AH-R1471 · Age 16 · School pupil and actor · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my mother attending a rehearsal with flour on her sleeve.” On Monday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Alvin can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I used to think my mother was choosing work over me. Watching her take off her apron in the front row changed the line I sang.”

**What Alvin wants.** To sing the full scene with the mother watching.

**Family.** [Lorenzo Mack](../03/15-the-tide-clock.md#ah-r0441) is Alvin’s older relative by marriage. Alvin is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Lorenzo calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The tide clock*, Lorenzo says, “I set that clock by a rock my father could see from the gate.”

**People in this story.** [Naomi Tate](#ah-r1472) (mother). The youth player gets to sing the line to the parent who finally knows what the rehearsal was for.

**Personality.** Alvin often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Alvin have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Alvin is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Alvin counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The working parent arrives after the curtain time. Alvin waits for the parent to be seated before asking the cast to start.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Alvin brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Alvin rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Naomi Tate

AH-R1472 · Age 45 · Parent and restaurant cook · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember watching an audience from the kitchen hatch.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Naomi by a small Thursday habit. Naomi checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I came straight from a kitchen shift expecting a quick song. They gave me a whole uneven, wonderful play.”

**What Naomi wants.** To see the complete play straight from work.

**Family.** [Teresa Arendt](../09/09-the-returned-daughter.md#ah-r1288) is Naomi’s cousin raised in the same household. Naomi chooses the music. Teresa puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The returned daughter*, Teresa says, “I agreed to fix the banister. Nobody asked me to mend the whole family and I won't promise it.”

**People in this story.** [Alvin Jeong](#ah-r1471) (child). The youth player gets to sing the line to the parent who finally knows what the rehearsal was for.

**Personality.** Naomi thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Naomi sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Naomi has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. When somebody else has the floor, Naomi steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- The stage manager asks if anybody can stay for a second ending. Naomi checks who can actually stay and which ride home would be lost.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Naomi asks for help with a task the neighbor does well, so both people get to contribute something.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Naomi asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Yusuf Solis

AH-R1474 · Age 15 · School pupil and actor · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being given a villain's line because I read it without fear.” People around the entrance to the Wash House know Yusuf by a small Wednesday habit. Yusuf stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I play the villain. My dad has never seen me do anything at school except get in trouble.”

**What Yusuf wants.** To let the father see the child in a lead scene.

**Family.** [Hollis Parker](../03/04-the-spare-spectacles.md#ah-r0331) is Yusuf’s older cousin. The household has two versions of an old recipe because somebody detested onions and never admitted it to the original cook. A person sitting down to eat gets to choose. Yusuf likes to give the old cook the first taste. Asked about *The spare spectacles*, Hollis says, “I signed something blurry once. I brought my own glasses and stayed until she read every word.”

**People in this story.** [Kai Bruno](#ah-r1480). The student villain and the stage manager both have memories riding on a parent getting into the room.

**Personality.** Yusuf uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Yusuf well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Yusuf has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for a long plait tucked inside the collar. Outside school, the familiar outfit is a charcoal zip cardigan with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Yusuf listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- The stage manager asks if anybody can stay for a second ending. Yusuf checks who can actually stay and which ride home would be lost.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Yusuf asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- After a family quarrel, Yusuf brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Yvette Allard

AH-R1475 · Age 36 · Kitchen worker · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember forgetting the onions while covering the early kitchen.” On Monday, near Juniper Street, Yvette knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I worked the parent's late shift and nearly burned the onions. Next time I want to see the show myself.”

**What Yvette wants.** To see a rehearsal on the next free shift.

**Family.** [Alicia Sáenz](../07/14-the-carer-s-afternoon.md#ah-r1032) is Yvette’s second cousin. Yvette chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Alicia brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The carer's afternoon*, Alicia says, “I offered to take Mum only if my sister finally saw a doctor.”

**People in this story.** [Gale Farah](#ah-r1478). A kitchen worker who covered this time takes a seat beside the quiet listener at the next rehearsal.

**Personality.** Yvette feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Yvette has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a cream pullover with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Yvette looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The working parent arrives after the curtain time. Yvette waits for the parent to be seated before asking the cast to start.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Yvette writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Yvette lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Gilbert Moss

AH-R1476 · Age 42 · Costume seamster · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember putting a real coin into a costume's real pocket.” On Saturday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Gilbert checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I sewed the costumes. This was the first audience member who noticed we had put real pockets in all the work clothes.”

**What Gilbert wants.** To put useful pockets in the next work costume too.

**Family.** [Anita Abbott](../06/09-a-room-for-a-father.md#ah-r0832) is Gilbert’s half-sibling. Gilbert sets the time to leave. Anita picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *A room for a father*, Anita says, “I forgave the man years ago. I am still furious about the rent he left and the teeth we couldn't afford to fix.”

**People in this story.** [Esme Gross](#ah-r1477). Pockets that actually work and captions a parent can read return two overlooked details to the show.

**Personality.** Gilbert treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Gilbert has auburn hair cut square at the nape, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. When somebody else has the floor, Gilbert gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- The stage manager asks if anybody can stay for a second ending. Gilbert checks who can actually stay and which ride home would be lost.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Gilbert draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Gilbert changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Esme Gross

AH-R1477 · Age 32 · Caption editor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember moving a caption so a child could see both it and the singer.” People around Breakwater Way know Esme by a small Thursday habit. Esme stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I do captions. The parent arrived without her reading glasses and I changed the size while the cast waited.”

**What Esme wants.** To make both the words and the singer visible.

**Family.** [Lee Ochoa](../08/12-the-high-road-home.md#ah-r1168) is Esme’s stepsibling. Esme decides what stays off the paper. Lee brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The high road home*, Lee says, “I booked a dawn bakery collection as a reason to try.”

**People in this story.** [Gilbert Moss](#ah-r1476). Pockets that actually work and captions a parent can read return two overlooked details to the show.

**Personality.** Esme is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Esme is concerned.

**Appearance.** Esme is easy to recognize by a center part and straight chestnut hair and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a brown corduroy jacket with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Esme presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The working parent arrives after the curtain time. Esme waits for the parent to be seated before asking the cast to start.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Esme brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- When someone invents an answer, Esme asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

**Under pressure**
