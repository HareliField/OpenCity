# Names on the night wall

[When the lights go out](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The city police station.** The city department makes a wall for staff to thank the person who makes coming to work possible. Almost every name belongs to somebody outside the station.

## Luz Dean

AH-R1381 · Age 37 · Patrol officer · Home — Eastside · Department ID ah-pd-139 · Patrol, watch 3, badge 139

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a grocer keeping my little girl's bag behind the oranges.” Saturday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Luz picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I wrote the name of the grocer who holds my daughter's school bag when my watch runs late.”

**What Luz wants.** To thank the grocer for protecting the school bag.

**Family.** [Sam Lim](../09/06-a-doctor-moves-away.md#ah-r1253) is Luz’s stepsibling. Luz asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Sam remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A doctor moves away*, Sam says, “I am the young physician replacing her. My first week's schedule is full of people who want to say goodbye.”

**People in this story.** [Marc Carr](#ah-r1389) (neighbor). A watch worker thanks the person who knows which child's school bag stays behind the orange boxes.

**Personality.** Luz talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Luz's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Luz has an aquiline nose and a square chin. Look for a center part and straight chestnut hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. In conversation Luz holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A colleague leaves a name off the thank-you wall. Luz allows a private thank-you to stay private even on unveiling day.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Luz takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Luz says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Max King

AH-R1382 · Age 41 · Patrol officer · Home — Old Town · Department ID ah-pd-140 · Patrol, watch 3, badge 140

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a tomato plant surviving three shift changes.” On Wednesday, near the market by Market Flats, Max often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I have covered my partner's Saturdays for a decade. My own card names the neighbor who waters my tomatoes.”

**What Max wants.** To bring tomatoes to the neighbor who watered them.

**Family.** [Helena Neal](../02/05-the-pawned-trumpet.md#ah-r0194) is Max’s cousin raised in the same household. Max is free to hang up first. Helena makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The pawned trumpet*, Helena says, “I took the loan to get home for my mother's funeral.”

**People in this story.** [Milan Gray](#ah-r1384). A late taxi escort and a neighbor watering tomatoes reveal the second shift behind an ordinary watch.

**Personality.** Max prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Max has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for a high puff tied with plain cord. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a plum-colored cardigan with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. In conversation Max starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor named on several cards asks for a favor in return. Max asks the neighbor what kind of help they actually need now.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Max asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Max clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Pia Burke

AH-R1383 · Age 45 · Metro SWAT officer · Home — Coast Ridge · Department ID ah-pd-141 · Metro SWAT, watch 3, badge 141

**Life in the city.** “I still remember watching a custody handover go perfectly quietly.” On Saturday, near the blocks below Foundry Lofts, Pia checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I thanked my ex. A good custody calendar is worth more to both of us than another argument over uniforms.”

**What Pia wants.** To recognize the ex's work on the custody calendar.

**Family.** [Jody Breen](../09/03-the-million-dollar-flat.md#ah-r1228) is Pia’s parent’s cousin. Pia chooses whether to use it. Jody saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The million-dollar flat*, Jody says, “I built the first kitchen cabinets here. I'd like to recover them before a rich buyer throws them away.”

**People in this story.** [Remy Cruz](#ah-r1388). A dependable custody handover and a year of treatment both earn places on the thank-you wall.

**Personality.** Pia never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Pia can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Pia has thick, tightly coiled hair, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Pia steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- A colleague leaves a name off the thank-you wall. Pia allows a private thank-you to stay private even on unveiling day.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Pia keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Pia may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Milan Gray

AH-R1384 · Age 39 · Metro SWAT officer · Home — Midtown · Department ID ah-pd-142 · Metro SWAT, watch 3, badge 142

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being walked to a taxi in two different sorts of uniform.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Milan by a small Sunday habit. Milan likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I put down the cleaner who sits on the steps until my taxi comes. I only learned her surname yesterday.”

**What Milan wants.** To learn and use the cleaner's full name.

**Family.** [Aria Starr](../07/09-a-tenant-who-stays.md#ah-r0981) is Milan’s cousin. Milan sets the time to leave. Aria picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *A tenant who stays*, Aria says, “I can finally afford to move home and did not know somebody else had spent a life there.”

**People in this story.** [Max King](#ah-r1382). A late taxi escort and a neighbor watering tomatoes reveal the second shift behind an ordinary watch.

**Personality.** Milan often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Milan have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Milan has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. When somebody else has the floor, Milan draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor named on several cards asks for a favor in return. Milan asks the neighbor what kind of help they actually need now.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Milan looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Milan rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Zoe Nash

AH-R1385 · Age 36 · Detective · Home — Eastside · Department ID ah-pd-143 · Detectives, watch 3, badge 143

**Life in the city.** “I still remember phoning a brother who only asked about the weather.” On Thursday, near the bank steps, Zoe takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I chose the brother who hears my ordinary complaints and never asks for an exciting case.”

**What Zoe wants.** To give the brother one ordinary story from home.

**Family.** [Jay Knight](../04/06-the-waiting-room-plant.md#ah-r0503) is Zoe’s cousin raised in the same household. A parcel came home years late with the handwriting still recognizable. Since then nobody in the family opens a hard letter alone unless they specifically ask to. Zoe stays for the silence after the call. Asked about *The waiting-room plant*, Jay says, “I know it by the smell on my fingers after my daughter's surgery.”

**People in this story.** [Oakley Roman](#ah-r1390). The sign painter asks how the brother who listens would want his name spelled.

**Personality.** Zoe thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Zoe sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Off duty, look for an old plum windbreaker and a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. Zoe has a long face and high cheekbones, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Zoe presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A colleague leaves a name off the thank-you wall. Zoe allows a private thank-you to stay private even on unveiling day.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Zoe tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Zoe asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Theo Hall

AH-R1386 · Age 48 · Detective · Home — Old Town · Department ID ah-pd-144 · Detectives, watch 3, badge 144

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an afternoon among readers who didn't know my job.” On Friday, near the bank steps, Theo takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I named the librarian who doesn't mention my job in the reading group. That hour makes me somebody else again.”

**What Theo wants.** To protect the quiet reading group.

**Family.** [Thomas Banks](../03/11-the-widow-s-razor.md#ah-r0403) is Theo’s half-sibling. Theo chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Thomas brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The widow's razor*, Thomas says, “I wanted to buy it for a collector. Then I saw the tiny rent figures written inside the lid.”

**People in this story.** [Kit Hale](#ah-r1387). A librarian's discretion helps the colleague whose helper has asked to stay unnamed.

**Personality.** Theo gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Theo privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Theo is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges, Theo leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor named on several cards asks for a favor in return. Theo asks the neighbor what kind of help they actually need now.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Theo takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Theo offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Kit Hale

AH-R1387 · Age 42 · Forensic analyst · Home — Midtown · Department ID ah-pd-146 · Forensics, watch 3, badge 146

**Life in the city.** “I still remember someone hanging an ordinary apron beside my uniform.” On Monday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Kit takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I wrote a blank because the person keeping my house together doesn't want their name known here.”

**What Kit wants.** To thank the unnamed person privately.

**Family.** [Owen Kerr](../09/09-the-returned-daughter.md#ah-r1284) is Kit’s cousin raised in the same household. Kit decides what stays off the paper. Owen brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The returned daughter*, Owen says, “I bought the groceries before her visit and chose the food she liked at sixteen.”

**People in this story.** [Theo Hall](#ah-r1386). A librarian's discretion helps the colleague whose helper has asked to stay unnamed.

**Personality.** Kit uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Kit well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Kit is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Kit squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A colleague leaves a name off the thank-you wall. Kit allows a private thank-you to stay private even on unveiling day.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Kit tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- After a family quarrel, Kit brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

AH-R1388 · Age 53 · Air support officer · Home — Eastside · Department ID ah-pd-147 · Air Support, watch 3, badge 147

**Life in the city.** “I still remember turning up on the day I wanted most to stay in bed.” Monday often includes a stop at Studio Boulevard, where Remy waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**Under pressure**

## Marc Carr

AH-R1389 · Age 50 · Neighbor and grocer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember finding my name thanked by a family I barely remembered helping.” Monday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Marc can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I am the neighbor named in three different cards. I asked if the station could help me move my sofa next month.”

**What Marc wants.** To collect the promised help moving the sofa.

**Family.** [Frankie Levin](../07/05-the-release-address.md#ah-r0946) is Marc’s half-sibling. Marc chooses the music. Frankie puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The release address*, Frankie says, “I used to bring him drugs. He asked me for the number of a furniture mover and nothing else.”

**People in this story.** [Luz Dean](#ah-r1381) (neighbor). A watch worker thanks the person who knows which child's school bag stays behind the orange boxes.

**Personality.** Marc treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Marc is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Marc looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A colleague leaves a name off the thank-you wall. Marc allows a private thank-you to stay private even on unveiling day.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Marc asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Marc changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Oakley Roman

AH-R1390 · Age 37 · Sign painter · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cutting a whole line of tiny names no camera would ever catch.” On Tuesday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Oakley checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I cut the lettering and found no mention of the founder or the chief. An ordinary wall has never required so much careful spelling.”

**What Oakley wants.** To spell every ordinary name correctly on the wall.

**Family.** [Victor Lowe](../07/07-the-first-language.md#ah-r0963) is Oakley’s cousin raised in the same household. Oakley can stop the walk at any corner. Victor chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The first language*, Victor says, “I buy books in our first language and never open them myself.”

**People in this story.** [Zoe Nash](#ah-r1385). The sign painter asks how the brother who listens would want his name spelled.

**Personality.** Oakley is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Oakley is concerned.

**Appearance.** Oakley has thick, tightly coiled hair, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Oakley turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor named on several cards asks for a favor in return. Oakley asks the neighbor what kind of help they actually need now.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Oakley looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- When someone invents an answer, Oakley asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.
