# A house divided kindly

[When the lights go out](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The western residential streets.** Two western neighbors want to join their front gardens after their children move away. Their deeds, family histories and tastes in flowers differ.

## Byron Sato

AH-R1431 · Age 66 · Retired groundskeeper · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember winning a gardening prize with flowers I hadn't really liked.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Byron by a small Saturday habit. Byron checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I spent years keeping my hedge straighter than hers. I want a gate now and no audience when I ask.”

**What Byron wants.** To ask for a gate privately.

**Family.** [Karim Klein](../06/13-the-almost-scholarship.md#ah-r0874) is Byron’s second cousin. An old family mantel clock gained three minutes a day. People who couldn't bear to be late liked it. Everybody else wondered why the household was always the first to arrive. Byron remembers to tell other people which clock the family used. Asked about *The almost scholarship*, Karim says, “I used to sleep in a practice room. I told the pupil because I don't want that passed off as romantic.”

**People in this story.** [Amelia Liu](#ah-r1432) (next-door neighbor). A locked gate can be both a new invitation and a respected boundary.

**Personality.** Byron treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Byron has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Byron leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- The older neighbors disagree on where the gate should go. Byron gets both neighbors to mark a trial gate position before any hedge is cut.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Byron keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Byron changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Amelia Liu

AH-R1432 · Age 65 · Retired office manager · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being handed the first front-door key nobody could take away.” On Tuesday, near Breakwater Way, Amelia stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I grew up with no front door of my own. I'd like a shared gate I can close at night.”

**What Amelia wants.** To get a shared gate that closes securely at night.

**Family.** [Leo Hart](../08/04-first-shift-back.md#ah-r1088) is Amelia’s younger cousin. Amelia can always ask for five minutes alone. Leo asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *First shift back*, Leo says, “I was the first person they asked for advice.”

**People in this story.** [Byron Sato](#ah-r1431) (next-door neighbor). A locked gate can be both a new invitation and a respected boundary.

**Personality.** Amelia is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Amelia is concerned.

**Appearance.** Amelia has silver temples and a dark fringe, one cheek that creases before the other, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Amelia holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A grandchild objects to the garden plan. Amelia asks the household members who will use the garden how they want to live in it now.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Amelia asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- When someone invents an answer, Amelia asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Joy Johannsen

AH-R1433 · Age 41 · Resident's daughter and designer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember finding my mother's plans more exact than mine.” People around the market by Market Flats know Joy by a small Monday habit. Joy often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I am the daughter afraid this garden scheme means Mum is getting confused. I saw the careful scale drawing in her kitchen.”

**What Joy wants.** To hear the mother's drawings explained on their merits.

**Family.** [Faye Daniels](../04/05-the-dead-man-s-tab.md#ah-r0495) is Joy’s older relative by marriage. Joy decides what stays off the paper. Faye brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The dead man's tab*, Faye says, “I want the deli to give the money to the daughter everyone speaks for.”

**People in this story.** [Jo Allard](#ah-r1438). The scale drawing lets two adult generations see the older gardeners' plan before objecting.

**Personality.** Joy loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Joy has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Joy holds eye contact until the other person has finished during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The older neighbors disagree on where the gate should go. Joy gets both neighbors to mark a trial gate position before any hedge is cut.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Joy keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Joy offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Noel Emery

AH-R1434 · Age 48 · Nursery worker · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember transplanting a rose from a neighbor's closing shop.” On Sunday, near Breakwater Way, Noel stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I can move one rosebush and cannot promise it will flower again. I told both owners before taking a spade to it.”

**What Noel wants.** To move only rosebushes capable of surviving.

**Family.** [Daniel Fraser](../08/01-reopening-at-six.md#ah-r1060) is Noel’s cousin’s child. Noel is free to hang up first. Daniel makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Reopening at six*, Daniel says, “I am the teenager everyone expects to cover late.”

**People in this story.** [Marco Lam](#ah-r1437). A path for a walking frame has to coexist with the rose that may not survive moving.

**Personality.** Noel enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Noel is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Out of work clothes, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Noel has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with a blunt cut just below the ears. Noel presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A grandchild objects to the garden plan. Noel asks the household members who will use the garden how they want to live in it now.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Noel asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Noel keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Charlie Black

AH-R1435 · Age 53 · Land surveyor · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember surveying a legal boundary that no fence ever followed.” People around Tideline Market know Charlie by a small Sunday habit. Charlie picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I drew the boundary as a junior surveyor. This is the first time a client asked me how to make it friendlier.”

**What Charlie wants.** To make the friendly boundary legal and clear.

**Family.** [Jo Wong](../06/15-winter-afloat.md#ah-r0896) is Charlie’s cousin. Charlie brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Jo makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Winter afloat*, Jo says, “I live in a flat above the coast road. I'm exchanging winter housing for summer sailing lessons I might be scared to take.”

**People in this story.** [Stella Choi](#ah-r1436). The old childhood gap is a better starting point for the survey than the center of the hedge.

**Personality.** Charlie is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a faded indigo overshirt and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Charlie has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Charlie looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The older neighbors disagree on where the gate should go. Charlie gets both neighbors to mark a trial gate position before any hedge is cut.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Charlie looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Charlie helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Stella Choi

AH-R1436 · Age 46 · Former neighbor and shop worker · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember catching my school coat in the gap between our gardens.” On Sunday, near the blocks below Foundry Lofts, Stella checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I used to squeeze through the gap to see my best friend. The new gate needs to be high enough for our grown-up heads.”

**What Stella wants.** To leave room for two former children now grown.

**Family.** [Fern Lin](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1232) is Stella’s cousin. Stella gets to change the plan. Fern offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Fern says, “I took the second call and inherited the trust the first voice had built.”

**People in this story.** [Charlie Black](#ah-r1435). The old childhood gap is a better starting point for the survey than the center of the hedge.

**Personality.** Stella finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Stella is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a rust-colored canvas coat with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Stella rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A grandchild objects to the garden plan. Stella asks the household members who will use the garden how they want to live in it now.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Stella helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Stella waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Marco Lam

**What Marco wants.** To get a paved route usable with the walking frame.

**Family.** [Miro Quade](../01/05-the-aquarium.md#ah-r0046) is Marco’s cousin’s child. Marco calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Miro leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The aquarium*, Miro says, “Those orange fish were the first things my boy named.”

**People in this story.** [Noel Emery](#ah-r1434). A path for a walking frame has to coexist with the rose that may not survive moving.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Marco has tightly curled gray hair, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Marco rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The older neighbors disagree on where the gate should go. Marco gets both neighbors to mark a trial gate position before any hedge is cut.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Marco offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.

## Jo Allard

AH-R1438 · Age 30 · Hotel assistant · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember visiting a childhood hedge after I had outgrown its gap.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Jo by a small Tuesday habit. Jo likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I am the grandchild who expected to inherit this house. The garden they enjoy now matters at least as much as my future fence.”

**What Jo wants.** To let the grandparents enjoy the present garden.

**Family.** [Mona Neri](../01/08-a-tide-at-noon.md#ah-r0077) is Jo’s parent’s cousin. Jo knows which ones can be shared. Mona keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A tide at noon*, Mona says, “I donated the prize bucket. The winning child was afraid to come near my white shirt.”

**People in this story.** [Joy Johannsen](#ah-r1433). The scale drawing lets two adult generations see the older gardeners' plan before objecting.

**Personality.** Jo is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Jo loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Jo has a high puff tied with plain cord, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. When somebody else has the floor, Jo looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- A grandchild objects to the garden plan. Jo asks the household members who will use the garden how they want to live in it now.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Jo stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- If somebody calls for applause, Jo gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Peyton Myers

AH-R1440 · Age 53 · Deli cook · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember putting two families' salt pots beside the same loaf.” On Wednesday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Peyton watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I offered the first shared meal. Both families brought their own salt, which suggests a useful arrangement for the gate.”

**What Peyton wants.** To host the first supper at the open gate.

**Family.** [Finn Heller](../09/02-the-worker-who-couldn-t-read-the-joke.md#ah-r1217) is Peyton’s cousin. Peyton chooses whether to use it. Finn saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The worker who couldn't read the joke*, Finn says, “I have hired the same crew for decades. This is the first time two of them have asked to meet me privately.”

**People in this story.** [Martha Laurent](#ah-r1439). A loaned bench and two salt pots give different households real things they can still choose separately.

**Personality.** Peyton can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a long navy raincoat and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Peyton has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with auburn hair cut square at the nape. Peyton takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A grandchild objects to the garden plan. Peyton asks the household members who will use the garden how they want to live in it now.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Peyton calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Peyton asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.
