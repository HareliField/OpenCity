## Basil Allard

AH-R0631 · Age 54 · Municipal payroll clerk · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the seat left for me when I returned a grade behind.” People around Maré Coffee know Basil by a small Sunday habit. Basil likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I remember exactly who sat beside me when I repeated a year. That chair is reserved.”

**What Basil wants.** To save the schoolmate's seat.

**Family.** [Ruth Sutton](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1302) is Basil’s stepsibling. Basil leaves the first voice message. Ruth calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Ruth says, “I loved the scar in the picture because my own hands look similar.”

**People in this story.** [Molly Neal](#ah-r0634). The remembered seat and the skipping rope give the reunion two different ways of welcoming someone back. Also in the story is [Teresa Lopez](#ah-r0632) (schoolmate).

**Personality.** Basil admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Basil learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Basil has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Basil holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A classmate arrives at the reunion in uniform. Basil welcomes the classmate before bringing up their working clothes.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Basil suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Basil describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Teresa Lopez

AH-R0632 · Age 49 · School liaison volunteer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember attending a family dinner in an unfamiliar uniform.” Monday often includes a stop at Paper & Pine Books, where Teresa starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I came in uniform because changing clothes would make me late. Half my friends thought I was there to stop the party.”

**What Teresa wants.** To join the reunion without stopping it.

**Family.** [Ramona Vargas](../09/07-school-after-sixty.md#ah-r1264) is Teresa’s half-sibling. Teresa chooses the familiar route. Ramona can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *School after sixty*, Ramona says, “I made a clean copy of the practice paper because my partner was embarrassed about crossing out every line.”

**People in this story.** [Samuel Molina](#ah-r0636). A uniform and a school nickname briefly belong to the same young-looking person. Also in the story is [Basil Allard](#ah-r0631) (schoolmate).

**Personality.** Teresa has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a plum-colored cardigan and one cuff folded back twice. Teresa has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with close-cropped dark curls. Teresa takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The person everyone expects to speak wants to sit down. Teresa gives the expected speaker a seat and lets the old classmates start talking amongst themselves.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Teresa writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- If accused of favoritism, Teresa explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Sam Yeo

AH-R0633 · Age 52 · Former class secretary · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember keeping score of classmates' changed surnames.” On Thursday, near Breakwater Way, Sam stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I carried the list of absent classmates and ended up talking about the living ones for once.”

**What Sam wants.** To give the living classmates a turn at the microphone.

**Family.** [Adrian Andrade](../08/15-the-skipper-goes-ashore.md#ah-r1198) is Sam’s second cousin. Sam brings the paper and a pen. Adrian decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The skipper goes ashore*, Adrian says, “I do not want to sell our house because she earns less ashore.”

**People in this story.** [Eden Tran](#ah-r0638). The absent names help the badge writer work out whom to invite next year.

**Personality.** Sam reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Sam trusts most.

**Appearance.** Sam has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for a loose dark braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a brown corduroy jacket with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Sam takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A classmate arrives at the reunion in uniform. Sam welcomes the classmate before bringing up their working clothes.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Sam sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- If told to back off, Sam does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Molly Neal

AH-R0634 · Age 48 · Wardrobe supervisor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember skipping rope on a badly tilted playground.” On Sunday, near Studio Boulevard, Molly waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I had a speech ready about service. My old desk mate handed me a skipping rope and counted out loud.”

**What Molly wants.** To play one round with the old skipping rope.

**Family.** [Casey King](../02/13-the-quiet-verse.md#ah-r0280) is Molly’s older relative by marriage. Molly sets the time to leave. Casey picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The quiet verse*, Casey says, “I was furious there was a secret verse until my grandson asked which page of my diary he could read.”

**People in this story.** [Basil Allard](#ah-r0631). The remembered seat and the skipping rope give the reunion two different ways of welcoming someone back.

**Personality.** Molly has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Molly's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Molly is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Molly draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The person everyone expects to speak wants to sit down. Molly gives the expected speaker a seat and lets the old classmates start talking amongst themselves.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Molly asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- If the joke lands badly, Molly seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Albert Bay

AH-R0635 · Age 51 · Former shoplifter and locksmith apprentice · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember giving back a lunch I was too hungry to steal.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Albert asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I used to steal lunches here. A woman whose sandwich I took asked if I had eaten today.”

**What Albert wants.** To accept a meal from the old classmate.

**Family.** [Jack Bellamy](../02/11-one-sleeve-short.md#ah-r0258) is Albert’s cousin raised in the same household. Albert sets the time to leave. Jack picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *One sleeve short*, Jack says, “I thought he was embarrassed about his arm.”

**People in this story.** [Bess Kaur](#ah-r0640). An unexpected offered meal leaves enough cake to take back to the watch.

**Personality.** Albert listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Albert has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. When somebody else has the floor, Albert turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A classmate arrives at the reunion in uniform. Albert welcomes the classmate before bringing up their working clothes.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Albert says what they know in their own name and refuses to treat a guess as something the child has to disprove.
- When a second witness disagrees, Albert keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Samuel Molina

AH-R0636 · Age 46 · Station administrator's partner and florist · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an old school nickname heard on the radio.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Samuel asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I only knew my partner's working voice. I heard his school nickname and he sounded fourteen.”

**What Samuel wants.** To hear the working partner's first nickname.

**Family.** [Nelson Carbone](../02/12-the-sheltered-corner.md#ah-r0264) is Samuel’s cousin. Samuel brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Nelson checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The sheltered corner*, Nelson says, “I sell coffee on the old corner. My first customer is now a pensioner who only comes to talk.”

**People in this story.** [Teresa Lopez](#ah-r0632). A uniform and a school nickname briefly belong to the same young-looking person.

**Personality.** Samuel talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Samuel's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Samuel is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand, Samuel looks toward the door whenever a name is called when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The person everyone expects to speak wants to sit down. Samuel gives the expected speaker a seat and lets the old classmates start talking amongst themselves.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Samuel writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Samuel says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Frankie Reyes

AH-R0637 · Age 47 · Ambulance dispatcher · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a reunion picture taken just before my bus arrived.” On Monday, near the market by Market Flats, Frankie often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I took a call during the photograph. My friends left a space and took another after I came back.”

**What Frankie wants.** To get into the second group photograph.

**Family.** [Irene Baines](../08/15-the-skipper-goes-ashore.md#ah-r1192) is Frankie’s cousin. Frankie puts the kettle on. Irene chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The skipper goes ashore*, Irene says, “I served under the skipper. She kept steering with a broken heart after her wife's death, so stopping now is hers to choose.”

**People in this story.** [Erin Parra](#ah-r0639). A late photograph adds the classmate identified from the old inhaler story.

**Personality.** Frankie prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Frankie has a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Look for short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Frankie rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A classmate arrives at the reunion in uniform. Frankie welcomes the classmate before bringing up their working clothes.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Frankie looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Frankie clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Eden Tran

AH-R0638 · Age 49 · Name badge printer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a badge that said visitor in my own school.” On Monday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Eden likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I hate reunion badges. I made ones that let people write what they're doing tomorrow.”

**What Eden wants.** To let every guest write a new badge.

**Family.** [Helena Alston](../08/14-the-new-language-at-dinner.md#ah-r1184) is Eden’s cousin raised in the same household. Some years the only new thing anybody in the family got was a pair of shoes. Buying the right size for someone with plans of their own mattered much more than buying a surprise. Eden can make accepting practical help feel like choosing a present. Asked about *The new language at dinner*, Helena says, “I am the carer he loves. I can cook his favorite food and I cannot ask where it hurts.”

**People in this story.** [Sam Yeo](#ah-r0633). The absent names help the badge writer work out whom to invite next year.

**Personality.** Eden never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Eden can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Eden is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Eden gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The person everyone expects to speak wants to sit down. Eden gives the expected speaker a seat and lets the old classmates start talking amongst themselves.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Eden says what they know in their own name and refuses to treat a guess as something the child has to disprove.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Eden may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Bess Kaur

AH-R0640 · Age 52 · Rail watchkeeper · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember carrying cake home after everyone had gone to bed.” On Wednesday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Bess usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I had to leave early for watch. Somebody packed the sheet cake in ten little boxes so I could keep talking about it.”

**What Bess wants.** To take enough cake to share through the watch.

**Family.** [Jade Cole](../08/08-a-summer-marriage.md#ah-r1128) is Bess’s cousin. Bess brings a spare shopping bag. Jade chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A summer marriage*, Jade says, “I am the parent staying away. I booked a room facing the beach because staying away is harder than I admitted.”

**People in this story.** [Albert Bay](#ah-r0635). An unexpected offered meal leaves enough cake to take back to the watch.

**Personality.** Bess thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Bess sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Bess has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Bess counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- The person everyone expects to speak wants to sit down. Bess gives the expected speaker a seat and lets the old classmates start talking amongst themselves.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Bess asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Bess asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.
