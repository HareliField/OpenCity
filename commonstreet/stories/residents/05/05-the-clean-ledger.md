## Anna Bishop

AH-R0641 · Age 64 · Retired dress buyer · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a graduation dress hemmed with someone else's thread.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Anna by a small Wednesday habit. Anna can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I want my niece's graduation dress back. A price in a notebook won't fit her.”

**What Anna wants.** To recover the niece's graduation dress.

**Family.** [Isla Joseph](../07/11-the-salon-on-strike.md#ah-r1003) is Anna’s younger cousin. Anna makes the call. Isla is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The salon on strike*, Isla says, “I booked the party and invited my millionaire boss.”

**People in this story.** [Hamish James](#ah-r0642) (ledger creditor). They have different odds of recovering the dress and the one surviving tool.

**Personality.** Anna treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Anna has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a neat white crop. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an old plum windbreaker with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Anna counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- The former fence offers to pay money for something irreplaceable. Anna asks the owner what returning money would and wouldn't settle.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Anna stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Anna changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Hamish James

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first shop closing while I still had the keys.” Thursday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Hamish usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I bought the man's tools and lost my shop anyway. I have one plane left to return.”

**What Hamish wants.** To return the one surviving plane.

**Family.** [Riley Macrae](../09/06-a-doctor-moves-away.md#ah-r1251) is Hamish’s half-sibling. Hamish leaves the first voice message. Riley calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A doctor moves away*, Riley says, “I asked my patients what mattered for years.”

**People in this story.** [Anna Bishop](#ah-r0641) (ledger creditor). They have different odds of recovering the dress and the one surviving tool.

**Personality.** Hamish is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Hamish is concerned.

**Appearance.** Hamish is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Hamish steadies a cup with both hands while listening when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A creditor asks for a conversation without witnesses. Hamish finds a private time when both people can still have someone nearby if requested.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Hamish asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- When someone invents an answer, Hamish asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.
