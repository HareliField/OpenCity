## Aisha Ortiz

AH-R0711 · Age 34 · Delivery passenger and editor · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a famous teacher buying very ordinary frozen peas.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Aisha by a small Sunday habit. Aisha can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I saw my old maths teacher cooking dinner and realized he'd lived here the entire time I was away.”

**What Aisha wants.** To visit the former maths teacher properly.

**Family.** [Elaine Marquez](../04/06-the-waiting-room-plant.md#ah-r0509) is Aisha’s second cousin. Aisha gets to change the plan. Elaine offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The waiting-room plant*, Elaine says, “I want a cutting in the grief room because the plastic flowers look the same every morning.”

**People in this story.** [William Nielsen](#ah-r0717). The passenger who recognized a teacher starts looking out for the match score.

**Personality.** Aisha never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Aisha can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Aisha has thick, tightly coiled hair, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a brown corduroy jacket. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Aisha raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- A passenger asks to photograph a stranger through the kitchen window. Aisha waits for the resident to give permission before a camera goes up.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Aisha takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Aisha may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Cian Davenport

AH-R0712 · Age 66 · Curtain fitter · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sewing a curtain a little too short for perfect privacy.” People around the bank steps know Cian by a small Tuesday habit. Cian takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I hung a little curtain for my partner. She asked me to leave a gap so she could see the buses.”

**What Cian wants.** To give the partner a private patch of curtain.

**Family.** [Jacob Bowen](../08/01-reopening-at-six.md#ah-r1055) is Cian’s half-sibling. Cian brings a spare shopping bag. Jacob chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Reopening at six*, Jacob says, “I can share a dryer with the cook but need a screen to sort my medical linen.”

**People in this story.** [Emma Carbone](#ah-r0718). A curtain makes it possible to offer a photograph without giving away the whole room.

**Personality.** Cian often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Cian have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Cian has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for a long silver braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Cian draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- The resident closes the curtains during the morning detour. Cian treats the closed curtain as the resident's answer for that day.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Cian brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Cian rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Jack Saito

AH-R0713 · Age 40 · Morning bus commuter · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember watching somebody across a road who seemed to recognize me.” People around the downtown fountain know Jack by a small Sunday habit. Jack keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I share a smile with a passenger every morning. The detour ends Friday and I'd like to learn her name.”

**What Jack wants.** To introduce the window neighbors before the detour ends.

**Family.** [Hollis Aranda](../07/11-the-salon-on-strike.md#ah-r1008) is Jack’s cousin. Jack asks before bringing somebody along. Hollis can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The salon on strike*, Hollis says, “I promoted the party online and quietly deleted the post.”

**People in this story.** [Ismael Gross](#ah-r0720). A commuter's wave and a newly reopened curtain survive the end of the detour.

**Personality.** Jack thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Jack sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. Jack has one cheek that creases before the other, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Jack raises one eyebrow long before saying yes during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A passenger asks to photograph a stranger through the kitchen window. Jack waits for the resident to give permission before a camera goes up.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Jack keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Jack asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Pablo Thomas

AH-R0714 · Age 71 · Retired window gardener · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember counting our plants after a storm blew two down.” Friday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Pablo checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I was angry about the noise until I saw the pensioners on the upper deck counting our flowerpots.”

**What Pablo wants.** To learn the upper-deck passengers' favorite plant.

**Family.** [Catherine Easton](../06/08-summer-staff.md#ah-r0827) is Pablo’s younger cousin. Pablo makes room at the table. Catherine brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Summer staff*, Catherine says, “I can move to a warm coast for another season.”

**People in this story.** [Sebastian Hudson](#ah-r0716). Two home decorators compare the view from an overfull bus.

**Personality.** Pablo gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Pablo privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Pablo is easy to recognize by short white hair and darker eyebrows and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a watch on a cracked leather band, Pablo counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The resident closes the curtains during the morning detour. Pablo treats the closed curtain as the resident's answer for that day.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Pablo asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Pablo offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Selena Sáenz

**Life in the city.** “I still remember eating at a planning desk while making a map of other people's houses.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Selena by a small Tuesday habit. Selena knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I manage the detour and have never had a route run through somebody's supper before.”

**What Selena wants.** To record what the traffic diversion costs the households.

**Family.** [Eva Bhandari](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1308) is Selena’s older cousin. Selena saves a place. Eva chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Eva says, “I own the wall where the advert would hang.”

**People in this story.** [Jo Johnson](#ah-r0719) (local resident). A cake for the driver arrives separately from the complaint for the planner.

**Personality.** Selena uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Selena well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Selena has close-cropped dark curls, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a brown corduroy jacket. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Selena squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- A passenger asks to photograph a stranger through the kitchen window. Selena waits for the resident to give permission before a camera goes up.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Selena offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- After a family quarrel, Selena brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Sebastian Hudson

AH-R0716 · Age 47 · Scenic painter · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember painting a horizon across a kitchen blind.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Sebastian by a small Sunday habit. Sebastian checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I painted a sea view on a screen because the real sea is behind the bus.”

**What Sebastian wants.** To finish the sea view for the screen.

**Family.** [Ruth Kim](../06/04-the-saturday-swap.md#ah-r0782) is Sebastian’s stepsibling. Sebastian picks the meeting place. Ruth sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The Saturday swap*, Ruth says, “I can work Saturday. My wife wants me to admit I prefer the station to another afternoon with her father.”

**People in this story.** [Pablo Thomas](#ah-r0714). Two home decorators compare the view from an overfull bus.

**Personality.** Sebastian feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Sebastian has short copper curls, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, an olive canvas jacket. It usually goes with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. When somebody else has the floor, Sebastian gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- The resident closes the curtains during the morning detour. Sebastian treats the closed curtain as the resident's answer for that day.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Sebastian finds out what the destination actually looks like to the traveler and gives directions in those terms.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Sebastian lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## William Nielsen

AH-R0717 · Age 56 · Sports fixture clerk · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember giving a goal score to a passenger who had missed his stop.” People around Maré Coffee know William by a small Wednesday habit. William likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I put up a score on match days. The passengers started holding up their predictions on the return journey.”

**What William wants.** To publish a match score the passengers can see.

**Family.** [Cora Adair](../09/14-the-missing-sunday.md#ah-r1332) is William’s half-sibling. William keeps the photographs. Cora knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The missing Sunday*, Cora says, “I arranged every important task. My father asked what hour I'd set aside to waste with him.”

**People in this story.** [Aisha Ortiz](#ah-r0711). The passenger who recognized a teacher starts looking out for the match score.

**Personality.** William treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a washed violet jersey and a thin silver ring worn on a cord. William has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. William presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A passenger asks to photograph a stranger through the kitchen window. William waits for the resident to give permission before a camera goes up.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. William asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- If a frightened person needs longer, William changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Emma Carbone

AH-R0718 · Age 29 · Street photographer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember taking family portraits through a café window.” On Friday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Emma stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I wanted to photograph the window from the bus. The resident asked to photograph me from her kitchen.”

**What Emma wants.** To exchange the pictures with the resident.

**Family.** [Frances Mendez](../08/10-the-first-delivery-fee.md#ah-r1143) is Emma’s stepsibling. Emma makes the call. Frances is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The first delivery fee*, Frances says, “My mother orders one small lunch. We can't pay a delivery charge five times a week and she can't store five meals.”

**People in this story.** [Cian Davenport](#ah-r0712). A curtain makes it possible to offer a photograph without giving away the whole room.

**Personality.** Emma is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Emma is concerned.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a boxy denim jacket and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Emma has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Emma draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The resident closes the curtains during the morning detour. Emma treats the closed curtain as the resident's answer for that day.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Emma asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- When someone invents an answer, Emma asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Jo Johnson

AH-R0719 · Age 59 · Residents' association volunteer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember holding an unsigned petition in a warm bakery.” People around Ferris Deli know Jo by a small Sunday habit. Jo asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I delivered a complaint and a cake to the same desk. The cake was for the driver who waved.”

**What Jo wants.** To thank the waving driver separately from the complaint.

**Family.** [Nadia Hayes](../03/10-the-broken-till.md#ah-r0391) is Jo’s cousin raised in the same household. Jo brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Nadia checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The broken till*, Nadia says, “I learned sums selling tomatoes. I ran the paper till and earned my first paid shift.”

**People in this story.** [Selena Sáenz](#ah-r0715) (detour manager). A cake for the driver arrives separately from the complaint for the planner.

**Personality.** Jo loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and a little coil of soft measuring tape. Jo has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears. Jo draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A passenger asks to photograph a stranger through the kitchen window. Jo waits for the resident to give permission before a camera goes up.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Jo separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Jo offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Ismael Gross

AH-R0720 · Age 64 · Home-based translator · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember recognizing the first silence after a road reopened.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Ismael by a small Friday habit. Ismael watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I kept my curtains closed for years. The final empty morning was when I missed strangers most.”

**What Ismael wants.** To reopen the curtain on an ordinary morning.

**Family.** [Pascal Boone](../09/07-school-after-sixty.md#ah-r1270) is Ismael’s cousin. Ismael chooses the familiar route. Pascal can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *School after sixty*, Pascal says, “I brought sandwiches for after, regardless of marks.”

**People in this story.** [Jack Saito](#ah-r0713). A commuter's wave and a newly reopened curtain survive the end of the detour.

**Personality.** Ismael enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Ismael is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a pale blue work shirt and one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. Ismael has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with two white streaks in otherwise dark hair. Ismael raises one eyebrow long before saying yes during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The resident closes the curtains during the morning detour. Ismael treats the closed curtain as the resident's answer for that day.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Ismael asks the cashier quietly which item can be set aside without making the customer explain their finances to the line.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Ismael keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.
