# The free fringe

[People behind the counters](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Coastal Barber.** Coastal Barber advertises one free fringe trim a day. The first person to queue wants to sit and talk without having a haircut.

## Percy Davila

AH-R0701 · Age 46 · Recently separated machinist · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember getting my first trim after I moved into a rented room.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Percy by a small Monday habit. Percy can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I used the free offer as an excuse to get out of my room after my husband left.”

**What Percy wants.** To get a morning outside the empty room.

**Family.** [Philip Meyer](../08/08-a-summer-marriage.md#ah-r1122) is Percy’s half-sibling. Percy brings a spare shopping bag. Philip chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A summer marriage*, Philip says, “I offered to officiate because I could do the ceremony in both their languages.”

**People in this story.** [Chris Valdez](#ah-r0702) (barber). A free fringe buys the customer and the barber a perfectly real conversation.

**Personality.** Percy keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Percy wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Percy is easy to recognize by a center part and straight chestnut hair and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf, Percy holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A customer uses the free haircut to talk. Percy lets the visitor say whether they would like a trim as well as a talk.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Percy offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Percy offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Chris Valdez

AH-R0702 · Age 38 · Barber · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the customer who kept asking for the same unfinished story.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the bank steps, where Chris takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I timed the conversation like a normal trim. When the bell rang we were halfway through a good story.”

**What Chris wants.** To offer conversation the time allowed for a haircut.

**Family.** [Orla March](../04/14-laundry-for-a-stranger.md#ah-r0581) is Chris’s first cousin once removed. Chris gets to change the plan. Orla offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *Laundry for a stranger*, Orla says, “I iron for the young man opposite because he held my hand when the ambulance took my wife.”

**People in this story.** [Percy Davila](#ah-r0701) (barber customer). A free fringe buys the customer and the barber a perfectly real conversation.

**Personality.** Chris always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Chris is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Chris is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a watch on a cracked leather band, Chris starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Someone waiting in line complains. Chris tells the waiting customer roughly how much of the appointment is still left.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Chris asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Chris suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Cora draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first when listening.

**Under pressure**

## Duncan Woods

AH-R0704 · Age 28 · Commission barber · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first pay packet listing cuts instead of hours.” People around Studio Boulevard know Duncan by a small Tuesday habit. Duncan waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I am paid by the cut. I'm asking the owner to count time spent with a customer who keeps their hair.”

**What Duncan wants.** To count uncut appointments in the wage calculation.

**Family.** [Una Roman](../03/12-one-unposted-detour.md#ah-r0420) is Duncan’s cousin raised in the same household. Duncan is free to hang up first. Una makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *One unposted detour*, Una says, “I am counting how many people stop to help.”

**People in this story.** [Percy Wade](#ah-r0710). They make the case for a paid quiet appointment and a usable after-school hour.

**Personality.** Duncan enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Duncan is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Duncan listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Someone waiting in line complains. Duncan tells the waiting customer roughly how much of the appointment is still left.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Duncan asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Duncan puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Cecil Hassan

AH-R0706 · Age 41 · Deli cashier · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember showing a scar I usually hid with my hand.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Cecil by a small Saturday habit. Cecil pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I used to dye my fringe to hide a scar. I came to have the scar shown for the first time.”

**What Cecil wants.** To wear the scar openly after the trim.

**Family.** [Celia Lai](../04/01-the-button-card.md#ah-r0457) is Cecil’s stepsibling. Cecil brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Celia checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The button card*, Celia says, “I need an enormous button I can close with one useful thumb.”

**People in this story.** [Loretta Lu](#ah-r0708). A client revealing a scar provides a child with a braver explanation for the chair.

**Personality.** Cecil takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Cecil can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Cecil has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for short black hair swept back. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation Cecil folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- Someone waiting in line complains. Cecil tells the waiting customer roughly how much of the appointment is still left.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Cecil puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Cecil turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Pat Fraser

AH-R0707 · Age 54 · Former shop partner · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my old business partner closing the shutter early for me.” On Wednesday, near Juniper Street, Pat knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I heard their conversation through the wall and realized I owed my old shop partner a call.”

**What Pat wants.** To call the former shop partner.

**Family.** [Anton Shah](../04/06-the-waiting-room-plant.md#ah-r0507) is Pat’s cousin. Pat is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Anton calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The waiting-room plant*, Anton says, “I used the leaves to teach shadows to a child in isolation.”

**People in this story.** [Eli Aranda](#ah-r0709). A conversation heard through the wall finds its way back into a school costume story.

**Personality.** Pat admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Pat learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Pat is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with one cuff folded back twice, Pat presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A customer uses the free haircut to talk. Pat lets the visitor say whether they would like a trim as well as a talk.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Pat keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Pat describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Percy Wade

AH-R0710 · Age 48 · After-school worker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember talking softly to a frightened child waiting for their older brother.” Thursday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Percy can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I want the free slot moved to after school once a week so the kid minding his sister gets a turn.”

**What Percy wants.** To save an after-school slot for a sibling carer.

**Family.** [Alvin Kapoor](../04/10-closing-soup.md#ah-r0548) is Percy’s half-sibling. Percy calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Alvin leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Closing soup*, Alvin says, “I hate celery and always dug it out. Tonight the cook asked me to choose tomorrow's base.”

**People in this story.** [Duncan Woods](#ah-r0704). They make the case for a paid quiet appointment and a usable after-school hour.

**Personality.** Percy has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Percy's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. Percy has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Percy raises one eyebrow long before saying yes during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Someone waiting in line complains. Percy tells the waiting customer roughly how much of the appointment is still left.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Percy offers to cover one concrete expense and leaves the friend room to say the expense isn't the real problem.
- If the joke lands badly, Percy seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.
