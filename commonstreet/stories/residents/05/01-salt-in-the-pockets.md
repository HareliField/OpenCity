## Dale Malik

AH-R0601 · Age 47 · Former deck worker · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my last night aboard with a coat too stiff to fold.” People around the downtown fountain know Dale by a small Thursday habit. Dale keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I kept the salt from my last deck shift in my cuff. I wish I'd said something before the rinse.”

**What Dale wants.** To take the last deck salt home intact.

**Family.** [Julia Cao](../09/10-a-four-o-clock-kitchen.md#ah-r1299) is Dale’s stepsibling. Dale calls about good news first. Julia is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *A four o'clock kitchen*, Julia says, “I want the port to subsidize a workers' meal without owning the menu or the cook's schedule.”

**People in this story.** [Teresa Ellis](#ah-r0605). They establish which salt coat belongs to which member of the deck crew.

**Personality.** Dale finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Dale has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. In conversation Dale gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- A coat's owner claims the crystals in the lining. Dale sets the coat aside until the owner can identify the crystals privately.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Dale puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Dale waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Winifred Reese

AH-R0602 · Age 40 · Workwear clerk · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember telling snow apart from salt by touch.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Winifred by a small Tuesday habit. Winifred likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “My mother used to shake out our coats over a blue plate and read the weather in the grains.”

**What Winifred wants.** To revive the family's winter weather plate.

**Family.** [Abel Weaver](../01/09-the-porch-light.md#ah-r0081) is Winifred’s parent’s cousin. The family has a birthday plate with a shallow crack. Whoever borrows it is responsible for returning it in person, which has salvaged more than one bad goodbye. Winifred makes sure it reaches the person who nearly didn't come. Asked about *The porch light*, Abel says, “My wife left the lamp on for the last nurse walking home from St.”

**People in this story.** [Cian Khan](#ah-r0610). They label the crystals in both scientific and household terms.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Winifred has one cheek that creases before the other, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Winifred leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An employer wants every coat returned before supper. Winifred asks the employer which garments are actually needed for tonight's shift.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Winifred keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Winifred listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Talia Barclay

AH-R0603 · Age 24 · Bakery worker · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first secret wage tucked in an empty loaf bag.” Monday often includes a stop at the bank steps, where Talia takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I thought the powder was plaster. It was the sugar from the bakery where I secretly work weekends.”

**What Talia wants.** To tell the household about the second bakery income.

**Family.** [Lydia Lai](../02/04-the-second-dog.md#ah-r0186) is Talia’s cousin. Talia decides what stays off the paper. Lydia brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The second dog*, Lydia says, “I offered free vaccination records and discovered our clinic had two birthdays for one animal.”

**People in this story.** [Winifred Osborne](#ah-r0607). A secret second wage and an asking-for-a-raise letter could both come out in the wash.

**Personality.** Talia is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Talia loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Talia is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Talia grins on one side while the eyes stay serious when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A coat's owner claims the crystals in the lining. Talia sets the coat aside until the owner can identify the crystals privately.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Talia asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- If somebody calls for applause, Talia gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Jean Shen

AH-R0604 · Age 35 · Neighborhood photographer · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember taking pictures on the day nobody remembered the camera.” People around Tideline Market know Jean by a small Monday habit. Jean picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I took a photograph because it looked like our street under the one snowfall I remember.”

**What Jean wants.** To print a picture of the white crystals.

**Family.** [Galen Howe](../01/11-ten-fingers.md#ah-r0108) is Jean’s cousin raised in the same household. Jean chooses whether to use it. Galen saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *Ten fingers*, Galen says, “We used to compete for the same corner lease.”

**People in this story.** [Desmond Baldwin](#ah-r0608). The photograph of the white table picks up a small embroidered fish.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Jean takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Jean has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. Jean squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An employer wants every coat returned before supper. Jean asks the employer which garments are actually needed for tonight's shift.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Jean asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Jean asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Teresa Ellis

AH-R0605 · Age 42 · Laundry sorter · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember three brothers ordering the same size jacket.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Teresa picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I wanted to return every coat to the right worker. It turns out three brothers trade them.”

**What Teresa wants.** To return all three coats to the brothers together.

**Family.** [Pari Zed](../01/11-ten-fingers.md#ah-r0104) is Teresa’s second cousin. Teresa brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Pari checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Ten fingers*, Pari says, “I made the wrong red three summers ago. She wore it to her anniversary dinner anyway.”

**People in this story.** [Dale Malik](#ah-r0601). They establish which salt coat belongs to which member of the deck crew.

**Personality.** Teresa can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Teresa has a long plait tucked inside the collar, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. When somebody else has the floor, Teresa takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A coat's owner claims the crystals in the lining. Teresa sets the coat aside until the owner can identify the crystals privately.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Teresa starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Teresa asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Daniel Song

AH-R0606 · Age 51 · Ferry attendant · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a ferry seat warmed through by a folded coat.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Daniel knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I need the big coat to keep my knees warm on the early ferry. The owner gave me the name of its maker.”

**What Daniel wants.** To find a warm coat cut like the old ferry one.

**Family.** [Gil Atwood](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0022) is Daniel’s second cousin. Daniel knows which ones can be shared. Gil keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The small signature*, Gil says, “My eleven dollars were the change regulars left in a jar after a boy broke the chess table.”

**People in this story.** [Miguel Grant](#ah-r0609) (workwear repairer). A familiar stain helps a family recognize a coat the ferry passenger wants to copy.

**Personality.** Daniel can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Daniel can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Daniel has a center part and straight chestnut hair, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Daniel steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- An employer wants every coat returned before supper. Daniel asks the employer which garments are actually needed for tonight's shift.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Daniel asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Daniel suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Winifred Osborne

**Life in the city.** “I still remember practicing a request for better wages into a sink.” Monday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Winifred takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**What Winifred wants.** To ask for the pay rise in person.

**Family.** [Taylor Lucas](../06/07-reading-the-payslip.md#ah-r0814) is Winifred’s second cousin. Winifred brings a spare shopping bag. Taylor chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Reading the payslip*, Taylor says, “I earned more in tips last month than my own manager.”

**People in this story.** [Talia Barclay](#ah-r0603). A secret second wage and an asking-for-a-raise letter could both come out in the wash.

**Personality.** Winifred carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Winifred has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for a high puff tied with plain cord. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Winifred takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A coat's owner claims the crystals in the lining. Winifred sets the coat aside until the owner can identify the crystals privately.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Winifred takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Winifred offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Miguel Grant

AH-R0609 · Age 39 · Furniture warehouse worker · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my child mistaking my new work clothes for a disguise.” On Monday, near Ferris Deli, Miguel asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I didn't recognize my own coat clean. My kid found it by the spot where I'd held her crayons.”

**What Miguel wants.** To let the child choose the recognizable work coat.

**Family.** [Petra Jones](../04/07-a-borrowed-name.md#ah-r0515) is Miguel’s stepsibling. Miguel calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Petra leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A borrowed name*, Petra says, “I sold out of copies until the real man stood in the doorway.”

**People in this story.** [Daniel Song](#ah-r0606) (ferry client). A familiar stain helps a family recognize a coat the ferry passenger wants to copy.

**Personality.** Miguel treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Miguel lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Miguel has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Miguel rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A coat's owner claims the crystals in the lining. Miguel sets the coat aside until the owner can identify the crystals privately.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Miguel asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Miguel says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Cian Khan

AH-R0610 · Age 31 · Chemistry technician · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember crystals growing on a saucer over my childhood bed.” On Sunday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Cian can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I wanted a scientific name for every crystal until the dockers started giving me better names.”

**What Cian wants.** To get the dock workers' words alongside the scientific names.

**Family.** [Gita Moss](../01/06-window-seven.md#ah-r0054) is Cian’s older relative by marriage. Cian sets the time to leave. Gita picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Window seven*, Gita says, “I delivered yellow roses to the wrong bed here once.”

**People in this story.** [Winifred Reese](#ah-r0602). They label the crystals in both scientific and household terms.

**Personality.** Cian is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Cian is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Cian is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Cian starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An employer wants every coat returned before supper. Cian asks the employer which garments are actually needed for tonight's shift.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Cian walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Cian agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.
