## Beatrice Ko

AH-R0721 · Age 38 · Restaurant worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a pan that sat at an angle on our first hob.” People around Harbour Bakery know Beatrice by a small Tuesday habit. Beatrice buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I chose a saucepan with a dent from my first flat. Its note is flatter than any kitchen I have now.”

**What Beatrice wants.** To play the saucepan's actual imperfect pitch.

**Family.** [Sol Lane](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0030) is Beatrice’s parent’s cousin. Beatrice chooses the music. Sol puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The small signature*, Sol says, “My wife and I used to play chess with bottle caps here.”

**People in this story.** [Kate Dinh](#ah-r0726). A dented saucepan and a typewriter find neighboring places in the same score.

**Personality.** Beatrice is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Beatrice loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Beatrice is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Beatrice listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An instrument breaks before the fountain piece begins. Beatrice asks the player whether a different everyday object can carry the missing note.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Beatrice keeps the neighbor's place and finds out whether the stranger needs a different kind of help.
- If somebody calls for applause, Beatrice gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Kai Williams

AH-R0722 · Age 54 · Joiner · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my apprentice rule snapping shut in an examiner's pocket.” On Friday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Kai takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I offered my carpenter's rule. Snapped shut, it sounds exactly like the fountain starting.”

**What Kai wants.** To use a snapping rule without spoiling the student's rhythm.

**Family.** [Julius Zhou](../08/11-the-hair-after-illness.md#ah-r1160) is Kai’s stepsibling. Kai checks the date twice. Julius brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The hair after illness*, Julius says, “I wanted a family picture. We gave her the remote shutter so she decided which smile stayed.”

**People in this story.** [Kai Chan](#ah-r0729). A rule snapping shut and a borrowed whistle stop the handwritten score sounding too polite. Also in the story is [Una Kapoor](#ah-r0730) (music teacher).

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Kai takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Kai has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a pale blue work shirt with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. In conversation Kai gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- The student asks the least confident player to start. Kai gives the player a clear first beat and permission to pass the opening on.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Kai finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Kai asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Howard Heller

AH-R0723 · Age 43 · Department store clerk · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a keyring from the place where I got my first job.” People around Breakwater Way know Howard by a small Thursday habit. Howard stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I came prepared to laugh at the performance. My car keys became the last instrument in it.”

**What Howard wants.** To bring the car keys back for the next concert.

**Family.** [Faye Tate](../07/12-the-crash-anniversary.md#ah-r1015) is Howard’s cousin raised in the same household. Howard brings a spare shopping bag. Faye chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The crash anniversary*, Faye says, “I take flowers home after they wilt so the family doesn't have to see them wet and gray.”

**People in this story.** [Malcolm Tan](#ah-r0727). Keys and the old packing tin place a shop closing inside the music.

**Personality.** Howard can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Howard has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Howard rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- An instrument breaks before the fountain piece begins. Howard asks the player whether a different everyday object can carry the missing note.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Howard asks each person to say what they believe is actually owed before anybody proposes a payment.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Howard asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Logan Hill

AH-R0724 · Age 41 · Night courier · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember hearing my voice break in the school hall.” On Sunday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Logan takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I was asked to hum. I haven't sung in public since my voice changed and nobody let me forget it.”

**What Logan wants.** To hum a whole line in public.

**Family.** [Magdalena Zhang](../09/05-a-clean-coat-for-court.md#ah-r1250) is Logan’s cousin. Logan asks before bringing somebody along. Magdalena can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A clean coat for court*, Magdalena says, “I want the coat returned unwashed because the lender's old dog knows its smell.”

**People in this story.** [Una Kapoor](#ah-r0730). The teacher adapting the piece inland hears a singer find a voice for the first time.

**Personality.** Logan can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Logan can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Logan has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Logan starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The student asks the least confident player to start. Logan gives the player a clear first beat and permission to pass the opening on.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Logan brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Logan suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Mara Massey

AH-R0725 · Age 53 · Sleep clinic receptionist · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember trying to sleep beside a fountain that ran all night.” Monday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Mara notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I can't stand the sound of running water at night. The student gave me the part that stops it.”

**What Mara wants.** To give the piece a real ending.

**Family.** [Isaac Nord](../07/05-the-release-address.md#ah-r0950) is Mara’s younger relative by marriage. Mara makes room at the table. Isaac brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The release address*, Isaac says, “I was the child writing envelopes for my mother.”

**People in this story.** [Damian Harris](#ah-r0728). Breaking a pitched glass gives the player who hates running water a way to write silence into it.

**Personality.** Mara carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Mara is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Mara starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An instrument breaks before the fountain piece begins. Mara asks the player whether a different everyday object can carry the missing note.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Mara offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Mara offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Kate Dinh

AH-R0726 · Age 62 · Former typist · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember typing my brother's first play without correcting it.” On Monday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Kate knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I typed the program on my late partner's machine and got cast as its percussionist.”

**What Kate wants.** To play the typewriter on a public stage.

**Family.** [Eileen Bloom](../08/12-the-high-road-home.md#ah-r1161) is Kate’s younger cousin. A family picnic once turned into a six-hour wait at a bus shelter. What they remember best is the oldest relative teaching everyone to keep score with gravel. Kate always checks the last journey home. Asked about *The high road home*, Eileen says, “I passed my test and kept turning down jobs east of the ridge.”

**People in this story.** [Beatrice Ko](#ah-r0721). A dented saucepan and a typewriter find neighboring places in the same score.

**Personality.** Kate has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Kate has salt-and-pepper curls, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a little coil of soft measuring tape. When somebody else has the floor, Kate grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- The student asks the least confident player to start. Kate gives the player a clear first beat and permission to pass the opening on.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Kate asks what the friend actually said last time and checks whether anybody is expecting them at home.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Kate says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Kai Chan

AH-R0729 · Age 36 · Whistle seller · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember selling my first whistle to my own uncle.” On Wednesday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Kai knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**What Kai wants.** To loan a tune without selling an instrument.

**Family.** [Rowan Reyes](../03/14-the-housecoat-commission.md#ah-r0440) is Kai’s second cousin. Kai sets the time to leave. Rowan picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The housecoat commission*, Rowan says, “I am the neighbor invited last. I promised to dress up until she told me to come straight from work.”

**People in this story.** [Kai Williams](#ah-r0722). A rule snapping shut and a borrowed whistle stop the handwritten score sounding too polite.

**Personality.** Kai tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Kai will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Kai has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. In conversation Kai gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- An instrument breaks before the fountain piece begins. Kai asks the player whether a different everyday object can carry the missing note.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Kai offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Kai repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

AH-R0730 · Age 47 · Primary music teacher · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember giving a concert for a class with no instruments.” People around Ferris Deli know Una by a small Saturday habit. Una asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I am writing the instructions so a class inland can play the same score with whatever is in their pockets.”

**What Una wants.** To write a playable version for an inland class.

**Family.** [Camille Marks](../06/07-reading-the-payslip.md#ah-r0817) is Una’s cousin. Una leaves the first voice message. Camille calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Reading the payslip*, Camille says, “I used a fake example until a cleaner spotted the exact deduction her daughter pays.”

**People in this story.** [Logan Hill](#ah-r0724). The teacher adapting the piece inland hears a singer find a voice for the first time. Also in the story is [Kai Williams](#ah-r0722) (musician).

**Personality.** Una is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Una asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Una has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Una listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The student asks the least confident player to start. Una gives the player a clear first beat and permission to pass the opening on.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Una tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Una accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.
