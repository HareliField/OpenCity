# The sandwich board

[What the neighbors kept](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Bank of the Coast.** A bank auction includes the sandwich board of a shuttered sandwich business. Someone has rubbed out every price except the cheapest.

## Harley Cao

AH-R0171 · Age 35 · Sandwich cart cook · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “Our family recipes survive on the backs of eleven greasy menus.” People around the bank steps know Harley by a small Friday habit. Harley takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “My mum wrote that last price for the kids from the court. I want to chalk it back in every morning.”

**What Harley wants.** To put the children's price back on the board.

**Family.** [Ashley West](../10/15-the-last-light-at-the-harbor.md#ah-r1491) is Harley’s stepsibling. Harley keeps the photographs. Ashley knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The last light at the harbor*, Ashley says, “I always leave my lamp longest because nobody is waiting in my flat.”

**People in this story.** [Sonia Lee](#ah-r0180). The person who wants the cheap price restored hears why all the other prices came off. Also in the story is [Rosemary Morris](#ah-r0172) (auction lender).

**Personality.** Harley has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Harley has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Harley counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers to erase the board and repaint it. Harley asks the former owner to record the board before the paint is changed.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Harley offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If accused of favoritism, Harley explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Rosemary Morris

AH-R0172 · Age 47 · Loan officer · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I once financed an ice machine without noticing the shop had no summer lease.” Monday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Rosemary checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I lent against the fridge. Seeing the board in the lot makes me wonder what else my signature swept up.”

**What Rosemary wants.** To get the lender to identify exactly what the auction includes.

**Family.** [Diana Romero](../07/09-a-tenant-who-stays.md#ah-r0983) is Rosemary’s half-sibling. Rosemary makes the call. Diana is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A tenant who stays*, Diana says, “I manage the house on a spreadsheet. The first visit changed which numbers I wanted beside the price.”

**People in this story.** [Lena Roth](#ah-r0177). They review exactly which business assets the auction swept up. Also in the story is [Harley Cao](#ah-r0171) (auction bidder).

**Personality.** Rosemary reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Rosemary trusts most.

**Appearance.** Rosemary has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cream pullover with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Rosemary squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer wants a debt to stay between two people. Rosemary keeps the amount beside the old customer's name off the public board.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Rosemary looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- If told to back off, Rosemary does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Emery Gordon

AH-R0173 · Age 59 · Theater sign painter · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “My wife and I painted theater flats in a room too narrow to lay them down.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Emery by a small Wednesday habit. Emery checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I bid because there is a second menu under the first. My wife painted it on our honeymoon.”

**What Emery wants.** To recover the older painted menu without damaging the top one.

**Family.** [Lucia Byrd](../09/01-a-room-above-the-dryers.md#ah-r1204) is Emery’s younger cousin. Emery leaves the first voice message. Lucia calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A room above the dryers*, Lucia says, “I am the worker's partner. I can give up the kitchen table for art but I cannot give up part of the rent for a studio.”

**People in this story.** [Levi Lucas](#ah-r0176). They document the hidden menu and credit the original chalk writing.

**Personality.** Emery has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Emery's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Emery has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for a neat white crop. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. In conversation Emery squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers to erase the board and repaint it. Emery asks the former owner to record the board before the paint is changed.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Emery stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- If the joke lands badly, Emery seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Kathleen Miranda

AH-R0174 · Age 53 · Hotel night clerk · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “The first lunch anybody bought me here came wrapped in a crossword puzzle.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Kathleen by a small Saturday habit. Kathleen prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I used to eat there on credit. I added my unpaid tab to the winning bid.”

**What Kathleen wants.** To settle an old sandwich debt with the former cook.

**Family.** [Esme Knight](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0975) is Kathleen’s cousin raised in the same household. Kathleen chooses the familiar route. Esme can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Esme says, “I am the landlord. I counted empty tables during a summer evening for the first time.”

**People in this story.** [Lara Emery](#ah-r0178). Getting the board home gives an old customer somewhere to settle the tab.

**Personality.** Kathleen listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. Kathleen has a wide brow and a narrow jaw, with auburn hair cut square at the nape. Kathleen looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer wants a debt to stay between two people. Kathleen keeps the amount beside the old customer's name off the public board.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Kathleen walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- When a second witness disagrees, Kathleen keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Hank Meyer

AH-R0175 · Age 31 · Produce buyer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I grew up translating market prices into the number of bedsheets Mum had to iron.” People around Breakwater Way know Hank by a small Sunday habit. Hank stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I thought the numbers were dates. My father can still remember what each filling cost.”

**What Hank wants.** To give the family a list of the original fillings.

**Family.** [Yasmin Mason](../09/02-the-worker-who-couldn-t-read-the-joke.md#ah-r1218) is Hank’s second cousin. Hank brings the paper and a pen. Yasmin decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The worker who couldn't read the joke*, Yasmin says, “I was the only one who understood both languages and looked away.”

**People in this story.** [Pascal Cullen](#ah-r0179). They try dating the menu before the millionaire bidder buys a personal memory.

**Personality.** Hank talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Hank's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Hank is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Hank holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers to erase the board and repaint it. Hank asks the former owner to record the board before the paint is changed.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Hank suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Hank says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Levi Lucas

AH-R0176 · Age 26 · Graphic designer · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “My first font was copied from a van parked outside our old flat.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Levi by a small Saturday habit. Levi watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I copied the typeface for a design job and never credited the woman holding the chalk.”

**What Levi wants.** To credit the chalk writer on the next design job.

**Family.** [Gabriel Ibrahim](../10/05-the-return-of-a-witness.md#ah-r1400) is Levi’s parent’s cousin. Levi calls about good news first. Gabriel is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The return of a witness*, Gabriel says, “I care for the aunt. We set a lunchtime end so a hard visit can still have an ordinary evening after it.”

**People in this story.** [Emery Gordon](#ah-r0173). They document the hidden menu and credit the original chalk writing.

**Personality.** Levi prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Levi has a high puff tied with plain cord, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Levi counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer wants a debt to stay between two people. Levi keeps the amount beside the old customer's name off the public board.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Levi asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Levi clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Lena Roth

AH-R0177 · Age 51 · Business adviser · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I started advising owners after failing at selling curtains myself.” On Saturday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Lena notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I promised the owner her business would survive. I want to return at least one thing I promised.”

**What Lena wants.** To return one promised piece of the closed shop.

**Family.** [Casey Hunter](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1303) is Lena’s cousin. Lena remembers the birthdays. Casey asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Casey says, “I am the fishing partner who was there that day.”

**People in this story.** [Rosemary Morris](#ah-r0172). They review exactly which business assets the auction swept up.

**Personality.** Lena never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Lena can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Lena has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cream pullover with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. In conversation Lena raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers to erase the board and repaint it. Lena asks the former owner to record the board before the paint is changed.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Lena takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Lena may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Lara Emery

AH-R0178 · Age 34 · Furniture courier · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I once delivered a wardrobe to the flat where I used to sleep on the floor.” On Thursday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Lara likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I carried the board home and it blocked my front door. That's how my new neighbor met me.”

**What Lara wants.** To build a cart that takes the board through the new doorway.

**Family.** [Elliot Reed](../06/01-the-upstairs-lease.md#ah-r0756) is Lara’s older relative by marriage. Lara offers the first invitation. Elliot gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The upstairs lease*, Elliot says, “I sublet my sofa last winter and learned the difference between having room and having privacy.”

**People in this story.** [Kathleen Miranda](#ah-r0174). Getting the board home gives an old customer somewhere to settle the tab.

**Personality.** Lara often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Lara have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Lara is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Lara takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer wants a debt to stay between two people. Lara keeps the amount beside the old customer's name off the public board.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Lara walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Lara rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Pascal Cullen

AH-R0179 · Age 69 · Retired supermarket millionaire · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “My first store had a dented freezer and a landlord who shopped elsewhere.” On Monday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Pascal pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I sold my own shop for millions. I'm bidding for my first customer's handwriting, which nobody can appraise.”

**What Pascal wants.** To buy the handwriting without taking it from the neighborhood.

**Family.** [Greta Ryan](../09/10-a-four-o-clock-kitchen.md#ah-r1300) is Pascal’s younger cousin. Pascal puts the kettle on. Greta chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *A four o'clock kitchen*, Greta says, “I came in on my day off to take one breakfast order from the cook and serve it to her with her children.”

**People in this story.** [Hank Meyer](#ah-r0175). They try dating the menu before the millionaire bidder buys a personal memory.

**Personality.** Pascal thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Pascal sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Pascal has a round face with a pronounced chin, with steel-colored hair cut square above the collar. Pascal gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer offers to erase the board and repaint it. Pascal asks the former owner to record the board before the paint is changed.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Pascal separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Pascal asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Sonia Lee

AH-R0180 · Age 40 · Counter assistant · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “At home the cheapest meal was always named for the person who invented it.” People around the downtown fountain know Sonia by a small Wednesday habit. Sonia keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I'm the one who wiped off the prices. I wanted people to ask what the cheap lunch actually bought.”

**What Sonia wants.** To tell the waiting bidders why one price survived.

**Family.** [Dora Beck](../01/01-the-blue-suit.md#ah-r0008) is Sonia’s cousin. Sonia chooses the music. Dora puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The blue suit*, Dora says, “The rinse valve came from a batch I signed for with my brother's pencil.”

**People in this story.** [Harley Cao](#ah-r0171). The person who wants the cheap price restored hears why all the other prices came off.

**Personality.** Sonia gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Sonia privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Sonia has a high puff tied with plain cord, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Sonia looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer wants a debt to stay between two people. Sonia keeps the amount beside the old customer's name off the public board.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Sonia takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Sonia offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.
