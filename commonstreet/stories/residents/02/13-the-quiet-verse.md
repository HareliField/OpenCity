# The quiet verse

## Bianca Sethi

AH-R0271 · Age 15 · Student songwriter · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I first wrote lyrics in the backs of permission slips nobody returned.” People around Maré Coffee know Bianca by a small Wednesday habit. Bianca likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I wrote the verse about my dad and then decided nobody gets to hear it yet.”

**What Bianca wants.** To decide personally if the private verse will ever be heard.

**Family.** [Samuel Choi](../03/03-the-widow-s-dividend.md#ah-r0323) is Bianca’s older relative by marriage. Bianca remembers the birthdays. Samuel asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The widow's dividend*, Samuel says, “I found a pencil debt to my mother between two lines about roofing nails.”

**People in this story.** [Annette Alder](#ah-r0277) (choir director). The conductor and writer agree who controls the unsung verse.

**Personality.** Bianca keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Bianca wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Bianca has a round face with a pronounced chin, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Bianca gives a little nod before asking a hard question during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The silent verse gets applause in the wrong place. Bianca gives the singer time to resume instead of trying to conduct the applause.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Bianca offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Bianca offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Marina Bly

AH-R0272 · Age 43 · Car body painter · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I once paid for a solo coach and my child wanted drumming instead.” On Friday, near Tideline Market, Marina picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I bought concert tickets for grandparents who expected a solo. I need to explain why silence is still a performance.”

**What Marina wants.** To help the grandparents understand their ticket bought a child's whole performance.

**Family.** [Basil Hunter](../10/05-the-return-of-a-witness.md#ah-r1395) is Marina’s stepsibling. Marina offers the first invitation. Basil gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The return of a witness*, Basil says, “I own the corner shop. I watched the old argument from my counter and want her to know what I saw afterward.”

**People in this story.** [Casey King](#ah-r0280). The adult buying the tickets meets a grandparent with a story about private pages.

**Personality.** Marina always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Marina is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Marina has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Marina counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A parent demands to hear the private words. Marina leaves the private verse with its writer.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Marina offers to cover one concrete expense and leaves the friend room to say the expense isn't the real problem.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Marina suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Wesley Carr

AH-R0273 · Age 16 · Student interpreter · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I signed prayers with Gran long before I could spell their opening words.” On Friday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Wesley knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I signed the words so my sister could follow. She saw the withheld verse in my hands at rehearsal.”

**What Wesley wants.** To agree with the writer which signs may appear at the show.

**Family.** [Gavin Roche](../08/03-the-guarantee.md#ah-r1073) is Wesley’s first cousin once removed. Wesley puts the kettle on. Gavin chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The guarantee*, Gavin says, “I processed the loan in six minutes and spent an hour telling the family how that was possible.”

**People in this story.** [Maeve Hamm](#ah-r0278). Signing in the pause makes room for a listener who heard the fountain underneath it.

**Personality.** Wesley has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Wesley knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Wesley has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. When somebody else has the floor, Wesley presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- The silent verse gets applause in the wrong place. Wesley gives the singer time to resume instead of trying to conduct the applause.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Wesley takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Wesley asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Lucinda Reyes

AH-R0274 · Age 47 · Choral accompanist · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I nearly quit piano when my teacher stopped counting during our rests.” People around Harbour Bakery know Lucinda by a small Sunday habit. Lucinda buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I count rests by tapping my left shoe. A child who stammers asked to stand beside me.”

**What Lucinda wants.** To give the pupil a quiet way to count the rest.

**Family.** [Jorge Nash](../04/14-laundry-for-a-stranger.md#ah-r0582) is Lucinda’s cousin raised in the same household. Lucinda chooses an ordinary story to tell. Jorge asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Laundry for a stranger*, Jorge says, “I thought my landlord paid for laundry. I want to repay the neighbor in a way she can decline.”

**People in this story.** [Martin Martin](#ah-r0279). The accompanist's foot tapping stays on the recording along with the held breath.

**Personality.** Lucinda enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Lucinda has short black hair swept back, a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. When somebody else has the floor, Lucinda draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A parent demands to hear the private words. Lucinda leaves the private verse with its writer.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Lucinda tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Lucinda puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Lily Thomas

AH-R0275 · Age 39 · Park events clerk · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I timed my earliest park shows with the clock on a kitchen oven.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Lily takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I booked the park for ninety minutes. The silent verse was the first minute I stopped watching the clock.”

**What Lily wants.** To let a park performance run to the singers' time.

**Family.** [Loretta Lu](../05/11-the-free-fringe.md#ah-r0708) is Lily’s second cousin. Lily checks the date twice. Loretta brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The free fringe*, Loretta says, “I ordered a fringe I didn't like just so my child would try the chair.”

**People in this story.** [Lucinda Duran](#ah-r0276). They sort out which interruption was an accident and which one belongs in the second show.

**Personality.** Lily has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Lily can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Lily has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Lily starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- The silent verse gets applause in the wrong place. Lily gives the singer time to resume instead of trying to conduct the applause.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Lily offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- If a friend borrows money, Lily agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Lucinda Duran

That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The absent signature*, Sage says, “I found her address on an old recipe card. I won't give it away until she asks me to.”

**People in this story.** [Lily Thomas](#ah-r0275). They sort out which interruption was an accident and which one belongs in the second show.

**Appearance.** Lucinda has a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears, a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Lucinda presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- A parent demands to hear the private words. Lucinda leaves the private verse with its writer.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Lucinda suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Lucinda turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Annette Alder

AH-R0277 · Age 53 · Children's choir director · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still have a report that said I sang too quietly for a lead.” People around Juniper Street know Annette by a small Sunday habit. Annette knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I conduct the choir. My instinct was to fill the space and I practiced keeping my hands down.”

**What Annette wants.** To teach the choir to hold the silence together.

**Family.** [Johan Vega](../08/14-the-new-language-at-dinner.md#ah-r1186) is Annette’s stepsibling. Annette asks before bringing somebody along. Johan can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The new language at dinner*, Johan says, “I can swap two calls and cover one dinner. Somebody needs to count the extra bus fare.”

**People in this story.** [Bianca Sethi](#ah-r0271) (choir student). The conductor and writer agree who controls the unsung verse.

**Personality.** Annette admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Annette learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Annette has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an old plum windbreaker with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Annette listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- The silent verse gets applause in the wrong place. Annette gives the singer time to resume instead of trying to conduct the applause.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Annette puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Annette describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

Asked about *A borrowed name*, Adrian says, “I hate the fictional nickname. I brought the author the one the children actually gave me.”

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a washed violet jersey and a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. Maeve has fine brows over wide-set eyes, with close-cropped dark curls. Maeve counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

## Casey King

AH-R0280 · Age 74 · Retired PE teacher · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “At school I sat through an assembly about a secret nobody should have told.” On Thursday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Casey watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I was furious there was a secret verse until my grandson asked which page of my diary he could read.”

**What Casey wants.** To share a memory with the grandson without asking for his diary.

**Family.** [Molly Neal](../05/04-one-clean-day.md#ah-r0634) is Casey’s niece by marriage. Casey picks the meeting place. Molly sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *One clean day*, Molly says, “I had a speech ready about service. My old desk mate handed me a skipping rope and counted out loud.”

**People in this story.** [Marina Bly](#ah-r0272). The adult buying the tickets meets a grandparent with a story about private pages.

**Personality.** Casey has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Casey's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Casey is easy to recognize by soft white hair brushed sideways and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Casey looks toward the door whenever a name is called when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A parent demands to hear the private words. Casey leaves the private verse with its writer.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Casey walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- If the joke lands badly, Casey seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.
