## Cory Palmer

AH-R0201 · Age 48 · Medical transcriptionist · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I worked first in a ward where patients signed their lunch trays with stickers.” On Monday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Cory takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I put it on the chair where my sister was sitting before her diagnosis. Somebody thought it was lost property.”

**What Cory wants.** To keep a chair available without making the ward think it is reserved forever.

**Family.** [Frances Duran](../07/12-the-crash-anniversary.md#ah-r1014) is Cory’s cousin raised in the same household. A holiday photograph lost its names when somebody replaced the frame. Between them they have identified every person except the one holding the camera. Cory can date a whole summer by somebody's shoes. Asked about *The crash anniversary*, Frances says, “I designed the crossing and have read four reports.”

**People in this story.** [Dale Nielsen](#ah-r0202). A saved seat and a child's game account for the mitten's first small journey.

**Personality.** Cory admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Cory learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Cory is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a moss-green cardigan with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Cory holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The waiting-room mitten is about to be thrown away. Cory asks whether the other mitten is still being looked for.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Cory takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Cory describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Dale Nielsen

AH-R0202 · Age 34 · Parent and school kitchen worker · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “My son learned the word tomorrow too early for my liking.” Monday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Dale checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “My toddler kept moving it to save a seat for his dad. We told him the truth on the fourth visit.”

**What Dale wants.** To explain the pencil schedule to the child before the next appointment.

**Family.** [Nina Khan](../10/10-the-kitchen-without-a-boss.md#ah-r1447) is Dale’s second cousin. Dale saves a place. Nina chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The kitchen without a boss*, Nina says, “I buy breakfast on a night wage. I want these new bosses to remember what two coins used to buy.”

**People in this story.** [Cory Palmer](#ah-r0201). A saved seat and a child's game account for the mitten's first small journey. Also in the story is [Astrid Reed](#ah-r0208) (ward property worker).

**Personality.** Dale has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. Dale has a round face with a pronounced chin, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Dale folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A patient wants the chair kept empty for a while. Dale saves the chair for a short agreed interval and checks who else needs it.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Dale suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- If accused of favoritism, Dale explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Allen Marin

AH-R0203 · Age 68 · Retired knitwear worker · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I once knitted a wedding shawl while waiting for a delayed ferry.” On Wednesday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Allen prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I knitted it left-handed. I can still make its mate if the owner remembers the stripes.”

**What Allen wants.** To knit the mate with the stripes in their proper order.

**Family.** [Karen Pierce](../04/05-the-dead-man-s-tab.md#ah-r0494) is Allen’s younger relative by marriage. Allen picks the meeting place. Karen sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The dead man's tab*, Karen says, “I need his lunch hours to date a letter he sent me.”

**People in this story.** [Katherine Lowe](#ah-r0210). They reunite the pair and choose the cord between them.

**Personality.** Allen reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Allen trusts most.

**Appearance.** Allen is easy to recognize by a thin scattering of white hair over the crown and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Allen listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The waiting-room mitten is about to be thrown away. Allen asks whether the other mitten is still being looked for.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Allen checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If told to back off, Allen does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Audrey Mason

AH-R0204 · Age 39 · Orderly · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “My first patient taught me to warm a metal chair with a folded newspaper.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Audrey by a small Thursday habit. Audrey prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I used the mitten to warm the metal armrest for a man in a thin shirt. He came back asking for it.”

**What Audrey wants.** To get a little warmth to the patient who needs the cold chair.

**Family.** [Mavis Yeo](../01/07-an-unfinished-atlas.md#ah-r0061) is Audrey’s cousin. Audrey chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Mavis brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *An unfinished atlas*, Mavis says, “I priced the atlas at four dollars before I saw the paste.”

**People in this story.** [Astrid Reed](#ah-r0208). They trace how the bit of warm wool reached the coldest waiting-room armrest.

**Personality.** Audrey has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Audrey's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an old plum windbreaker and a little coil of soft measuring tape. Audrey has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Audrey takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A patient wants the chair kept empty for a while. Audrey saves the chair for a short agreed interval and checks who else needs it.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Audrey asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- If the joke lands badly, Audrey seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## George Crawford

AH-R0205 · Age 37 · Hospital property clerk · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I sort lost objects because I once waited three years for a returned ring.” People around the market by Market Flats know George by a small Saturday habit. George often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I photograph everything we find. In every frame there is a different hand reaching into the corner.”

**What George wants.** To identify who moved the mitten without posting ward photographs.

**Family.** [Sonia Garza](../06/09-a-room-for-a-father.md#ah-r0837) is George’s half-sibling. George makes the call. Sonia is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A room for a father*, Sonia says, “I gave him my old bed. I asked the family to keep one night a week when I can stay too.”

**People in this story.** [Peter Patel](#ah-r0206). The property photograph catches the patient discovering that the mitten will fit.

**Personality.** George listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** George has close-cropped dark curls, a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a watch on a cracked leather band. When somebody else has the floor, George squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- The waiting-room mitten is about to be thrown away. George asks whether the other mitten is still being looked for.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. George walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- When a second witness disagrees, George keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Peter Patel

AH-R0206 · Age 45 · Woodcarver · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “My workshop changed when I learned to hold a chisel with my other hand.” On Wednesday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Peter notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “It fit my prosthetic thumb. For five minutes I remembered the weight of holding hands with a child.”

**What Peter wants.** To find a glove that feels as comfortable over the prosthetic thumb.

**Family.** [Bruno Gao](../04/01-the-button-card.md#ah-r0452) is Peter’s cousin. Peter leaves the first voice message. Bruno calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The button card*, Bruno says, “I've been sewing pockets shut since my mother began losing coins.”

**People in this story.** [George Crawford](#ah-r0205). The property photograph catches the patient discovering that the mitten will fit.

**Personality.** Peter talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Peter's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Peter has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a little coil of soft measuring tape. When somebody else has the floor, Peter squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- A patient wants the chair kept empty for a while. Peter saves the chair for a short agreed interval and checks who else needs it.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Peter tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Peter says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Aria Colón

AH-R0207 · Age 64 · Retired postal inspector · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I delivered the hospital mail long before I needed a hospital bed.” Monday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Aria checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I was sitting in that chair on the worst day of my life. I came back to sit in it on a good day.”

**What Aria wants.** To come back to sit in the chair on a day of good news.

**Family.** [Joanna Zhou](../09/01-a-room-above-the-dryers.md#ah-r1203) is Aria’s younger cousin. Aria chooses the familiar route. Joanna can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *A room above the dryers*, Joanna says, “I sat for the first drawing. I love it and absolutely do not want strangers to see it.”

**People in this story.** [Casey Armas](#ah-r0209). They choose a time when returning to the chair will feel like an ordinary visit.

**Personality.** Aria prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a red windbreaker and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Aria has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, with soft white hair brushed sideways. Aria turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The waiting-room mitten is about to be thrown away. Aria asks whether the other mitten is still being looked for.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Aria asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Aria clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

**Family.** [Sam Lucas](../09/10-a-four-o-clock-kitchen.md#ah-r1298) is Astrid’s cousin raised in the same household. Astrid brings the paper and a pen. Sam decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *A four o'clock kitchen*, Sam says, “I designed a breakfast box the cook can bring home intact.”

**People in this story.** [Audrey Mason](#ah-r0204). They trace how the bit of warm wool reached the coldest waiting-room armrest. Also in the story is [Dale Nielsen](#ah-r0202) (ward visitor).

**Appearance.** Astrid has black hair twisted into a small knot, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a washed violet jersey. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Astrid touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- When a relative hints at needing company, Astrid may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Casey Armas

AH-R0209 · Age 58 · Theater dresser · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “My first stage job was sewing two mittens into one enormous crab claw.” People around the entrance to the Wash House know Casey by a small Thursday habit. Casey stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “My sister and I disagreed about the color. Our mother called it tomato and settled a thirty-year argument.”

**What Casey wants.** To agree on a family name for the shade of red.

**Family.** [Remy Monroe](../03/12-one-unposted-detour.md#ah-r0414) is Casey’s half-sibling. Casey calls about good news first. Remy is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *One unposted detour*, Remy says, “I stood by the turn and gave directions in three languages.”

**People in this story.** [Aria Colón](#ah-r0207). They choose a time when returning to the chair will feel like an ordinary visit.

**Personality.** Casey often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Casey have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Casey is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a cropped bottle-green coat with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Casey takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The waiting-room mitten is about to be thrown away. Casey asks whether the other mitten is still being looked for.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Casey offers to cover one concrete expense and leaves the friend room to say the expense isn't the real problem.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Casey rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Katherine Lowe

AH-R0210 · Age 31 · Lift maintenance apprentice · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I repaired a stuck hospital lift on the morning my daughter was born upstairs.” Thursday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Katherine takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I found the other mitten in the lift. I've put the pair on a cord long enough to cover two chairs.”

**What Katherine wants.** To put the two mittens on a cord long enough to share.

**Family.** [Noel Holmes](../03/04-the-spare-spectacles.md#ah-r0340) is Katherine’s older cousin. Katherine remembers the birthdays. Noel asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The spare spectacles*, Noel says, “I sat beside her because my own witness statement had cost me a friend.”

**People in this story.** [Allen Marin](#ah-r0203). They reunite the pair and choose the cord between them.

**Personality.** Katherine thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Katherine sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Katherine is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Katherine starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A patient wants the chair kept empty for a while. Katherine saves the chair for a short agreed interval and checks who else needs it.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Katherine suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Katherine asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.
