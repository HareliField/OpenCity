## Teresa Glass

AH-R0291 · Age 42 · Hospital messenger · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “My first shopping bag was a pillowcase turned lengthways.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Teresa likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I cut out a grocery bag and found the boat's number across the bottom. It brings strangers over in the market.”

**What Teresa wants.** To carry the boat number through a whole season's grocery trips.

**Family.** [Cassandra Joseph](../09/05-a-clean-coat-for-court.md#ah-r1242) is Teresa’s stepsibling. Teresa brings the paper and a pen. Cassandra decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *A clean coat for court*, Cassandra says, “I lent the coat because his daughter will see him in it.”

**People in this story.** [Ignacio Paige](#ah-r0299). A grocery bag with a sail number serves as the first pattern for the care-route bags.

**Personality.** Teresa has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Teresa's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Teresa is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Teresa squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two makers want the same piece of sail. Teresa lays both paper patterns on the piece before anybody cuts.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Teresa takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- If the joke lands badly, Teresa seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Lily Wilcox

AH-R0292 · Age 57 · Boat carpenter · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I started boat work building shelves for a ferry lunchroom.” Thursday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Lily stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I wanted the stained piece because my niece spilled her first cup of tea there in a swell.”

**What Lily wants.** To give the stained panel to the niece who made the mark.

**Family.** [Javier Aguilar](../07/06-the-empty-chair-on-nights.md#ah-r0951) is Lily’s younger cousin. Lily calls about good news first. Javier is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The empty chair on nights*, Javier says, “I want the farewell because last time I left work sick nobody even phoned.”

**People in this story.** [Mateo Alston](#ah-r0298). The tea stain and the racing patch tell two stories from the very same piece of sail.

**Personality.** Lily listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Lily is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Lily raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The marina asks to put its name on every finished piece. Lily asks the makers whether they want a brand name on what they take home.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Lily looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- When a second witness disagrees, Lily keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Daryl Mills

AH-R0293 · Age 40 · Upholsterer · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “My best customer buys nothing twice because everything can be resewn.” Sunday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Daryl prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I'm stitching a sunshade for a neighbor who will never set foot on a boat again.”

**What Daryl wants.** To install a shade where a frightened former sailor can use it.

**Family.** [Mona Brandt](../09/03-the-million-dollar-flat.md#ah-r1226) is Daryl’s cousin. Daryl remembers the birthdays. Mona asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The million-dollar flat*, Mona says, “I deliver her lunch. I love the joke about a millionaire buying soup on tick but I wish she'd let me balance the account.”

**People in this story.** [Gloria Smith](#ah-r0300). A shorebound neighbor can keep a piece of water as a shade or a passport square.

**Personality.** Daryl talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Daryl's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Daryl has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Daryl counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- Two makers want the same piece of sail. Daryl lays both paper patterns on the piece before anybody cuts.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Daryl asks where they stood and what they could hear, leaving the rest open instead of filling it with guesses.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Daryl says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Marin Ramos

AH-R0294 · Age 52 · Boat owner and architect · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I renamed a boat once and regretted it before the paint dried.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Marin by a small Tuesday habit. Marin watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I sold the boat but kept its name. I didn't know I wanted the letter H until somebody made a hat of it.”

**What Marin wants.** To take the letter from the old boat name home.

**Family.** [Elias Rios](../10/06-two-birthdays-in-one-ward.md#ah-r1408) is Marin’s younger cousin. Marin offers the first invitation. Elias gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *Two birthdays in one ward*, Elias says, “I play guitar on the ward. The old worker wanted a saucy song at a volume the child couldn't hear.”

**People in this story.** [Owen Brandt](#ah-r0296). They negotiate a useful pattern around the letter wanted for a hat.

**Personality.** Marin prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a soft blue checked shirt and one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. Marin has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a center part and straight chestnut hair. Marin draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The marina asks to put its name on every finished piece. Marin asks the makers whether they want a brand name on what they take home.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Marin keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Marin clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Ian Palmer

AH-R0295 · Age 32 · Sailing instructor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I taught on a dinghy that could just carry two children and a picnic.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Ian by a small Friday habit. Ian checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I can make six small sails for beginners. Their parents asked why every panel was a different shade.”

**What Ian wants.** To make six safe sails from the different-colored panels.

**Family.** [Benedict Lund](../06/13-the-almost-scholarship.md#ah-r0880) is Ian’s cousin raised in the same household. Ian puts the kettle on. Benedict chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The almost scholarship*, Benedict says, “I offered a lift to the first lesson. I'm asking everybody who came to the celebration where they will be in February.”

**People in this story.** [Charlotte Barker](#ah-r0297). Untwisting the seam provides the cord for six little practice sails. Also in the story is [Owen Brandt](#ah-r0296) (pattern cutter).

**Personality.** Ian never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Ian can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a pale blue work shirt and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Ian has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with auburn hair cut square at the nape. Ian starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Two makers want the same piece of sail. Ian lays both paper patterns on the piece before anybody cuts.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Ian asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Ian may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Owen Brandt

AH-R0296 · Age 39 · Pattern cutter · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I cut my first kite from the wrong side of an old tent.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Owen knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I cut a pattern from the wrong edge and ruined my plan. My son designed a kite around the missing corner.”

**What Owen wants.** To fly a kite shaped around the missing corner.

**Family.** [Adrian Trent](../03/01-spin-cycle-choir.md#ah-r0306) is Owen’s older cousin. Owen chooses an ordinary story to tell. Adrian asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Spin cycle choir*, Adrian says, “I remember a shipboard work song with the same interval.”

**People in this story.** [Marin Ramos](#ah-r0294). They negotiate a useful pattern around the letter wanted for a hat. Also in the story is [Ian Palmer](#ah-r0295) (sail borrower).

**Personality.** Owen often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Owen have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an old plum windbreaker and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Owen has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Owen holds eye contact until the other person has finished during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The marina asks to put its name on every finished piece. Owen asks the makers whether they want a brand name on what they take home.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Owen asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Owen rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Charlotte Barker

AH-R0297 · Age 73 · Retired deckhand · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I used to work knots in the dark to keep the light out of a sleeping bunk.” Monday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Charlotte knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I took the seam apart strand by strand after my hands stopped tolerating heavy rope. It is still boat work.”

**What Charlotte wants.** To salvage enough good cord to repair one more bag.

**Family.** [Ines Baldwin](../06/13-the-almost-scholarship.md#ah-r0871) is Charlotte’s younger relative by marriage. Charlotte checks the date twice. Ines brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The almost scholarship*, Ines says, “I won the tuition and haven't dared tell my little sister that going means leaving her school runs to somebody else.”

**People in this story.** [Ian Palmer](#ah-r0295). Untwisting the seam provides the cord for six little practice sails.

**Personality.** Charlotte thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Charlotte sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Charlotte has a long silver braid, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Charlotte turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- Two makers want the same piece of sail. Charlotte lays both paper patterns on the piece before anybody cuts.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Charlotte asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Charlotte asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Mateo Alston

AH-R0298 · Age 66 · Former competition sailor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I raced a full season with a rival's borrowed rudder.” On Sunday, near Juniper Street, Mateo knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I know where my old racing rival patched the sail. I want the patch to hang beside my silver medal.”

**What Mateo wants.** To display the rival's repair beside the silver medal.

**Family.** [Daryl Trent](../03/03-the-widow-s-dividend.md#ah-r0325) is Mateo’s cousin. Mateo brings a spare shopping bag. Daryl chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The widow's dividend*, Daryl says, “My pension owns a sliver of this firm. I want the tenants to know we're tied together.”

**People in this story.** [Lily Wilcox](#ah-r0292). The tea stain and the racing patch tell two stories from the very same piece of sail.

**Personality.** Mateo gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Mateo privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a pale blue work shirt and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Mateo has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a neat white crop. Mateo gives a little nod before asking a hard question during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The marina asks to put its name on every finished piece. Mateo asks the makers whether they want a brand name on what they take home.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Mateo clears the dishes, lets the angriest speaker cool off, and resumes the conversation with a smaller audience.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Mateo offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Ignacio Paige

AH-R0299 · Age 50 · Meal delivery worker · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I mapped the meal route using the smell of the kitchens.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Ignacio can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I promised a bag to each carer on the western route. I'm asking the sailing club to lend us its sewing machines.”

**What Ignacio wants.** To make and deliver the carers' bags before the wet season.

**Family.** [Tali Martin](../06/05-the-first-straight-paycheck.md#ah-r0798) is Ignacio’s cousin. Ignacio asks before bringing somebody along. Tali can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The first straight paycheck*, Tali says, “I lend money too easily. I agreed to help with bus fare and refused to take over another month's rent.”

**People in this story.** [Teresa Glass](#ah-r0291). A grocery bag with a sail number serves as the first pattern for the care-route bags.

**Personality.** Ignacio uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Ignacio well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Ignacio has black hair twisted into a small knot, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Ignacio counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- Two makers want the same piece of sail. Ignacio lays both paper patterns on the piece before anybody cuts.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Ignacio stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- After a family quarrel, Ignacio brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Gloria Smith

AH-R0300 · Age 75 · Retired cleaner · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “My first passport photograph shows the earrings I sold to pay for the passport.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Gloria usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I kept a postage-stamp square. It fits inside the passport I could never afford when that boat was new.”

**What Gloria wants.** To keep the little sail square in a passport finally put to use.

**Family.** [Cecilia Woods](../05/06-two-left-slippers.md#ah-r0651) is Gloria’s first cousin once removed. Gloria makes room at the table. Cecilia brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Two left slippers*, Cecilia says, “I bought my dad those slippers after he fell.”

**People in this story.** [Daryl Mills](#ah-r0293). A shorebound neighbor can keep a piece of water as a shade or a passport square.

**Personality.** Gloria feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Gloria has silver temples and a dark fringe, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Gloria rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The marina asks to put its name on every finished piece. Gloria asks the makers whether they want a brand name on what they take home.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Gloria asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Gloria lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.
