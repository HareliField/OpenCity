## Norman Armas

AH-R0251 · Age 44 · Recruiter · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “My first office had a mirror where candidates could see the interviewers arriving.” On Wednesday, near Harbour Bakery, Norman buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I interview people at a desk. I never realized I only see their top half until I saw the chalk lines.”

**What Norman wants.** To change how a job applicant's clothing is assessed across a desk.

**Family.** [Agnes Lin](../07/13-a-dance-for-two-fathers.md#ah-r1021) is Norman’s first cousin once removed. Norman puts the kettle on. Agnes chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *A dance for two fathers*, Agnes says, “I am the graduate. I choreographed a turn that leaves each man momentarily dancing with the other.”

**People in this story.** [Quinn Pereira](#ah-r0254). The interviewer and applicant eventually discuss the deliberate switch of handshake.

**Personality.** Norman often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Norman have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Norman is easy to recognize by a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a narrow brass watch set a little fast, Norman looks toward the door whenever a name is called when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Somebody offers to buy a complete suit for the applicant. Norman asks the applicant whether a whole donated suit would actually be welcome.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Norman repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Norman rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Amira Lam

AH-R0252 · Age 41 · Accounts assistant · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I bought my first suit with a refund from my canceled wedding.” Saturday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Amira asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I gave him my old cuff button. It got me through the interview that paid for my divorce.”

**What Amira wants.** To get the cuff button back if the applicant decides to change jobs.

**Family.** [Naomi Jones](../08/06-two-names-on-the-chart.md#ah-r1108) is Amira’s cousin raised in the same household. Amira chooses an ordinary story to tell. Naomi asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Two names on the chart*, Naomi says, “I am her grown daughter. Reading the childhood name made me realize she was someone before she became Mum.”

**People in this story.** [Grey Foster](#ah-r0255). The donated button makes sense to the tailor who left cloth for growth.

**Personality.** Amira thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Amira sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Amira has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with a blunt cut just below the ears. Amira listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The interview is advanced before the jacket is ready. Amira checks which part of the jacket can be finished in the time available.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Amira asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Amira asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Emilio Walker

AH-R0253 · Age 60 · Wardrobe volunteer · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I set out the wardrobe cupboard so a job seeker can browse without asking.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Emilio by a small Saturday habit. Emilio likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I offered a loan jacket and he refused. I had forgotten what it feels like to want one thing that's yours.”

**What Emilio wants.** To give the customer a choice between alterations and a loan.

**Family.** [Jean King](../08/01-reopening-at-six.md#ah-r1058) is Emilio’s cousin. Emilio checks the date twice. Jean brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Reopening at six*, Jean says, “I own the doors and the landlord owns the timer.”

**People in this story.** [Jack Bellamy](#ah-r0258). They both learn why the wearer wanted to keep one personal, unfinished sleeve.

**Personality.** Emilio gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Emilio privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Emilio is easy to recognize by short white hair and darker eyebrows and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Emilio squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Somebody offers to buy a complete suit for the applicant. Emilio asks the applicant whether a whole donated suit would actually be welcome.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Emilio stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Emilio offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Grey Foster

AH-R0255 · Age 66 · Retired tailor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I used to leave a spare fold in every apprentice's coat.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Lighthouse Cove, where Grey takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I shortened the sleeve too far on purpose for a child to grow into it. I can reverse my own work.”

**What Grey wants.** To reverse the old allowance before replacing good cloth.

**Family.** [Cara Carvalho](../07/07-the-first-language.md#ah-r0961) is Grey’s first cousin once removed. Grey asks before bringing somebody along. Cara can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The first language*, Cara says, “I can read every word but my classmates think my voice changes when I do.”

**People in this story.** [Amira Lam](#ah-r0252). The donated button makes sense to the tailor who left cloth for growth.

**Personality.** Grey feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Grey has gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears, a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Grey gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- Somebody offers to buy a complete suit for the applicant. Grey asks the applicant whether a whole donated suit would actually be welcome.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Grey moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Grey lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

**In their own words.** “I paid for a matching cuff lining on condition the customer never learns my name. He guessed from the fabric.”

**What Gale wants.** To fit the donated lining without attaching a personal debt.

**Family.** [Kit Curtis](../10/12-three-minutes-at-the-bridge.md#ah-r1464) is Gale’s stepsibling. Gale makes room at the table. Kit brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Three minutes at the bridge*, Kit says, “I have been paying taxi fares with credit. I showed the crew my actual monthly statement before the vote.”

**Appearance.** Gale has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Look for a high puff tied with plain cord. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a narrow-striped sweater with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Gale looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- The interview is advanced before the jacket is ready. Gale checks which part of the jacket can be finished in the time available.
