# The hospital radio

## Hector Lang

AH-R0351 · Age 43 · Bedside support worker · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the click between the fishing and football stations.” On Thursday, near Tideline Market, Hector picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I used to tune the coast forecast for my father. I want that little wheel on his bedside drawer.”

**What Hector wants.** To fit a tuning wheel in the father's drawer.

**Family.** [Scout Mendez](../04/15-the-engine-lullaby.md#ah-r0596) is Hector’s stepsibling. Hector saves a place. Scout chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The engine lullaby*, Scout says, “My child thinks there's a whale in the harbor.”

**People in this story.** [Maria Ayala](#ah-r0356). They discuss which old parts can be kept by the bedside.

**Personality.** Hector carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Hector has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a gray fisher's sweater with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. In conversation Hector looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- The replacement radio drowns out a patient's match. Hector asks the two patients which part of the match each wants to hear.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Hector keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Hector offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Adela Griffin

AH-R0352 · Age 65 · Hospital volunteer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an evening announcer who always sounded forgiving.” On Saturday, near Maré Coffee, Adela likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I never learned the stations' names. I memorized how far the dial turned from the crack.”

**What Adela wants.** To mark the favorite station position for the next listener.

**Family.** [Gregory Lang](../10/11-an-ordinary-mirror.md#ah-r1452) is Adela’s cousin. Adela picks the meeting place. Gregory sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *An ordinary mirror*, Gregory says, “I own the mirror and have seen a hundred other lives in it.”

**People in this story.** [Lisa Anders](#ah-r0358). The wheel that needn't work can keep the position remembered by touch.

**Personality.** Adela has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Out of work clothes, look for a red windbreaker and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Adela has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, with steel-colored hair cut square above the collar. Adela grins on one side while the eyes stay serious during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The old tuning wheel goes missing from the shelf. Adela checks the repair bench before assuming anyone took the dial.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Adela offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Adela says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Stevie Heath

AH-R0353 · Age 56 · Radio repairer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a long aerial made from our first clothesline.” On Thursday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Stevie prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I fixed the radio with a shoelace during a strike. The lace was a present from my daughter.”

**What Stevie wants.** To save the old shoelace during the repair.

**Family.** [June Sawyer](../10/01-a-closing-hour.md#ah-r1352) is Stevie’s younger cousin. At home an argument could last exactly as long as it took the kettle to boil. Whoever put it back on was admitting there was more to say. Stevie is usually the one who refills it. Asked about *A closing hour*, June says, “I am his regular dawn customer. His first day off was the first time I remembered to ask his name.”

**People in this story.** [Oakley Ibrahim](#ah-r0360). A shoelace repair makes the retired keeper rethink sending the radio to a collector. Also in the story is [Lisa Anders](#ah-r0358) (cabinet maker).

**Personality.** Stevie treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Stevie lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Stevie has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a watch on a cracked leather band. In conversation Stevie gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- The replacement radio drowns out a patient's match. Stevie asks the two patients which part of the match each wants to hear.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Stevie stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Stevie says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Linden Lewis

AH-R0354 · Age 48 · Football commentator · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember hearing an entire crowd through one small speaker.” On Tuesday, near Ferris Deli, Linden asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I announce local football and heard my own voice from a hospital bed. I'd like the next patient to choose silence.”

**What Linden wants.** To let both patients choose when the commentary plays.

**Family.** [Blake Liu](../10/07-an-empty-dedication.md#ah-r1419) is Linden’s cousin raised in the same household. Linden makes the call. Blake is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *An empty dedication*, Blake says, “I used to be her wife. I know there's a reason the first page is blank and I'm not asking for my name.”

**People in this story.** [Cory Walsh](#ah-r0359). They arrange a shared score without making the entire ward hear the same game.

**Personality.** Linden is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Linden is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a little coil of soft measuring tape. Linden has a wide brow and a narrow jaw, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Linden draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The old tuning wheel goes missing from the shelf. Linden checks the repair bench before assuming anyone took the dial.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Linden asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Linden agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Dale Zhou

AH-R0355 · Age 39 · Wedding musician · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember finding my own request on the night playlist.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Dale picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I found a folded list of songs behind the battery cover. One is the song at my first wedding.”

**What Dale wants.** To rebuild the list of songs from the battery cover.

**Family.** [Taylor Vargas](../06/02-nine-unpaid-hours.md#ah-r0763) is Dale’s cousin. Dale leaves the first voice message. Taylor calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Nine unpaid hours*, Taylor says, “I worked through a break nobody signed for.”

**People in this story.** [Charlotte Hsu](#ah-r0357). They photograph the folded requests and the pink tuning mark before removing the set.

**Personality.** Dale tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Dale will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Dale has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a washed violet jersey with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Dale raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- The replacement radio drowns out a patient's match. Dale asks the two patients which part of the match each wants to hear.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Dale puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Dale repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Maria Ayala

AH-R0356 · Age 74 · Retired ward electrician · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an apprentice knitting a cover for the radio.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Maria by a small Wednesday habit. Maria looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I hid a note to my successor inside the back. Thirty years later I'm well enough to answer it.”

**What Maria wants.** To answer the note addressed to a future repairer.

**Family.** [Merritt Parker](../04/14-laundry-for-a-stranger.md#ah-r0590) is Maria’s younger cousin. Maria chooses the familiar route. Merritt can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Laundry for a stranger*, Merritt says, “I noticed the sleeves differ in length. I want my arthritic husband to meet whoever figured out that cut.”

**People in this story.** [Hector Lang](#ah-r0351). They discuss which old parts can be kept by the bedside.

**Personality.** Maria is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Maria asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Maria is easy to recognize by soft white hair brushed sideways and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread, Maria raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The old tuning wheel goes missing from the shelf. Maria checks the repair bench before assuming anyone took the dial.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Maria tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Maria accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Charlotte Hsu

AH-R0357 · Age 32 · Shop window artist · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a hospital talent show in the wrong key.” Friday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Charlotte likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I was the child who painted the tuning mark pink. My mum recognizes the set by that stain.”

**What Charlotte wants.** To preserve the pink tuning mark.

**Family.** [Luis Gross](../08/14-the-new-language-at-dinner.md#ah-r1181) is Charlotte’s first cousin once removed. Charlotte brings the paper and a pen. Luis decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The new language at dinner*, Luis says, “I know what I mean. At home the words come out in the language I used before primary school.”

**People in this story.** [Dale Zhou](#ah-r0355). They photograph the folded requests and the pink tuning mark before removing the set.

**Personality.** Charlotte is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Charlotte is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Charlotte draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The replacement radio drowns out a patient's match. Charlotte asks the two patients which part of the match each wants to hear.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Charlotte asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Charlotte thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Lisa Anders

AH-R0358 · Age 57 · Cabinet maker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sanding the stand for an aunt's transistor.” People around Harbour Bakery know Lisa by a small Monday habit. Lisa buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I can rebuild the cabinet as a bedside shelf. The patient asked me to include the knob that does nothing.”

**What Lisa wants.** To build a usable shelf around the redundant knob.

**Family.** [Dale Crawford](../07/13-a-dance-for-two-fathers.md#ah-r1027) is Lisa’s cousin raised in the same household. Lisa calls about good news first. Dale is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *A dance for two fathers*, Dale says, “I lend formal shoes. I exchanged two right shoes so the men had to meet before the ceremony.”

**People in this story.** [Adela Griffin](#ah-r0352). The wheel that needn't work can keep the position remembered by touch. Also in the story is [Stevie Heath](#ah-r0353) (radio restorer).

**Personality.** Lisa loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Lisa remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Lisa has short copper curls, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Lisa starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- The old tuning wheel goes missing from the shelf. Lisa checks the repair bench before assuming anyone took the dial.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Lisa stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- When invited to tell the story later, Lisa includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

**Appearance.** Cory has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a gray fisher's sweater with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. In conversation Cory leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

## Oakley Ibrahim

AH-R0360 · Age 68 · Retired ward attendant · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a cabinet brought into care as somebody's one luxury.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Oakley by a small Sunday habit. Oakley likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I promised the old one to a collector. I told him the people who touched it get first choice.”

**What Oakley wants.** To ask regular ward listeners before selling any radio part.

**Family.** [Alex Carbone](../04/15-the-engine-lullaby.md#ah-r0600) is Oakley’s younger relative by marriage. Oakley offers the first invitation. Alex gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The engine lullaby*, Alex says, “I counted the pulses and set a cradle-rocking tune to them.”

**People in this story.** [Stevie Heath](#ah-r0353). A shoelace repair makes the retired keeper rethink sending the radio to a collector.

**Personality.** Oakley finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Oakley is easy to recognize by a gray streak running back from the forehead and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with one cuff folded back twice, Oakley presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The old tuning wheel goes missing from the shelf. Oakley checks the repair bench before assuming anyone took the dial.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Oakley walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- When thanked for a shared success, Oakley points to the person who noticed the original mistake.
