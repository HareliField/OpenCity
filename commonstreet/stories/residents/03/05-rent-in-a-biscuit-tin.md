# Rent in a biscuit tin

## Grey Ibrahim

AH-R0341 · Age 38 · Former street dealer and cleaner · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember earning a landlord's trust one damp banknote at a time.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the downtown fountain, where Grey keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I used her kitchen as my savings bank when I sold pills. It's the only clean rent record I have.”

**What Grey wants.** To get a dated rent receipt the old neighbors recognize.

**Family.** [Lou Khan](../02/10-the-bitter-orange.md#ah-r0243) is Grey’s cousin raised in the same household. Grey chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Lou brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The bitter orange*, Lou says, “She ordered two slices after her husband died.”

**People in this story.** [Reina Gallagher](#ah-r0344). They get the former dealer a reliable record of which rent was actually collected.

**Personality.** Grey tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Grey will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Grey has thick, tightly coiled hair, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Grey turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- One renter wants the tin counted in public. Grey asks each renter how much of the count they want heard in public.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Grey keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Grey repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Tariq Banks

AH-R0342 · Age 24 · Taxi trainee · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember three attempts at the same blind corner.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Tariq likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “My envelope held money for driving lessons. I was too embarrassed to say I failed again.”

**What Tariq wants.** To save for another honest attempt at the driving test.

**Family.** [Daniel Morris](../04/02-the-soft-wharf-chalk.md#ah-r0466) is Tariq’s older relative by marriage. Tariq keeps the photographs. Daniel knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The soft wharf chalk*, Daniel says, “I was supposed to wash the underside. My wrists gave out halfway through and spared the words.”

**People in this story.** [Gareth Yeo](#ah-r0345). The envelope for driving lessons goes into the count only with its owner's permission.

**Personality.** Tariq is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Tariq asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Tariq is easy to recognize by a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip, Tariq counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks to come back later without explanation. Tariq arranges a second visit without repeating the family's explanation.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Tariq suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Tariq accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Graham Jackson

AH-R0343 · Age 69 · Widowed delivery driver · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the stationery my wife ordered just to write rent checks.” On Wednesday, near Breakwater Way, Graham stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “The handwriting on my envelope is my late wife's. I asked for the paper back as well as the cash.”

**What Graham wants.** To recover the paper bearing the late wife's handwriting.

**Family.** [Pat Garrett](../08/07-a-prize-nobody-entered.md#ah-r1120) is Graham’s cousin. Graham makes the call. Pat is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A prize nobody entered*, Pat says, “I kept a diary nobody read for sixty years.”

**People in this story.** [Ramona Ahmed](#ah-r0346). They date the old tin without discarding the wife's envelope.

**Personality.** Graham is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Graham has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for a long silver braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Graham looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- One renter wants the tin counted in public. Graham asks each renter how much of the count they want heard in public.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Graham stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Graham thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Reina Gallagher

AH-R0344 · Age 47 · Rent collector · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember collecting cash in the year nobody had change.” Friday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Reina checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I collected rent in the rain and swapped the tins. I traced every tenant before closing my books.”

**What Reina wants.** To match every envelope before month-end.

**Family.** [Clement Mitchell](../04/14-laundry-for-a-stranger.md#ah-r0588) is Reina’s cousin. Reina leaves the first voice message. Clement calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Laundry for a stranger*, Clement says, “I can't stand a starched collar since catering school.”

**People in this story.** [Grey Ibrahim](#ah-r0341). They get the former dealer a reliable record of which rent was actually collected. Also in the story is [Gareth Yeo](#ah-r0345) (budget tutor).

**Personality.** Reina loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Reina remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. Reina has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with short copper curls. Reina listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks to come back later without explanation. Reina arranges a second visit without repeating the family's explanation.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Reina asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- When invited to tell the story later, Reina includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Gareth Yeo

AH-R0345 · Age 35 · Household budget tutor · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember borrowing a pot and returning it full of rice.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Gareth by a small Tuesday habit. Gareth likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I wanted the money counted in public. Then I learned somebody was saving to leave home quietly.”

**What Gareth wants.** To give the person saving to leave a private chance to collect.

**Family.** [Marin Lim](../09/06-a-doctor-moves-away.md#ah-r1256) is Gareth’s first cousin once removed. Gareth chooses the familiar route. Marin can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *A doctor moves away*, Marin says, “I never liked her manner and liked her thoroughness.”

**People in this story.** [Tariq Banks](#ah-r0342). The envelope for driving lessons goes into the count only with its owner's permission. Also in the story is [Reina Gallagher](#ah-r0344) (rent collector).

**Personality.** Gareth has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Gareth has a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Look for a high puff tied with plain cord. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a boxy denim jacket with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Gareth looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- One renter wants the tin counted in public. Gareth asks each renter how much of the count they want heard in public.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Gareth calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- When somebody trades on a title, Gareth asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Ramona Ahmed

AH-R0346 · Age 59 · Biscuit factory packer · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the factory tour where we ate all the samples.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Ramona by a small Sunday habit. Ramona pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “My bakery made the biscuits. I can date the tin from the misspelled ingredients.”

**What Ramona wants.** To date the biscuit tin from the factory stamp.

**Family.** [Morgan Gil](../07/07-the-first-language.md#ah-r0966) is Ramona’s cousin. Ramona brings the paper and a pen. Morgan decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The first language*, Morgan says, “I come after work in floury trousers because there are no books in our language at the staff flat.”

**People in this story.** [Graham Jackson](#ah-r0343). They date the old tin without discarding the wife's envelope.

**Personality.** Ramona finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Ramona has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for silvery hair gathered at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. In conversation Ramona gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks to come back later without explanation. Ramona arranges a second visit without repeating the family's explanation.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Ramona brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- When thanked for a shared success, Ramona points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Scout Nord

AH-R0347 · Age 36 · Unemployed shelf stocker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my child's delight in stiff new shoelaces.” On Tuesday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Scout prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I thought the extra rent was a gift. I had already bought school shoes when the owner knocked.”

**What Scout wants.** To explain the school shoe purchase and settle the difference.

**Family.** [John Hong](../02/02-five-missing-lunches.md#ah-r0165) is Scout’s older cousin. Scout is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. John calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Five missing lunches*, John says, “I wanted the red tin for our museum display.”

**People in this story.** [Ren Alston](#ah-r0348). The two renters compare finding money that somebody else had quietly put aside.

**Personality.** Scout keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Scout wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Scout has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. In conversation Scout presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- One renter wants the tin counted in public. Scout asks each renter how much of the count they want heard in public.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Scout starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Scout offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Ren Alston

AH-R0348 · Age 29 · Deli cashier · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember renting a mattress from a cousin for a single winter.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Ren by a small Wednesday habit. Ren pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I owed her one month and found she'd put in a little every week herself.”

**What Ren wants.** To thank the elder who quietly covered the shortfall.

**Family.** [Reuben Grant](../04/10-closing-soup.md#ah-r0543) is Ren’s stepsibling. Ren remembers the birthdays. Reuben asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *Closing soup*, Reuben says, “I donated the fennel nobody bought. In the soup people asked me to grow extra.”

**People in this story.** [Scout Nord](#ah-r0347). The two renters compare finding money that somebody else had quietly put aside.

**Personality.** Ren always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Ren is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Ren has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. When somebody else has the floor, Ren holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks to come back later without explanation. Ren arranges a second visit without repeating the family's explanation.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Ren takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Ren suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Chris Anders

AH-R0349 · Age 44 · Car body apprentice · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember counting bus coins with oily thumbs.” Thursday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Chris knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I was sure my envelope had been opened. The crease was mine from the night I nearly spent it.”

**What Chris wants.** To clear the accusation over the opened envelope.

**Family.** [Bianca Cole](../02/02-five-missing-lunches.md#ah-r0168) is Chris’s cousin. Chris chooses the music. Bianca puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *Five missing lunches*, Bianca says, “I was asked to throw them away. I put a forwarding card in each one instead.”

**People in this story.** [Lola Lowe](#ah-r0350). The deli-table count gives the worried owner a chance to recognize a familiar crease.

**Personality.** Chris has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Chris knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an old plum windbreaker and a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. Chris has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with a blunt cut just below the ears. Chris takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- One renter wants the tin counted in public. Chris asks each renter how much of the count they want heard in public.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Chris accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Chris asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.
