# The tide clock

[Small things that travel](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**South Shore Harbor.** A marina wall clock shows the tide instead of the hour. A new employee misses an appointment after trusting its hands.

## Lorenzo Mack

AH-R0441 · Age 52 · Dock timekeeper · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember guessing the tide by the height of a painted boot.” On Sunday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Lorenzo watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I set that clock by a rock my father could see from the gate. The rock moved in last winter's storm.”

**What Lorenzo wants.** To reset the clock from a fixed harbor reference.

**Family.** [Alvin Jeong](../10/13-one-last-dress-rehearsal.md#ah-r1471) is Lorenzo’s younger relative by marriage. Lorenzo calls about good news first. Alvin is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *One last dress rehearsal*, Alvin says, “I used to think my mother was choosing work over me.”

**People in this story.** [Victor Anders](#ah-r0443) (new marina worker). The tide setter shows the worker exactly what time the wall never promised.

**Personality.** Lorenzo listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Lorenzo has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with a loose dark braid. Lorenzo touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A supervisor blames the new hire for missing the time. Lorenzo checks which dial the worker saw before accepting a lateness entry.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Lorenzo asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- When a second witness disagrees, Lorenzo keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Jesse Starr

AH-R0442 · Age 44 · Marina shift supervisor · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a half shift recorded as a full day's absence.” Friday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Jesse likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I gave the employee a warning before I checked the dial. I want the correction in her file.”

**What Jesse wants.** To correct the warning in the employee's file.

**Family.** [Vera Price](../04/03-the-savings-club.md#ah-r0480) is Jesse’s half-sibling. Jesse remembers the birthdays. Vera asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The savings club*, Vera says, “I'm the bookkeeper. I found the money for a second ticket in a refund none of us claimed.”

**People in this story.** [Harley Hooper](#ah-r0446). They arrange for a real time clock beside the one that misled the new hire.

**Personality.** Jesse talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Jesse's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Jesse has a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, a gray fisher's sweater. It usually goes with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. When somebody else has the floor, Jesse takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The owner considers taking the tide clock down. Jesse asks the workers whether a second, clearly labeled clock would solve the actual problem.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Jesse asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Jesse says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

**Family.** [Elsa Guzman](../04/06-the-waiting-room-plant.md#ah-r0505) is Victor’s older cousin. Victor offers the first invitation. Elsa gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The waiting-room plant*, Elsa says, “I never have visitors. I booked the plant for Thursday and three gardeners turned up.”

**People in this story.** [Lorenzo Mack](#ah-r0441) (clock setter). The tide setter shows the worker exactly what time the wall never promised.

**Personality.** Victor prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. Victor has one cheek that creases before the other, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Victor squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A supervisor blames the new hire for missing the time. Victor checks which dial the worker saw before accepting a lateness entry.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Victor finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Victor clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

Asked about *The worker who couldn't read the joke*, Olivia says, “I translated it too gently the first time. My friend deserves to know which actual word upset everybody.”

**Appearance.** Harold has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for soft white hair brushed sideways. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Harold holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- If a colleague lies to save face, Harold offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.
