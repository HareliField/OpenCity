## Nadia Hayes

AH-R0391 · Age 53 · Tomato vendor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my mother totaling a stall's takings in song.” On Monday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Nadia prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I learned sums selling tomatoes. I ran the paper till and earned my first paid shift.”

**What Nadia wants.** To earn a first paid shift on the counter.

**Family.** [Jo Johnson](../05/12-level-with-the-window.md#ah-r0719) is Nadia’s cousin raised in the same household. Nadia checks the date twice. Jo brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Level with the window*, Jo says, “I delivered a complaint and a cake to the same desk.”

**People in this story.** [Nadia Holland](#ah-r0396). Drawings and mental arithmetic keep the lunch queue moving.

**Personality.** Nadia has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Nadia knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an olive canvas jacket and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Nadia has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Nadia steadies a cup with both hands while listening during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A customer cannot pay until the till opens. Nadia asks the cashier what can safely go on a temporary tab.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Nadia accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Nadia asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Marlowe Paige

AH-R0392 · Age 28 · Office messenger · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember needing the price of a stamp before payroll.” People around the bank steps know Marlowe by a small Saturday habit. Marlowe takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I put down too much money to avoid looking broke. The cashier brought my change to work.”

**What Marlowe wants.** To settle the deliberate overpayment in private.

**Family.** [Lennon Sanders](../07/15-the-day-sailor-s-divorce.md#ah-r1045) is Marlowe’s half-sibling. Marlowe brings a spare shopping bag. Lennon chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The day sailor's divorce*, Lennon says, “I teach their son. His favorite part of Sunday is actually the walk to breakfast afterward.”

**People in this story.** [Billie Wilson](#ah-r0394). A few coins needed for a bus change hands without becoming charity.

**Personality.** Marlowe enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Marlowe is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread, Marlowe touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A manager asks for the handwritten sandwich tally. Marlowe makes sure customers' names needn't travel with the tally.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Marlowe starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Marlowe puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Cian Malik

AH-R0393 · Age 55 · Bakery accounts clerk · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember shorthand drawn in the flour on a worktop.” Friday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Cian notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I recognized my first boss's pencil shorthand. I can read the tab nobody else understands.”

**What Cian wants.** To read the shorthand on the oldest tab.

**Family.** [Malcolm Tan](../05/13-the-fountain-score.md#ah-r0727) is Cian’s second cousin. Cian asks before bringing somebody along. Malcolm can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The fountain score*, Malcolm says, “I brought an empty milk tin from the shop my parents lost.”

**People in this story.** [Duncan Griffin](#ah-r0395). An old shorthand entry and a foreign coin give the fault a history.

**Personality.** Cian has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Cian can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Cian has black hair twisted into a small knot, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Cian squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- A customer cannot pay until the till opens. Cian asks the cashier what can safely go on a temporary tab.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Cian stays where the child can see the shop door and asks the neighboring counter to help find the adult expected there.
- If a friend borrows money, Cian agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Billie Wilson

AH-R0394 · Age 34 · Bus mechanic · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the bus driver who accepted my last foreign coin.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Billie by a small Tuesday habit. Billie starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I came for change for the bus. I stayed because somebody owed exactly the fare I needed.”

**What Billie wants.** To recover bus fare without interrupting the lunch rush.

**Family.** [Rowan Peters](../06/11-a-chair-of-one-s-own.md#ah-r0860) is Billie’s stepsibling. Billie makes room at the table. Rowan brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *A chair of one's own*, Rowan says, “I offered two days a week instead of six. I have a sick partner and I know exactly what three empty days cost.”

**People in this story.** [Marlowe Paige](#ah-r0392). A few coins needed for a bus change hands without becoming charity.

**Personality.** Billie takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Billie can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Billie is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Billie draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A manager asks for the handwritten sandwich tally. Billie makes sure customers' names needn't travel with the tally.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Billie asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Billie turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Duncan Griffin

AH-R0395 · Age 47 · Till repairer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a jammed drawer my mentor opened with patience.” On Saturday, near Ferris Deli, Duncan asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I offered to repair the till free. The spring had been bent around a coin from my old country.”

**What Duncan wants.** To repair the till and name the coin that bent its spring.

**Family.** [Silas Rubio](../07/05-the-release-address.md#ah-r0948) is Duncan’s cousin. Duncan saves a place. Silas chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The release address*, Silas says, “I own the shop he robbed. My own kids know him only as the man who sends birthday cards.”

**People in this story.** [Cian Malik](#ah-r0393). An old shorthand entry and a foreign coin give the fault a history.

**Personality.** Duncan admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Duncan learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Duncan has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for a high puff tied with plain cord. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Duncan looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- A customer cannot pay until the till opens. Duncan asks the cashier what can safely go on a temporary tab.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Duncan separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Duncan describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Nadia Holland

AH-R0396 · Age 23 · Menu illustrator · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sketching my first lunch before eating it.” On Wednesday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Nadia pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I wrote everyone's sandwiches in pictures. The cook hung the sheet beside the menu.”

**What Nadia wants.** To give the kitchen the illustrated tally.

**Family.** [Warren Lyle](../01/01-the-blue-suit.md#ah-r0006) is Nadia’s older cousin. Nadia asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Warren remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The blue suit*, Warren says, “I used to work on a hotel laundry line. I found three identical claims under three different machine numbers.”

**People in this story.** [Nadia Hayes](#ah-r0391). Drawings and mental arithmetic keep the lunch queue moving. Also in the story is [Noel Berry](#ah-r0398) (lunch collector).

**Personality.** Nadia has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Nadia has a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. In conversation Nadia folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A manager asks for the handwritten sandwich tally. Nadia makes sure customers' names needn't travel with the tally.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Nadia calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- If accused of favoritism, Nadia explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Nicholas Mitchell

AH-R0397 · Age 68 · Pensioned caregiver · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a neighbor guaranteeing a single loaf for me.” People around the downtown fountain know Nicholas by a small Friday habit. Nicholas keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “My prepaid meal card wouldn't work. A stranger trusted me to pay it forward at breakfast.”

**What Nicholas wants.** To repay the breakfast favor at the next meal.

**Family.** [Emilio Starr](../07/14-the-carer-s-afternoon.md#ah-r1039) is Nicholas’s cousin raised in the same household. When a relative became too weak to cook, the family started chopping at the kitchen table with the cook directing from a chair. Revising the seasoning remained a point of honor. Nicholas knows how to let somebody else finish a job slowly. Asked about *The carer's afternoon*, Emilio says, “I owned a care agency and thought four hours was simple.”

**People in this story.** [Noel Berry](#ah-r0398). Two separate meal debts get recorded correctly before the till reopens.

**Personality.** Nicholas reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Nicholas trusts most.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a narrow brass watch set a little fast. Nicholas has a wide brow and a narrow jaw, with a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. Nicholas turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A customer cannot pay until the till opens. Nicholas asks the cashier what can safely go on a temporary tab.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Nicholas keeps the neighbor's place and finds out whether the stranger needs a different kind of help.
- If told to back off, Nicholas does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Noel Berry

AH-R0398 · Age 40 · Hospital food runner · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember delivering a meal still labeled for yesterday's ward.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Noel by a small Saturday habit. Noel looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I took a lunch for a patient and forgot to sign. I've come back to put the right name beside the debt.”

**What Noel wants.** To add the correct patient name to the account.

**Family.** [Leah Smith](../07/11-the-salon-on-strike.md#ah-r1005) is Noel’s cousin raised in the same household. Noel makes the call. Leah is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The salon on strike*, Leah says, “I thought a free manicure made it fair. The technician asked who would watch her sleeping children.”

**People in this story.** [Nicholas Mitchell](#ah-r0397). Two separate meal debts get recorded correctly before the till reopens. Also in the story is [Nadia Holland](#ah-r0396) (counter note taker).

**Personality.** Noel has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Noel's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Noel is easy to recognize by a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a watch on a cracked leather band, Noel takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A manager asks for the handwritten sandwich tally. Noel makes sure customers' names needn't travel with the tally.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Noel alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- If the joke lands badly, Noel seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Marlowe May

AH-R0399 · Age 48 · Shop manager · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first audit being off by one sweet.” People around Harbour Bakery know Marlowe by a small Thursday habit. Marlowe buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I manage a cashless shop. I want to know why the pencil line moved faster than ours.”

**What Marlowe wants.** To time the paper method on a busy day.

**Family.** [Manuel Peters](../07/12-the-crash-anniversary.md#ah-r1013) is Marlowe’s half-sibling. Marlowe leaves the first voice message. Manuel calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The crash anniversary*, Manuel says, “I was driving the other vehicle. My letter to the family has stayed in the same drawer for eleven months.”

**People in this story.** [Sasha Crane](#ah-r0400). A manager timing the line hears from a customer who liked how long the conversation took.

**Personality.** Marlowe listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. Marlowe has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. Marlowe starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A customer cannot pay until the till opens. Marlowe asks the cashier what can safely go on a temporary tab.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Marlowe asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- When a second witness disagrees, Marlowe keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Sasha Crane

AH-R0400 · Age 51 · Divorced taxi dispatcher · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember buying our divorce dinner from two counters.” On Tuesday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Sasha prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I wasn't hungry. I joined the queue because it felt like the first real conversation since my divorce.”

**What Sasha wants.** To find another reason to join the deli regulars.

**Family.** [Ruben Keller](../02/08-low-water-music.md#ah-r0225) is Sasha’s half-sibling. Sasha sets the time to leave. Ruben picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Low water music*, Ruben says, “Our boat club wants an official name. I brought nine unofficial ones on a damp receipt.”

**People in this story.** [Marlowe May](#ah-r0399). A manager timing the line hears from a customer who liked how long the conversation took.

**Personality.** Sasha talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Sasha's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. Sasha has a round face with a pronounced chin, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Sasha looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A manager asks for the handwritten sandwich tally. Sasha makes sure customers' names needn't travel with the tally.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Sasha asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Sasha says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.
