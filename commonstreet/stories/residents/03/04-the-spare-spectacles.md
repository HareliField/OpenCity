## Hollis Parker

AH-R0331 · Age 50 · Retired union recorder · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a council hearing where I mistook a four for a nine.” People around the entrance to the Wash House know Hollis by a small Tuesday habit. Hollis stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I signed something blurry once. I brought my own glasses and stayed until she read every word.”

**What Hollis wants.** To let the witness read every word at her own pace.

**Family.** [Yusuf Solis](../10/13-one-last-dress-rehearsal.md#ah-r1474) is Hollis’s younger cousin. The household has two versions of an old recipe because somebody detested onions and never admitted it to the original cook. A person sitting down to eat gets to choose. Hollis will defend the changed version in front of purists. Asked about *One last dress rehearsal*, Yusuf says, “I play the villain. My dad has never seen me do anything at school except get in trouble.”

**People in this story.** [Alan Williams](#ah-r0335). They make sure difficulty seeing the form is never recorded as uncertainty about the event.

**Personality.** Hollis loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a dark green knitted vest and a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. Hollis has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Hollis draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The witness asks to read the page alone. Hollis finds a quiet place for the witness and waits without reading over a shoulder.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Hollis takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Hollis offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Jody Mehta

AH-R0332 · Age 37 · Optical repairer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember mending a pair of spectacles with fishing line.” On Tuesday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Jody notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I know that bent temple. I repaired it for my former partner after we split up.”

**What Jody wants.** To identify the old repair without involving the former partner unnecessarily.

**Family.** [Amanda Kerr](../04/15-the-engine-lullaby.md#ah-r0594) is Jody’s half-sibling. Jody makes the call. Amanda is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The engine lullaby*, Amanda says, “I sang against the hum to steady my voice for an audition.”

**People in this story.** [Geraldine Quintero](#ah-r0337). The temple repair identifies the frame and the note in its case.

**Personality.** Jody enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Jody is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a pale blue work shirt and a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. Jody has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Jody rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The counter is busy and another visitor starts to hurry the signing. Jody puts a time on when the counter will be free again and protects the witness's time to read.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Jody separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Jody keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Quinn Carvalho

AH-R0333 · Age 44 · Station desk interpreter · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a witness asking me to pronounce their job correctly.” Monday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Quinn usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I read the statement aloud too fast. I asked her to tell me where to breathe.”

**What Quinn wants.** To record the witness's phrasing where the form allows it.

**Family.** [Agnes Kramer](../06/01-the-upstairs-lease.md#ah-r0757) is Quinn’s half-sibling. Quinn leaves the first voice message. Agnes calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The upstairs lease*, Agnes says, “My aunt collects the rent. I told the tenants before offering to help with their figures.”

**People in this story.** [Billie Warren](#ah-r0339) (community interpreter). They turn the printed account into language the witness will actually sign.

**Personality.** Quinn is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Quinn is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Quinn holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The witness asks to read the page alone. Quinn finds a quiet place for the witness and waits without reading over a shoulder.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Quinn writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Quinn helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Dario Allard

AH-R0336 · Age 29 · Menu designer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my dad rubbing prices off his own menu.” Friday often includes a stop at Studio Boulevard, where Dario waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I keep my dad's spare pair in my bag to see menus the way he did.”

**What Dario wants.** To let a diner borrow reading glasses without fuss.

**Family.** [Evelyn Wade](../07/11-the-salon-on-strike.md#ah-r1009) is Dario’s older cousin. Their family had a rule about visiting someone who had fallen on hard times. Bring enough food for the next day and bring a reason for an ordinary conversation. Dario knows when a neighbor would rather come over than receive visitors. Asked about *The salon on strike*, Evelyn says, “I have savings. I promised to cover a day's wages for the youngest worker if she wanted to speak.”

**People in this story.** [George Stahl](#ah-r0334). The father's glasses help test the new reading basket at the desk.

**Personality.** Dario is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Dario loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Dario is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf, Dario takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The counter is busy and another visitor starts to hurry the signing. Dario puts a time on when the counter will be free again and protects the witness's time to read.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Dario asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- If somebody calls for applause, Dario gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a narrow-striped sweater with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. **Under pressure**

I've put the pair on a cord long enough to cover two chairs.”

**People in this story.** [Elise Kapoor](#ah-r0338). A drawing of the room gives the hesitant witness a less public way to begin.

**Personality.** Noel carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a black overshirt with pearl buttons and a watch on a cracked leather band. Noel has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Noel leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Noel offers to help correct the printed name and leaves it to the colleague whether any public explanation is wanted.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Noel offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.
