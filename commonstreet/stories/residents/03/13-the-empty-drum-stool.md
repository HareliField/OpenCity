## Samuel Drew

AH-R0421 · Age 41 · Tour drummer · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a gig where I played as the curtain fell.” Monday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Samuel stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I walked away because my wrists hurt. I want the new drummer to know that was a choice, too.”

**What Samuel wants.** To explain the choice to stop drumming to the replacement.

**Family.** [Julius Mitchell](../08/11-the-hair-after-illness.md#ah-r1156) is Samuel’s stepsibling. The eldest person in the family used to sign cards with a large unsteady X. Everyone took this as a flourish until she asked someone to teach her the rest of her name. Samuel checks whether there is a way to sign or answer in private. Asked about *The hair after illness*, Julius says, “I print portrait cards. I offered one photograph from behind so she could look before sharing it.”

**People in this story.** [Riley Fox](#ah-r0429) (regular substitute). The original drummer promises the overlooked substitute a private rehearsal.

**Personality.** Samuel finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a thin silver ring worn on a cord. Samuel has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Samuel draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The drummer asks the substitute to keep the stool. Samuel asks whether the drummer means a loan or a gift.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Samuel asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Samuel waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Alexei Aguilar

AH-R0422 · Age 18 · Music student · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a drummer trusting me with one wooden stick.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Alexei by a small Thursday habit. Alexei watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “My grandmother made me sit on my hands at concerts. I climbed up and played her favorite rhythm.”

**What Alexei wants.** To play the grandmother's rhythm without asking permission twice.

**Family.** [Neal Day](../07/01-the-uniform-money.md#ah-r0907) is Alexei’s older cousin. Alexei puts the kettle on. Neal chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The uniform money*, Neal says, “I showed up in a football shirt on my first day.”

**People in this story.** [Billie Abbott](#ah-r0423). An audience volunteer receives the first paid booking.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Alexei has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Look for a loose dark braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Alexei listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A child freezes halfway through the song. Alexei lets the beat drop out and waits for the child to choose whether to rejoin.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Alexei keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Alexei listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Billie Abbott

AH-R0423 · Age 53 · Concert promoter · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember booking a band that refused its own encore.” On Sunday, near Lighthouse Cove, Billie takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I booked a full band and got an audience member. She kept better time than the headliner.”

**What Billie wants.** To book the unexpected drummer for a paid set.

**Family.** [Nita Ellisby](../01/12-the-last-green.md#ah-r0117) is Billie’s cousin. Billie can always ask for five minutes alone. Nita asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The last green*, Nita says, “The driver's seat has a dip the shape of his hip.”

**People in this story.** [Alexei Aguilar](#ah-r0422). An audience volunteer receives the first paid booking.

**Personality.** Billie is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Billie loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Billie has one cheek that creases before the other, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Billie gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The drummer asks the substitute to keep the stool. Billie asks whether the drummer means a loan or a gift.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Billie asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- If somebody calls for applause, Billie gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Hollis Fong

AH-R0424 · Age 35 · Restaurant dishwasher · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember learning syncopation from spoons in a sink.” On Saturday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Hollis usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I only play on kitchen pots. The stool was higher than our worktop, so I stood up.”

**What Hollis wants.** To try the stage kit again at kitchen height.

**Family.** [Ariel Sawyer](../10/02-a-last-ship-together.md#ah-r1362) is Hollis’s older relative by marriage. Hollis checks the date twice. Ariel brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A last ship together*, Ariel says, “I had planned to sit in the cab alone and cry.”

**People in this story.** [Sofia Yates](#ah-r0426). The kitchen percussionist and the teacher discover how to practice standing together.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Hollis takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Hollis has a high puff tied with plain cord, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a black overshirt with pearl buttons. It usually goes with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. When somebody else has the floor, Hollis folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A child freezes halfway through the song. Hollis lets the beat drop out and waits for the child to choose whether to rejoin.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Hollis brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Hollis asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Esther Mills

AH-R0425 · Age 57 · Divorced rehearsals director · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the envelope my former wife folded backward.” Sunday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Esther knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I recognized the handwriting as my old wife's. We still use the same little double underline.”

**What Esther wants.** To return the handwritten invitation to the ex.

**Family.** [Cynthia Bloom](../07/12-the-crash-anniversary.md#ah-r1020) is Esther’s cousin raised in the same household. Esther brings a spare shopping bag. Cynthia chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The crash anniversary*, Cynthia says, “I am arranging a meal instead of a ceremony.”

**People in this story.** [Ren Trent](#ah-r0430). The old invitation helps an orchestra librarian make a new, personal one.

**Personality.** Esther can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Esther has black hair twisted into a small knot, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Esther draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- The drummer asks the substitute to keep the stool. Esther asks whether the drummer means a loan or a gift.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Esther tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Esther asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Sofia Yates

AH-R0426 · Age 43 · Percussion teacher · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first pupil saying I played too fast.” Monday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Sofia buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I was scared my students would laugh at my playing. One of them started the count for me.”

**What Sofia wants.** To let pupils hear an honest adult practice session.

**Family.** [Rosemary Bhandari](../06/03-money-after-marriage.md#ah-r0777) is Sofia’s older cousin. Sofia asks before bringing somebody along. Rosemary can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Money after marriage*, Rosemary says, “I was their first lender. The woman I underestimated has been paying every installment.”

**People in this story.** [Hollis Fong](#ah-r0424). The kitchen percussionist and the teacher discover how to practice standing together.

**Personality.** Sofia can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Sofia can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a plum-colored cardigan and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Sofia has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. Sofia draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child freezes halfway through the song. Sofia lets the beat drop out and waits for the child to choose whether to rejoin.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Sofia asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Sofia suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Anthony Carey

AH-R0427 · Age 39 · Piano bench restorer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a bench with one foot perched on bus tokens.” On Wednesday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Anthony prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I mended the cracked foot by wrapping it in cloth from my graduation gown.”

**What Anthony wants.** To make the stool safe without discarding the cloth.

**Family.** [Nico Lin](../10/15-the-last-light-at-the-harbor.md#ah-r1499) is Anthony’s cousin raised in the same household. Anthony makes room at the table. Nico brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The last light at the harbor*, Nico says, “I wait on the public benches for my wife's shift to end.”

**People in this story.** [Michael Hubbard](#ah-r0428). The repaired stool goes to the shop for a brief last song.

**Personality.** Anthony carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Anthony is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a black overshirt with pearl buttons with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread, Anthony gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The drummer asks the substitute to keep the stool. Anthony asks whether the drummer means a loan or a gift.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Anthony suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Anthony offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Michael Hubbard

AH-R0428 · Age 24 · Deli counter worker · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember listening to park concerts behind the loading door.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Michael by a small Saturday habit. Michael looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I watched through a shop window on my break. The drummer came inside and finished the song for me.”

**What Michael wants.** To bring the next shop colleague out to hear the ending.

**Family.** [Sofia Cruz](../07/04-a-civilian-apology.md#ah-r0936) is Michael’s half-sibling. Michael saves a place. Sofia chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *A civilian apology*, Sofia says, “I can still hear the call from that day. The caller described a red door and we all heard red coat.”

**People in this story.** [Anthony Carey](#ah-r0427). The repaired stool goes to the shop for a brief last song.

**Personality.** Michael has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Michael has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for short black hair swept back. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a pale blue work shirt with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. In conversation Michael folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A child freezes halfway through the song. Michael lets the beat drop out and waits for the child to choose whether to rejoin.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Michael offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Michael says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Riley Fox

AH-R0429 · Age 46 · Session percussionist · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a substitute who played my part beautifully.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Studio Boulevard, where Riley waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I was the usual replacement and nobody called. I played the tambourine until the jealousy passed.”

**What Riley wants.** To offer percussion support without trying to reclaim the stool.

**Family.** [Nia Brooks](../06/04-the-saturday-swap.md#ah-r0787) is Riley’s cousin raised in the same household. Riley picks the meeting place. Nia sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The Saturday swap*, Nia says, “I need to sing at a funeral. The man we're burying once complained about my conduct, and he was right.”

**People in this story.** [Samuel Drew](#ah-r0421) (regular drummer). The original drummer promises the overlooked substitute a private rehearsal.

**Personality.** Riley treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Riley lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Riley is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Riley presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The drummer asks the substitute to keep the stool. Riley asks whether the drummer means a loan or a gift.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Riley asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Riley says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Ren Trent

AH-R0430 · Age 34 · Orchestra librarian · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the smell of rosin above worn rehearsal carpets.” Saturday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Ren stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I kept the invitation because I want to offer my chair at the orchestra rehearsal the same way.”

**What Ren wants.** To pass the orchestra chair to a willing guest.

**Family.** [Ingrid Kaur](../09/01-a-room-above-the-dryers.md#ah-r1209) is Ren’s older relative by marriage. A relative left a box of miscellaneous screws beside a perfectly labeled set of good tools. The box was the first thing the family fought over after the funeral. Ren remembers who needs to be asked before the repair. Asked about *A room above the dryers*, Ingrid says, “I can fit a sink if the room is used after school one evening a week.”

**People in this story.** [Esther Mills](#ah-r0425). The old invitation helps an orchestra librarian make a new, personal one.

**Personality.** Ren is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Ren is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Ren has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. Ren turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child freezes halfway through the song. Ren lets the beat drop out and waits for the child to choose whether to rejoin.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Ren offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Ren agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.
