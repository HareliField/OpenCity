## Eileen Bloom

AH-R1161 · Age 27 · New driver and job seeker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember tearing up a job application that mentioned the ridge.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Eileen stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I passed my test and kept turning down jobs east of the ridge. Nobody knew the real reason.”

**What Eileen wants.** To reach the far side at a pace the learner chooses.

**Family.** [Kate Dinh](../05/13-the-fountain-score.md#ah-r0726) is Eileen’s older cousin. A family picnic once turned into a six-hour wait at a bus shelter. What they remember best is the oldest relative teaching everyone to keep score with gravel. Eileen is very good company when everybody is stranded. Asked about *The fountain score*, Kate says, “I typed the program on my late partner's machine and got cast as its percussionist.”

**People in this story.** [David Soto](#ah-r1163) (parent). A parent has to let the learner speak first about what happened on the ridge.

**Personality.** Eileen gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Eileen privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Eileen is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Eileen gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The learner turns back before the crest. Eileen treats turning back as a completed decision and finds somewhere calm to stop.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Eileen offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Eileen offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Jacob Nelson

AH-R1162 · Age 44 · Driving instructor · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a student who could turn a wheel and still couldn't leave the driveway.” On Thursday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Jacob can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I trained the driver. I taught a maneuver and missed the part where the view disappears for a second.”

**What Jacob wants.** To teach the disappearing view as well as the maneuver.

**Family.** [Victor Marks](../09/10-a-four-o-clock-kitchen.md#ah-r1295) is Jacob’s half-sibling. Jacob saves a place. Victor chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *A four o'clock kitchen*, Victor says, “I keep the books. Early breakfasts double sales but also the number of taxis the kitchen staff buy.”

**People in this story.** [Briar Barrett](#ah-r1166). The instructor and painter compare the view through a moving windscreen.

**Personality.** Jacob uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Jacob well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Jacob has thick, tightly coiled hair, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. When somebody else has the floor, Jacob leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- A friend insists the return journey must happen that night. Jacob asks the learner when they want to try again, leaving the friend's timetable out of it.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Jacob brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- After a family quarrel, Jacob brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## David Soto

AH-R1163 · Age 55 · Parent and tire fitter · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember standing at a crossing long after my daughter reached the other side.” Monday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where David buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I trust my daughter and distrust the road. I had to say both things without making her fear bigger.”

**What David wants.** To offer support without lending the daughter extra fear.

**Family.** [Marcel Lai](../05/09-an-address-in-pencil.md#ah-r0688) is David’s stepsibling. David decides what stays off the paper. Marcel brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *An address in pencil*, Marcel says, “I saved the old numeral for scrap and realized it matched the dent on my first motorcycle.”

**People in this story.** [Eileen Bloom](#ah-r1161) (daughter). A parent has to let the learner speak first about what happened on the ridge.

**Personality.** David feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** David has a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Look for a center part and straight chestnut hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a small enamel bird on the lapel. In conversation David leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- The learner turns back before the crest. David treats turning back as a completed decision and finds somewhere calm to stop.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. David offers an ordinary errand that lets the distressed person leave without being followed by an audience.
- If an argument gets loud at home, David lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Pablo Armas

**Life in the city.** “I still remember counting the change after my first driving lesson.” On Saturday, near Maré Coffee, Pablo likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I offer rides for cash. Free practice sounds nice until I remember how much it cost me to learn.”

**What Pablo wants.** To get learning costs out in the open.

**Family.** [Basil Guzman](../06/03-money-after-marriage.md#ah-r0775) is Pablo’s cousin raised in the same household. Pablo chooses the music. Basil puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *Money after marriage*, Basil says, “I want to buy the delivery side with the drivers.”

**People in this story.** [Lee Ochoa](#ah-r1168). The taxi owner's fuel bill makes a useful comparison with the optional bakery run.

**Appearance.** Pablo is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Pablo turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The learner turns back before the crest. Pablo treats turning back as a completed decision and finds somewhere calm to stop.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Pablo offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When someone invents an answer, Pablo asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Briar Barrett

AH-R1166 · Age 49 · Road painter · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember repainting a line that was hard to see only in drizzle.” People around the brick passage off the shop row know Briar by a small Sunday habit. Briar takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I paint the edge line. The driver pointed out the place where the existing paint disappears in reflected light.”

**What Briar wants.** To repaint the section that vanishes under reflection.

**Family.** [Cole Dunn](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1237) is Briar’s cousin raised in the same household. Briar leaves the first voice message. Cole calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Cole says, “I typed the reply and changed saved you to stayed with you.”

**People in this story.** [Jacob Nelson](#ah-r1162). The instructor and painter compare the view through a moving windscreen.

**Personality.** Briar loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Briar has close-cropped dark curls, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with one cuff folded back twice. When somebody else has the floor, Briar presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- A friend insists the return journey must happen that night. Briar asks the learner when they want to try again, leaving the friend's timetable out of it.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Briar offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Briar offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## John Starr

AH-R1167 · Age 66 · Widowed freight driver · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first long journey with the passenger seat freshly empty.” People around the downtown fountain know John by a small Saturday habit. John keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I used to drive straight over every night until my companion died. Being on the passenger side is my practice too.”

**What John wants.** To try the ridge from the passenger seat.

**Family.** [Toby Webb](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0974) is John’s second cousin. John can stop the walk at any corner. Toby chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Toby says, “I ordered the cheapest thing and hated realizing my grandson was doing the same calculation.”

**People in this story.** [Karina Heller](#ah-r1164). Two passengers who dislike the height each find a different fixed place to look.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a watch on a cracked leather band. John has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with a long silver braid. John gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The learner turns back before the crest. John treats turning back as a completed decision and finds somewhere calm to stop.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. John writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- If a family plan changes at the door, John keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.
