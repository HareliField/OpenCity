## Lars James

AH-R1061 · Age 24 · Port trainee · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember writing office applications beside a fogged loading window.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Lars looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I want a desk job here someday, but the new rents won't buy one for anyone I know.”

**What Lars wants.** To secure an actual entry-level job in the redevelopment.

**Family.** [Milo Roche](../07/11-the-salon-on-strike.md#ah-r1006) is Lars’s first cousin once removed. Lars calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Milo leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The salon on strike*, Milo says, “I rent the space next door. I offered enough folding chairs for the meeting, whatever they decide.”

**People in this story.** [Ellen Boone](#ah-r1068). A desk-job ambition gets its first practical offer from a warehouse evening student. Also in the story is [Bianca Gallagher](#ah-r1065) (colleague at the rail siding).

**Personality.** Lars is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a moss-green cardigan and a little coil of soft measuring tape. Lars has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, with a loose dark braid. Lars touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A developer brings a drawing of the finished offices. Lars asks where existing workers are shown in the finished picture.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Lars asks for help with a task the neighbor does well, so both people get to contribute something.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Lars helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Darcy Lewis

AH-R1062 · Age 51 · Fisherman · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember moving my old livelihood from a shed to a locker.” Sunday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Darcy stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I stored my first fishing gear in that shed. I can afford a new locker and my apprentice can't.”

**What Darcy wants.** To keep affordable storage for apprentices.

**Family.** [Georgia Kwan](../02/07-the-margin-reader.md#ah-r0219) is Darcy’s stepsibling. In this family birthdays are measured by the breakfast somebody requests. A person choosing plain toast or an elaborate fried meal gets the same fuss for one morning. Darcy writes down the exact order. Asked about *The margin reader*, Georgia says, “I need one clean copy for class. I traded it away because the messy one explains why anybody reads.”

**People in this story.** [Maria Kent](#ah-r1067). The apprentice's kit and the informal food cart need to be counted in the space plan.

**Personality.** Darcy finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Darcy has a loose dark braid, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Darcy listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A worker says their job was left out of the consultation. Darcy records the missing job in the worker's own words for the consultation.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Darcy stays where the child can see the shop door and asks the neighboring counter to help find the adult expected there.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Darcy waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Karina May

AH-R1063 · Age 32 · Architectural illustrator · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember drawing a shop sign for a building nobody had asked to keep.” On Wednesday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Karina prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I draw architectural renderings. I put a worker in mine and realized there'd be no job for the person I drew.”

**What Karina wants.** To show real jobs in the redevelopment drawings.

**Family.** [Blake Baker](../02/10-the-bitter-orange.md#ah-r0245) is Karina’s cousin raised in the same household. Karina brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Blake makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The bitter orange*, Blake says, “I'm the new owner. I promised a memorial special before I'd ever tasted what she ate.”

**People in this story.** [Adrian Marsh](#ah-r1069). The rendering of empty offices omits a dance floor the old steward can still locate.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Karina has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation Karina touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A developer brings a drawing of the finished offices. Karina asks where existing workers are shown in the finished picture.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Karina walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Karina listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Roman Wade

AH-R1064 · Age 40 · Former prisoner and warehouse cleaner · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember getting a broom on my first morning after release.” People around Maré Coffee know Roman by a small Sunday habit. Roman likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I got my first contract sweeping that floor after a conviction. Offices need cleaners too, and I want the hiring terms in writing.”

**What Roman wants.** To get fair hiring terms recorded.

**Family.** [Priya Solis](../05/07-a-shelf-of-apologies.md#ah-r0665) is Roman’s half-sibling. Roman can stop the walk at any corner. Priya chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *A shelf of apologies*, Priya says, “I took mine on a ship and carried it through three oceans.”

**People in this story.** [Damian Fadel](#ah-r1070). The worker hired after prison shows the keyholder which overlooked jobs should be visible on the tour.

**Personality.** Roman is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Roman loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Roman has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Roman takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A worker says their job was left out of the consultation. Roman records the missing job in the worker's own words for the consultation.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Roman writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- If somebody calls for applause, Roman gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Bianca Gallagher

AH-R1065 · Age 55 · Rail worker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember one detour adding a whole hour to an easy shift.” Friday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Bianca stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I work the rail siding. Moving the warehouse shifts half my day onto the road.”

**What Bianca wants.** To protect workable freight access.

**Family.** [Essie Good](../01/14-two-portions.md#ah-r0132) is Bianca’s younger cousin. Bianca is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Essie calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Two portions*, Essie says, “The spare portion is mine. I go home before the register calls me a resident and come back before breakfast.”

**People in this story.** [Bailey Sato](#ah-r1066). A rail route and an old wall give the retired shareholder something tangible to show the grandson. Also in the story is [Lars James](#ah-r1061) (warehouse trainee).

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Bianca takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Bianca has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Bianca folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A developer brings a drawing of the finished offices. Bianca asks where existing workers are shown in the finished picture.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Bianca asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Bianca asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Bailey Sato

AH-R1066 · Age 72 · Retired shareholder · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember buying my first share while wearing a paint-stained coat.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Bailey by a small Tuesday habit. Bailey checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I own shares in the redevelopment. My grandson asked me to show him which wall I helped build.”

**What Bailey wants.** To walk the old wall with the grandson before voting.

**Family.** [Lena Sanders](../05/06-two-left-slippers.md#ah-r0657) is Bailey’s first cousin once removed. Bailey asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Lena remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *Two left slippers*, Lena says, “I hated getting dressed in front of people after surgery.”

**People in this story.** [Bianca Gallagher](#ah-r1065). A rail route and an old wall give the retired shareholder something tangible to show the grandson.

**Personality.** Bailey can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Bailey is easy to recognize by silvery hair gathered at the nape and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with one cuff folded back twice, Bailey touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A worker says their job was left out of the consultation. Bailey records the missing job in the worker's own words for the consultation.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Bailey brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Bailey asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Maria Kent

AH-R1067 · Age 47 · Food cart owner · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember opening a lunch cart nobody counted as a workplace.” Sunday often includes a stop at Juniper Street, where Maria knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I make lunch for sixteen day laborers there. They're missing from the formal occupancy list.”

**What Maria wants.** To count the lunch customers in the occupancy review.

**Family.** [Lara Moss](../07/06-the-empty-chair-on-nights.md#ah-r0953) is Maria’s cousin. Maria decides what stays off the paper. Lara brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The empty chair on nights*, Lara says, “I covered four shifts and I am tired of being generous in ways that make me unreliable at home.”

**People in this story.** [Darcy Lewis](#ah-r1062). The apprentice's kit and the informal food cart need to be counted in the space plan.

**Personality.** Maria can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Maria can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Maria is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a small enamel bird on the lapel, Maria holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A developer brings a drawing of the finished offices. Maria asks where existing workers are shown in the finished picture.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Maria checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Maria suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Ellen Boone

AH-R1068 · Age 29 · Warehouse worker and evening student · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a class I rushed to in my warehouse shoes.” People around Harbour Bakery know Ellen by a small Saturday habit. Ellen buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I am taking an office course in secret. I want the conversion to include a room where I could actually learn.”

**What Ellen wants.** To find a training room inside the conversion.

**Family.** [Aubrey Yates](../05/08-the-misplaced-umbrella.md#ah-r0672) is Ellen’s stepsibling. Ellen sets the time to leave. Aubrey picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The misplaced umbrella*, Aubrey says, “I manage the hotel that owns the stripes. I took the patched one to lunch and liked the shade better.”

**People in this story.** [Lars James](#ah-r1061). A desk-job ambition gets its first practical offer from a warehouse evening student.

**Personality.** Ellen carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Ellen has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. In conversation Ellen folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A worker says their job was left out of the consultation. Ellen records the missing job in the worker's own words for the consultation.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Ellen asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Ellen offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.
