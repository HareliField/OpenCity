## Luis Gross

AH-R1181 · Age 73 · Retired reservoir worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the sounds I made before learning my school language.” On Thursday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Luis notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I know what I mean. At home the words come out in the language I used before primary school.”

**What Luis wants.** To be understood in the language that comes most easily.

**Family.** [Charlotte Hsu](../03/06-the-hospital-radio.md#ah-r0357) is Luis’s first cousin once removed. Luis decides what stays off the paper. Charlotte brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The hospital radio*, Charlotte says, “I was the child who painted the tuning mark pink.”

**People in this story.** [Laura Bhandari](#ah-r1182) (daughter). The father needs a familiar language now and the daughter wants to learn it for the future.

**Personality.** Luis has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Luis knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Luis has tightly curled gray hair, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, a black overshirt with pearl buttons. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Luis looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- The client uses a word nobody in the room knows. Luis asks the client which person they want to help with the word.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Luis writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Luis asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Laura Bhandari

AH-R1182 · Age 46 · Family carer · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my father's first joke I only understood in translation.” People around the bank steps know Laura by a small Tuesday habit. Laura takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I am his daughter and never learned it. I want lessons even if he gets better faster than I do.”

**What Laura wants.** To learn the father's words while there is time.

**Family.** [Eli Fong](../06/07-reading-the-payslip.md#ah-r0815) is Laura’s second cousin. Laura decides what stays off the paper. Eli brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Reading the payslip*, Eli says, “I translate payroll terms into two languages.”

**People in this story.** [Luis Gross](#ah-r1181) (father). The father needs a familiar language now and the daughter wants to learn it for the future.

**Personality.** Laura enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Laura has short black hair swept back, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a boxy denim jacket. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Laura squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- Someone asks a family member to translate an intimate question. Laura offers a qualified interpreter and lets the client decide how much family should hear.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Laura asks the cashier quietly which item can be set aside without making the customer explain their finances to the line.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Laura puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Rain Yang

AH-R1183 · Age 35 · Bilingual office cleaner · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a client thanking me in the language I hadn't put on my form.” People around Lighthouse Cove know Rain by a small Saturday habit. Rain takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I speak his language but the agency only knows I clean offices. I want to train without losing my rent money.”

**What Rain wants.** To qualify for care work without losing rent income.

**Family.** [Edgar Joseph](../03/02-a-ship-without-postcards.md#ah-r0311) is Rain’s cousin raised in the same household. Rain calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Edgar leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A ship without postcards*, Edgar says, “I draw the alley bins because my mother knows cities by their cats.”

**People in this story.** [Lina Barrett](#ah-r1187). Training applications and job letters reveal two workers' long-hidden language skills.

**Personality.** Rain has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Rain can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Rain is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a brown corduroy jacket with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Rain presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The client uses a word nobody in the room knows. Rain asks the client which person they want to help with the word.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Rain asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- If a friend borrows money, Rain agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Helena Alston

**What Helena wants.** To learn how the client names discomfort.

**Family.** [Eden Tran](../05/04-one-clean-day.md#ah-r0638) is Helena’s cousin raised in the same household. Some years the only new thing anybody in the family got was a pair of shoes. Buying the right size for someone with plans of their own mattered much more than buying a surprise. Helena does the unglamorous measuring. Asked about *One clean day*, Eden says, “I hate reunion badges. I made ones that let people write what they're doing tomorrow.”

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and one cuff folded back twice. Helena has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Helena gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Someone asks a family member to translate an intimate question. Helena offers a qualified interpreter and lets the client decide how much family should hear.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Helena helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Helena turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Ariel Pinto

AH-R1185 · Age 41 · Interpreter · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my own parent's accent surprising me at someone else's bedside.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Ariel asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I translated our first visit and cried in the lift because he sounded exactly like my late father.”

**What Ariel wants.** To interpret the dinner visit accurately.

**Family.** [Della Keys](../01/09-the-porch-light.md#ah-r0090) is Ariel’s half-sibling. Ariel calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Della leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The porch light*, Della says, “We offered to buy the lamp as a landmark. He reminded me his wife's sister is still alive.”

**People in this story.** [Allen Baker](#ah-r1188). A joke recorded before the stroke gives the interpreter a familiar voice to begin with.

**Personality.** Ariel admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Ariel learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a faded indigo overshirt and a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. Ariel has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with short black hair swept back. Ariel turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The client uses a word nobody in the room knows. Ariel asks the client which person they want to help with the word.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Ariel sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Ariel describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Johan Vega

AH-R1186 · Age 39 · Care rota coordinator · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember taking two buses to the home across the valley.” Sunday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Johan stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I can swap two calls and cover one dinner. Somebody needs to count the extra bus fare.”

**What Johan wants.** To pay for the extra journey behind a shift swap.

**Family.** [Annette Alder](../02/13-the-quiet-verse.md#ah-r0277) is Johan’s stepsibling. Johan can always ask for five minutes alone. Annette asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The quiet verse*, Annette says, “I conduct the choir. My instinct was to fill the space and I practiced keeping my hands down.”

**People in this story.** [Remy Knight](#ah-r1190). An extra dinner trip puts a worker who recognizes the client's hand beside the plate.

**Personality.** Johan has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Johan has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with one cuff folded back twice. When somebody else has the floor, Johan draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- Someone asks a family member to translate an intimate question. Johan offers a qualified interpreter and lets the client decide how much family should hear.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Johan offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- If accused of favoritism, Johan explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Lina Barrett

AH-R1187 · Age 29 · Job-seeking retail worker · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember losing my hometown vowels in my first interview.” Friday often includes a stop at the downtown fountain, where Lina keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**What Lina wants.** To list the home language on a real application.

**Family.** [Carl Reeve](../01/07-an-unfinished-atlas.md#ah-r0066) is Lina’s cousin raised in the same household. Lina knows which ones can be shared. Carl keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. **People in this story.** [Rain Yang](#ah-r1183). Training applications and job letters reveal two workers' long-hidden language skills.

**Appearance.** Lina is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Lina holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

## Allen Baker

AH-R1188 · Age 55 · Sound technician · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember finding an old voice in the middle of a music cassette.” Thursday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Allen looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I have recordings of him telling jokes before his stroke. The new carer laughed before the punchline was even finished.”

**What Allen wants.** To bring the recorded jokes to a shared meal.

**Family.** [Logan Hamm](../05/02-the-crane-shadow.md#ah-r0615) is Allen’s younger cousin. Allen chooses the music. Logan puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The crane shadow*, Logan says, “I got teased for being sentimental. My foreman quietly gave me his lunch break to watch.”

**People in this story.** [Ariel Pinto](#ah-r1185). A joke recorded before the stroke gives the interpreter a familiar voice to begin with.

**Personality.** Allen has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Allen's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Allen is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Allen gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Someone asks a family member to translate an intimate question. Allen offers a qualified interpreter and lets the client decide how much family should hear.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Allen asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- If the joke lands badly, Allen seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember putting a family's pet names onto a practice card.” Saturday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Elise prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The misplaced umbrella*, Denise says, “I am teaching mending. **People in this story.** [Helena Alston](#ah-r1184). The existing carer helps the therapy assistant collect the client's own domestic words.

**Personality.** Elise listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Elise is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Elise draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The client uses a word nobody in the room knows. Elise asks the client which person they want to help with the word.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Elise stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- When a second witness disagrees, Elise keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Remy Knight

AH-R1190 · Age 30 · Evening home visitor · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being recognized by touch before the elderly client could name me.” People around Lighthouse Cove know Remy by a small Sunday habit. Remy takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I hold the client's hand at meals. Yesterday he said my name correctly in a language I have never spoken.”

**What Remy wants.** To answer when the client personally calls a name.

**Family.** [Julian Oh](../02/07-the-margin-reader.md#ah-r0220) is Remy’s first cousin once removed. When a family elder realized nobody could pronounce a friend's original name, she spent a whole evening teaching the table the three difficult sounds. Under pressure the family still says the names slowly. Remy knows how to correct it without making the person a spectacle. Asked about *The margin reader*, Julian says, “I drew a moustache on the author. Decades later my granddaughter guessed it was me from the shape.”

**People in this story.** [Johan Vega](#ah-r1186). An extra dinner trip puts a worker who recognizes the client's hand beside the plate.

**Personality.** Remy talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Remy's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Remy has an aquiline nose and a square chin. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a narrow-striped sweater with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Remy rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- Someone asks a family member to translate an intimate question. Remy offers a qualified interpreter and lets the client decide how much family should hear.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Remy finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Remy says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.
