# The boy on the corner

[Who gets to decide](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The brick passage.** A young street dealer who has been looking after his younger brother loses his informal babysitter. His regulars know where to find him and his family knows little about his work.

## Charles Mack

AH-R1091 · Age 22 · Young street dealer · Home — the brick passage

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my brother asking for a badge like the drivers wear.” On Monday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Charles takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I told my brother I worked in deliveries. He drew me in a uniform I have never owned.”

**What Charles wants.** To arrange reliable care and tell the younger brother the truth.

**Family.** [Ren Cullen](../04/11-the-fitting-mirror.md#ah-r0551) is Charles’s first cousin once removed. Charles brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Ren checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The fitting mirror*, Ren says, “I like the tailor's light better. I saw the silver in my hair there and canceled my dye appointment.”

**People in this story.** [Sam Knight](#ah-r1095). They need to agree where the younger child can wait at the end of school. Other connections include [Jade Hunter](#ah-r1097) (aunt), [Noel Gomes](#ah-r1094) (former babysitter).

**Personality.** Charles enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Charles is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Charles is easy to recognize by a high puff tied with plain cord and full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Usually dressed in a rust-colored canvas coat with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Charles draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The boy's babysitter takes a new job. Charles helps the family identify the next uncovered school pickup.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Charles starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Charles keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Beatrice Su

AH-R1092 · Age 49 · Market cleaner · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a customer asking if I could wait by his sleeping baby's room.” On Saturday, near Harbour Bakery, Beatrice buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I used to buy from the boy's dad. I'm offering childcare and he has good reason to check on me first.”

**What Beatrice wants.** To offer childcare on terms the family can check.

**Family.** [Lark Kane](../01/11-ten-fingers.md#ah-r0106) is Beatrice’s younger cousin. Beatrice can always ask for five minutes alone. Lark asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Ten fingers*, Lark says, “I picked a green that flashes under stage light because I watch hands for a living.”

**People in this story.** [Jade Hunter](#ah-r1097). A former customer offering childcare can be asked for references by the prospective foster aunt.

**Personality.** Beatrice is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Beatrice has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Beatrice folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- His younger brother asks what he delivers for a living. Beatrice lets the older brother choose honest words the younger boy can understand.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Beatrice asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Beatrice helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## David Ramos

AH-R1093 · Age 57 · Shop tenant and factory worker · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember someone smoking on our doorstep where the rain couldn't reach.” On Friday, near the entrance to the Wash House, David stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I live over the corner shop. I like the boy and I hate what brings people to our door.”

**What David wants.** To keep the boy welcome while protecting the shop entrance.

**Family.** [Neal Ferraro](../07/09-a-tenant-who-stays.md#ah-r0990) is David’s younger relative by marriage. David chooses the music. Neal puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *A tenant who stays*, Neal says, “My kid draws the bungalow every day. The tenant and daughter both asked for a copy and neither asked to be in it.”

**People in this story.** [Rosemary Heller](#ah-r1098). The tenant above the shop backs the shopkeeper's protected homework table.

**Personality.** David finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** David has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a boxy denim jacket with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation David gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- The boy's babysitter takes a new job. David helps the family identify the next uncovered school pickup.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. David agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, David waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Noel Gomes

AH-R1094 · Age 30 · Childcare worker and new payroll clerk · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember counting babysitting money into my first proper work purse.” On Sunday, near Ferris Deli, Noel asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I watched the little brother for grocery money. I have a proper job now and I won't apologize for taking it.”

**What Noel wants.** To hold onto the new paid job.

**Family.** [Andre Bell](../01/12-the-last-green.md#ah-r0112) is Noel’s second cousin. Noel gets to change the plan. Andre offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The last green*, Andre says, “He once drove my mother to hospital and refused a fare.”

**People in this story.** [Victoria Drew](#ah-r1099). The former babysitter and the salaried cousin can each explain why the first rush of help didn't last. Also in the story is [Charles Mack](#ah-r1091) (former childcare client).

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Noel is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Usually dressed in a charcoal zip cardigan with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve, Noel draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- His younger brother asks what he delivers for a living. Noel lets the older brother choose honest words the younger boy can understand.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Noel asks where they stood and what they could hear, leaving the rest open instead of filling it with guesses.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Noel listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Sam Knight

AH-R1095 · Age 38 · Primary teacher · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember closing a classroom door after a child's ride hadn't come.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Sam by a small Monday habit. Sam watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I am the teacher who noticed the uncollected child. I want him to know he can eat breakfast at school tomorrow.”

**What Sam wants.** To make tomorrow's school breakfast easy to claim.

**Family.** [Jeff Jiang](../03/12-one-unposted-detour.md#ah-r0416) is Sam’s older cousin. The family once kept the wrong suitcase for a week while the carrier found its owner. They never opened it. Everybody still has a different private theory about what it held. Sam can admit curiosity without pretending it is a right. Asked about *One unposted detour*, Jeff says, “I recognized the detour from a flood when I was small.”

**People in this story.** [Charles Mack](#ah-r1091). They need to agree where the younger child can wait at the end of school.

**Personality.** Sam is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Sam loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Sam has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Sam draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- The boy's babysitter takes a new job. Sam helps the family identify the next uncovered school pickup.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Sam draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- If somebody calls for applause, Sam gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Jody Dunn

AH-R1096 · Age 27 · Street drug dealer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a friend becoming inconvenient the day he needed a favor.” Saturday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Jody likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I sell drugs too. This family crisis exposes how little loyalty there is when a friend stops being useful.”

**What Jody wants.** To decide what loyalty costs after a friend stops selling.

**Family.** [Noel Wade](../04/10-closing-soup.md#ah-r0546) is Jody’s second cousin. Jody knows which ones can be shared. Noel keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Closing soup*, Noel says, “I count what goes in because somebody eating with us has allergies.”

**People in this story.** [Clyde Starr](#ah-r1100). A school play gives another dealer a concrete reason to cover for a brother who needs to attend.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Jody takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Jody is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Jody raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- His younger brother asks what he delivers for a living. Jody lets the older brother choose honest words the younger boy can understand.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Jody offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Jody asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

**Appearance.** Jade is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Jade draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

**People in this story.** [Jody Dunn](#ah-r1096). A school play gives another dealer a concrete reason to cover for a brother who needs to attend.

**Personality.** Clyde has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Clyde is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip, Clyde rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- His younger brother asks what he delivers for a living. Clyde lets the older brother choose honest words the younger boy can understand.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Clyde offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Clyde says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.
