# The carer's afternoon

[The families who come back](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The western residential streets.** An exhausted western carer asks her family for four hours every Wednesday without explaining how she will spend them.

## Maria Delgado

AH-R1031 · Age 43 · Full-time family carer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember reading the same paragraph beside an unanswered care bell.” On Saturday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Maria likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I want to sit in Paper & Pine and read the same page four times if that's what the afternoon takes.”

**What Maria wants.** To keep the weekly four hours free of conditions.

**Family.** [Oscar Salas](../09/05-a-clean-coat-for-court.md#ah-r1245) is Maria’s cousin raised in the same household. Maria checks the date twice. Oscar brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A clean coat for court*, Oscar says, “I let the coat out at the shoulders. You can undo my stitches without leaving marks, which matters to both men.”

**People in this story.** [Nelson Darzi](#ah-r1034) (parent). Mother and daughter both have a use for four unscheduled hours apart. Also in the story is [Alicia Sáenz](#ah-r1032) (sibling).

**Personality.** Maria always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Maria is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Maria is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Maria takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The carer's Wednesday relief calls in sick. Maria tells the carer plainly how much of Wednesday can still be covered.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Maria keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Maria suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Alicia Sáenz

AH-R1032 · Age 41 · Auto electrician · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember arranging respite when I didn't trust anyone to carry Mum's bag.” People around Ferris Deli know Alicia by a small Wednesday habit. Alicia asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I offered to take Mum only if my sister finally saw a doctor. I heard how that made my help another appointment.”

**What Alicia wants.** To offer respite without prescribing how it is used.

**Family.** [Yvette Allard](../10/13-one-last-dress-rehearsal.md#ah-r1475) is Alicia’s second cousin. Alicia brings a spare shopping bag. Yvette chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *One last dress rehearsal*, Yvette says, “I worked the parent's late shift and nearly burned the onions.”

**People in this story.** [Evelyn Gupta](#ah-r1035). A sibling offering help checks the proposed rota against real travel time. Also in the story is [Maria Delgado](#ah-r1031) (sister).

**Personality.** Alicia has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Alicia knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Alicia is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Usually dressed in a cropped bottle-green coat with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Alicia draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative demands to know how the free time was spent. Alicia leaves the free afternoon private unless the carer wants to talk about it.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Alicia keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Alicia asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Rae Stein

AH-R1033 · Age 16 · Student caregiver · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember doing fractions beside a stack of pill boxes.” On Wednesday, near the downtown fountain, Rae keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I cover the hour between school and the evening shift. I want homework space at Gran's table.”

**What Rae wants.** To make room for schoolwork during care.

**Family.** [Kit Evans](../04/03-the-savings-club.md#ah-r0475) is Rae’s older cousin. Rae asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Kit remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The savings club*, Kit says, “I deposited a week's overtime just before getting laid off.”

**People in this story.** [Emilio Starr](#ah-r1039). An experienced care manager learns the routine beside a pupil doing homework at Gran's.

**Personality.** Rae enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Rae is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a narrow-striped sweater with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip, Rae touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The carer's Wednesday relief calls in sick. Rae tells the carer plainly how much of Wednesday can still be covered.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Rae asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Rae puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Evelyn Gupta

AH-R1035 · Age 47 · School scheduler · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a care calendar too small for the journeys between visits.” Sunday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Evelyn often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I made a rota that divided time equally and forgot my brother's bus takes an hour each way.”

**What Evelyn wants.** To correct the rota for the real travel time.

**Family.** [Adela Ferraro](../02/04-the-second-dog.md#ah-r0189) is Evelyn’s first cousin once removed. Evelyn gets to change the plan. Adela offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The second dog*, Adela says, “I turned the photographs into a spot-the-difference game so my little niece would give evidence in her own words.”

**People in this story.** [Alicia Sáenz](#ah-r1032). A sibling offering help checks the proposed rota against real travel time.

**Personality.** Evelyn takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Evelyn can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Evelyn has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a brown corduroy jacket. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Evelyn takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The carer's Wednesday relief calls in sick. Evelyn tells the carer plainly how much of Wednesday can still be covered.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Evelyn calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Evelyn turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Marina Liu

AH-R1036 · Age 44 · Self-employed foreman · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember paying for respite out of an invoice that hadn't cleared.” On Monday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Marina can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I said I'd pay for respite and then lost my biggest account. I told the family before they counted on the money.”

**What Marina wants.** To renegotiate the lost payment before it causes a gap.

**Family.** [Pat Doran](../01/05-the-aquarium.md#ah-r0044) is Marina’s half-sibling. Marina knows which ones can be shared. Pat keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The aquarium*, Pat says, “I chipped a pane carrying the tank out. Once I'd have disappeared before anybody saw.”

**People in this story.** [Finn Ryan](#ah-r1038). Lost income and a night shift both set limits on who can safely cover Wednesday.

**Personality.** Marina admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Marina learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Marina has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. In conversation Marina turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A relative demands to know how the free time was spent. Marina leaves the free afternoon private unless the carer wants to talk about it.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Marina agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Marina describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

AH-R1037 · Age 38 · Neighbor and tattoo artist · Home — the blocks north of the port

**What Amir wants.** To keep the Wednesday meal shared.

**Family.** [Amanda Wong](../06/02-nine-unpaid-hours.md#ah-r0770) is Amir’s half-sibling. Amir chooses whether to use it. Amanda saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. **People in this story.** [Benedict Holt](#ah-r1040). The neighbor who enjoys Wednesdays helps the family let the carer come home without a report.

**Appearance.** Amir has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Amir folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- The carer's Wednesday relief calls in sick. Amir tells the carer plainly how much of Wednesday can still be covered.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Amir starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- If accused of favoritism, Amir explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Finn Ryan

AH-R1038 · Age 33 · Night porter · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember falling asleep in the passenger seat on the way to a care shift.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Finn by a small Saturday habit. Finn watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I work the night before. I can do tea but nobody should ask me to drive.”

**What Finn wants.** To contribute without driving while exhausted.

**Family.** [Dale Hamm](../08/03-the-guarantee.md#ah-r1075) is Finn’s second cousin. Finn makes the call. Dale is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The guarantee*, Dale says, “I bought supplies for the new business. My own small mortgage now depends on that family's next argument.”

**People in this story.** [Marina Liu](#ah-r1036). Lost income and a night shift both set limits on who can safely cover Wednesday.

**Personality.** Finn reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Finn trusts most.

**Appearance.** Finn has a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. When somebody else has the floor, Finn starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A relative demands to know how the free time was spent. Finn leaves the free afternoon private unless the carer wants to talk about it.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Finn asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- If told to back off, Finn does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Emilio Starr

AH-R1039 · Age 61 · Former care agency owner · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being paid to arrange things I couldn't arrange for my family.” On Friday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Emilio looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I owned a care agency and thought four hours was simple. My dad taught me the order of fifteen little things.”

**What Emilio wants.** To learn the father's routine from him.

**Family.** [Nicholas Mitchell](../03/10-the-broken-till.md#ah-r0397) is Emilio’s cousin raised in the same household. When a relative became too weak to cook, the family started chopping at the kitchen table with the cook directing from a chair. Revising the seasoning remained a point of honor. Emilio will argue about the seasoning like a proper guest. Asked about *The broken till*, Nicholas says, “My prepaid meal card wouldn't work. A stranger trusted me to pay it forward at breakfast.”

**People in this story.** [Rae Stein](#ah-r1033). An experienced care manager learns the routine beside a pupil doing homework at Gran's.

**Personality.** Emilio has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Emilio's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Emilio has a gray streak running back from the forehead, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. When somebody else has the floor, Emilio steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- The carer's Wednesday relief calls in sick. Emilio tells the carer plainly how much of Wednesday can still be covered.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Emilio takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- If the joke lands badly, Emilio seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Benedict Holt

AH-R1040 · Age 39 · Home help · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember waiting for a sister to volunteer her own piece of good news.” On Thursday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Benedict prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I always ask what she did with her time. This Wednesday I put the kettle on and let her offer the first word.”

**What Benedict wants.** To allow a returning carer to choose what to share.

**Family.** [Nell Zane](../01/15-the-empty-berth.md#ah-r0143) is Benedict’s cousin raised in the same household. Benedict is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Nell calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The empty berth*, Nell says, “The best sample is right in the private berth.”

**People in this story.** [Amir Jain](#ah-r1037). The neighbor who enjoys Wednesdays helps the family let the carer come home without a report.

**Personality.** Benedict listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Benedict has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with a center part and straight chestnut hair. Benedict squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A relative demands to know how the free time was spent. Benedict leaves the free afternoon private unless the carer wants to talk about it.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Benedict checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- When a second witness disagrees, Benedict keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.
