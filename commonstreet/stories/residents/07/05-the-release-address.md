## Cian Farrell

AH-R0941 · Age 47 · Recently released warehouse applicant · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the old family doorbell ringing in my dreams inside prison.” On Saturday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Cian prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I have addressed birthday envelopes to a demolition site for two years. My girl kept every forwarded card.”

**What Cian wants.** To send the daughter a letter to the right address.

**Family.** [Sasha Gil](../06/15-winter-afloat.md#ah-r0899) is Cian’s half-sibling. Cian knows which ones can be shared. Sasha keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Winter afloat*, Sasha says, “I want the berth clear on Friday. The owner asked me what time checkout is for someone whose home floats.”

**People in this story.** [Isaac Nord](#ah-r0950) (adult child). Returning parent and grown child try addressing one another by the right names. Also in the story is [Joel Gomes](#ah-r0947) (uncle).

**Personality.** Cian loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a faded indigo overshirt and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Cian has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with brown hair pinned away from the temples. Cian rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The returning father asks to visit the old address. Cian checks whether the person living there now wants visitors.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Cian offers an ordinary errand that lets the distressed person leave without being followed by an audience.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Cian offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Lou Reed

AH-R0942 · Age 43 · Separated school cook · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember packing children's books before clothes on moving day.” People around Lighthouse Cove know Lou by a small Saturday habit. Lou takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I moved without giving him the new number because I needed one quiet Christmas. I can say that to his face now.”

**What Lou wants.** To tell the father why the new address was withheld.

**Family.** [Opal Felix](../09/08-the-other-side-of-the-cove.md#ah-r1277) is Lou’s cousin raised in the same household. Their relatives kept a holiday fund in a boot that still had its mate. Even after the family acquired bank accounts, an envelope of change at supper meant somebody was planning a journey. Lou asks whether the person being visited is ready for company. Asked about *The other side of the cove*, Opal says, “I saved the original donor letter. It specified a sea view and made no mention of who's looking.”

**People in this story.** [Douglas Waters](#ah-r0943). Two households sort out where letters ought to go without making a public forwarding list.

**Personality.** Lou enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Lou is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a moss-green cardigan and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Lou has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Lou listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants to leave the homecoming meal early. Lou gives the child an ordinary excuse to step outside without suggesting they reject the father.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Lou takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Lou keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Douglas Waters

AH-R0943 · Age 31 · Parcel handler · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a letter addressed to somebody who didn't own my room anymore.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Douglas by a small Monday habit. Douglas knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I rented his old room. The first letter that arrived asked my permission to send another.”

**What Douglas wants.** To give a clear answer about receiving more forwarded mail.

**Family.** [Rafael Sloan](../05/02-the-crane-shadow.md#ah-r0618) is Douglas’s cousin raised in the same household. Douglas chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Rafael brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The crane shadow*, Rafael says, “I spent my first year here looking down. This gull was the first thing that made me look up.”

**People in this story.** [Lou Reed](#ah-r0942). Two households sort out where letters ought to go without making a public forwarding list.

**Personality.** Douglas is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Douglas has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a gray fisher's sweater with a train token tied to a zipper pull. In conversation Douglas steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- The returning father asks to visit the old address. Douglas checks whether the person living there now wants visitors.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Douglas asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Douglas helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

It usually goes with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. When somebody else has the floor, Darcy looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- If a stranger returns something damaged, Sofia listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

**What Joel wants.** To agree how long the guest can stay.

**Family.** [Cecil Aubin](../04/15-the-engine-lullaby.md#ah-r0599) is Joel’s stepsibling. Joel chooses whether to use it. Cecil saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. **Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a narrow brass watch set a little fast. Joel has a long face and high cheekbones, with a long silver braid. Joel folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The broken till*, Duncan says, “I offered to repair the till free. **People in this story.** [Darcy Ray](#ah-r0944). The victim's representative and the robbed shopkeeper decide which notice their families need.

**Personality.** Silas can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Silas has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with salt-and-pepper curls. Silas looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants to leave the homecoming meal early. Silas gives the child an ordinary excuse to step outside without suggesting they reject the father.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Silas asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Silas asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Isaac Nord

AH-R0950 · Age 23 · Retail apprentice · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember buying my first stationery that didn't have a parent's name on it.” People around the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio know Isaac by a small Monday habit. Isaac notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I was the child writing envelopes for my mother. I would like one letter addressed to me under the name I use now.”

**What Isaac wants.** To get a letter in the child's current name.

**Family.** [Mara Massey](../05/13-the-fountain-score.md#ah-r0725) is Isaac’s older relative by marriage. Isaac brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Mara makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The fountain score*, Mara says, “I can't stand the sound of running water at night.”

**People in this story.** [Cian Farrell](#ah-r0941) (parent). Returning parent and grown child try addressing one another by the right names.

**Personality.** Isaac carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. Isaac has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with brown hair pinned away from the temples. Isaac presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants to leave the homecoming meal early. Isaac gives the child an ordinary excuse to step outside without suggesting they reject the father.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Isaac says what they know in their own name and refuses to treat a guess as something the child has to disprove.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Isaac offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.
