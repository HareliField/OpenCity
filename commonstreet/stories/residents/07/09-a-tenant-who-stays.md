## Aria Starr

AH-R0981 · Age 35 · Recently separated groundskeeper · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember bringing my last flat's key to a family supper.” People around the market by Market Flats know Aria by a small Thursday habit. Aria often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I can finally afford to move home and did not know somebody else had spent a life there.”

**What Aria wants.** To find secure housing after the breakup.

**Family.** [Milan Gray](../10/04-names-on-the-night-wall.md#ah-r1384) is Aria’s cousin. Aria picks the meeting place. Milan sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Names on the night wall*, Milan says, “I put down the cleaner who sits on the steps until my taxi comes.”

**People in this story.** [Tariq Hamilton](#ah-r0987) (sibling). Siblings have to hear the same old voicemail before arguing over who was promised the house.

**Personality.** Aria gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Aria privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Aria has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, a straight nose and deep-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a brown corduroy jacket. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Aria listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- The future tenant asks when the garden will be empty. Aria asks the gardener which roots are most vulnerable before agreeing to a date.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Aria offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Aria offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

**What Kai wants.** To get time to preserve the garden's private memorials.

That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The split visit*, Ethan says, “I usually get the cheerful version of our client.”

**People in this story.** [Emery Rogers](#ah-r0989). The tenant's trees have an actual place to go because a neighbor offers some ground.

**Personality.** Kai uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Kai well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Kai has silvery hair gathered at the nape, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Kai takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An elderly gardener wants to move just one tree. Kai checks that the tree can survive the move before it is lifted.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Kai clears the dishes, lets the angriest speaker cool off, and resumes the conversation with a smaller audience.
- After a family quarrel, Kai brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The sandwich board*, Rosemary says, “I lent against the fridge. **Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an old plum windbreaker and one cuff folded back twice. Diana has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Diana presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The future tenant asks when the garden will be empty. Diana asks the gardener which roots are most vulnerable before agreeing to a date.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Diana finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Diana lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Hollis Osei

AH-R0984 · Age 70 · Retired library assistant · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sharing a study with a stranger who became a friend.” People around the bank steps know Hollis by a small Sunday habit. Hollis takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I would share the big room for a season. Nobody asked because they thought an old person always wants silence.”

**What Hollis wants.** To test a shared-room arrangement for a season.

**Family.** [Fen Ellis](../01/02-a-chair-on-the-quay.md#ah-r0017) is Hollis’s younger relative by marriage. Hollis asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Fen remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A chair on the quay*, Fen says, “The chalk gull underneath is mine. I used to draw birds under things at friends' houses.”

**People in this story.** [Neal Ferraro](#ah-r0990). A penciled drawing gives both women a chance to say what they recognize as home.

**Personality.** Hollis treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Hollis has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for silver temples and a dark fringe. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. In conversation Hollis looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- An elderly gardener wants to move just one tree. Hollis checks that the tree can survive the move before it is lifted.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Hollis asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Hollis changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

**Under pressure**
