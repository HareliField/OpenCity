## Cara Carvalho

AH-R0961 · Age 14 · Secondary school pupil · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember reading grandad's letter in the stairwell.” On Monday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Cara pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I can read every word but my classmates think my voice changes when I do.”

**What Cara wants.** To choose when to read aloud.

**Family.** [Grey Foster](../02/11-one-sleeve-short.md#ah-r0255) is Cara’s first cousin once removed. Cara can always ask for five minutes alone. Grey asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *One sleeve short*, Grey says, “I shortened the sleeve too far on purpose for a child to grow into it.”

**People in this story.** [Edgar Carbone](#ah-r0962) (grandparent). A grandparent practicing listening can stay in the room when the child changes languages. Also in the story is [Victor Lowe](#ah-r0963) (parent).

**Personality.** Cara has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Cara has thick, tightly coiled hair, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a boxy denim jacket. It usually goes with a red pencil tucked into a stitched pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Cara counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A pupil chooses another language halfway through the reading. Cara lets the pupil finish in the language they chose.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Cara asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- If accused of favoritism, Cara explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Edgar Carbone

AH-R0962 · Age 68 · Grandparent and retired translator · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my old schoolteacher mocking a single soft vowel.” People around Tideline Market know Edgar by a small Saturday habit. Edgar picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I corrected her accent too often. I sounded exactly like the teacher who frightened me at her age.”

**What Edgar wants.** To replace corrections with an invitation.

**Family.** [Lara Finch](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1233) is Edgar’s cousin raised in the same household. Edgar leaves the first voice message. Lara calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Lara says, “I supervise the desk. I was about to post a single staff photograph before I checked the two time entries.”

**People in this story.** [Cara Carvalho](#ah-r0961) (grandchild). A grandparent practicing listening can stay in the room when the child changes languages.

**Personality.** Edgar reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Edgar trusts most.

**Appearance.** Edgar has a long silver braid, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Edgar grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- A grandparent tries to correct a word in front of classmates. Edgar asks the grandparent to save the correction until the pupil asks for it.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Edgar offers to cover one concrete expense and leaves the friend room to say the expense isn't the real problem.
- If told to back off, Edgar does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Victor Lowe

AH-R0963 · Age 41 · Bus operator · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the book I bought to show I still knew my mother's language.” Friday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Victor checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I buy books in our first language and never open them myself. She's begun asking why.”

**What Victor wants.** To open one home-language book alongside the child.

**Family.** [Oakley Roman](../10/04-names-on-the-night-wall.md#ah-r1390) is Victor’s cousin raised in the same household. Victor chooses the familiar route. Oakley can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Names on the night wall*, Oakley says, “I cut the lettering and found no mention of the founder or the chief.”

**People in this story.** [Sage Barclay](#ah-r0968). The books bought by one generation finally get used in a lesson taught by another. Also in the story is [Cara Carvalho](#ah-r0961) (child).

**Personality.** Victor has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Victor's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Victor has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. In conversation Victor looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- A pupil chooses another language halfway through the reading. Victor lets the pupil finish in the language they chose.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Victor asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- If the joke lands badly, Victor seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Lina Ryan

AH-R0964 · Age 39 · Literacy tutor · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember losing my place in the simplest nursery rhyme.” On Sunday, near Harbour Bakery, Lina buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I read the wrong page aloud to show her adults can survive a mistake. She thought I'd forgotten my glasses.”

**What Lina wants.** To make classroom mistakes safe to hear.

**Family.** [Clyde Ho](../02/01-hem-by-hem.md#ah-r0153) is Lina’s half-sibling. Lina decides what stays off the paper. Clyde brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Hem by hem*, Clyde says, “My fingers know the aunt's stitch. She always pulled too hard when she was angry.”

**People in this story.** [Peyton Mitchell](#ah-r0969). A tutor admits a deliberate reading mistake to the child practicing with the shop cat.

**Personality.** Lina listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a black overshirt with pearl buttons and a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. Lina has a wide brow and a narrow jaw, with short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. Lina looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A grandparent tries to correct a word in front of classmates. Lina asks the grandparent to save the correction until the pupil asks for it.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Lina asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- When a second witness disagrees, Lina keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Edward Roy

AH-R0965 · Age 53 · Community interpreter · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my family's name read wrong over a graduation speaker.” People around the downtown fountain know Edward by a small Thursday habit. Edward keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I once changed my name to make school easier. I will pronounce hers however she asks.”

**What Edward wants.** To honor the chosen pronunciation.

**Family.** [Kai Berry](../10/11-an-ordinary-mirror.md#ah-r1458) is Edward’s stepsibling. Edward calls about good news first. Kai is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *An ordinary mirror*, Kai says, “I clean the mirrors on quiet mornings. I offered the widow those mornings before we decide who takes the glass.”

**People in this story.** [Wynn Martin](#ah-r0970). The classmate trying the line hears how a chosen name ought to sound aloud.

**Personality.** Edward talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Edward's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Edward is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Edward takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A pupil chooses another language halfway through the reading. Edward lets the pupil finish in the language they chose.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Edward repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Edward says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Morgan Gil

AH-R0966 · Age 48 · Bakery worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember reading a page on the factory lunch step.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Morgan checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I come after work in floury trousers because there are no books in our language at the staff flat.”

**What Morgan wants.** To obtain home-language books for the staff flat.

**Family.** [Ramona Ahmed](../03/05-rent-in-a-biscuit-tin.md#ah-r0346) is Morgan’s cousin. Morgan decides what stays off the paper. Ramona brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Rent in a biscuit tin*, Ramona says, “My bakery made the biscuits. I can date the tin from the misspelled ingredients.”

**People in this story.** [Cynthia Barrett](#ah-r0967). An adult practicing after a flour shift shares a quiet place with the learner practicing on a tin.

**Personality.** Morgan prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Morgan has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a straight nose and deep-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. When somebody else has the floor, Morgan draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A grandparent tries to correct a word in front of classmates. Morgan asks the grandparent to save the correction until the pupil asks for it.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Morgan asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Morgan clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Cynthia Barrett

AH-R0967 · Age 30 · Language student and shelf stocker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first word I wrote under a lunch tin.” On Monday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Cynthia looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I wrote an alphabet on the inside of my lunch tin so I could practice without answering questions.”

**What Cynthia wants.** To read a whole page without hiding the practice.

**Family.** [Ivan Ruiz](../10/03-the-gift-nobody-wants.md#ah-r1375) is Cynthia’s second cousin. Cynthia offers the first invitation. Ivan gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The gift nobody wants*, Ivan says, “I would give my right arm to inherit a place.”

**People in this story.** [Morgan Gil](#ah-r0966). An adult practicing after a flour shift shares a quiet place with the learner practicing on a tin.

**Personality.** Cynthia never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Cynthia can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Cynthia has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a little coil of soft measuring tape. When somebody else has the floor, Cynthia looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- A pupil chooses another language halfway through the reading. Cynthia lets the pupil finish in the language they chose.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Cynthia offers to help correct the printed name and leaves it to the colleague whether any public explanation is wanted.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Cynthia may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Sage Barclay

AH-R0968 · Age 76 · Retired music teacher · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember learning a neighborhood word from my granddaughter.” Thursday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Sage likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “My grandchild knows words for the city that I don't. I asked for a lesson before offering one.”

**What Sage wants.** To learn the city's words from the grandchild.

**Family.** [Emery Fields](../08/15-the-skipper-goes-ashore.md#ah-r1196) is Sage’s first cousin once removed. Sage puts the kettle on. Emery chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The skipper goes ashore*, Emery says, “I keep marine accounts. She has forgotten more about invoices than our last consultant knew.”

**People in this story.** [Victor Lowe](#ah-r0963). The books bought by one generation finally get used in a lesson taught by another.

**Personality.** Sage often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Sage have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a moss-green cardigan and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Sage has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. Sage folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A grandparent tries to correct a word in front of classmates. Sage asks the grandparent to save the correction until the pupil asks for it.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Sage tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Sage rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Peyton Mitchell

AH-R0969 · Age 10 · Primary pupil · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember reading a hospital leaflet to our sleeping cat.” Friday often includes a stop at the bank steps, where Peyton takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I want to read to the shop cat first. It has yet to interrupt anybody's pronunciation.”

**What Peyton wants.** To read once to an undemanding listener.

**Family.** [Priya Tal](../01/04-the-wrong-bicycle.md#ah-r0034) is Peyton’s first cousin once removed. Peyton is free to hang up first. Priya makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The wrong bicycle*, Priya says, “My client had a receipt and no photograph. I found myself asking him for proof I'd never ask my neighbors for.”

**People in this story.** [Lina Ryan](#ah-r0964). A tutor admits a deliberate reading mistake to the child practicing with the shop cat.

**Personality.** Peyton thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Peyton sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Peyton is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in a rust-colored canvas coat with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Peyton rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A pupil chooses another language halfway through the reading. Peyton lets the pupil finish in the language they chose.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Peyton draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Peyton asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Wynn Martin

AH-R0970 · Age 14 · School pupil · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember trying my classmate's tongue-twister on my big sister.” On Thursday, near the bank steps, Wynn takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I am the classmate she fears. I thought she was showing off until I tried one of those lines myself.”

**What Wynn wants.** To try the classmate's line before judging it.

**Family.** [Parker Parker](../10/05-the-return-of-a-witness.md#ah-r1394) is Wynn’s parent’s cousin. Wynn checks the date twice. Parker brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The return of a witness*, Parker says, “I invited her for lunch and didn't tell her who would be at the table.”

**People in this story.** [Edward Roy](#ah-r0965). The classmate trying the line hears how a chosen name ought to sound aloud.

**Personality.** Wynn gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Wynn privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Wynn is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with a narrow brass watch set a little fast, Wynn gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A grandparent tries to correct a word in front of classmates. Wynn asks the grandparent to save the correction until the pupil asks for it.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Wynn agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Wynn offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.
