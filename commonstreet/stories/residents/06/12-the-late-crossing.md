## Ariel Crawford

AH-R0861 · Age 65 · Retired traffic examiner · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the license renewal I put off one winter.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Ariel by a small Monday habit. Ariel likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I can drive Mondays until my eye treatment begins. I have promised to tell the rota before I tell my boss.”

**What Ariel wants.** To give the rota notice of the eye treatment.

**Family.** [Maia Molina](../09/14-the-missing-sunday.md#ah-r1340) is Ariel’s younger cousin. Ariel brings a spare shopping bag. Maia chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The missing Sunday*, Maia says, “I am the daughter who lives too far away. I started calling just after a loss because that's when he has most to say.”

**People in this story.** [Judith Dai](#ah-r0868). A lift promised through eye treatment becomes actual evidence for a late-bus case.

**Personality.** Ariel often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Ariel have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a rust-colored canvas coat and a narrow brass watch set a little fast. Ariel has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with silvery hair gathered at the nape. Ariel grins on one side while the eyes stay serious during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A driver on the rota asks for petrol money. Ariel agrees what the fuel actually cost and discusses the contribution privately.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Ariel draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Ariel rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Ellen Randall

AH-R0862 · Age 41 · Bus mechanic · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a taxi window stuck shut with four passengers inside.” People around Maré Coffee know Ellen by a small Thursday habit. Ellen likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I can help if nobody smokes in the car. Asking made me feel more fragile than giving lifts.”

**What Ellen wants.** To keep the shared van smoke-free.

**Family.** [Gavin Jones](../05/11-the-free-fringe.md#ah-r0705) is Ellen’s second cousin. Ellen sets the time to leave. Gavin picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The free fringe*, Gavin says, “I was the next in line and angry. The customer gave me a ride to my interview when our time ran over.”

**People in this story.** [Bianca Spencer](#ah-r0867). The no-smoking driver agrees to handle fuel money out of the rider's children's hearing.

**Personality.** Ellen thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Ellen sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an old plum windbreaker and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Ellen has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Ellen presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A shift worker is late to the meeting point. Ellen checks whether waiting will make the whole shift late and tells the rider the decision.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Ellen gets the people at the next doorway away from the smoke and asks a bystander to make the call.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Ellen asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Denise Ho

AH-R0863 · Age 39 · Unlicensed childcare worker · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember watching somebody else's children beside a car I couldn't drive.” On Wednesday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Denise checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I lost my license and kept quiet. I contribute by watching the driver's children for the round trip.”

**What Denise wants.** To earn a place in the rota by providing childcare.

**Family.** [Bea Rial](../01/06-window-seven.md#ah-r0056) is Denise’s half-sibling. Denise is free to hang up first. Bea makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Window seven*, Bea says, “You can see an old handprint when the sun catches that pane.”

**People in this story.** [Leon Osei](#ah-r0866). Watching children gives a rider at the furthest stop enough time to reach the lift.

**Personality.** Denise gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Denise privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Denise is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with one cuff folded back twice, Denise folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A driver on the rota asks for petrol money. Denise agrees what the fuel actually cost and discusses the contribution privately.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Denise alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Denise offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Patricia Ramos

AH-R0864 · Age 32 · Hotel housekeeper · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember belonging to a ride scheme where favors became overtime.” On Friday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Patricia knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I used to get lifts from my boss until I felt I couldn't refuse overtime. I want the rota to belong to the riders.”

**What Patricia wants.** To get the rota into the riders' ownership.

**Family.** [August Jackson](../03/02-a-ship-without-postcards.md#ah-r0316) is Patricia’s older relative by marriage. Patricia gets to change the plan. August offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *A ship without postcards*, August says, “I fold the cards into boats. My granddaughter can open them only by reading flags.”

**People in this story.** [Carol Dunn](#ah-r0870). One person wants a rider-owned rota. The other wants each rider to have a way out. Also in the story is [Judith Dai](#ah-r0868) (route planner).

**Personality.** Patricia uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Patricia well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Patricia has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Patricia looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- A shift worker is late to the meeting point. Patricia checks whether waiting will make the whole shift late and tells the rider the decision.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Patricia asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- After a family quarrel, Patricia brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Charlotte Sawyer

AH-R0865 · Age 27 · Shop night clerk · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first drive after dark I could choose for myself.” People around Harbour Bakery know Charlotte by a small Tuesday habit. Charlotte buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I sit in the back because being driven after dark reminds me of my parents driving when they fought.”

**What Charlotte wants.** To take a back seat without having to explain it each journey.

**Family.** [Fara Bloom](../01/07-an-unfinished-atlas.md#ah-r0070) is Charlotte’s first cousin once removed. Charlotte decides what stays off the paper. Fara brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *An unfinished atlas*, Fara says, “There is a copy of that blue ink under the third coat of paint in our map cupboard.”

**People in this story.** [Jay Leung](#ah-r0869). The anxious passenger decides when a sing-along in the shared car will help rather than hurt.

**Personality.** Charlotte feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Charlotte has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. Charlotte looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A driver on the rota asks for petrol money. Charlotte agrees what the fuel actually cost and discusses the contribution privately.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Charlotte accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Charlotte lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Leon Osei

AH-R0866 · Age 34 · Early shift cashier · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember walking to a lift after my colleagues finished their tea.” On Sunday, near Ferris Deli, Leon asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I have the furthest walk to the pickup. The others moved their tea break by eight minutes for me.”

**What Leon wants.** To keep the pickup time reachable from the far end.

**Family.** [Denise Xu](../09/15-the-missing-name-on-the-stern.md#ah-r1345) is Leon’s second cousin. Leon keeps the photographs. Denise knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The missing name on the stern*, Denise says, “I am the daughter with both spellings on my documents.”

**People in this story.** [Denise Ho](#ah-r0863). Watching children gives a rider at the furthest stop enough time to reach the lift.

**Personality.** Leon treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Leon has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Leon folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A shift worker is late to the meeting point. Leon checks whether waiting will make the whole shift late and tells the rider the decision.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Leon checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Leon changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

Asked about *The trust with a typo*, Rae says, “I threw out the letter as a joke. The post carrier came back after work to tell me it counted.”

**Under pressure**

Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. In conversation Jay draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**
