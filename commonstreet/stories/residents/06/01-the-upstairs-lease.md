## Dale Lloyd

AH-R0751 · Age 38 · Hotel cleaner · Home — the shop row

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first rent receipt written in hotel laundry ink.” People around the corner outside Coastal Barber know Dale by a small Monday habit. Dale usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I took the larger bedroom after the divorce. Now the little one costs more than my old house payment.”

**What Dale wants.** To renew without sacrificing the children's room.

**Family.** [George Stahl](../03/04-the-spare-spectacles.md#ah-r0334) is Dale’s cousin. Dale can stop the walk at any corner. George chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The spare spectacles*, George says, “My market stall sells cheap frames. I want a basket of new pairs where witnesses can reach.”

**People in this story.** [Amelia Kahn](#ah-r0759). They price real school clothes and real rent in the same week's money. Also in the story is [Aidan Song](#ah-r0754) (mortgage adviser).

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. Dale has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Dale counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The household must choose between paying a deposit and renewing. Dale lays out the two household costs and asks which deadline comes first.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Dale keeps the neighbor's place and finds out whether the stranger needs a different kind of help.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Dale listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

**Appearance.** Aubrey is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a cropped bottle-green coat with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Aubrey steadies a cup with both hands while listening when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative offers the family a sofa with conditions. Aubrey asks what using the sofa would mean for the people already living there.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Aubrey finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- If somebody calls for applause, Aubrey gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Aidan Song

AH-R0754 · Age 47 · Mortgage clerk · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember our mortgage officer closing the blinds for a difficult meeting.” People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Aidan by a small Sunday habit. Aidan checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I can approve reasonable leases. Nobody at the bank has ever told me what reasonable means for a night worker.”

**What Aidan wants.** To get a rent figure the family can use through winter.

**Family.** [Merritt Gomes](../02/05-the-pawned-trumpet.md#ah-r0193) is Aidan’s cousin raised in the same household. Aidan knows which ones can be shared. Merritt keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The pawned trumpet*, Merritt says, “I used to buy stolen instruments. This time there's a receipt and a real hurt that the receipt doesn't cover.”

**People in this story.** [Jacqueline Darzi](#ah-r0753). A paid dental bill shows why a bank's reasonable deposit wasn't sitting in the account. Also in the story is [Dale Lloyd](#ah-r0751) (tenant).

**Personality.** Aidan can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Aidan is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Aidan turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative offers the family a sofa with conditions. Aidan asks what using the sofa would mean for the people already living there.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Aidan stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Aidan asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Elliot Reed

**People in this story.** [Aubrey Carver](#ah-r0752). The offered sofa has to work for two children and the person already sleeping there.

**Personality.** Elliot carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Elliot has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for a long silver braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a charcoal zip cardigan with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. In conversation Elliot draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- A relative offers the family a sofa with conditions. Elliot asks what using the sofa would mean for the people already living there.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Elliot takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Elliot offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Agnes Kramer

AH-R0757 · Age 33 · Catering clerk · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my aunt returning every receipt folded in quarters.” Monday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Agnes stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**People in this story.** [Annette Levin](#ah-r0758). A family tie to the rent collector makes honesty with the next-door neighbor essential.

**Personality.** Agnes has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Agnes has close-cropped dark curls, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. When somebody else has the floor, Agnes rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The household must choose between paying a deposit and renewing. Agnes lays out the two household costs and asks which deadline comes first.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Agnes offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Agnes says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.
