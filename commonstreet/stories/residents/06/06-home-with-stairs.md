## Joel Garza

AH-R0801 · Age 75 · Retired alterationist · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember counting steps in my slippers after work.” People around the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio know Joel by a small Sunday habit. Joel notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I counted them twice with a walking stick. My discharge sheet had a box for stairs and space for yes or no.”

**What Joel wants.** To walk into the flat under personal control.

**Family.** [Joy Hall](../08/08-a-summer-marriage.md#ah-r1125) is Joel’s younger relative by marriage. Joel makes the call. Joy is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A summer marriage*, Joy says, “I can cook for twenty. The number became sixty when everybody started calling the beach public property.”

**People in this story.** [Harriet Chapman](#ah-r0802) (adult child). They plan the welcome around the mother's feet and the adult child's strength.

**Personality.** Joel is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Joel is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Joel is easy to recognize by closely cropped salt-and-pepper hair and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it, Joel gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A hospital car arrives before the handrail is ready. Joel checks the landing with the patient before calling the trip home ready.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Joel offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Joel agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Harriet Chapman

AH-R0802 · Age 47 · Kitchen assistant · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember trying to lift someone who told me to stop.” On Sunday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Harriet usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I want my mother at home but can't lift her. I canceled the welcome lunch until she can tell me what she needs.”

**What Harriet wants.** To prepare a welcome the mother can physically enjoy.

**Family.** [Willa Bishop](../10/14-the-last-home-visit.md#ah-r1490) is Harriet’s older relative by marriage. Harriet leaves the first voice message. Willa calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The last home visit*, Willa says, “I knitted her a retirement scarf with a loose end deliberately sticking out.”

**People in this story.** [Joel Garza](#ah-r0801) (parent). They plan the welcome around the mother's feet and the adult child's strength.

**Personality.** Harriet tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Harriet will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Harriet has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Harriet touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A relative offers to carry the patient up the stairs. Harriet asks the patient whether being carried is an offer they want to take up.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Harriet asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Harriet repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

Asked about *The empty berth*, Fenn says, “I've been selling an imaginary fifth berth in people's daydreams.”

**Personality.** Jules is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Jules asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Jules has a center part and straight chestnut hair, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Jules gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A hospital car arrives before the handrail is ready. Jules checks the landing with the patient before calling the trip home ready.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Jules asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Jules accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

**Under pressure**

- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Kira thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Neal Heller

AH-R0807 · Age 36 · Family caregiver and loader · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the unfair generosity of a relative offering a piggyback.” People around the brick passage off the shop row know Neal by a small Thursday habit. Neal takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I could give her a piggyback ride. She wants to enter her own flat on her own feet.”

**What Neal wants.** To help without lifting someone who wants to walk.

**Family.** [Elaine Aguilar](../05/07-a-shelf-of-apologies.md#ah-r0667) is Neal’s half-sibling. Neal is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Elaine calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *A shelf of apologies*, Elaine says, “I donated the clean replacement. **People in this story.** [Max Carbone](#ah-r0806). The discharge planner hears why a free lift up the stairs isn't the patient's chosen solution.

**Appearance.** Neal is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf, Neal draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A hospital car arrives before the handrail is ready. Neal checks the landing with the patient before calling the trip home ready.
