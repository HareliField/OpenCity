# The first straight paycheck

[What a working week costs](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The warehouse district and the brick passage.** A man leaving the street drug trade receives his first warehouse check. Old customers, friends and creditors keep showing up on the passage.

## Santiago Montoya

AH-R0791 · Age 30 · Former drug dealer and warehouse worker · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember buying a birthday present with money I couldn't explain.” Monday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Santiago looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I kept expecting that fat pay envelope. Seeing tax withheld made me furious until my daughter asked what the receipt proved.”

**What Santiago wants.** To bring the first honest payslip home.

**Family.** [Bea Morgan](../07/04-a-civilian-apology.md#ah-r0937) is Santiago’s second cousin. Santiago leaves the first voice message. Bea calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A civilian apology*, Bea says, “I checked the dates by my own maternity leave.”

**People in this story.** [Elise Cho](#ah-r0796) (mother). A smaller birthday gift tells the mother more about the new wage than a big speech would. Also in the story is [Basil Levi](#ah-r0793) (warehouse trainer).

**Personality.** Santiago is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Santiago is concerned.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. Santiago has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. Santiago draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer arrives during the new warehouse shift. Santiago asks the two people to talk away from the work floor and leaves disclosure to the new hire.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Santiago keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- When someone invents an answer, Santiago asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Briar Poole

AH-R0792 · Age 39 · Hotel caretaker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being asked about my supper by a man selling pills.” On Sunday, near the market by Market Flats, Briar often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I bought from him regularly. Losing his number matters less than losing the only person who asked if I'd eaten.”

**What Briar wants.** To keep a friendship after buying stops.

**Family.** [Mateo Serrat](../01/02-a-chair-on-the-quay.md#ah-r0015) is Briar’s older cousin. Briar decides what stays off the paper. Mateo brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *A chair on the quay*, Mateo says, “I remember carrying that chair up four flights to my first shore flat.”

**People in this story.** [Cara Holt](#ah-r0800). A former customer and a retired officer both have to let the new worker choose how they greet in public.

**Personality.** Briar loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a faded indigo overshirt and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Briar has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with short copper curls. Briar looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A relative says the first check is embarrassingly small. Briar asks what the check has paid for this week before making a comparison with street money.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Briar calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Briar offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Basil Levi

AH-R0793 · Age 48 · Warehouse trainer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the warehouse induction where nobody knew my last address.” People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Basil by a small Sunday habit. Basil checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I taught him to lift boxes. When someone recognized him on the floor he had to decide who told the foreman first.”

**What Basil wants.** To let the new hire speak to the foreman first.

**Family.** [Liam Roberts](../03/07-overdue-by-a-lifetime.md#ah-r0367) is Basil’s stepsibling. Basil can stop the walk at any corner. Liam chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Overdue by a lifetime*, Liam says, “I translated the difficult words for my desk mate.”

**People in this story.** [Tali Martin](#ah-r0798). The warehouse trainer and the bus-fare lender can make the next Monday possible together. Also in the story is [Santiago Montoya](#ah-r0791) (trainee).

**Personality.** Basil enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Basil is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Basil has thick, tightly coiled hair, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, Basil steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer arrives during the new warehouse shift. Basil asks the two people to talk away from the work floor and leaves disclosure to the new hire.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Basil takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Basil keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Eileen Teo

AH-R0794 · Age 29 · Street drug dealer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first rent increase that made dealing look tempting.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Eileen by a small Friday habit. Eileen checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**What Eileen wants.** To decide whether leaving the trade would pay the present rent.

**Family.** [Keiko Ames](../03/08-the-painted-footprint.md#ah-r0379) is Eileen’s older cousin. Eileen decides what stays off the paper. Keiko brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The painted footprint*, Keiko says, “I saw that foot in my wedding photograph. I'm meeting a stranger to compare summers.”

**People in this story.** [Frankie Kent](#ah-r0799). The dealer wavering about leaving hears what three Mondays of a good reference could mean.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Eileen has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with short copper curls. Eileen counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Eileen finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Eileen helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Ansel Perry

AH-R0795 · Age 55 · Glazier · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a shop window covered with cardboard for a whole season.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Ansel by a small Monday habit. Ansel likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “My shop window went through in one of our old rows. I want regular payments for the glass and no speeches.”

**What Ansel wants.** To get a regular repayment for the window.

**Family.** [Frank Moran](../04/04-station-steps-in-rain.md#ah-r0486) is Ansel’s younger cousin. Ansel asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Frank remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *Station steps in rain*, Frank says, “I was handing out biscuits and learned that offering a uniformed stranger food takes nerve on both sides.”

**People in this story.** [Katherine Nielsen](#ah-r0797). An old window bill and a found wage slip bring the young worker's finances into two households.

**Personality.** Ansel finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Ansel has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for a high puff tied with plain cord. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an olive canvas jacket with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. In conversation Ansel holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer arrives during the new warehouse shift. Ansel asks the two people to talk away from the work floor and leaves disclosure to the new hire.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Ansel draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Ansel waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Elise Cho

AH-R0796 · Age 57 · School meals supervisor · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember pretending to like the most expensive gift on the table.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Elise knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I am the mother who always took his gifts. I need to learn to accept a cheaper birthday present without asking why.”

**What Elise wants.** To receive a modest birthday present with genuine pleasure.

**Family.** [Etta Webb](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0026) is Elise’s younger cousin. Elise decides what stays off the paper. Etta brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The small signature*, Etta says, “Our best player can't grip the heavy replacement pieces.”

**People in this story.** [Santiago Montoya](#ah-r0791) (son). A smaller birthday gift tells the mother more about the new wage than a big speech would.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Elise is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in a striped cotton shirt with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Elise grins on one side while the eyes stay serious when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative says the first check is embarrassingly small. Elise asks what the check has paid for this week before making a comparison with street money.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Elise gets the people at the next doorway away from the smoke and asks a bystander to make the call.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Elise listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember returning somebody's wage slip in a blank envelope.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Katherine usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**Family.** [Dora Frost](../09/01-a-room-above-the-dryers.md#ah-r1201) is Katherine’s cousin. Katherine puts the kettle on. Dora chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *A room above the dryers*, Dora says, “I draw the people who wait for their washing and forget what their own faces are doing.”

**People in this story.** [Ansel Perry](#ah-r0795). An old window bill and a found wage slip bring the young worker's finances into two households.

**Appearance.** Katherine has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. In conversation Katherine rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer arrives during the new warehouse shift. Katherine asks the two people to talk away from the work floor and leaves disclosure to the new hire.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Katherine alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- If somebody calls for applause, Katherine gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Tali Martin

AH-R0798 · Age 45 · Bus mechanic · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember lending bus fare out of my own lunch money.” On Tuesday, near Paper & Pine Books, Tali starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I lend money too easily. I agreed to help with bus fare and refused to take over another month's rent.”

**What Tali wants.** To cover a bus fare without taking over the household.

**Family.** [Ignacio Paige](../02/15-a-sail-cut-small.md#ah-r0299) is Tali’s cousin. Tali can always ask for five minutes alone. Ignacio asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A sail cut small*, Ignacio says, “I promised a bag to each carer on the western route.”

**People in this story.** [Basil Levi](#ah-r0793). The warehouse trainer and the bus-fare lender can make the next Monday possible together.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Tali takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Tali has short copper curls, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. When somebody else has the floor, Tali looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- A relative says the first check is embarrassingly small. Tali asks what the check has paid for this week before making a comparison with street money.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Tali asks what the friend actually said last time and checks whether anybody is expecting them at home.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Tali asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The waiting-room plant*, Sara says, “I watered it so often its roots rotted. I am learning that leaving something alone counts as care.”

**People in this story.** [Eileen Teo](#ah-r0794). The dealer wavering about leaving hears what three Mondays of a good reference could mean.

**Personality.** Frankie can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Frankie has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Frankie listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- An old customer arrives during the new warehouse shift. Frankie asks the two people to talk away from the work floor and leaves disclosure to the new hire.

Asked about *A play in a pocket*, Hazel says, “I brought my own folding stool because being offered a chair makes me feel ancient.”

On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Cara looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- A relative says the first check is embarrassingly small. Cara asks what the check has paid for this week before making a comparison with street money.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Cara keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
