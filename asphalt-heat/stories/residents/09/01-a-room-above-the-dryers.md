## Dora Frost

AH-R1201 · Age 31 · Laundry worker and portraitist · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sketching a face in the blur on the laundry window.” Monday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Dora often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I draw the people who wait for their washing and forget what their own faces are doing.”

**What Dora wants.** To rent a small studio while retaining control of the portraits.

**Family.** [Katherine Nielsen](../06/05-the-first-straight-paycheck.md#ah-r0797) is Dora’s cousin. Dora chooses the music. Katherine puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The first straight paycheck*, Katherine says, “I found the wage slip on the pavement. I knew what it might cost him if the wrong friend saw it.”

**People in this story.** [Joanna Zhou](#ah-r1203). Artist and sitter discuss what making a likeness entitles anyone else to see. Also in the story is [Lucia Byrd](#ah-r1204) (partner).

**Personality.** Dora talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Dora's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Dora has a blunt cut just below the ears, a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Dora listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A portrait sitter asks for the drawing back. Dora asks the sitter whether the drawing can be stored privately or should go home with them.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Dora accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Dora says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Gregory Chen

AH-R1202 · Age 64 · Building owner · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember saving a room for a son who loved another city.” People around Studio Boulevard know Gregory by a small Wednesday habit. Gregory waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I own the room and was saving it for a child who has no intention of moving home.”

**What Gregory wants.** To decide who can actually use the empty room now.

**Family.** [Cara Mann](../05/15-a-boat-for-sale-twice.md#ah-r0747) is Gregory’s cousin’s child. Gregory brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Cara makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *A boat for sale twice*, Cara says, “I bought a yard of the same blue canvas to take home.”

**People in this story.** [Lucia Byrd](#ah-r1204). The partner's rent limit meets the owner's price for a long-empty studio.

**Personality.** Gregory prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Gregory has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for silver temples and a dark fringe. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Gregory takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An investor offers free publicity for the studio. Gregory asks what cost the publicity will cover before agreeing to an exhibition.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Gregory offers an ordinary errand that lets the distressed person leave without being followed by an audience.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Gregory clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

**Appearance.** Joanna has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for a loose dark braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an old plum windbreaker with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Joanna steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

## Lucia Byrd

AH-R1204 · Age 34 · Laundry worker's partner and hotel clerk · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember negotiating kitchen space for an unfinished picture.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Lucia looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I am the worker's partner. I can give up the kitchen table for art but I cannot give up part of the rent for a studio.”

**What Lucia wants.** To agree a studio cost the couple can carry.

**Family.** [Emery Gordon](../02/03-the-sandwich-board.md#ah-r0173) is Lucia’s older cousin. Lucia calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Emery leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The sandwich board*, Emery says, “I bid because there is a second menu under the first.”

**People in this story.** [Gregory Chen](#ah-r1202). The partner's rent limit meets the owner's price for a long-empty studio. Also in the story is [Dora Frost](#ah-r1201) (partner).

**Personality.** Lucia often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Lucia have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Lucia has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. When somebody else has the floor, Lucia leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- An investor offers free publicity for the studio. Lucia asks what cost the publicity will cover before agreeing to an exhibition.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Lucia offers to help correct the printed name and leaves it to the colleague whether any public explanation is wanted.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Lucia rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Adela Hull

AH-R1205 · Age 55 · Commercial gallerist · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember evaluating art from the coatroom of a rich family's house.” Monday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Adela likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I buy art from millionaires and want to pay the laundress in exposure. My daughter pointed out how that sounded.”

**What Adela wants.** To offer real payment for a drawing displayed publicly.

**Family.** [Cory Stahl](../04/12-the-ladder-crossing.md#ah-r0570) is Adela’s half-sibling. Adela calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Cory leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The ladder crossing*, Cory says, “I rang the crossing bell with my elbow. The blind man beside me thought I'd done it for him, and then I did.”

**People in this story.** [Fiona Kelly](#ah-r1210). The paying buyer gives the commercial gallerist a useful example of what support actually costs.

**Personality.** Adela thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Adela sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. Adela has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, with a loose dark braid. Adela squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A portrait sitter asks for the drawing back. Adela asks the sitter whether the drawing can be stored privately or should go home with them.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Adela writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Adela asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Emma Davis

AH-R1206 · Age 69 · Neighbor and retired mechanic · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a pensioner posing proudly in a mended cardigan.” On Tuesday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Emma takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I live across the landing. I asked for free sittings for the old people nobody photographs unless they're blowing candles out.”

**What Emma wants.** To secure portrait sittings for overlooked neighbors.

**Family.** [Vera Rios](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0021) is Emma’s second cousin. Emma sets the time to leave. Vera picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The small signature*, Vera says, “For years I bought my crews soup from that cart.”

**People in this story.** [Ingrid Kaur](#ah-r1209). A free sitting for a pensioner and an after-school session share the same sink.

**Personality.** Emma gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Emma privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Emma is easy to recognize by two white streaks in otherwise dark hair and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a brown corduroy jacket with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Emma gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An investor offers free publicity for the studio. Emma asks what cost the publicity will cover before agreeing to an exhibition.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Emma asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Emma offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Isaac Mendez

AH-R1208 · Age 36 · Illustrator · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember drawing a person I had good reason to distrust.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Isaac by a small Tuesday habit. Isaac pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I want to draw somebody who dislikes me. The worker offered to sit after I explained the complaint I made.”

**What Isaac wants.** To sit for a portrait after discussing the complaint.

**Family.** [John Machado](../10/03-the-gift-nobody-wants.md#ah-r1376) is Isaac’s older relative by marriage. Isaac keeps the photographs. John knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The gift nobody wants*, John says, “I am the father's old partner and I remember what he sacrificed to get out from under his own father.”

**People in this story.** [Robin Atwood](#ah-r1207). An unfinished art course and a difficult portrait bring two adult learners to the doors of the same studio.

**Personality.** Isaac feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Isaac has close-cropped dark curls, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. When somebody else has the floor, Isaac steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- An investor offers free publicity for the studio. Isaac asks what cost the publicity will cover before agreeing to an exhibition.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Isaac starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Isaac lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

**Appearance.** Fiona has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for auburn hair cut square at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with a watch on a cracked leather band. In conversation Fiona rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- An investor offers free publicity for the studio. Fiona asks what cost the publicity will cover before agreeing to an exhibition.
