# The worker who couldn't read the joke

[The price of being known](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Port of Asphalt.** A joke left in the port break room circulates online. A worker pictured laughing never understood the language it was written in.

## Darcy Fadel

AH-R1211 · Age 37 · Freight handler · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember laughing at a break-room story a moment too late.” People around Juniper Street know Darcy by a small Thursday habit. Darcy knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I laughed because my boss looked right at me. I want the people who saw the picture to know that.”

**What Darcy wants.** To tell the crew what the laugh actually meant.

**Family.** [Franklin Pike](../06/01-the-upstairs-lease.md#ah-r0760) is Darcy’s half-sibling. Darcy sets the time to leave. Franklin picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The upstairs lease*, Franklin says, “I offered boxes and got asked to be a witness at the lease meeting.”

**People in this story.** [Olivia Nielsen](#ah-r1214) (bilingual colleague). A truthful translation gives the photographed worker the words to challenge the apparent laugh.

**Personality.** Darcy finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Darcy has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, with short copper curls. Darcy gives a little nod before asking a hard question during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A manager wants the photographed worker to address the crew. Darcy offers the pictured worker a private conversation before any meeting with the crew.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Darcy finds out what the destination actually looks like to the traveler and gives directions in those terms.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Darcy waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Remy Macrae

AH-R1212 · Age 44 · Shift fitter · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a trainee asking me why my jokes always needed a victim.” Monday often includes a stop at Paper & Pine Books, where Remy starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I wrote the joke before I knew who would be on shift. I have to answer to a person I usually only nod at.”

**What Remy wants.** To account for the words with the pictured worker present.

**Family.** [Victoria Drew](../08/05-the-boy-on-the-corner.md#ah-r1099) is Remy’s half-sibling. Remy can always ask for five minutes alone. Victoria asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The boy on the corner*, Victoria says, “I am the cousin with steady wages who went missing first.”

**People in this story.** [Yasmin Mason](#ah-r1218). The writer and the silent bystander each owe a different conversation to the person in the picture.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Remy has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for a long plait tucked inside the collar. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation Remy listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A relative forwards the break-room picture. Remy asks that the picture stop traveling while the worker decides what to say.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Remy writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Remy listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

**Appearance.** Barbara is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a charcoal zip cardigan with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Barbara turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

## Bianca Breen

AH-R1215 · Age 49 · Break room manager · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember serving lunch the week two crews stopped sharing tables.” On Saturday, near Studio Boulevard, Bianca waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I run the break room. I won't make the wronged worker stand up at a meeting unless he wants to.”

**What Bianca wants.** To protect the worker's choice about speaking publicly.

**Family.** [Joss Farin](../01/10-the-thirteenth-doughnut.md#ah-r0091) is Bianca’s stepsibling. Bianca chooses the music. Joss puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The thirteenth doughnut*, Joss says, “I made the first one with lemon because my husband hated lemon and wouldn't eat it on the way.”

**People in this story.** [Finn Heller](#ah-r1217). The break-room manager and staffing contractor agree on a private way to hear individual workers.

**Personality.** Bianca can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Bianca has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. When somebody else has the floor, Bianca draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A manager wants the photographed worker to address the crew. Bianca offers the pictured worker a private conversation before any meeting with the crew.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Bianca asks the person already using it whether they can comfortably stand, rather than presuming who needs it most.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Bianca asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

**Under pressure**

**People in this story.** [Barbara Frost](#ah-r1213). A family copy of the crew portrait becomes evidence of how far the joke traveled.

**Personality.** Oliver is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Oliver is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Oliver has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with short black hair swept back. Oliver touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A relative forwards the break-room picture. Oliver asks that the picture stop traveling while the worker decides what to say.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Oliver writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Oliver agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.
