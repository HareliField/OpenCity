## Nell Tang

AH-R1221 · Age 73 · Retired seamstress and homeowner · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember making my first mortgage deposit in tailor's chalky shoes.” On Tuesday, near the downtown fountain, Nell keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I can sell for a fortune but can't buy a smaller place anywhere near my sister at these prices.”

**What Nell wants.** To stay near the sister without selling away every option.

**Family.** [Isaac Jensen](../07/12-the-crash-anniversary.md#ah-r1018) is Nell’s younger relative by marriage. Nell can stop the walk at any corner. Isaac chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The crash anniversary*, Isaac says, “I was the witness nobody called because I was twelve.”

**People in this story.** [Loretta Osei](#ah-r1229) (sister). The two sisters have to decide where companionship, stairs and ownership intersect. Also in the story is [Philip Yu](#ah-r1223) (adult son).

**Personality.** Nell loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Nell remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Nell is easy to recognize by steel-colored hair cut square above the collar and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole, Nell gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The homeowner is asked to sell while the market is high. Nell checks which affordable addresses leave the sister within visiting distance.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Nell asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- When invited to tell the story later, Nell includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Gloria Batista

AH-R1222 · Age 42 · Property valuer · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember carrying a valuer's bag up six flights in August.” People around Lighthouse Cove know Gloria by a small Monday habit. Gloria takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I valued the view and overlooked the years she carried her shopping up without a lift. I returned with my own bags to try it.”

**What Gloria wants.** To value the stairs as well as the view.

**Family.** [Eva Bay](../10/07-an-empty-dedication.md#ah-r1411) is Gloria’s stepsibling. Gloria puts the kettle on. Eva chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *An empty dedication*, Eva says, “I wrote the book while my husband cooked. He hates public thanks and likes a hot meal waiting for him.”

**People in this story.** [Ruben Ali](#ah-r1230). The journalist tests the dramatic valuation against the unglamorous price of a usable bath.

**Personality.** Gloria has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Gloria has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Gloria looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks for a loan against the house. Gloria puts the monthly cost of the suggested loan beside the home's paper value.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Gloria brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When somebody trades on a title, Gloria asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember asking my mother for money with a full fridge at home.” On Saturday, near the market by Market Flats, Philip often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I am the son who asked for an advance against the inheritance. Seeing Mum count coins for coffee made me withdraw it.”

**Family.** [Sasha Wong](../05/15-a-boat-for-sale-twice.md#ah-r0741) is Philip’s cousin. Philip gets to change the plan. Sasha offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *A boat for sale twice*, Sasha says, “I wrote my ex's number the first time. We haven't decided which of us owns the good years aboard.”

**People in this story.** [Mona Brandt](#ah-r1226). The old lady's son discovers who has been extending lunch credit. Also in the story is [Nell Tang](#ah-r1221) (mother).

**Personality.** Philip finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Philip has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a plum-colored cardigan. It usually goes with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. When somebody else has the floor, Philip looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- The homeowner is asked to sell while the market is high. Philip checks which affordable addresses leave the sister within visiting distance.

**Appearance.** Elias is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Elias gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

## Mona Brandt

AH-R1226 · Age 31 · Lunch courier · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember delivering yesterday's soup to the richest person in the building.” Sunday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Mona likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I deliver her lunch. I love the joke about a millionaire buying soup on tick but I wish she'd let me balance the account.”

**What Mona wants.** To settle the soup account without turning it into a public joke.

**Family.** [Daryl Mills](../02/15-a-sail-cut-small.md#ah-r0293) is Mona’s cousin. Mona asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Daryl remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A sail cut small*, Daryl says, “I'm stitching a sunshade for a neighbor who will never set foot on a boat again.”

**People in this story.** [Philip Yu](#ah-r1223). The old lady's son discovers who has been extending lunch credit.

**Personality.** Mona has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Mona knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Mona is easy to recognize by a long plait tucked inside the collar and a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges, Mona draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks for a loan against the house. Mona puts the monthly cost of the suggested loan beside the home's paper value.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Mona asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Mona asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Jody Breen

AH-R1228 · Age 69 · Retired cabinet maker · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the cupboard I built with a compartment for bus money.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Jody by a small Wednesday habit. Jody can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I built the first kitchen cabinets here. I'd like to recover them before a rich buyer throws them away.”

**What Jody wants.** To rescue the original cabinets if the flat is sold.

**Family.** [Pia Burke](../10/04-names-on-the-night-wall.md#ah-r1383) is Jody’s cousin’s child. Jody saves a place. Pia chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *Names on the night wall*, Pia says, “I thanked my ex. A good custody calendar is worth more to both of us than another argument over uniforms.”

**People in this story.** [Noel Hudson](#ah-r1224). A former lodger and the original cabinet maker both know what the flat was like before it became valuable.

**Personality.** Jody has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Jody can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Jody is easy to recognize by soft white hair brushed sideways and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a narrow brass watch set a little fast, Jody turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks for a loan against the house. Jody puts the monthly cost of the suggested loan beside the home's paper value.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Jody agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- If a friend borrows money, Jody agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.
