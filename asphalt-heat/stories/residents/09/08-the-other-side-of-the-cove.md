# The other side of the cove

[The price of being known](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Lighthouse Cove.** Lighthouse Cove residents are asked to help name a public bench. One name belongs to a millionaire donor, another to a woman who cleaned the beach for forty summers.

## Aria Machado

AH-R1271 · Age 72 · Retired beach cleaner · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cleaning a beach the day after a public honor nobody invited me to.” On Wednesday, near Paper & Pine Books, Aria starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I was that cleaner. I'm still alive and I would like the seat low enough for me to stand up from.”

**What Aria wants.** To sit and stand comfortably at the new bench.

**Family.** [Belinda Lee](../06/06-home-with-stairs.md#ah-r0810) is Aria’s stepsibling. Aria brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Belinda checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Home with stairs*, Belinda says, “I rented my old flat out too soon after my own illness.”

**People in this story.** [Daphne Ng](#ah-r1272). A working cleaner and a millionaire decide what, besides two names, the bench will give its users. Also in the story is [Walter Ray](#ah-r1274) (adult child).

**Personality.** Aria takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Aria can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Aria is easy to recognize by a long silver braid and a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Usually dressed in a moss-green cardigan with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand, Aria gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A public donor asks to approve the wording on the bench. Aria asks the cleaner and the donor for separate views on the wording.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Aria offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Aria turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Daphne Ng

AH-R1272 · Age 65 · Millionaire business seller · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first company changing hands under a borrowed umbrella.” On Saturday, near Ferris Deli, Daphne asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I gave the money after selling my second company. I wanted anonymity until my late partner's name entered the discussion.”

**What Daphne wants.** To agree whether the late partner's name should appear.

**Family.** [Nathan Neal](../05/14-the-split-visit.md#ah-r0731) is Daphne’s cousin’s child. Daphne chooses the music. Nathan puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The split visit*, Nathan says, “I stayed for breakfast because the woman always eats faster when she thinks we're in a hurry.”

**People in this story.** [Aria Machado](#ah-r1271). A working cleaner and a millionaire decide what, besides two names, the bench will give its users.

**Personality.** Daphne admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Daphne learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a washed violet jersey and a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. Daphne has fine brows over wide-set eyes, with silvery hair gathered at the nape. Daphne turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A former cleaner wants to try the seat before it is installed. Daphne gets the seat tested by the person who will actually need to stand up from it.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Daphne asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Daphne describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Peyton Waters

AH-R1273 · Age 69 · Retired newspaper courier · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember delivering a paper to a beach hut that had no number.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Peyton by a small Sunday habit. Peyton prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I brought the cleaner a newspaper every summer. She let me use her bucket for the sea glass.”

**What Peyton wants.** To return the beach bucket with a picture of its use.

**Family.** [Hector Clay](../04/05-the-dead-man-s-tab.md#ah-r0497) is Peyton’s stepsibling. Peyton asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Hector remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The dead man's tab*, Hector says, “He charged me rent for a room he didn't own.”

**People in this story.** [Warren Ito](#ah-r1275). The person who used to carry newspapers finds the lettering artist space on the public proof.

**Personality.** Peyton has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Peyton has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for silvery hair gathered at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a plum-colored cardigan with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. In conversation Peyton grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- A public donor asks to approve the wording on the bench. Peyton asks the cleaner and the donor for separate views on the wording.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Peyton stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- If accused of favoritism, Peyton explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Walter Ray

AH-R1274 · Age 46 · Catering supervisor · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember hearing rented hands called willing volunteers.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Walter looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I want my mother's work named with her proper job title. The city keeps calling her a volunteer.”

**What Walter wants.** To get the mother's paid work accurately acknowledged.

**Family.** [Carlo Gil](../05/10-midnight-cinnamon.md#ah-r0692) is Walter’s stepsibling. Walter brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Carlo makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Midnight cinnamon*, Carlo says, “I sell cinnamon by weight. I am trying to price the little packets customers buy just to carry home.”

**People in this story.** [Yusuf Bennett](#ah-r1278). Mother and successor become visible to the committee as two paid workers. Also in the story is [Aria Machado](#ah-r1271) (mother).

**Personality.** Walter reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Walter trusts most.

**Appearance.** Walter has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for auburn hair cut square at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Walter squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Walter offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- If told to back off, Walter does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Warren Ito

AH-R1275 · Age 44 · Lettering artist · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cutting one long name in brass without losing its final letter.” People around the bank steps know Warren by a small Thursday habit. Warren takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I work in metal lettering. Both names fit if we give up the decorative crest.”

**What Warren wants.** To fit both names clearly on the metal.

**Family.** [Nelson Mercer](../06/14-care-with-a-clock.md#ah-r0890) is Warren’s younger cousin. Warren calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Nelson leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Care with a clock*, Nelson says, “I am the newest visitor. I asked for training time with the slow client rather than the tidy example house.”

**People in this story.** [Peyton Waters](#ah-r1273). The person who used to carry newspapers finds the lettering artist space on the public proof.

**Personality.** Warren has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Warren's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Warren is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Warren grins on one side while the eyes stay serious when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A public donor asks to approve the wording on the bench. Warren asks the cleaner and the donor for separate views on the wording.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Warren keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- If the joke lands badly, Warren seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

AH-R1276 · Age 67 · Retired teacher · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember talking to a friend's bench after a hard week.” On Thursday, near Harbour Bakery, Vincent buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**What Vincent wants.** To make space for a friend's remembered name.

**Family.** [Clyde Starr](../08/05-the-boy-on-the-corner.md#ah-r1100) is Vincent’s younger relative by marriage. Vincent brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Clyde makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. **Appearance.** Vincent has iron-gray waves, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a washed violet jersey. It usually goes with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. When somebody else has the floor, Vincent listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- When a second witness disagrees, Vincent keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Opal Felix

AH-R1277 · Age 50 · Park maintenance clerk · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the donor who cared more about direction than inscription.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Opal by a small Sunday habit. Opal prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I saved the original donor letter. It specified a sea view and made no mention of who's looking.”

**What Opal wants.** To verify the original gift's view requirement.

**Family.** [Lou Reed](../07/05-the-release-address.md#ah-r0942) is Opal’s cousin raised in the same household. Their relatives kept a holiday fund in a boot that still had its mate. Even after the family acquired bank accounts, an envelope of change at supper meant somebody was planning a journey. Opal works out what it costs to bring everybody. Asked about *The release address*, Lou says, “I moved without giving him the new number because I needed one quiet Christmas.”

**People in this story.** [Vincent Albright](#ah-r1276). A saved donor letter helps a reluctant walker see how a name could earn its place on the bench.

**Personality.** Opal talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Opal's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a faded indigo overshirt and a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. Opal has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Opal looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A public donor asks to approve the wording on the bench. Opal asks the cleaner and the donor for separate views on the wording.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Opal tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Opal says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.
