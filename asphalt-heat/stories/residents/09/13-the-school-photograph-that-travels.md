## Casey Zhou

AH-R1321 · Age 16 · Secondary pupil · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a breakfast roll wrapped around a small encouraging note.” Friday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Casey checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I marked the bakery worker who fed me on the way in. Her name turns out to have two more syllables than we used.”

**What Casey wants.** To thank the breakfast worker under her full name.

**Family.** [Toby Miles](../08/01-reopening-at-six.md#ah-r1053) is Casey’s cousin raised in the same household. Casey brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Toby checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Reopening at six*, Toby says, “I close the shop. The last train leaves thirteen minutes before the last dryer stops.”

**People in this story.** [Quinn Avery](#ah-r1324). The pupil leaving the school and the classmate leaving for another coast choose copies for different reasons. Also in the story is [Aldo Cao](#ah-r1323) (classroom teacher).

**Personality.** Casey carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Casey has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Outside school, the familiar outfit is an olive canvas jacket with a small enamel bird on the lapel. In conversation Casey folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- The departing pupil leaves a teacher off the photograph list. Casey asks the pupil privately what the photograph list means to them.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Casey offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Casey offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Jasmine Moreau

AH-R1322 · Age 48 · School cleaner · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember unlocking a school library while the streetlights were on.” People around the corner outside Coastal Barber know Jasmine by a small Monday habit. Jasmine usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I am the cleaner who opened the library early. I kept my copy inside the first book she asked to borrow.”

**What Jasmine wants.** To keep the class picture in the child's favorite book.

**Family.** [Ren Ruiz](../10/10-the-kitchen-without-a-boss.md#ah-r1441) is Jasmine’s second cousin. Jasmine saves a place. Ren chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The kitchen without a boss*, Ren says, “I have wanted this job for years. I learned on day two that the deliveries call before the children wake.”

**People in this story.** [Marlowe Nunes](#ah-r1329). The library copy and the bicycle drawing are two ways a helper can receive thanks.

**Personality.** Jasmine has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Jasmine has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Jasmine looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A recipient wants to say thanks without being pictured. Jasmine offers a written reply or an ordinary visit without requiring a photograph in return.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Jasmine starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Jasmine says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Aldo Cao

AH-R1323 · Age 43 · Classroom teacher · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my name missing from a pupil's list of trusted adults.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Aldo by a small Friday habit. Aldo starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I taught the class and wasn't on the list. It took me a week to ask what I missed.”

**What Aldo wants.** To hear why the teacher didn't appear on the list.

**Family.** [Erin Parra](../05/04-one-clean-day.md#ah-r0639) is Aldo’s half-sibling. Aldo brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Erin checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *One clean day*, Erin says, “I looked for the boy who gave me his inhaler.”

**People in this story.** [Rae Montoya](#ah-r1327). Teacher and former bully are both surprised by a pupil's choice of recipient. Also in the story is [Casey Zhou](#ah-r1321) (departing pupil).

**Personality.** Aldo treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Aldo lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Aldo is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Aldo grins on one side while the eyes stay serious when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The departing pupil leaves a teacher off the photograph list. Aldo asks the pupil privately what the photograph list means to them.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Aldo asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Aldo says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Quinn Avery

AH-R1324 · Age 16 · Secondary pupil · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember folding a class photo into the bag I packed to leave.” On Monday, near Breakwater Way, Quinn stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I am moving to another coast and the picture is proof there was a time when my whole world fitted on two rows.”

**What Quinn wants.** To carry an ordinary school-day memory across the water.

**Family.** [Cedric Arrieta](../02/09-a-wall-of-birthdays.md#ah-r0236) is Quinn’s older cousin. Quinn sets the time to leave. Cedric picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *A wall of birthdays*, Cedric says, “I took down one sheet too fast and split a date.”

**People in this story.** [Casey Zhou](#ah-r1321). The pupil leaving the school and the classmate leaving for another coast choose copies for different reasons.

**Personality.** Quinn is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Quinn is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Quinn has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. Outside school, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Quinn presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- A recipient wants to say thanks without being pictured. Quinn offers a written reply or an ordinary visit without requiring a photograph in return.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Quinn takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Quinn agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Albert Hsu

**Family.** [Rafael Nunes](../01/01-the-blue-suit.md#ah-r0003) is Albert’s second cousin. Albert can stop the walk at any corner. Rafael chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. **People in this story.** [Ismael Wu](#ah-r1330). A bus number and a new home address help the final copy get where it belongs.

**Appearance.** Albert has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Look for short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a charcoal zip cardigan with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Albert squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- The departing pupil leaves a teacher off the photograph list. Albert asks the pupil privately what the photograph list means to them.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Albert gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Albert repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Drew Ferraro

AH-R1326 · Age 49 · Uniform supplier · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a family paying its school account on a summer morning.” People around Harbour Bakery know Drew by a small Thursday habit. Drew buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I supplied uniforms on credit until her mother's work picked up. I want her to know the debt was paid years ago.”

**What Drew wants.** To tell the family the uniform debt was fully settled.

**Family.** [Seth Massey](../10/11-an-ordinary-mirror.md#ah-r1459) is Drew’s first cousin once removed. Drew leaves the first voice message. Seth calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *An ordinary mirror*, Seth says, “I cut a little sample from the replacement glass.”

**People in this story.** [Benjamin Sun](#ah-r1328). Wet hair and a school blazer remind two helpers how differently they have known the same child.

**Personality.** Drew is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Drew asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Drew has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Drew rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A recipient wants to say thanks without being pictured. Drew offers a written reply or an ordinary visit without requiring a photograph in return.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Drew asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Drew accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Rae Montoya

AH-R1327 · Age 17 · Former classmate and student · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a joke I told in class that a friend took home for years.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Rae by a small Sunday habit. Rae pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I was the child who made her life miserable. She sent me a photograph with no message and I have to choose my own words.”

**What Rae wants.** To choose an honest reply to the former classmate.

**Family.** [Olive Kapoor](../02/04-the-second-dog.md#ah-r0182) is Rae’s older cousin. Rae knows which ones can be shared. Olive keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The second dog*, Olive says, “I trained that whistle to get my father indoors before dark.”

**People in this story.** [Aldo Cao](#ah-r1323). Teacher and former bully are both surprised by a pupil's choice of recipient.

**Personality.** Rae is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Rae has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Rae looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The departing pupil leaves a teacher off the photograph list. Rae asks the pupil privately what the photograph list means to them.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Rae stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Rae thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Benjamin Sun

AH-R1328 · Age 39 · Swimming coach · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember recognizing a former pupil by their perfect dive.” On Friday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Benjamin knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I taught her to swim at Pier Beach. I didn't recognize the quiet uniformed girl with her hair dry.”

**What Benjamin wants.** To visit the former pupil before departure.

**Family.** [Jamie Calloway](../02/12-the-sheltered-corner.md#ah-r0266) is Benjamin’s older cousin. Benjamin can always ask for five minutes alone. Jamie asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The sheltered corner*, Jamie says, “Our porch got quieter. I miss knowing school is out by the sudden noise.”

**People in this story.** [Drew Ferraro](#ah-r1326). Wet hair and a school blazer remind two helpers how differently they have known the same child.

**Personality.** Benjamin loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Benjamin remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Benjamin has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Benjamin draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A recipient wants to say thanks without being pictured. Benjamin offers a written reply or an ordinary visit without requiring a photograph in return.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Benjamin offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- When invited to tell the story later, Benjamin includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Ismael Wu

AH-R1330 · Age 46 · Postal clerk · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember addressing a letter to a father whose home had changed.” Saturday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Ismael takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**What Ismael wants.** To deliver a letter to the father's current home.

**Family.** [Jody Gao](../04/08-the-rescue-hut-mural.md#ah-r0526) is Ismael’s cousin raised in the same household. Ismael calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Jody leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The rescue hut mural*, Jody says, “I recognize the scar on her painted calf. My old teammate got it in the same meet I lost.”

**People in this story.** [Albert Hsu](#ah-r1325). A bus number and a new home address help the final copy get where it belongs.

**Personality.** Ismael finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and one cuff folded back twice. Ismael has one cheek that creases before the other, with short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. Ismael gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A recipient wants to say thanks without being pictured. Ismael offers a written reply or an ordinary visit without requiring a photograph in return.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Ismael asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
