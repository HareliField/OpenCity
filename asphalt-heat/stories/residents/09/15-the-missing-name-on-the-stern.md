## Jay Choi

AH-R1341 · Age 24 · Marina paint apprentice · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember mixing the right color before understanding the lettering.” Thursday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Jay can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I transposed two letters in my first language and put the brush down when I saw the owner seeing it.”

**What Jay wants.** To acknowledge the spelling error to its owners.

**Family.** [Leon Mehta](../08/11-the-hair-after-illness.md#ah-r1154) is Jay’s older cousin. Jay is free to hang up first. Leon makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The hair after illness*, Leon says, “I sold her the wig. I want to help her give it away without telling the next owner its history.”

**People in this story.** [Shay Gray](#ah-r1342) (skipper). The apprentice and the old skipper can admit the error without losing the family's preferred spelling.

**Personality.** Jay finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a boxy denim jacket and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Jay has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with short black hair swept back. Jay starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The apprentice notices the missing letter on the stern. Jay checks the intended spelling with the family before painting over anything.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Jay keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- When thanked for a shared success, Jay points to the person who noticed the original mistake.
