## Ada Murphy

AH-R1261 · Age 69 · Retired market trader · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember turning my exam desk toward the one classroom window.” Thursday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Ada asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I can do sums if I face a window. Facing the board takes me right back to the day I stopped going.”

**What Ada wants.** To take the exam facing the window.

**Family.** [Ellen Avila](../04/13-a-play-in-a-pocket.md#ah-r0575) is Ada’s first cousin once removed. Ada can always ask for five minutes alone. Ellen asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A play in a pocket*, Ellen says, “I came to supervise the children and got cast as a lost umbrella.”

**People in this story.** [Grace Reeves](#ah-r1263) (class tutor). The tutor can change the seat without making the oldest learner explain school in front of the class.

**Personality.** Ada treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Ada lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Ada has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Ada looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- An older learner freezes in front of the blackboard. Ada offers to turn the learner's chair without announcing the reason to the class.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Ada asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Ada says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Yvette Tsai

AH-R1262 · Age 64 · Retired line worker · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being asked to spell my surname for a form I couldn't read.” On Wednesday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Yvette notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I left school for a wage and spent my working life hiding my spelling. I want a result in my own name.”

**What Yvette wants.** To receive a result under the full name.

**Family.** [Carmen Daniels](../04/08-the-rescue-hut-mural.md#ah-r0528) is Yvette’s half-sibling. Yvette decides what stays off the paper. Carmen brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The rescue hut mural*, Carmen says, “I design signs for low vision. I asked regulars to describe the mural with their eyes closed.”

**People in this story.** [Rowan Davis](#ah-r1268). Two workers using different strategies for private reading try the same examination paper.

**Personality.** Yvette is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Yvette is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Yvette is easy to recognize by salt-and-pepper curls and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with one cuff folded back twice, Yvette draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An examiner offers to read a question aloud. Yvette asks the learner how they want the question presented before the examiner begins.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Yvette writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Yvette agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Grace Reeves

AH-R1263 · Age 33 · Adult education tutor · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my oldest student attending in shiny interview shoes.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Grace by a small Monday habit. Grace pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I am younger than everyone taking my class. I need to ask before calling anyone by their first name.”

**What Grace wants.** To let each mature pupil choose how to be addressed.

**Family.** [Priya Vance](../07/03-a-widow-bids-again.md#ah-r0921) is Grace’s older cousin. Grace is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Priya calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *A widow bids again*, Priya says, “I can outbid them without touching my everyday account.”

**People in this story.** [Ada Murphy](#ah-r1261) (adult student). The tutor can change the seat without making the oldest learner explain school in front of the class.

**Personality.** Grace tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Grace will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Grace has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. Grace listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An older learner freezes in front of the blackboard. Grace offers to turn the learner's chair without announcing the reason to the class.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Grace calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Grace repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Ramona Vargas

AH-R1264 · Age 34 · Bookshop clerk · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember smudging a practice paper after getting the answer right.” On Sunday, near the downtown fountain, Ramona keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I made a clean copy of the practice paper because my partner was embarrassed about crossing out every line.”

**What Ramona wants.** To give the partner a clean practice paper.

**Family.** [Teresa Lopez](../05/04-one-clean-day.md#ah-r0632) is Ramona’s half-sibling. Ramona can stop the walk at any corner. Teresa chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *One clean day*, Teresa says, “I came in uniform because changing clothes would make me late.”

**People in this story.** [Kai Mercer](#ah-r1267). A clean practice copy lets an adult decide when a translating relative can help.

**Personality.** Ramona is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Ramona asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Ramona is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it, Ramona touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An examiner offers to read a question aloud. Ramona asks the learner how they want the question presented before the examiner begins.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Ramona gets the people at the next doorway away from the smoke and asks a bystander to make the call.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Ramona accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Una Curtis

AH-R1265 · Age 57 · University lecturer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember teaching difficult poems while dreading my own sums.” On Monday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Una checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I teach at the university. This is my first attempt to learn bookkeeping and I am the slowest in class.”

**What Una wants.** To pass an unfamiliar subject without hiding the difficulty.

**Family.** [Layla Colón](../10/11-an-ordinary-mirror.md#ah-r1455) is Una’s stepsibling. Una offers the first invitation. Layla gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *An ordinary mirror*, Layla says, “I polished the silvering after the shop flooded.”

**People in this story.** [Maria Tanner](#ah-r1269). The lecturer and former bully both return to a room where their previous authority has no special force.

**Personality.** Una is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Una has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a watch on a cracked leather band. In conversation Una gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- An older learner freezes in front of the blackboard. Una offers to turn the learner's chair without announcing the reason to the class.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Una stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Una thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Miles Sato

AH-R1266 · Age 73 · Retired school cook · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember buying a fountain pen with one week's first wages.” People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Miles by a small Wednesday habit. Miles checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I brought the fountain pen I saved for finishing school. It's older than the examiner.”

**What Miles wants.** To write the exam with the long-saved pen.

**Family.** [Lane Clegg](../01/10-the-thirteenth-doughnut.md#ah-r0092) is Miles’s younger relative by marriage. Miles asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Lane chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The thirteenth doughnut*, Lane says, “I cut the gift into four and gave three pieces away.”

**People in this story.** [Pascal Boone](#ah-r1270). An old pen and an unconditional lunch make this examination day quite unlike school.

**Personality.** Miles loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Miles remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Miles has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with silver temples and a dark fringe. Miles presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An examiner offers to read a question aloud. Miles asks the learner how they want the question presented before the examiner begins.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Miles takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When invited to tell the story later, Miles includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Kai Mercer

AH-R1267 · Age 38 · Evening interpreter · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my aunt taking my dictionary away before I could answer for her.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Kai by a small Monday habit. Kai looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I can translate the questions but not the answer my aunt wants to give. She asked to sit the exam on her own.”

**What Kai wants.** To let the aunt give her own answers.

**Family.** [Chris Moss](../07/11-the-salon-on-strike.md#ah-r1004) is Kai’s second cousin. Kai knows which ones can be shared. Chris keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The salon on strike*, Chris says, “I make commission on the bottles. I brought my sales figures so the assistants can point to what their work earns.”

**People in this story.** [Ramona Vargas](#ah-r1264). A clean practice copy lets an adult decide when a translating relative can help.

**Personality.** Kai has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Kai is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Kai looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An older learner freezes in front of the blackboard. Kai offers to turn the learner's chair without announcing the reason to the class.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Kai offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When somebody trades on a title, Kai asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Rowan Davis

AH-R1268 · Age 47 · Bank cleaner · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember reading shop notices with the help of the lunchtime cleaner.” Thursday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Rowan notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I took the day off with a stomach ache. I don't want my boss at the bank to know I'm learning to read his notices.”

**What Rowan wants.** To read workplace notices without disclosing the class.

**Family.** [Fiona Mann](../02/10-the-bitter-orange.md#ah-r0249) is Rowan’s half-sibling. Rowan is free to hang up first. Fiona makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The bitter orange*, Fiona says, “I can't eat the memorial sandwich. I chose the paper it will be wrapped in.”

**People in this story.** [Yvette Tsai](#ah-r1262). Two workers using different strategies for private reading try the same examination paper.

**Personality.** Rowan finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a boxy denim jacket and a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. Rowan has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a center part and straight chestnut hair. Rowan rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An examiner offers to read a question aloud. Rowan asks the learner how they want the question presented before the examiner begins.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Rowan says what they know in their own name and refuses to treat a guess as something the child has to disprove.
- When thanked for a shared success, Rowan points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

**People in this story.** [Una Curtis](#ah-r1265). The lecturer and former bully both return to a room where their previous authority has no special force.

**Personality.** Maria keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Maria wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. Maria has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with silvery hair gathered at the nape. Maria listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- When somebody asks who said it first, Maria offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Pascal Boone

AH-R1270 · Age 54 · Community cook · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cutting sandwiches before anybody knew their marks.” People around Tideline Market know Pascal by a small Friday habit. Pascal picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I brought sandwiches for after, regardless of marks. We all know what a celebration conditional on passing feels like.”

**What Pascal wants.** To serve everyone the same lunch after the exam.

**Family.** [Ismael Gross](../05/12-level-with-the-window.md#ah-r0720) is Pascal’s cousin. Pascal can stop the walk at any corner. Ismael chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Level with the window*, Ismael says, “I kept my curtains closed for years. The final empty morning was when I missed strangers most.”

**People in this story.** [Miles Sato](#ah-r1266). An old pen and an unconditional lunch make this examination day quite unlike school.

**Personality.** Pascal always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Pascal is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a cream pullover and a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. Pascal has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with auburn hair cut square at the nape. Pascal draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An examiner offers to read a question aloud. Pascal asks the learner how they want the question presented before the examiner begins.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Pascal brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Pascal suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.
