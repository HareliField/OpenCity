# A borrowed name

**Paper & Pine Books.** A local writer at Paper & Pine discovers that a minor character shares a name and a childhood incident with a regular customer.

## Darcy Hardy

AH-R0511 · Age 36 · Novelist and typesetter · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being recognized in a story I never told.” Thursday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Darcy usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I gave the character a limp like mine. We got it in different places and we laugh differently about it.”

**What Darcy wants.** To explain the fictional limp without claiming the real life.

**Family.** [Albert Wilson](../10/15-the-last-light-at-the-harbor.md#ah-r1494) is Darcy’s half-sibling. On moving day a family cupboard wouldn't fit up the stairs. Their household spent the night dining off drawers stacked on the floor. There is still one drawer nobody wants to part with. Darcy will help carry furniture once the owner has said what it means to them. Asked about *The last light at the harbor*, Albert says, “I work nights at the hospital. Four cabin lights going out means it's time to put on my own work shoes.”

**People in this story.** [Lennon Price](#ah-r0516). They decide where a borrowed family joke deserves a credit in the book. Also in the story is [Lena Crane](#ah-r0513) (publisher).

**Personality.** Darcy has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Darcy can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Darcy has auburn hair cut square at the nape, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Darcy holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- An author is asked to identify the living inspiration. Darcy asks the person being represented whether they want to be identified.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Darcy walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- If a friend borrows money, Darcy agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Josephine Fields

AH-R0512 · Age 45 · Home baker · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my mother telling private jokes at public markets.” People around the bank steps know Josephine by a small Sunday habit. Josephine takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I thought my mother had told the author our story. She'd been telling half the city since before I was born.”

**What Josephine wants.** To ask the mother how far the family story traveled.

**Family.** [Shay Poole](../02/08-low-water-music.md#ah-r0228) is Josephine’s second cousin. Josephine calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Shay leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Low water music*, Shay says, “I sell postcards of a silent cove. I'm leaving a blank on the next edition for people to write what they heard.”

**People in this story.** [Adrian Dias](#ah-r0514). A mother's widely told story sounds different in the real person's preferred words.

**Personality.** Josephine takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Josephine can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Josephine is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a rust-colored canvas coat with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Josephine steadies a cup with both hands while listening when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks the real person to autograph the novel. Josephine gives the customer a graceful way to decline the autograph.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Josephine offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Josephine turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Lena Crane

AH-R0513 · Age 58 · Literary publisher · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a first printing with the dedication upside down.” Friday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Lena can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I published the book and missed the echo because I never go home to that street.”

**What Lena wants.** To introduce the publisher to the people behind the echo.

**Family.** [Jae Alder](../01/14-two-portions.md#ah-r0137) is Lena’s younger cousin. Lena brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Jae checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Two portions*, Jae says, “My mother missed parents' nights because she was doing this kind of care.”

**People in this story.** [Sage Rhodes](#ah-r0517). Hearing the customer teach makes the publisher rethink the old publicity copy. Also in the story is [Darcy Hardy](#ah-r0511) (novelist).

**Personality.** Lena admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Lena learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Lena has a wide brow and a narrow jaw, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Lena rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An author is asked to identify the living inspiration. Lena asks the person being represented whether they want to be identified.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Lena calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Lena describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Adrian Dias

AH-R0514 · Age 71 · Retired fisherman · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember disliking my nickname long before I learned its origin.” On Tuesday, near Maré Coffee, Adrian likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I hate the fictional nickname. I brought the author the one the children actually gave me.”

**What Adrian wants.** To let the author hear the nickname actually used.

**Family.** [Maeve Hamm](../02/13-the-quiet-verse.md#ah-r0278) is Adrian’s cousin raised in the same household. Adrian brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Maeve makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The quiet verse*, Maeve says, “I heard the fountain through that gap. I've lived beside it for years and never knew it had two rhythms.”

**People in this story.** [Josephine Fields](#ah-r0512). A mother's widely told story sounds different in the real person's preferred words.

**Personality.** Adrian has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a charcoal zip cardigan and a watch on a cracked leather band. Adrian has a round face with a pronounced chin, with tightly curled gray hair. Adrian gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks the real person to autograph the novel. Adrian gives the customer a graceful way to decline the autograph.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Adrian brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If accused of favoritism, Adrian explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Petra Jones

AH-R0515 · Age 47 · Bookseller · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember selling my last copy to the author's enemy.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Petra can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I sold out of copies until the real man stood in the doorway. Now I ask who wants to read and who wants to stare.”

**What Petra wants.** To keep the real resident from becoming a shop exhibit.

**Family.** [Miguel Grant](../05/01-salt-in-the-pockets.md#ah-r0609) is Petra’s stepsibling. Petra leaves the first voice message. Miguel calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Salt in the pockets*, Miguel says, “I didn't recognize my own coat clean. My kid found it by the spot where I'd held her crayons.”

**People in this story.** [Stevie Rose](#ah-r0520). A bookseller guarding a customer's privacy finds a host for a quieter discussion.

**Personality.** Petra reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Petra trusts most.

**Appearance.** Petra has short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a brown corduroy jacket. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Petra looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- An author is asked to identify the living inspiration. Petra asks the person being represented whether they want to be identified.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Petra checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If told to back off, Petra does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Lennon Price

AH-R0516 · Age 34 · Print workshop worker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a pencil joke stamped onto a toolbox lid.” Monday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Lennon notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I want royalties for the joke my granddad wrote on his toolbox. Mostly I want the author to get the punchline right.”

**What Lennon wants.** To get the grandfather's punchline printed accurately.

**Family.** [Milo Neal](../10/08-no-lifeguard-at-dusk.md#ah-r1429) is Lennon’s older cousin. Lennon chooses the familiar route. Milo can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *No lifeguard at dusk*, Milo says, “I am the sibling who asked her to stop swimming.”

**People in this story.** [Darcy Hardy](#ah-r0511). They decide where a borrowed family joke deserves a credit in the book.

**Personality.** Lennon has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Lennon's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Lennon has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a small enamel bird on the lapel. In conversation Lennon turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks the real person to autograph the novel. Lennon gives the customer a graceful way to decline the autograph.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Lennon asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- If the joke lands badly, Lennon seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Sage Rhodes

AH-R0517 · Age 50 · Reading tutor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a boisterous reader taking the shy pupil's part.” People around Breakwater Way know Sage by a small Tuesday habit. Sage stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I read it aloud in class. The customer came in and taught us what fiction leaves out.”

**What Sage wants.** To let the visitor show pupils what the novel omitted.

**Family.** [Eli Moreno](../06/04-the-saturday-swap.md#ah-r0788) is Sage’s second cousin. Sage brings the paper and a pen. Eli decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The Saturday swap*, Eli says, “I made the spreadsheet. Seeing every family obligation as a blank square made me ring each person instead.”

**People in this story.** [Lena Crane](#ah-r0513). Hearing the customer teach makes the publisher rethink the old publicity copy.

**Personality.** Sage listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Sage has a dark undercut with a longer wave on top, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a charcoal zip cardigan. It usually goes with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. When somebody else has the floor, Sage looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- An author is asked to identify the living inspiration. Sage asks the person being represented whether they want to be identified.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Sage agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- When a second witness disagrees, Sage keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Melanie Breen

AH-R0518 · Age 33 · Hotel receptionist · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a border official asking which signature felt more mine.” People around the entrance to the Wash House know Melanie by a small Sunday habit. Melanie stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “My own name was changed at immigration. I lent the writer the two spellings I live under.”

**What Melanie wants.** To keep both spellings in the writer's notes.

**Family.** [Eileen Reed](../05/08-the-misplaced-umbrella.md#ah-r0675) is Melanie’s parent’s cousin. Melanie calls about good news first. Eileen is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The misplaced umbrella*, Eileen says, “I can name the maker of the stripe from the faded blue.”

**People in this story.** [Valentina Villa](#ah-r0519). They compare a name that changed in public with a story a parent wants to correct in private.

**Personality.** Melanie talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Melanie's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Melanie has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, and a favorite piece of clothing, a mustard canvas overshirt. It usually goes with a watch on a cracked leather band. When somebody else has the floor, Melanie starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks the real person to autograph the novel. Melanie gives the customer a graceful way to decline the autograph.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Melanie alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Melanie says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Valentina Villa

AH-R0519 · Age 57 · Bus driver · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my daughter editing my telling of a bedtime story.” People around the corner outside Coastal Barber know Valentina by a small Wednesday habit. Valentina usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I was afraid my daughter would believe the ending. She brought me a pencil and asked for my version.”

**What Valentina wants.** To write a version the daughter knows came from home.

**Family.** [Parker Gao](../09/12-the-driving-lesson-fund.md#ah-r1312) is Valentina’s second cousin. Valentina remembers the birthdays. Parker asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The driving lesson fund*, Parker says, “I have the best reason and the worst eyesight.”

**People in this story.** [Melanie Breen](#ah-r0518). They compare a name that changed in public with a story a parent wants to correct in private.

**Personality.** Valentina prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Valentina is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve, Valentina leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An author is asked to identify the living inspiration. Valentina asks the person being represented whether they want to be identified.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Valentina takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Valentina clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Stevie Rose

AH-R0520 · Age 49 · Restaurant host · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cooking a dinner for two people who loathed each other's books.” On Saturday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Stevie checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I dislike the book and adore the man. I'm organizing a dinner where nobody has to choose.”

**What Stevie wants.** To host dinner without requiring agreement about the novel.

**Family.** [Marnie Phelps](../01/01-the-blue-suit.md#ah-r0004) is Stevie’s younger cousin. Stevie decides what stays off the paper. Marnie brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The blue suit*, Marnie says, “I signed the machine off yesterday because the test towel came back clean.”

**People in this story.** [Petra Jones](#ah-r0515). A bookseller guarding a customer's privacy finds a host for a quieter discussion.

**Personality.** Stevie never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Stevie can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Stevie is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in a striped cotton shirt with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread, Stevie presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks the real person to autograph the novel. Stevie gives the customer a graceful way to decline the autograph.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Stevie puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Stevie may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.
