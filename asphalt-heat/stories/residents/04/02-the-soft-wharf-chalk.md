## Ella Tran

AH-R0461 · Age 62 · Retired crew runner · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember chalk dust on a brother's borrowed collar.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Ella prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I wrote WAIT FOR ME there when my brother was posted away. I finally want it photographed.”

**What Ella wants.** To bring the brother a photograph of the old message.

**Family.** [Paula Tan](../06/06-home-with-stairs.md#ah-r0808) is Ella’s half-sibling. Ella chooses the familiar route. Paula can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Home with stairs*, Paula says, “I manage the building. The owner approved a handrail after I showed him how his own dad climbs.”

**People in this story.** [Sage Nielsen](#ah-r0470). The message at last reaches the person it asked to wait.

**Personality.** Ella is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Ella is easy to recognize by soft white hair brushed sideways and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Ella rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A contractor wants to scrub the chalk under the quay. Ella takes a photograph and asks whose permission is needed before the chalk is touched.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Ella alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Ella thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Evelyn Atwood

AH-R0462 · Age 57 · Port timekeeper · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember saving a whole gang's signatures after a shutdown.” Sunday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Evelyn prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I know the hook in that W from the attendance board. Our best tallyman never learned joined-up writing.”

**What Evelyn wants.** To identify the chalk writer from a shared shift.

**Family.** [Lydia Macrae](../03/03-the-widow-s-dividend.md#ah-r0329) is Evelyn’s younger cousin. Evelyn asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Lydia chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The widow's dividend*, Lydia says, “I can't forgive the first owner's eviction of my aunt.”

**People in this story.** [Dennis Shaw](#ah-r0468). One recognizes the chalk hand and the other recognizes its possible second language.

**Personality.** Evelyn loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Evelyn remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. Evelyn has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Evelyn looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A handwriting expert contradicts an old dock worker. Evelyn puts the expert's reading beside the dock worker's account and asks for the date of each.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Evelyn asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- When invited to tell the story later, Evelyn includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Jo Bloom

AH-R0463 · Age 37 · Delivery driver · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember calling a halt from the back of a van.” People around Breakwater Way know Jo by a small Thursday habit. Jo stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I thought it was a safety warning and stopped a delivery. I'm glad I did.”

**What Jo wants.** To reopen the platform after the wording is understood.

**Family.** [Joseph Moran](../10/03-the-gift-nobody-wants.md#ah-r1371) is Jo’s older cousin. Jo calls about good news first. Joseph is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The gift nobody wants*, Joseph says, “I made money selling buildings. I thought the finest gift I could give her was never having to ask a landlord for anything.”

**People in this story.** [Ruth Tran](#ah-r0464). The shift stopped for a warning gives a carpenter time to save the marked board.

**Personality.** Jo has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Jo has short copper curls, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, an olive canvas jacket. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Jo counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A contractor wants to scrub the chalk under the quay. Jo takes a photograph and asks whose permission is needed before the chalk is touched.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Jo accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- When somebody trades on a title, Jo asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Ruth Tran

AH-R0464 · Age 43 · Salvage joiner · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a salvage crate that smelled of figs.” On Sunday, near Tideline Market, Ruth picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I saved the piece of wood during repairs. It still smells of the crate my mother unpacked.”

**What Ruth wants.** To keep the marked wood in the family's possession.

**Family.** [Hugo Barron](../02/10-the-bitter-orange.md#ah-r0246) is Ruth’s younger cousin. Ruth chooses whether to use it. Hugo saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The bitter orange*, Hugo says, “I used to hide from her behind the fridge because she'd ask about my exams.”

**People in this story.** [Jo Bloom](#ah-r0463). The shift stopped for a warning gives a carpenter time to save the marked board.

**Personality.** Ruth finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Ruth is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Ruth raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A handwriting expert contradicts an old dock worker. Ruth puts the expert's reading beside the dock worker's account and asks for the date of each.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Ruth tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When thanked for a shared success, Ruth points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Sara Hall

AH-R0465 · Age 45 · Immigration document typist · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember writing a date four ways for one government office.” On Tuesday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Sara pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “The dates put my dad here months after he supposedly left town. I'd like a private answer.”

**What Sara wants.** To ask the father privately about the disputed date.

**Family.** [Kai Bruno](../10/13-one-last-dress-rehearsal.md#ah-r1480) is Sara’s stepsibling. Sara offers the first invitation. Kai gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *One last dress rehearsal*, Kai says, “I was the stage manager who almost canceled.”

**People in this story.** [Daniel Morris](#ah-r0466). The family researching dates meets the worker whose unfinished washing preserved them.

**Personality.** Sara keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Sara wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Sara has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for brown hair pinned away from the temples. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Sara looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- A contractor wants to scrub the chalk under the quay. Sara takes a photograph and asks whose permission is needed before the chalk is touched.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Sara keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Sara offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Daniel Morris

AH-R0466 · Age 68 · Retired platform cleaner · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember scrubbing a dock wall before I could read the words on it.” On Tuesday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Daniel notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I was supposed to wash the underside. My wrists gave out halfway through and spared the words.”

**What Daniel wants.** To meet the writer whose words accidentally survived.

**Family.** [Tariq Banks](../03/05-rent-in-a-biscuit-tin.md#ah-r0342) is Daniel’s younger relative by marriage. Daniel knows which ones can be shared. Tariq keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Rent in a biscuit tin*, Tariq says, “My envelope held money for driving lessons.”

**People in this story.** [Sara Hall](#ah-r0465). The family researching dates meets the worker whose unfinished washing preserved them. Also in the story is [Lara Hart](#ah-r0469) (conservation volunteer).

**Personality.** Daniel always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Daniel is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Daniel has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for iron-gray waves. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a watch on a cracked leather band. In conversation Daniel steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- A handwriting expert contradicts an old dock worker. Daniel puts the expert's reading beside the dock worker's account and asks for the date of each.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Daniel takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Daniel suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Anthony Ortiz

AH-R0467 · Age 54 · School tour volunteer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a child asking which bit of the port belonged to her.” On Thursday, near the market by Market Flats, Anthony often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I remember the sweet chalk we stole from school. I want the children who come on tours to draw their own messages.”

**What Anthony wants.** To let tour children make messages of their own.

**Family.** [Nina Fraser](../05/10-midnight-cinnamon.md#ah-r0699) is Anthony’s stepsibling. Anthony chooses an ordinary story to tell. Nina asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Midnight cinnamon*, Nina says, “I know the grinding marks on the sack. My aunt filled it before she retired inland.”

**People in this story.** [Lara Hart](#ah-r0469). They decide how to let children make new marks while safeguarding the old ones.

**Personality.** Anthony has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Anthony knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Anthony is easy to recognize by short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Anthony presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Anthony finds out what the destination actually looks like to the traveler and gives directions in those terms.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Anthony asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Dennis Shaw

AH-R0468 · Age 50 · Cargo interpreter · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember dreaming in two versions of the same sentence.” On Thursday, near Breakwater Way, Dennis stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I read the line in another language first. Both readings fit the two workers on that shift.”

**What Dennis wants.** To record the two plausible translations.

**Family.** [Peyton Ho](../08/11-the-hair-after-illness.md#ah-r1152) is Dennis’s stepsibling. Dennis checks the date twice. Peyton brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The hair after illness*, Peyton says, “I kept her old brush through treatment and got angry when she didn't want it back.”

**People in this story.** [Evelyn Atwood](#ah-r0462). One recognizes the chalk hand and the other recognizes its possible second language.

**Personality.** Dennis enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Dennis has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Dennis draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A handwriting expert contradicts an old dock worker. Dennis puts the expert's reading beside the dock worker's account and asks for the date of each.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Dennis takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Dennis puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Sage Nielsen

AH-R0470 · Age 71 · Retired harbor dispatcher · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a missed sailing followed by an unexpectedly good dinner.” Thursday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Sage knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I was the one they were waiting for. I missed the ship and built my whole life here.”

**What Sage wants.** To tell the writer what missing the ship made possible.

**Family.** [Katherine Mack](../08/09-the-third-bedroom.md#ah-r1137) is Sage’s first cousin once removed. Sage asks before bringing somebody along. Katherine can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The third bedroom*, Katherine says, “I was fostered on this street and hated sharing.”

**People in this story.** [Ella Tran](#ah-r0461). The message at last reaches the person it asked to wait.

**Personality.** Sage takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Sage can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Sage has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with salt-and-pepper curls. Sage gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A handwriting expert contradicts an old dock worker. Sage puts the expert's reading beside the dock worker's account and asks for the date of each.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Sage stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Sage turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.
