# A play in a pocket

[What the street remembers](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The downtown fountain park.** A youth group at the downtown fountain performs short plays for one audience member at a time.

## Rowan Jimenez

AH-R0571 · Age 17 · Student playwright · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember writing my first play on a bread wrapper.” On Saturday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Rowan watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I wrote mine for my sister who hasn't entered a theater since the panic attacks began.”

**What Rowan wants.** To perform the short play privately for the sister.

**Family.** [Ingrid Moss](../06/06-home-with-stairs.md#ah-r0805) is Rowan’s stepsibling. Rowan puts the kettle on. Ingrid chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *Home with stairs*, Ingrid says, “I deliver prescriptions here. I marked every landing where a person can set down a bag.”

**People in this story.** [Clara Ferreira](#ah-r0578) (drama tutor). They test whether a private scene works across a language difference.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Rowan is easy to recognize by a long plait tucked inside the collar and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a cropped bottle-green coat with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Rowan gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A young actor asks the listener to stop the pocket play. Rowan lets the actor stop without calling the audience's attention to the interruption.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Rowan offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Rowan listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Gerard Marquez

AH-R0572 · Age 19 · Youth theater student · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember forgetting my line when my uncle sat down.” On Saturday, near Studio Boulevard, Gerard waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I played a landlord until my actual landlord sat down. I delivered the same lines and held his gaze.”

**What Gerard wants.** To keep the scene honest with the landlord watching.

**Family.** [Wynn Montoya](../07/03-a-widow-bids-again.md#ah-r0930) is Gerard’s older relative by marriage. Gerard chooses an ordinary story to tell. Wynn asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *A widow bids again*, Wynn says, “I put in a deliberately low bid to find out who would laugh.”

**People in this story.** [Diego Mitchell](#ah-r0580). The pretend landlord is the listener who guesses where the missing character went.

**Personality.** Gerard is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Gerard loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Gerard has a center part and straight chestnut hair, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. When somebody else has the floor, Gerard leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor wants to record a private performance. Gerard asks the actor and the sole listener separately before allowing a recording.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Gerard offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- If somebody calls for applause, Gerard gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Hazel Fraser

AH-R0573 · Age 69 · Retired dentist · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember taking my traveling stool on my last day at work.” People around Harbour Bakery know Hazel by a small Friday habit. Hazel buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I brought my own folding stool because being offered a chair makes me feel ancient.”

**What Hazel wants.** To sit on the familiar stool for a complete performance.

**Family.** [Cara Holt](../06/05-the-first-straight-paycheck.md#ah-r0800) is Hazel’s cousin. Hazel checks the date twice. Cara brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The first straight paycheck*, Cara says, “I used to arrest him. We're both waiting at the same bank window and I want to let him greet me first.”

**People in this story.** [Hamish Baxter](#ah-r0574). A familiar stool gives the hesitant listener a place to pause the performance.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Hazel takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Hazel has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Look for silvery hair gathered at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with a train token tied to a zipper pull. In conversation Hazel listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A young actor asks the listener to stop the pocket play. Hazel lets the actor stop without calling the audience's attention to the interruption.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Hazel takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Hazel asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Hamish Baxter

AH-R0574 · Age 16 · Student performer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an audience member giving me time to remember a word.” People around Studio Boulevard know Hamish by a small Sunday habit. Hamish waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I had ten lines. The listener cried after the first two and asked if we could wait.”

**What Hamish wants.** To let the distressed listener choose when to resume.

**Family.** [Saul Rosner](../01/12-the-last-green.md#ah-r0111) is Hamish’s older cousin. Hamish asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Saul remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The last green*, Saul says, “I can name the color of each light with my eyes closed.”

**People in this story.** [Hazel Fraser](#ah-r0573). A familiar stool gives the hesitant listener a place to pause the performance.

**Personality.** Hamish can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Hamish is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Hamish counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor wants to record a private performance. Hamish asks the actor and the sole listener separately before allowing a recording.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Hamish asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Hamish asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Ellen Avila

AH-R0575 · Age 40 · Teaching aide · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a child recasting my entire classroom in two minutes.” People around Tideline Market know Ellen by a small Saturday habit. Ellen picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I came to supervise the children and got cast as a lost umbrella.”

**What Ellen wants.** To finish the scene in the role the children assigned.

**Family.** [Ada Murphy](../09/07-school-after-sixty.md#ah-r1261) is Ellen’s first cousin once removed. Ellen asks before bringing somebody along. Ada can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *School after sixty*, Ada says, “I can do sums if I face a window. Facing the board takes me right back to the day I stopped going.”

**People in this story.** [Greta Ray](#ah-r0579). The accidental cast member gets the camera put away for one private audience.

**Personality.** Ellen can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Ellen can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a striped cotton shirt and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Ellen has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with a blunt cut just below the ears. Ellen listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A young actor asks the listener to stop the pocket play. Ellen lets the actor stop without calling the audience's attention to the interruption.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Ellen starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Ellen suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Mia Taylor

AH-R0576 · Age 26 · Wardrobe trainee · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first bus pass worn through at the fold.” Thursday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Mia pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I used a fare ticket for the curtain. It came from the bus that brought me to my first audition.”

**What Mia wants.** To keep the ticket curtain from the first audition trip.

**Family.** [Duncan Hsu](../08/11-the-hair-after-illness.md#ah-r1155) is Mia’s cousin. Mia makes room at the table. Duncan brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The hair after illness*, Duncan says, “I used to say she looked brave. I asked what she'd like me to notice and she said her earrings.”

**People in this story.** [Lydia Avila](#ah-r0577). A bus ticket curtain gives the child's imitation of a parent a little stage.

**Personality.** Mia carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Mia has short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak, one cheek that creases before the other, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Mia holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor wants to record a private performance. Mia asks the actor and the sole listener separately before allowing a recording.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Mia takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Mia offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Lydia Avila

AH-R0577 · Age 14 · Young actor · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember ordering coffee in my dad's important telephone voice.” On Monday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Lydia checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I played my dad ordering coffee. He heard it and for once remembered to say please.”

**What Lydia wants.** To get the father to hear his café manners kindly.

**Family.** [Dolores Silva](../10/10-the-kitchen-without-a-boss.md#ah-r1449) is Lydia’s older relative by marriage. Lydia saves a place. Dolores chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The kitchen without a boss*, Dolores says, “I manage the other bakery. I lent a mixer and did not mention our own sale while I carried it through the door.”

**People in this story.** [Mia Taylor](#ah-r0576). A bus ticket curtain gives the child's imitation of a parent a little stage.

**Personality.** Lydia has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Lydia has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Lydia listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A young actor asks the listener to stop the pocket play. Lydia lets the actor stop without calling the audience's attention to the interruption.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Lydia brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Lydia says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Clara Ferreira

AH-R0578 · Age 44 · School drama tutor · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember making a stranger laugh without knowing the language.” On Monday, near Lighthouse Cove, Clara takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I want to perform to someone who speaks no words of my language and still make them laugh.”

**What Clara wants.** To make one listener laugh across a language difference.

**Family.** [Jonathan Grant](../09/05-a-clean-coat-for-court.md#ah-r1246) is Clara’s stepsibling. Clara picks the meeting place. Jonathan sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *A clean coat for court*, Jonathan says, “I found a cinema ticket in the pocket. The lender and I were at that showing the week the business closed.”

**People in this story.** [Rowan Jimenez](#ah-r0571) (playwright). They test whether a private scene works across a language difference.

**Personality.** Clara treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Clara lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a red windbreaker and a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. Clara has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Clara squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor wants to record a private performance. Clara asks the actor and the sole listener separately before allowing a recording.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Clara asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Clara says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Greta Ray

AH-R0579 · Age 30 · Freelance videographer · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember recording a performance with the sound turned off by mistake.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Greta by a small Tuesday habit. Greta knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I was filming until I understood the invitation was for one person. I put the phone in my hat.”

**What Greta wants.** To give one audience member a genuinely private performance.

**Family.** [Emery Holland](../03/08-the-painted-footprint.md#ah-r0375) is Greta’s older cousin. Greta knows which ones can be shared. Emery keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The painted footprint*, Emery says, “I covered it during repairs and felt awful.”

**People in this story.** [Ellen Avila](#ah-r0575). The accidental cast member gets the camera put away for one private audience.

**Personality.** Greta is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Greta is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Greta has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Greta holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A young actor asks the listener to stop the pocket play. Greta lets the actor stop without calling the audience's attention to the interruption.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Greta asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Greta agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Diego Mitchell

AH-R0580 · Age 74 · Retired public librarian · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the missing brother in our first classroom play.” People around the brick passage off the shop row know Diego by a small Sunday habit. Diego takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I did the same scene six times and only the oldest listener guessed where the missing brother had gone.”

**What Diego wants.** To meet the listener who understood the ending.

**Family.** [Patrick Osborne](../08/09-the-third-bedroom.md#ah-r1134) is Diego’s first cousin once removed. Diego makes the call. Patrick is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The third bedroom*, Patrick says, “I am the lodger who offered to leave. I need six weeks and a guarantor, which nobody remembered to ask about.”

**People in this story.** [Gerard Marquez](#ah-r0572). The pretend landlord is the listener who guesses where the missing character went.

**Personality.** Diego tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Diego will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a charcoal zip cardigan and a watch on a cracked leather band. Diego has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with salt-and-pepper curls. Diego holds eye contact until the other person has finished during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor wants to record a private performance. Diego asks the actor and the sole listener separately before allowing a recording.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Diego asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Diego repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.
