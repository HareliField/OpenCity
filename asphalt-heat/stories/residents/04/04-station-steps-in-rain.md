# Station steps in rain

[What the street remembers](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Old Town police station.** The Old Town station invites neighbors to sit in on an open afternoon. Heavy rain sends everyone to the same shallow portico.

## Vivian Acosta

AH-R0481 · Age 39 · Retail worker · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an officer sharing my dripping shop awning.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Vivian by a small Wednesday habit. Vivian checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I came to complain about my street and held an umbrella over the officer I was going to name.”

**What Vivian wants.** To deliver the complaint to the officer who shared the umbrella.

**Family.** [Duncan Stahl](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1301) is Vivian’s half-sibling. Vivian makes the call. Duncan is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Duncan says, “I agreed to show my nails. Nobody asked whether my accident was part of the product.”

**People in this story.** [Adrian Bhandari](#ah-r0488). They arrange two private ways to finish a complaint after the storm.

**Personality.** Vivian enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Vivian is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Vivian is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a narrow-striped sweater with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Vivian starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Rain blurs the names on the petition. Vivian records the petitioners who are present and asks them how to recover the others.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Vivian brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Vivian keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Marlowe Nolasco

AH-R0482 · Age 45 · Estate porter · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember passing a raincoat to a stranger in a waiting room.” On Friday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Marlowe can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “A soaked man mistook my coat for a uniform. I listened long enough to find the person he needed.”

**What Marlowe wants.** To take the wet visitor to someone who can help.

**Family.** [Annette Levin](../06/01-the-upstairs-lease.md#ah-r0758) is Marlowe’s second cousin. Marlowe leaves the first voice message. Annette calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The upstairs lease*, Annette says, “I like our flat less than I like the neighbors who know my son's sounds through the ceiling.”

**People in this story.** [Quinn Navarro](#ah-r0484). Someone mistaken for staff can still hold the sheltered entrance clear.

**Personality.** Marlowe is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Marlowe is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf, Marlowe listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- An officer asks whether the wet event should still count as a hearing. Marlowe asks who missed the chance to speak before calling the hearing complete.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Marlowe offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Marlowe helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Nora Nolasco

AH-R0483 · Age 56 · Tenants' association secretary · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my own name becoming illegible in a school register.” On Tuesday, near the market by Market Flats, Nora often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I brought a petition and the names ran together. My opponents helped me remember who had signed.”

**What Nora wants.** To reconstruct the names with both sides present.

**Family.** [Ivy Knight](../03/09-the-upstairs-piano.md#ah-r0384) is Nora’s second cousin. Nora chooses whether to use it. Ivy saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The upstairs piano*, Ivy says, “I'm deaf in one ear. The landing is the only place in this building where I hear the bass clearly.”

**People in this story.** [Lucia Webb](#ah-r0487). The petitions reach signers in more than one language. Also in the story is [Cecilia Aranda](#ah-r0490) (station host).

**Personality.** Nora finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Nora has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. When somebody else has the floor, Nora squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- Rain blurs the names on the petition. Nora records the petitioners who are present and asks them how to recover the others.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Nora asks the cashier quietly which item can be set aside without making the customer explain their finances to the line.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Nora waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Quinn Navarro

AH-R0484 · Age 33 · Access volunteer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an accessible door nobody ever unlocked.” On Thursday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Quinn checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I kept the wheelchair space clear and missed the speech my daughter wrote.”

**What Quinn wants.** To keep the sheltered entrance accessible.

**Family.** [Philip Moreau](../10/14-the-last-home-visit.md#ah-r1483) is Quinn’s second cousin. Quinn brings the paper and a pen. Philip decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The last home visit*, Philip says, “I am the successor. I would like to make one mistake that hasn't already been made by someone everyone adores.”

**People in this story.** [Marlowe Nolasco](#ah-r0482). Someone mistaken for staff can still hold the sheltered entrance clear.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Quinn has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Quinn leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- An officer asks whether the wet event should still count as a hearing. Quinn asks who missed the chance to speak before calling the hearing complete.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Quinn stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Quinn listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Jasmine Laurent

AH-R0485 · Age 42 · Former shoplifter and mechanic · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember returning a stolen lunch box full of clean spoons.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Jasmine by a small Saturday habit. Jasmine starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I used to dread these steps. This time I came to collect my own repaired camera.”

**What Jasmine wants.** To collect the old camera and go home openly.

**Family.** [Jorge Crane](../07/15-the-day-sailor-s-divorce.md#ah-r1044) is Jasmine’s older relative by marriage. Jasmine calls about good news first. Jorge is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The day sailor's divorce*, Jorge says, “I bought them the sail when they married. I told them dividing it into two napkins was an option.”

**People in this story.** [Cecilia Aranda](#ah-r0490). The new side-door invitation lets a nervous visitor come back for property without a crowd.

**Personality.** Jasmine is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Jasmine loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a plum-colored cardigan and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Jasmine has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, with a loose dark braid. Jasmine counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Rain blurs the names on the petition. Jasmine records the petitioners who are present and asks them how to recover the others.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Jasmine finds out what the destination actually looks like to the traveler and gives directions in those terms.
- If somebody calls for applause, Jasmine gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Frank Moran

AH-R0486 · Age 31 · Refreshments worker · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember pouring tea for somebody who had searched our flat.” On Friday, near the downtown fountain, Frank keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I was handing out biscuits and learned that offering a uniformed stranger food takes nerve on both sides.”

**What Frank wants.** To offer both sides refreshments on the same terms.

**Family.** [Ansel Perry](../06/05-the-first-straight-paycheck.md#ah-r0795) is Frank’s older cousin. Frank remembers the birthdays. Ansel asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The first straight paycheck*, Ansel says, “My shop window went through in one of our old rows.”

**People in this story.** [Gloria Sloan](#ah-r0489). A warm drink at the gathering becomes a reason to ask before publishing the photograph.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Frank takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a dark green knitted vest and a watch on a cracked leather band. Frank has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Frank touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An officer asks whether the wet event should still count as a hearing. Frank asks who missed the chance to speak before calling the hearing complete.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Frank keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Frank asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Lucia Webb

AH-R0487 · Age 39 · Interpreter · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember different languages using the same word for thunder.” People around Studio Boulevard know Lucia by a small Sunday habit. Lucia waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I came to interpret for one family and ended up translating an argument about a wet dog.”

**What Lucia wants.** To let each family hear the wet-dog story correctly.

**Family.** [Esme Valejo](../01/15-the-empty-berth.md#ah-r0148) is Lucia’s stepsibling. Lucia brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Esme checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The empty berth*, Esme says, “I put a price on a storm that might come once in fifty years.”

**People in this story.** [Nora Nolasco](#ah-r0483). The petitions reach signers in more than one language.

**Personality.** Lucia can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Lucia is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Lucia touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Rain blurs the names on the petition. Lucia records the petitioners who are present and asks them how to recover the others.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Lucia asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Lucia asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Gloria Sloan

AH-R0489 · Age 47 · Community photographer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember taking a shop picture reflected in a puddle.” On Wednesday, near Breakwater Way, Gloria stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I took the photo everyone liked. The man in the front doesn't want his face online and I'm collecting the copies.”

**What Gloria wants.** To withdraw unauthorized copies of the open-day picture.

**Family.** [Sofia Brady](../10/07-an-empty-dedication.md#ah-r1413) is Gloria’s cousin raised in the same household. Gloria chooses an ordinary story to tell. Sofia asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *An empty dedication*, Sofia says, “I held the author's baby every Thursday. I was paid and I'm proud of the work that payment bought.”

**People in this story.** [Frank Moran](#ah-r0486). A warm drink at the gathering becomes a reason to ask before publishing the photograph.

**Personality.** Gloria carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Gloria has short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, an old plum windbreaker. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Gloria counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- Rain blurs the names on the petition. Gloria records the petitioners who are present and asks them how to recover the others.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Gloria asks what the friend actually said last time and checks whether anybody is expecting them at home.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Gloria offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Cecilia Aranda

AH-R0490 · Age 51 · Station premises custodian · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the long walk around to the only open side door.” People around Lighthouse Cove know Cecilia by a small Thursday habit. Cecilia takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I opened the side door too late. I owe the person who left before we found a dry route a second invitation.”

**What Cecilia wants.** To invite the visitor back through the accessible entrance.

**Family.** [Carlo Sharp](../06/06-home-with-stairs.md#ah-r0809) is Cecilia’s cousin raised in the same household. Cecilia checks the date twice. Carlo brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Home with stairs*, Carlo says, “I clean the stairs after midnight. I cleared a place for a resting stool on the twenty-eighth.”

**People in this story.** [Jasmine Laurent](#ah-r0485). The new side-door invitation lets a nervous visitor come back for property without a crowd. Also in the story is [Nora Nolasco](#ah-r0483) (petition organizer).

**Personality.** Cecilia has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Cecilia has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Cecilia draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- An officer asks whether the wet event should still count as a hearing. Cecilia asks who missed the chance to speak before calling the hearing complete.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Cecilia tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Cecilia says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.
