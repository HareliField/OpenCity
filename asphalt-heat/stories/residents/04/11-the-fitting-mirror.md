## Ren Cullen

AH-R0551 · Age 55 · Salon customer and architect · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember seeing gray at my temple in a train window.” Friday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Ren stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I like the tailor's light better. I saw the silver in my hair there and canceled my dye appointment.”

**What Ren wants.** To leave the new silver hair visible.

**Family.** [Charles Mack](../08/05-the-boy-on-the-corner.md#ah-r1091) is Ren’s first cousin once removed. Ren checks the date twice. Charles brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The boy on the corner*, Charles says, “I told my brother I worked in deliveries. He drew me in a uniform I have never owned.”

**People in this story.** [Darcy Lopez](#ah-r0555). The mirror shows one person's silver hair and another's remembered fingerprint.

**Personality.** Ren gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Ren privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** After the last appointment, look for an old plum windbreaker and a thin silver ring worn on a cord. Ren has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Ren starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A client asks to sit outside the shared mirror's view. Ren puts the person's chair outside the reflection and lets them choose when to look.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Ren keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Ren offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Merritt Levi

AH-R0552 · Age 48 · Shop fitter · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember receiving the final receipt in a torn pink envelope.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Merritt by a small Wednesday habit. Merritt knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I paid for the mirror in monthly installments and still have the last little receipt.”

**What Merritt wants.** To keep the last mirror receipt with its owner.

**Family.** [Jonathan Lund](../10/01-a-closing-hour.md#ah-r1353) is Merritt’s cousin raised in the same household. Merritt brings a spare shopping bag. Jonathan chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A closing hour*, Jonathan says, “I can run the place alone and the owner knows it.”

**People in this story.** [Daryl Maciel](#ah-r0558). The last purchase receipt stays in the same envelope as the new wheel bearing.

**Personality.** Merritt uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Merritt well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Merritt has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Merritt looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The mirror owner wants it back during a fitting. Merritt asks the two shops for enough time to finish the fitting in privacy.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Merritt draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- After a family quarrel, Merritt brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Avery Lai

AH-R0553 · Age 38 · Job-seeking events clerk · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember practicing a greeting in a dark shopfront.” On Friday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Avery watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I used it to practice asking for my old job back. A customer heard and offered me a different one.”

**What Avery wants.** To interview for the unexpected job.

**Family.** [Caroline Diaz](../09/09-the-returned-daughter.md#ah-r1282) is Avery’s aunt by marriage. Avery asks before bringing somebody along. Caroline can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The returned daughter*, Caroline says, “I raised her after she left. I want her to know going back doesn't cancel the years she calls me Mum.”

**People in this story.** [Marisol Lane](#ah-r0559). A new interview and an old wedding both turn up unexpectedly in reflections.

**Personality.** Avery feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Avery has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Avery holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A client asks to sit outside the shared mirror's view. Avery puts the person's chair outside the reflection and lets them choose when to look.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Avery asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Avery lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

**Under pressure**

Asked about *Three minutes at the bridge*, Santiago says, “I used to travel in complete silence. I asked the others to let me have one quiet morning a week.”

**Appearance.** Nadia has close-cropped dark curls, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Nadia looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Nadia offers one about their own embarrassment instead.
