# The porch auction

[What the street remembers](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**The western residential streets.** A household sells its porch chairs before moving into a smaller flat. Each chair brings a different neighbor to the gate.

## Grace Palmer

AH-R0531 · Age 63 · Retired maternity clerk · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember gripping wicker through a late summer contraction.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Grace by a small Sunday habit. Grace can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I want the wicker one that held me through my first labor pains.”

**What Grace wants.** To get the wicker chair back for a family birth.

**Family.** [Jay Owens](../07/04-a-civilian-apology.md#ah-r0940) is Grace’s stepsibling. Grace asks before bringing somebody along. Jay can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A civilian apology*, Jay says, “I arranged a private apology first. The woman said the damage was public and asked me to open the doors.”

**People in this story.** [Kathleen Garza](#ah-r0534). Both want chairs for memories that wouldn't appear in the auction catalogue.

**Personality.** Grace carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Grace has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Look for silvery hair gathered at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Grace looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- Two visitors bid against each other for the porch chair. Grace asks whether both people want the object itself or a particular memory of it.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Grace asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Grace offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Talia Bloom

AH-R0532 · Age 46 · Dog walker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my grandmother believing a remarkably innocent dog.” On Saturday, near the downtown fountain, Talia keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I broke a rung at sixteen and blamed the dog. I've brought a new one and an apology.”

**What Talia wants.** To replace the broken rung and confess the dog's innocence.

**Family.** [Marco Bennett](../08/06-two-names-on-the-chart.md#ah-r1106) is Talia’s second cousin. Talia makes room at the table. Marco brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Two names on the chart*, Marco says, “I make labels that stay readable after washing.”

**People in this story.** [Ruben Choi](#ah-r0536). A childhood repair and a new small chair make different use of the same rung.

**Personality.** Talia has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Talia is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a narrow-striped sweater with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Talia turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A repair reveals a family name under the cushion. Talia invites the family to see the full name before the repair is closed.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Talia waits at the lit stop with them and checks whether the next service is one they can actually board.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Talia says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Catherine Leung

AH-R0533 · Age 68 · Retired speech coach · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember getting married in a chair with one short leg.” People around the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio know Catherine by a small Wednesday habit. Catherine notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I proposed to my second husband in the low chair. He bought the pair without telling me.”

**What Catherine wants.** To keep the low pair together in the new home.

**Family.** [Rowan Katz](../05/03-the-trust-with-a-typo.md#ah-r0621) is Catherine’s second cousin. Catherine saves a place. Rowan chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The trust with a typo*, Rowan says, “I chose that nickname to survive school. Seeing it on bank stationery made me feel sixteen again.”

**People in this story.** [Logan Walker](#ah-r0538). They compare the hidden repair to the actual curve left by a seated couple.

**Personality.** Catherine treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Catherine lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Catherine is easy to recognize by soft white hair brushed sideways and full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a small enamel bird on the lapel, Catherine presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two visitors bid against each other for the porch chair. Catherine asks whether both people want the object itself or a particular memory of it.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Catherine alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Catherine says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Kathleen Garza

AH-R0534 · Age 52 · Shop mechanic · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember padding an envelope with the lining of a cushion.” On Sunday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Kathleen takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I left my weekly pay in the cushion years ago. Finding the envelope would mean more than the money.”

**What Kathleen wants.** To find the old pay envelope even if it is empty.

**Family.** [Dale Pierce](../08/12-the-high-road-home.md#ah-r1170) is Kathleen’s younger cousin. Kathleen picks the meeting place. Dale sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The high road home*, Dale says, “I waited at the far end with coffee. I am proud that the first cup went cold and they still tried again.”

**People in this story.** [Grace Palmer](#ah-r0531). Both want chairs for memories that wouldn't appear in the auction catalogue.

**Personality.** Kathleen is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Kathleen is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a red windbreaker and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Kathleen has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Kathleen gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A repair reveals a family name under the cushion. Kathleen invites the family to see the full name before the repair is closed.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Kathleen takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Kathleen agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Laurence Salcedo

AH-R0535 · Age 49 · Physiotherapy patient and editor · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first porch I could reach after surgery.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Laurence by a small Monday habit. Laurence likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I needed somewhere to sit on my way home after surgery. They left that chair outside for a whole season.”

**What Laurence wants.** To leave a resting chair outside for the next recovering walker.

**Family.** [Cameron Kelly](../08/03-the-guarantee.md#ah-r1080) is Laurence’s half-sibling. Laurence keeps the photographs. Cameron knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The guarantee*, Cameron says, “I want to downsize while the house is still ours to choose.”

**People in this story.** [Grey Lopez](#ah-r0537). A resting place proves more useful than an empty porch someone always envied.

**Personality.** Laurence tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Laurence will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Laurence has short copper curls, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Laurence gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- Two visitors bid against each other for the porch chair. Laurence asks whether both people want the object itself or a particular memory of it.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Laurence keeps the neighbor's place and finds out whether the stranger needs a different kind of help.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Laurence repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Ruben Choi

AH-R0536 · Age 47 · Toy maker · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember building doll chairs from an old school desk.” Saturday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Ruben often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I wanted the wood for toys. The owner asked me to make one chair small enough for her new kitchen.”

**What Ruben wants.** To build one small kitchen chair from the old stock.

**Family.** [Grey Clement](../06/15-winter-afloat.md#ah-r0897) is Ruben’s half-sibling. Ruben makes the call. Grey is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Winter afloat*, Grey says, “I found an old lease with a shore room included.”

**People in this story.** [Talia Bloom](#ah-r0532). A childhood repair and a new small chair make different use of the same rung. Also in the story is [Logan Walker](#ah-r0538) (cushion restorer).

**Personality.** Ruben is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Ruben asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Ruben has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Ruben listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A repair reveals a family name under the cushion. Ruben invites the family to see the full name before the repair is closed.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Ruben offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Ruben accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Grey Lopez

AH-R0537 · Age 35 · Bank cashier · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember counting lit porches from the back of a moving van.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the steps at Pier Beach, where Grey watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I always envied their porch. Buying a chair taught me I liked the people who sat on it more.”

**What Grey wants.** To get to know the neighbors after they move.

**Family.** [Patricia Lang](../03/11-the-widow-s-razor.md#ah-r0406) is Grey’s stepsibling. Grey chooses whether to use it. Patricia saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The widow's razor*, Patricia says, “I want to polish just the bit her thumb wore bright.”

**People in this story.** [Laurence Salcedo](#ah-r0535). A resting place proves more useful than an empty porch someone always envied.

**Personality.** Grey is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Grey has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a plum-colored cardigan. It usually goes with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. When somebody else has the floor, Grey looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- Two visitors bid against each other for the porch chair. Grey asks whether both people want the object itself or a particular memory of it.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Grey offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Grey thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Logan Walker

AH-R0538 · Age 50 · Costume repairer · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a cushion stitched with a curve from my thumb.” Sunday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Logan looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I took the torn cushion because I sewed the hidden patch for pocket money.”

**What Logan wants.** To save the torn cushion's hidden patch.

**Family.** [Alex Yu](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1234) is Logan’s half-sibling. Logan chooses the familiar route. Alex can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Alex says, “I heard my colleague say a silly phrase that calmed the caller.”

**People in this story.** [Catherine Leung](#ah-r0533). They compare the hidden repair to the actual curve left by a seated couple. Also in the story is [Ruben Choi](#ah-r0536) (chair buyer).

**Personality.** Logan loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Logan remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. Logan has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with close-cropped dark curls. Logan gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A repair reveals a family name under the cushion. Logan invites the family to see the full name before the repair is closed.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Logan offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When invited to tell the story later, Logan includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Tariq Wells

AH-R0539 · Age 39 · Estate auctioneer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an aunt who always bought her own chairs back.” On Monday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Tariq stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I came to haggle and found my aunt's name underneath the most expensive chair.”

**What Tariq wants.** To identify the aunt who marked the chair.

**Family.** [Kai Chen](../06/04-the-saturday-swap.md#ah-r0789) is Tariq’s cousin. Tariq brings the paper and a pen. Kai decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The Saturday swap*, Kai says, “I want my old partner to cover for me and we've barely spoken since I got the promotion we both wanted.”

**People in this story.** [Miguel Tsai](#ah-r0540). An aunt's name on the furniture leads to a Sunday invitation at the smaller flat.

**Personality.** Tariq has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Tariq is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a washed violet jersey with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Tariq counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two visitors bid against each other for the porch chair. Tariq asks whether both people want the object itself or a particular memory of it.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Tariq starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- When somebody trades on a title, Tariq asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Miguel Tsai

AH-R0540 · Age 44 · Catering driver · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first Sunday invitation addressed to my new flat.” On Monday, near Maré Coffee, Miguel likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I left without a chair. I got the new address and a Sunday invitation instead.”

**What Miguel wants.** To keep the Sunday invitation at the new address.

**Family.** [Marlowe Nord](../06/03-money-after-marriage.md#ah-r0772) is Miguel’s stepsibling. Miguel calls about good news first. Marlowe is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Money after marriage*, Marlowe says, “I own a small share from the year we couldn't pay wages.”

**People in this story.** [Tariq Wells](#ah-r0539). An aunt's name on the furniture leads to a Sunday invitation at the smaller flat.

**Personality.** Miguel finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Miguel is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Miguel gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A repair reveals a family name under the cushion. Miguel invites the family to see the full name before the repair is closed.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Miguel suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- When thanked for a shared success, Miguel points to the person who noticed the original mistake.
