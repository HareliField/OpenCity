## Milo Saito

AH-R0541 · Age 42 · Soup cook · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my dad tasting burnt toast and saying it was perfect.” On Tuesday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Milo prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I taste the stock first because Dad lost his sense of smell before he told us.”

**What Milo wants.** To share a tasting with the father whose smell is fading.

**Family.** [Walter Colón](../08/09-the-third-bedroom.md#ah-r1139) is Milo’s cousin raised in the same household. Milo brings a spare shopping bag. Walter chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The third bedroom*, Walter says, “I am one boy's teacher. I met the brother and discovered which drawings I'd been praising the wrong child for.”

**People in this story.** [Rae Cole](#ah-r0542). The family rebuilding an exact recipe needs the first cook's taste of the stock.

**Personality.** Milo enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Milo is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Milo touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The cook asks for an exact recipe. Milo records what went into this particular pot and when the cooks tasted it.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Milo stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Milo puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Rae Cole

AH-R0542 · Age 52 · Family caregiver · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember keeping a partner's lunch warm inside my coat.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Rae knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I need tonight's exact recipe because this was the first full meal my wife kept down in weeks.”

**What Rae wants.** To make one meal the wife can tolerate.

**Family.** [Gregory Lowe](../10/08-no-lifeguard-at-dusk.md#ah-r1428) is Rae’s cousin. Rae asks before bringing somebody along. Gregory can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *No lifeguard at dusk*, Gregory says, “I sold the swimmer a warm coat on credit. She leaves it in my stall until the bus touches the corner.”

**People in this story.** [Milo Saito](#ah-r0541). The family rebuilding an exact recipe needs the first cook's taste of the stock.

**Personality.** Rae has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Rae can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Rae has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Rae listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- Someone in the soup line cannot eat one ingredient. Rae asks the cook to identify the ingredients before offering a separate bowl.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Rae separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- If a friend borrows money, Rae agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Reuben Grant

AH-R0543 · Age 46 · Market gardener · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my worst crop becoming the market cook's favorite.” On Sunday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Reuben watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I donated the fennel nobody bought. In the soup people asked me to grow extra.”

**What Reuben wants.** To get a standing fennel order from the kitchen.

**Family.** [Ren Alston](../03/05-rent-in-a-biscuit-tin.md#ah-r0348) is Reuben’s stepsibling. Reuben asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Ren remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *Rent in a biscuit tin*, Ren says, “I owed her one month and found she'd put in a little every week herself.”

**People in this story.** [Noel Wade](#ah-r0546). A fennel donation changes what the late cook has left to take home. Also in the story is [Alex Scott](#ah-r0545) (soup ingredient recorder).

**Personality.** Reuben takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Reuben can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Reuben has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with short copper curls. Reuben listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The cook asks for an exact recipe. Reuben records what went into this particular pot and when the cooks tasted it.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Reuben sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Reuben turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Ken Calloway

AH-R0544 · Age 34 · Catering assistant · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember counting the family change into a cabbage basket.” On Thursday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Ken pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I was ashamed my family lived on leftovers. The visiting cook asked if she could come to our house.”

**What Ken wants.** To invite the visiting cook for a family supper.

**Family.** [Ivy Burke](../08/09-the-third-bedroom.md#ah-r1133) is Ken’s cousin raised in the same household. Ken saves a place. Ivy chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The third bedroom*, Ivy says, “I want to stay their carer. Our spare room pays the mortgage with a lodger in it.”

**People in this story.** [Ashley Lang](#ah-r0550). A leftover meal brings the visiting cook and a family language into one kitchen.

**Personality.** Ken admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Ken learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Ken has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. In conversation Ken holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- Someone in the soup line cannot eat one ingredient. Ken asks the cook to identify the ingredients before offering a separate bowl.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Ken takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Ken describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Alex Scott

AH-R0545 · Age 43 · Dietary clerk · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a cheese rind kept for a difficult Friday.” On Thursday, near Studio Boulevard, Alex waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I put a rind in that was meant for my own dinner. Now I have to explain why I'm bringing soup again.”

**What Alex wants.** To bring the next rind without giving up dinner.

**Family.** [Agnes Osei](../01/15-the-empty-berth.md#ah-r0145) is Alex’s cousin. Alex gets to change the plan. Agnes offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The empty berth*, Agnes says, “My juniors need a place to learn how to step off a dock as much as they need water.”

**People in this story.** [Alvin Kapoor](#ah-r0548). The ingredients record gives the celery-hater a real say in tomorrow's batch. Also in the story is [Reuben Grant](#ah-r0543) (produce donor).

**Personality.** Alex has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Alex has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. In conversation Alex looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- The cook asks for an exact recipe. Alex records what went into this particular pot and when the cooks tasted it.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Alex asks where they stood and what they could hear, leaving the rest open instead of filling it with guesses.
- If accused of favoritism, Alex explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Noel Wade

AH-R0546 · Age 29 · Night baker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a diner turning pale over an unlisted ingredient.” Friday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Noel buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I count what goes in because somebody eating with us has allergies. Guessing is a luxury I can't afford.”

**What Noel wants.** To list every ingredient a diner might react to.

**Family.** [Jody Dunn](../08/05-the-boy-on-the-corner.md#ah-r1096) is Noel’s second cousin. Noel keeps the photographs. Jody knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The boy on the corner*, Jody says, “I sell drugs too. This family crisis exposes how little loyalty there is when a friend stops being useful.”

**People in this story.** [Reuben Grant](#ah-r0543). A fennel donation changes what the late cook has left to take home.

**Personality.** Noel reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Noel trusts most.

**Appearance.** Noel is easy to recognize by a high puff tied with plain cord and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Noel squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Someone in the soup line cannot eat one ingredient. Noel asks the cook to identify the ingredients before offering a separate bowl.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Noel moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If told to back off, Noel does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Nico Ibrahim

AH-R0547 · Age 50 · Park gardener · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember one thermos shared between two addresses.” On Tuesday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Nico checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I brought a thermos for my ex. We still buy food for each other even though we cannot share a table.”

**What Nico wants.** To keep one thermos moving between the separated homes.

**Family.** [Robin Cao](../05/09-an-address-in-pencil.md#ah-r0683) is Nico’s stepsibling. Nico makes the call. Robin is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *An address in pencil*, Robin says, “I make numbers for office buildings. I cut a new one big enough to see without crossing the road.”

**People in this story.** [Asa Roberts](#ah-r0549). A thermos for a separated household travels with the names of the closing crew.

**Personality.** Nico has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Nico's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Nico is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in a brown corduroy jacket with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread, Nico starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The cook asks for an exact recipe. Nico records what went into this particular pot and when the cooks tasted it.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Nico asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- If the joke lands badly, Nico seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Alvin Kapoor

AH-R0548 · Age 39 · Deli regular and warehouse worker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember picking celery strings off a school pudding spoon.” People around Breakwater Way know Alvin by a small Monday habit. Alvin stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I hate celery and always dug it out. Tonight the cook asked me to choose tomorrow's base.”

**What Alvin wants.** To choose a different base for tomorrow's pot.

**Family.** [Percy Wade](../05/11-the-free-fringe.md#ah-r0710) is Alvin’s half-sibling. Alvin leaves the first voice message. Percy calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The free fringe*, Percy says, “I want the free slot moved to after school once a week so the kid minding his sister gets a turn.”

**People in this story.** [Alex Scott](#ah-r0545). The ingredients record gives the celery-hater a real say in tomorrow's batch.

**Personality.** Alvin listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Alvin is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Alvin squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Someone in the soup line cannot eat one ingredient. Alvin asks the cook to identify the ingredients before offering a separate bowl.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Alvin helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- When a second witness disagrees, Alvin keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Asa Roberts

AH-R0549 · Age 54 · Food cooperative organizer · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a closing crew that never appeared on the menus.” Saturday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Asa can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I want the recipe credited to the people who chop after their shift ends.”

**What Asa wants.** To credit the closing crew by name.

**Family.** [Oscar Guzman](../05/05-the-clean-ledger.md#ah-r0645) is Asa’s cousin. Asa chooses the familiar route. Oscar can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The clean ledger*, Oscar says, “I carried a debt for my brother because the old man never knew which twin was which.”

**People in this story.** [Nico Ibrahim](#ah-r0547). A thermos for a separated household travels with the names of the closing crew.

**Personality.** Asa talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Asa's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Asa is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Asa takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The cook asks for an exact recipe. Asa records what went into this particular pot and when the cooks tasted it.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Asa calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Asa says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Ashley Lang

AH-R0550 · Age 48 · Community interpreter · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember hearing my grandmother's kitchen language on a city bus.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Studio Boulevard, where Ashley waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I heard my mother say supper in a language I haven't used for years. I wrote the recipe in that language first.”

**What Ashley wants.** To write the kitchen's recipe in its first language.

**Family.** [Dolores Taylor](../10/08-no-lifeguard-at-dusk.md#ah-r1426) is Ashley’s cousin. Ashley brings the paper and a pen. Dolores decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *No lifeguard at dusk*, Dolores says, “I came to collect the flags and found the guard unable to hand them over.”

**People in this story.** [Ken Calloway](#ah-r0544). A leftover meal brings the visiting cook and a family language into one kitchen.

**Personality.** Ashley prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Ashley has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for a long plait tucked inside the collar. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Ashley touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- Someone in the soup line cannot eat one ingredient. Ashley asks the cook to identify the ingredients before offering a separate bowl.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Ashley tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Ashley clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.
