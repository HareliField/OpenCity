## Eden Sun

AH-R0181 · Age 38 · Florist's delivery driver · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I've driven flowers to seven apologies at one address.” On Thursday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Eden stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I adopted him after he jumped into my van. I never noticed the other family's number under his tag.”

**What Eden wants.** To find the household whose number is under the dog's tag.

**Family.** [June Wicks](../01/09-the-porch-light.md#ah-r0088) is Eden’s younger cousin. Eden knows which ones can be shared. June keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The porch light*, June says, “I thought the dark porch meant he'd died. I left six papers to make sure people would stop.”

**People in this story.** [Shay Lloyd](#ah-r0184) (former dog fosterer). The fosterer helps the new guardian distinguish two members of one litter.

**Personality.** Eden is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Eden is concerned.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Eden has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with short black hair swept back. Eden takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child takes the other dog's lead. Eden separates the leads and asks both families what the dogs respond to.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Eden offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- When someone invents an answer, Eden asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Olive Kapoor

AH-R0182 · Age 46 · Family carer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “Whistling was the only thing that reliably cut through Dad's bad hearing days.” People around Harbour Bakery know Olive by a small Sunday habit. Olive buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I trained that whistle to get my father indoors before dark. Now a dog comes too.”

**What Olive wants.** To keep the whistle useful for a parent who wanders.

**Family.** [Rae Montoya](../09/13-the-school-photograph-that-travels.md#ah-r1327) is Olive’s younger cousin. Olive keeps the photographs. Rae knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The school photograph that travels*, Rae says, “I was the child who made her life miserable.”

**People in this story.** [Lydia Lai](#ah-r0186). They put two records beside the two responses to a single whistle.

**Personality.** Olive loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Olive has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a boxy denim jacket. It usually goes with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. When somebody else has the floor, Olive touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A parent wants both dogs handed over immediately. Olive waits until the dogs and the children are settled before discussing the handover.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Olive tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Olive offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Ivy Perreault

AH-R0183 · Age 33 · Police counter volunteer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I used to wait at this same counter for the mother who cleaned it.” Monday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Ivy asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I took the first report and wrote brown. The little girl gave me nine different browns to choose from.”

**What Ivy wants.** To record the child's description of the dog's coat.

**Family.** [Fay March](../01/11-ten-fingers.md#ah-r0102) is Ivy’s older relative by marriage. Ivy chooses the music. Fay puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *Ten fingers*, Fay says, “I am the birthday girl. Everybody has taken to speaking softly around me.”

**People in this story.** [Adela Ferraro](#ah-r0189). The child's drawing gives the counter volunteer more to record than the color brown.

**Personality.** Ivy enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Ivy is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Ivy has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. Off duty, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a train token tied to a zipper pull. In conversation Ivy draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A child takes the other dog's lead. Ivy separates the leads and asks both families what the dogs respond to.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Ivy asks the person crying whether they want to go somewhere quieter, leaving them control over the next step.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Ivy keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

**Under pressure**

Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The guarantee*, Parker says, “I once inherited a similar debt. **Appearance.** Charlotte has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Charlotte presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Charlotte tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.

## Lydia Lai

AH-R0186 · Age 29 · Veterinary receptionist · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I first worked at the clinic making little paper envelopes for lost tags.” Friday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Lydia prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I offered free vaccination records and discovered our clinic had two birthdays for one animal.”

**What Lydia wants.** To get the clinic to separate the two birth records.

**Family.** [Talia Barclay](../05/01-salt-in-the-pockets.md#ah-r0603) is Lydia’s cousin. Lydia brings the paper and a pen. Talia decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Salt in the pockets*, Talia says, “I thought the powder was plaster. It was the sugar from the bakery where I secretly work weekends.”

**People in this story.** [Olive Kapoor](#ah-r0182). They put two records beside the two responses to a single whistle.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Lydia has short copper curls, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, an old plum windbreaker. It usually goes with a little coil of soft measuring tape. When somebody else has the floor, Lydia squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- A parent wants both dogs handed over immediately. Lydia waits until the dogs and the children are settled before discussing the handover.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Lydia asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Lydia listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

**Life in the city.** “A spelling mistake got my first picture book stocked under detective fiction.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the steps at Pier Beach, where Adela watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The carer's afternoon*, Evelyn says, “I made a rota that divided time equally and forgot my brother's bus takes an hour each way.”

**People in this story.** [Ivy Perreault](#ah-r0183). The child's drawing gives the counter volunteer more to record than the color brown.

**Personality.** Adela can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Adela is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a rust-colored canvas coat with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Adela touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A child takes the other dog's lead. Adela separates the leads and asks both families what the dogs respond to.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Adela asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Adela asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.
