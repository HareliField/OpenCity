## Wesley Joseph

AH-R0161 · Age 36 · Port forklift driver · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I began moving freight on a borrowed forklift with a missing armrest.” People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Wesley by a small Sunday habit. Wesley checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “We used to swap the sweet things. With my new shift I don't know anybody's favorite.”

**What Wesley wants.** To exchange lunch treats with one new shift mate.

**Family.** [Imani Ford](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1231) is Wesley’s half-sibling. Wesley makes the call. Imani is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Imani says, “I took the first call and lost the connection.”

**People in this story.** [John Hong](#ah-r0165). They argue over whether a working lunch tin is ready to become a museum piece.

**Personality.** Wesley tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Wesley will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Wesley has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Wesley listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- An old tin is claimed by two crews. Wesley invites both crews to describe the lid without putting either at fault.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Wesley tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Wesley repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Vincent Farah

AH-R0162 · Age 41 · Tug engineer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “My boy and I used to draw animals on the fogged windows before dawn.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Vincent buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “My son painted a whale on the lid. It's the only thing he drew before his hand operation.”

**What Vincent wants.** To bring the whale lid home for the boy to see.

**Family.** [Bailey Moran](../09/06-a-doctor-moves-away.md#ah-r1252) is Vincent’s older cousin. Vincent leaves the first voice message. Bailey calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A doctor moves away*, Bailey says, “I have no claim on her evenings and want an introduction to the person who will read what she wrote about me.”

**People in this story.** [Bianca Cole](#ah-r0168). They make sure the whale lid goes into the correct forwarding bag.

**Personality.** Vincent is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Vincent asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Vincent has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cream pullover with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. In conversation Vincent looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The night shift misses the collection time. Vincent puts the unclaimed tin by for a later handover.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Vincent offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Vincent accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Hamish Nichols

AH-R0163 · Age 35 · Freight loader · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I kept my first union card in a tin that once held my sister's paints.” People around the bank steps know Hamish by a small Sunday habit. Hamish takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “The tins got blamed on casual workers. I found my own name under the stack.”

**What Hamish wants.** To correct the story about casual workers abandoning the tins.

**Family.** [Tom Crane](../10/03-the-gift-nobody-wants.md#ah-r1379) is Hamish’s second cousin. Hamish chooses the familiar route. Tom can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The gift nobody wants*, Tom says, “I am the property manager. I offered to explain the tenants' part before the family wrote another letter.”

**People in this story.** [Teal Alder](#ah-r0164). They help return the named tins without blaming the casual crews.

**Personality.** Hamish is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Hamish has a dark undercut with a longer wave on top, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a boxy denim jacket. It usually goes with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. When somebody else has the floor, Hamish steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- An old tin is claimed by two crews. Hamish invites both crews to describe the lid without putting either at fault.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Hamish asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Hamish thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Teal Alder

AH-R0164 · Age 29 · Workwear courier · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “My first delivery bag had a strap so long it banged my knee.” Monday often includes a stop at Juniper Street, where Teal knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I offered to deliver them and discovered that two people who worked together for years never knew each other's addresses.”

**What Teal wants.** To deliver all five tins to the people who used them.

**Family.** [Iva Salas](../09/09-the-returned-daughter.md#ah-r1281) is Teal’s older cousin. Teal brings the paper and a pen. Iva decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The returned daughter*, Iva says, “I thought the house would look smaller. The door still feels exactly as high as the day I left.”

**People in this story.** [Hamish Nichols](#ah-r0163). They help return the named tins without blaming the casual crews.

**Personality.** Teal loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Teal remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a plum-colored cardigan and one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. Teal has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with short copper curls. Teal takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The night shift misses the collection time. Teal puts the unclaimed tin by for a later handover.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Teal asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- When invited to tell the story later, Teal includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## John Hong

AH-R0165 · Age 62 · Local museum registrar · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I learned to label exhibits from a mother who wrote dates on jam jars.” People around Maré Coffee know John by a small Thursday habit. John likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I wanted the red tin for our museum display. Its owner still needs somewhere to put lunch.”

**What John wants.** To record the tin without taking it out of useful circulation.

**Family.** [Scout Nord](../03/05-rent-in-a-biscuit-tin.md#ah-r0347) is John’s younger cousin. John calls about good news first. Scout is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Rent in a biscuit tin*, Scout says, “I thought the extra rent was a gift. I had already bought school shoes when the owner knocked.”

**People in this story.** [Wesley Joseph](#ah-r0161). They argue over whether a working lunch tin is ready to become a museum piece.

**Personality.** John has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** John has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for a neat white crop. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an old plum windbreaker with a small enamel bird on the lapel. In conversation John gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- An old tin is claimed by two crews. John invites both crews to describe the lid without putting either at fault.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. John calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- When somebody trades on a title, John asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Olivia Hu

AH-R0166 · Age 58 · Charity shop sorter · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I used to be too proud to let the charity shop save things for me.” People around the bank steps know Olivia by a small Saturday habit. Olivia takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I bought the empty tins by weight at the charity sale. I'm trying to buy the lunches back too.”

**What Olivia wants.** To trace the lunches that ought to have gone with the tins.

**Family.** [Maya Scott](../07/04-a-civilian-apology.md#ah-r0934) is Olivia’s second cousin. Olivia remembers the birthdays. Maya asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A civilian apology*, Maya says, “I don't like apologies delivered in uniform.”

**People in this story.** [Logan Calder](#ah-r0167). They compare the price of buying back a tin with the cost of losing a shared break.

**Personality.** Olivia finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Olivia is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Olivia takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The night shift misses the collection time. Olivia puts the unclaimed tin by for a later handover.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Olivia brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- When thanked for a shared success, Olivia points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Logan Calder

AH-R0167 · Age 47 · Port shift planner · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I once swapped shifts with a stranger for an entire month before meeting him.” On Monday, near Tideline Market, Logan picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I changed the roster to save wages. Now I eat alone next to five imaginary people.”

**What Logan wants.** To offer a shared break to the people split by the new rota.

**Family.** [Lev Adair](../01/09-the-porch-light.md#ah-r0083) is Logan’s second cousin. Logan chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Lev brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The porch light*, Lev says, “The switch worked. He'd unscrewed the bulb to see who would notice.”

**People in this story.** [Olivia Hu](#ah-r0166). They compare the price of buying back a tin with the cost of losing a shared break. Also in the story is [Bianca Cole](#ah-r0168) (break-room caretaker).

**Personality.** Logan keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Logan wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Logan has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Logan gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- An old tin is claimed by two crews. Logan invites both crews to describe the lid without putting either at fault.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Logan writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Logan offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Bianca Cole

AH-R0168 · Age 56 · Warehouse cleaner · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “My old cleaning partner could identify every crane crew by its sandwich crumbs.” People around Breakwater Way know Bianca by a small Thursday habit. Bianca stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I was asked to throw them away. I put a forwarding card in each one instead.”

**What Bianca wants.** To attach forwarding tags to the uncollected containers.

**Family.** [Chris Anders](../03/05-rent-in-a-biscuit-tin.md#ah-r0349) is Bianca’s cousin. Bianca puts the kettle on. Chris chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *Rent in a biscuit tin*, Chris says, “I was sure my envelope had been opened. The crease was mine from the night I nearly spent it.”

**People in this story.** [Vincent Farah](#ah-r0162). They make sure the whale lid goes into the correct forwarding bag. Also in the story is [Logan Calder](#ah-r0167) (roster planner).

**Personality.** Bianca always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Bianca is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Bianca is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Bianca looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The night shift misses the collection time. Bianca puts the unclaimed tin by for a later handover.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Bianca asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Bianca suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Joanna Ellis

AH-R0169 · Age 65 · Retired stevedore · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “The first game we played ashore was scored in pencil on a packing list.” On Saturday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Joanna watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “The dents on those lids came from our lunchtime domino games. I still owe the last winner ten points.”

**What Joanna wants.** To play out the unfinished domino score on the old table.

**Family.** [Carmen Ali](../04/01-the-button-card.md#ah-r0454) is Joanna’s stepsibling. Joanna chooses an ordinary story to tell. Carmen asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The button card*, Carmen says, “I found a brass anchor from a uniform my sister swore she'd burned.”

**People in this story.** [Ellen Baran](#ah-r0170). They arrange a lunchtime domino rematch with actual food in the tins.

**Personality.** Joanna has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Joanna knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Joanna has salt-and-pepper curls, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Joanna draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- An old tin is claimed by two crews. Joanna invites both crews to describe the lid without putting either at fault.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Joanna brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Joanna asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Ellen Baran

AH-R0170 · Age 44 · Rail maintenance worker · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I left machine work for the railroad when the cold started to hurt my fingers.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Ellen by a small Friday habit. Ellen looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I store nuts in my tin. Finding somebody else's lunch inside made me remember to eat.”

**What Ellen wants.** To bring something edible to the next railway break.

**Family.** [Jo Emery](../09/12-the-driving-lesson-fund.md#ah-r1317) is Ellen’s cousin raised in the same household. Ellen checks the date twice. Jo brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The driving lesson fund*, Jo says, “I cannot fund another hour. I offered a place to wait for children whose parents are out with the car.”

**People in this story.** [Joanna Ellis](#ah-r0169). They arrange a lunchtime domino rematch with actual food in the tins.

**Personality.** Ellen enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Ellen has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Ellen presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The night shift misses the collection time. Ellen puts the unclaimed tin by for a later handover.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Ellen gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Ellen puts the deal away and suggests a walk.
