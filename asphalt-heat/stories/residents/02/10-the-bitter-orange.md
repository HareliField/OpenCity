## Jody Graves

AH-R0241 · Age 46 · Deli sandwich maker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I learned fillings from the person who remembered which customers had dentures.” Friday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Jody prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I remember mustard because she'd send it back if we skimped. Her son insists she hated mustard at home.”

**What Jody wants.** To give the regular's son the chance to taste the mustard version.

**Family.** [Alma Faye](../01/01-the-blue-suit.md#ah-r0009) is Jody’s cousin. Jody asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Alma chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The blue suit*, Alma says, “In the photograph you can see one white cuff.”

**People in this story.** [Anya Li](#ah-r0242). They compare the mustard remembered at the counter with the orange remembered on the walk home.

**Personality.** Jody always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Jody is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Jody has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a brown corduroy jacket with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Jody gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- The deli proposes serving the memorial sandwich every Sunday. Jody asks the family what they would want to eat on an ordinary Sunday.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Jody asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Jody suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Anya Li

AH-R0242 · Age 51 · Former home-care worker · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “For a year I tasted everything on a client's plate before she would eat.” On Monday, near Juniper Street, Anya knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I squeezed the orange she carried in her pocket. Nobody knew her medicine left a bitter taste.”

**What Anya wants.** To re-create the orange's bitterness in the patient's usual order.

**Family.** [Morgan Freeman](../07/03-a-widow-bids-again.md#ah-r0927) is Anya’s cousin. Anya checks the date twice. Morgan brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A widow bids again*, Morgan says, “I still have a lease written on a birthday card.”

**People in this story.** [Jody Graves](#ah-r0241). They compare the mustard remembered at the counter with the orange remembered on the walk home.

**Personality.** Anya has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Anya knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Anya is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Anya gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative hates the ingredient everyone remembers. Anya lets the relative choose a different sandwich without defending the menu.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Anya gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Anya asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Lou Khan

AH-R0243 · Age 48 · Counter cook · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I worked breakfast on the day our street lost power until noon.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Lou picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “She ordered two slices after her husband died. I wrapped the other half for whoever sat at his table.”

**What Lou wants.** To keep one lunchtime place open for an unaccompanied diner.

**Family.** [Grey Ibrahim](../03/05-rent-in-a-biscuit-tin.md#ah-r0341) is Lou’s cousin raised in the same household. Lou brings a spare shopping bag. Grey chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Rent in a biscuit tin*, Grey says, “I used her kitchen as my savings bank when I sold pills.”

**People in this story.** [Blake Baker](#ah-r0245). A new deli owner learns who used to eat the other half.

**Personality.** Lou enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Lou is easy to recognize by a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Lou folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The deli proposes serving the memorial sandwich every Sunday. Lou asks the family what they would want to eat on an ordinary Sunday.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Lou keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Lou puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Jules Drake

AH-R0244 · Age 27 · Weekend cashier · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I kept exact change in the hem of my coat for my first weekend job.” Monday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Jules prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I still have her loose change in a jar. It buys exactly one sandwich at the old price.”

**What Jules wants.** To buy one sandwich at the remembered price.

**Family.** [Joel Sun](../10/15-the-last-light-at-the-harbor.md#ah-r1495) is Jules’s older relative by marriage. Jules asks before bringing somebody along. Joel can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The last light at the harbor*, Joel says, “I lived on the smallest boat after my divorce and still count its light even though another family sleeps there.”

**People in this story.** [Fiona Mann](#ah-r0249). The remaining change and the wrapper give the anniversary order a price and a proper finish.

**Personality.** Jules has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Jules can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Jules is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Jules starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative hates the ingredient everyone remembers. Jules lets the relative choose a different sandwich without defending the menu.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Jules tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- If a friend borrows money, Jules agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Blake Baker

AH-R0245 · Age 38 · Deli partner · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I bought the keys and inherited eighteen decades of other people's expectations.” People around Breakwater Way know Blake by a small Monday habit. Blake stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I'm the new owner. I promised a memorial special before I'd ever tasted what she ate.”

**What Blake wants.** To approve a memorial special only after tasting it.

**Family.** [Karina May](../08/02-land-without-cranes.md#ah-r1063) is Blake’s cousin raised in the same household. Blake makes room at the table. Karina brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Land without cranes*, Karina says, “I draw architectural renderings. I put a worker in mine and realized there'd be no job for the person I drew.”

**People in this story.** [Lou Khan](#ah-r0243). A new deli owner learns who used to eat the other half. Also in the story is [Freya King](#ah-r0250) (menu adviser).

**Personality.** Blake takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Blake can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Blake has short black hair swept back, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a washed violet jersey. It usually goes with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. When somebody else has the floor, Blake turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- The deli proposes serving the memorial sandwich every Sunday. Blake asks the family what they would want to eat on an ordinary Sunday.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Blake asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Blake turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Hugo Barron

AH-R0246 · Age 24 · Graduate apprentice · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I passed my exams behind the chip fryer while it heated up.” On Wednesday, near Harbour Bakery, Hugo buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I used to hide from her behind the fridge because she'd ask about my exams. I passed and never told her.”

**What Hugo wants.** To tell the family about the exam the regular used to ask after.

**Family.** [Ruth Tran](../04/02-the-soft-wharf-chalk.md#ah-r0464) is Hugo’s older cousin. Hugo saves a place. Ruth chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The soft wharf chalk*, Ruth says, “I saved the piece of wood during repairs. It still smells of the crate my mother unpacked.”

**People in this story.** [Freya King](#ah-r0250). The son hears about a long-ago exam his mother asked after.

**Personality.** Hugo admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Hugo learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Hugo has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a washed violet jersey. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Hugo listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A relative hates the ingredient everyone remembers. Hugo lets the relative choose a different sandwich without defending the menu.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Hugo asks what the friend actually said last time and checks whether anybody is expecting them at home.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Hugo describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Martin Chen

AH-R0247 · Age 60 · Bread driver · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I once delivered Sunday bread in my daughter's decorated sports wagon.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Martin by a small Saturday habit. Martin watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I delivered bread six mornings a week. On the seventh she ate with my family. I know the Sunday order.”

**What Martin wants.** To put the family's Sunday sandwich into the record.

**Family.** [Cora Yates](../05/11-the-free-fringe.md#ah-r0703) is Martin’s younger cousin. Martin picks the meeting place. Cora sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The free fringe*, Cora says, “I gave up my slot because I remembered the first day I had nowhere to go before work.”

**People in this story.** [Cynthia Moran](#ah-r0248). The delivery driver's Sunday version belongs on the card under the nickname the regular remembered.

**Personality.** Martin has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a black overshirt with pearl buttons and one cuff folded back twice. Martin has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, with a neat white crop. Martin looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The deli proposes serving the memorial sandwich every Sunday. Martin asks the family what they would want to eat on an ordinary Sunday.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Martin looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- If accused of favoritism, Martin explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Cynthia Moran

AH-R0248 · Age 31 · Fruit seller · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “My first boss called everybody darling because he couldn't read our name badges.” On Monday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Cynthia pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “She had my name wrong for twenty years. I put the wrong name on my contribution to the recipe.”

**What Cynthia wants.** To write the customer's version of the cook's name on the menu.

**Family.** [Arlo Lee](../04/12-the-ladder-crossing.md#ah-r0561) is Cynthia’s older cousin. A family member returned from a difficult year elsewhere and wanted dinner without a welcome speech. They made such a point of ordinary dinner that it became a permanent tradition. Cynthia can eat a whole meal beside a person who isn't ready to talk. Asked about *The ladder crossing*, Arlo says, “I was at the front and couldn't see the painter crying at the back.”

**People in this story.** [Martin Chen](#ah-r0247). The delivery driver's Sunday version belongs on the card under the nickname the regular remembered.

**Personality.** Cynthia reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Cynthia trusts most.

**Appearance.** Cynthia is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in a charcoal zip cardigan with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Cynthia grins on one side while the eyes stay serious when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative hates the ingredient everyone remembers. Cynthia lets the relative choose a different sandwich without defending the menu.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Cynthia keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- If told to back off, Cynthia does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Fiona Mann

AH-R0249 · Age 40 · Event planner · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I paid for my kitchen course making favors for weddings I never saw.” On Wednesday, near Ferris Deli, Fiona asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I can't eat the memorial sandwich. I chose the paper it will be wrapped in.”

**What Fiona wants.** To choose paper that will carry the order without collapsing.

**Family.** [Rowan Davis](../09/07-school-after-sixty.md#ah-r1268) is Fiona’s half-sibling. Fiona makes the call. Rowan is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *School after sixty*, Rowan says, “I took the day off with a stomach ache. I don't want my boss at the bank to know I'm learning to read his notices.”

**People in this story.** [Jules Drake](#ah-r0244). The remaining change and the wrapper give the anniversary order a price and a proper finish.

**Personality.** Fiona has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Fiona's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Fiona is easy to recognize by a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a watch on a cracked leather band, Fiona counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The deli proposes serving the memorial sandwich every Sunday. Fiona asks the family what they would want to eat on an ordinary Sunday.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Fiona walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- If the joke lands badly, Fiona seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Freya King

AH-R0250 · Age 56 · Hotel night auditor · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “A night audit teaches you how many solitary meals get charged to double rooms.” People around the entrance to the Wash House know Freya by a small Monday habit. Freya stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I brought her son in expecting tears. He ordered something completely different and made everyone laugh.”

**What Freya wants.** To make room at the remembrance lunch for a different order.

**Family.** [Cassandra Collins](../10/03-the-gift-nobody-wants.md#ah-r1372) is Freya’s younger cousin. Freya leaves the first voice message. Cassandra calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The gift nobody wants*, Cassandra says, “I am the daughter. Dad has offered me a valuable building with his office on the top floor.”

**People in this story.** [Hugo Barron](#ah-r0246). The son hears about a long-ago exam his mother asked after. Also in the story is [Blake Baker](#ah-r0245) (shop owner).

**Personality.** Freya listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Freya has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a plum-colored cardigan with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Freya starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A relative hates the ingredient everyone remembers. Freya lets the relative choose a different sandwich without defending the menu.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Freya clears the dishes, lets the angriest speaker cool off, and resumes the conversation with a smaller audience.
- When a second witness disagrees, Freya keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.
