## Bess Lai

AH-R0211 · Age 52 · Retired advertising editor · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I used to defend terrible headlines just because my name was under them.” On Friday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Bess likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I wrote the first objection when I was seventeen. I returned to disagree with myself at fifty.”

**What Bess wants.** To answer a teenage argument from the viewpoint of later life.

**Family.** [Eli Brady](../04/05-the-dead-man-s-tab.md#ah-r0496) is Bess’s younger relative by marriage. Bess asks before bringing somebody along. Eli can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The dead man's tab*, Eli says, “I forged my father's signature once. Seeing the real one in the book made me own up.”

**People in this story.** [Miles Price](#ah-r0215). One writer's teenage opinion survived a divorce in somebody else's carton. Also in the story is [Julian Oh](#ah-r0220) (fellow margin writer).

**Personality.** Bess has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Bess knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Bess has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Bess presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks to erase a personal inscription. Bess puts a clean sheet over the inscription while its writer decides.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Bess keeps the neighbor's place and finds out whether the stranger needs a different kind of help.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Bess asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Fiona Nord

AH-R0212 · Age 33 · Library clerk · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I learned to shelve books before I learned how late their readers stayed up.” Sunday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Fiona pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I recognized my old teacher by the way she corrects a question mark. I want to answer the one she left me.”

**What Fiona wants.** To ask the retired teacher one more question in the margin.

**Family.** [Allen Clay](../10/03-the-gift-nobody-wants.md#ah-r1380) is Fiona’s older cousin. Fiona makes room at the table. Allen brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The gift nobody wants*, Allen says, “I found the father's first rent receipt. It is framed in the corner where he tells young people to start from nothing.”

**People in this story.** [Carlo Ueda](#ah-r0216). They turn a long argument in pencil into something an older reader can hear aloud.

**Personality.** Fiona enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Fiona is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket, Fiona turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two people remember writing the same line. Fiona lets both readers identify the line privately before adding a name.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Fiona asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Fiona puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Malcolm Soto

AH-R0213 · Age 45 · Hotel banqueting coordinator · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I set tables for strangers while my first marriage turned into notes on the fridge.” Friday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Malcolm prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I thought the pencil insults were aimed at me. Now I'm meeting the reader who was arguing with a fictional husband.”

**What Malcolm wants.** To read the notes with the person who lived through the same disappointment.

**Family.** [June Brady](../07/12-the-crash-anniversary.md#ah-r1012) is Malcolm’s older cousin. Malcolm saves a place. June chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The crash anniversary*, June says, “I bought the cheapest flowers every Friday.”

**People in this story.** [Nell Marks](#ah-r0218). A blunt translation establishes who the old insults actually addressed.

**Personality.** Malcolm has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Malcolm can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Malcolm has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Look for brown hair pinned away from the temples. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cream pullover with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Malcolm draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks to erase a personal inscription. Malcolm puts a clean sheet over the inscription while its writer decides.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Malcolm asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- If a friend borrows money, Malcolm agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Keir Zhou

AH-R0214 · Age 49 · Secondhand book sorter · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “Books arrive damp more often from dry flats than owners like to admit.” On Saturday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Keir stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I erased three lines before I understood. I've written down the one I remember.”

**What Keir wants.** To restore a penciled sentence before memory loses it.

**Family.** [Fernando Arendt](../08/06-two-names-on-the-chart.md#ah-r1109) is Keir’s older cousin. Keir picks the meeting place. Fernando sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Two names on the chart*, Fernando says, “I kept a clinic letter because the doctor spelled our village correctly.”

**People in this story.** [Georgia Kwan](#ah-r0219). The teacher wants the erased sentence restored before the class reads the copy.

**Personality.** Keir takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Keir can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Keir has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a brown corduroy jacket with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. In conversation Keir draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- Two people remember writing the same line. Keir lets both readers identify the line privately before adding a name.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Keir agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Keir turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Miles Price

AH-R0215 · Age 51 · Divorced tax preparer · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “My divorce came with six cartons we couldn't bear to divide.” On Monday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Miles watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I donated the books during a divorce. My ex kept replying after I'd already moved out.”

**What Miles wants.** To exchange the final set of notes with the former spouse.

**Family.** [Mae Linford](../01/14-two-portions.md#ah-r0131) is Miles’s cousin. Miles decides what stays off the paper. Mae brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Two portions*, Mae says, “I nearly canceled the extra dinner. Then I remembered the winter my father fed our neighbor off his own plate.”

**People in this story.** [Bess Lai](#ah-r0211). One writer's teenage opinion survived a divorce in somebody else's carton.

**Personality.** Miles admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Miles learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a pale blue work shirt and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Miles has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Miles starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks to erase a personal inscription. Miles puts a clean sheet over the inscription while its writer decides.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Miles asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Miles describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Carlo Ueda

AH-R0216 · Age 27 · Audiobook narrator · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I recorded my first book in a closet lined with winter coats.” Sunday often includes a stop at Studio Boulevard, where Carlo waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “My dad hated the book but loved the notes. I'm recording just the margins for his walks.”

**What Carlo wants.** To record a version for a father who walks while listening.

**Family.** [Daphne Perez](../10/06-two-birthdays-in-one-ward.md#ah-r1404) is Carlo’s parent’s cousin. Carlo makes the call. Daphne is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Two birthdays in one ward*, Daphne says, “I dealt the first hand and lost to a woman I used to supervise on night shift.”

**People in this story.** [Fiona Nord](#ah-r0212). They turn a long argument in pencil into something an older reader can hear aloud.

**Personality.** Carlo has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Carlo has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Carlo counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- Two people remember writing the same line. Carlo lets both readers identify the line privately before adding a name.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Carlo writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- If accused of favoritism, Carlo explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Lina King

AH-R0217 · Age 60 · Night class student and shelf stocker · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “My first reading tutor let me skip the chapters I was frightened of.” On Saturday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Lina checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “The copy I could afford was missing chapter eight. I've finally found out who I was defending.”

**What Lina wants.** To read the missing chapter before defending its husband again.

**Family.** [Shay Stein](../03/15-the-tide-clock.md#ah-r0444) is Lina’s half-sibling. Lina leaves the first voice message. Shay calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The tide clock*, Shay says, “I sold the clock as a conversation piece. I never thought anyone would put it over the time cards.”

**People in this story.** [Julian Oh](#ah-r0220). The missing chapter and the penciled mustache finally find their way into the same conversation.

**Personality.** Lina reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Lina trusts most.

**Appearance.** Lina has closely cropped salt-and-pepper hair, one cheek that creases before the other, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a watch on a cracked leather band. When somebody else has the floor, Lina listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks to erase a personal inscription. Lina puts a clean sheet over the inscription while its writer decides.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Lina stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- If told to back off, Lina does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Nell Marks

AH-R0218 · Age 42 · Freelance translator · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I learned my second language translating the insults in our football league.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Nell buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I'm translating the swear words first. They tell me where two polite people finally trusted each other.”

**What Nell wants.** To produce a translation that preserves the risky jokes.

**Family.** [Rain Salcedo](../03/03-the-widow-s-dividend.md#ah-r0322) is Nell’s older cousin. Nell chooses the familiar route. Rain can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The widow's dividend*, Rain says, “I had a silent share. My children still think my wealth came from my second husband.”

**People in this story.** [Malcolm Soto](#ah-r0213). A blunt translation establishes who the old insults actually addressed.

**Personality.** Nell has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Nell's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Nell is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Nell looks toward the door whenever a name is called when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two people remember writing the same line. Nell lets both readers identify the line privately before adding a name.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Nell tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If the joke lands badly, Nell seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Georgia Kwan

AH-R0219 · Age 37 · Secondary school teacher · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “My least promising student became the adult who recommended my best book.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Georgia by a small Monday habit. Georgia looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I need one clean copy for class. I traded it away because the messy one explains why anybody reads.”

**What Georgia wants.** To teach the class from the readers' disputed edition.

**Family.** [Darcy Lewis](../08/02-land-without-cranes.md#ah-r1062) is Georgia’s stepsibling. In this family birthdays are measured by the breakfast somebody requests. A person choosing plain toast or an elaborate fried meal gets the same fuss for one morning. Georgia gets the guest away from the sink before the dishes need doing. Asked about *Land without cranes*, Darcy says, “I stored my first fishing gear in that shed.”

**People in this story.** [Keir Zhou](#ah-r0214). The teacher wants the erased sentence restored before the class reads the copy.

**Personality.** Georgia listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Georgia has short black hair swept back, a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Georgia steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks to erase a personal inscription. Georgia puts a clean sheet over the inscription while its writer decides.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Georgia keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- When a second witness disagrees, Georgia keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Julian Oh

AH-R0220 · Age 73 · Retired packaging designer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I used to paint fake whiskers on packages with badly printed faces.” People around the downtown fountain know Julian by a small Tuesday habit. Julian keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I drew a moustache on the author. Decades later my granddaughter guessed it was me from the shape.”

**What Julian wants.** To give the granddaughter the original mustache drawing.

**Family.** [Remy Knight](../08/14-the-new-language-at-dinner.md#ah-r1190) is Julian’s first cousin once removed. When a family elder realized nobody could pronounce a friend's original name, she spent a whole evening teaching the table the three difficult sounds. Under pressure the family still says the names slowly. Julian will correct a mistake even if it makes a meal awkward. Asked about *The new language at dinner*, Remy says, “I hold the client's hand at meals. Yesterday he said my name correctly in a language I have never spoken.”

**People in this story.** [Lina King](#ah-r0217). The missing chapter and the penciled mustache finally find their way into the same conversation. Also in the story is [Bess Lai](#ah-r0211) (fellow margin writer).

**Personality.** Julian talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Julian's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Julian is easy to recognize by tightly curled gray hair and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Julian raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two people remember writing the same line. Julian lets both readers identify the line privately before adding a name.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Julian asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Julian says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.
