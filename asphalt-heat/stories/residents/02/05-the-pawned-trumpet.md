## Frank Hull

AH-R0191 · Age 27 · Restaurant server · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I could afford an instrument or a season pass, so I played in the park.” On Monday, near the downtown fountain, Frank keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I paid cash without asking why it was cheap. Now the kid wants one more rehearsal on it.”

**What Frank wants.** To give the teenager a last rehearsal before settling ownership.

**Family.** [Nadia Fraser](../08/08-a-summer-marriage.md#ah-r1121) is Frank’s half-sibling. Frank saves a place. Nadia chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *A summer marriage*, Nadia says, “I do not want free hotel flowers if every guest is going to hear where they came from.”

**People in this story.** [Lydia Ames](#ah-r0197). They compare the player's receipt with the mute still on the pawn counter.

**Personality.** Frank is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Frank is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Frank has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Frank rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands the trumpet stop playing. Frank asks the player and the neighbor to choose a practice time they can both live with.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Frank repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Frank agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Erin Hubbard

AH-R0192 · Age 42 · Brass band tutor · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I got thrown out of my first band for always asking to play the slow piece.” On Thursday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Erin watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I recognized that bent third valve from downstairs. I kept quiet until they played my late teacher's exercise.”

**What Erin wants.** To identify the former teacher by the exercise.

**Family.** [Harold Levin](../05/06-two-left-slippers.md#ah-r0660) is Erin’s older relative by marriage. Erin picks the meeting place. Harold sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Two left slippers*, Harold says, “I am keeping a single slipper by the front door.”

**People in this story.** [Talia Valdez](#ah-r0198). Two teachers recognize two different stages of the trumpet's working life.

**Personality.** Erin tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Erin will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Erin has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a pale blue work shirt with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. In conversation Erin raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- The teenager gets an audition the morning the horn is due back. Erin contacts the owner before treating an audition as permission to keep the horn.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Erin asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Erin repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Merritt Gomes

AH-R0193 · Age 50 · Former receiver of stolen goods and shop porter · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I left the trade in stolen things after my cousin bought her own camera back from me.” Thursday often includes a stop at Lighthouse Cove, where Merritt takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I used to buy stolen instruments. This time there's a receipt and a real hurt that the receipt doesn't cover.”

**What Merritt wants.** To put the buyer and seller together without assuming a crime.

**Family.** [Aidan Song](../06/01-the-upstairs-lease.md#ah-r0754) is Merritt’s cousin raised in the same household. Merritt keeps the photographs. Aidan knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The upstairs lease*, Aidan says, “I can approve reasonable leases. Nobody at the bank has ever told me what reasonable means for a night worker.”

**People in this story.** [Winifred Starr](#ah-r0199). They find a legal sale behind a rumor that had already attached itself to a dealer.

**Personality.** Merritt is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Merritt asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Merritt has black hair twisted into a small knot, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a gray fisher's sweater. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, Merritt steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands the trumpet stop playing. Merritt asks the player and the neighbor to choose a practice time they can both live with.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Merritt offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Merritt accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Helena Neal

AH-R0194 · Age 47 · Funeral caterer · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “My family feeds mourners before anyone tells them where to hang their coats.” Saturday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Helena prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I took the loan to get home for my mother's funeral. I haven't told my son what the train cost.”

**What Helena wants.** To tell the child what the funeral journey actually cost.

**Family.** [Max King](../10/04-names-on-the-night-wall.md#ah-r1382) is Helena’s cousin raised in the same household. Helena makes the call. Max is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Names on the night wall*, Max says, “I have covered my partner's Saturdays for a decade.”

**People in this story.** [Clyde Darzi](#ah-r0195). They discuss a loan horn without making a parent explain the funeral money in public.

**Personality.** Helena is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Helena is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket, Helena squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The teenager gets an audition the morning the horn is due back. Helena contacts the owner before treating an audition as permission to keep the horn.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Helena keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Helena thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Clyde Darzi

AH-R0195 · Age 33 · Brass repairer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I started repairing brass with a dented cornet rescued after a school flood.” On Tuesday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Clyde takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I repair brass at night. I can give the player a different instrument but I can't transfer those teeth marks.”

**What Clyde wants.** To provide a playable loan horn during the argument.

**Family.** [Lia Banks](../01/08-a-tide-at-noon.md#ah-r0076) is Clyde’s cousin raised in the same household. Clyde sets the time to leave. Lia picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *A tide at noon*, Lia says, “The judging route crossed a soft patch I knew wouldn't hold a wheel.”

**People in this story.** [Helena Neal](#ah-r0194). They discuss a loan horn without making a parent explain the funeral money in public. Also in the story is [Lydia Ames](#ah-r0197) (pawn counter contact).

**Personality.** Clyde loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Clyde remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Clyde has a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Clyde turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands the trumpet stop playing. Clyde asks the player and the neighbor to choose a practice time they can both live with.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Clyde asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- When invited to tell the story later, Clyde includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Hannah Peters

AH-R0196 · Age 55 · Apartment building caretaker · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “Our tenants once held a full concert for the man who mended the lift.” On Tuesday, near the downtown fountain, Hannah keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I recorded the tune through the ceiling because I wanted to complain about the noise. Now it's evidence of a reunion.”

**What Hannah wants.** To give the recording to the family before registering a complaint.

**Family.** [Sam Rose](../05/14-the-split-visit.md#ah-r0738) is Hannah’s cousin raised in the same household. One year the family all bought gifts for the same child and overlooked the adult cooking supper. Nobody wants an important celebration planned without a place for the cook to sit. Hannah counts the chairs before the guests. Asked about *The split visit*, Sam says, “I pay the carers and once claimed overlap was waste.”

**People in this story.** [Remy Rizzo](#ah-r0200). A noise complaint and a rehearsal invite turn one ceiling recording into something worth keeping.

**Personality.** Hannah has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Hannah has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. In conversation Hannah presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- The teenager gets an audition the morning the horn is due back. Hannah contacts the owner before treating an audition as permission to keep the horn.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Hannah lets the child choose whether to stand by the door or sit beside the window while another adult gets help.
- When somebody trades on a title, Hannah asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Lydia Ames

AH-R0197 · Age 60 · Pawnbroker's assistant · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I hate valuing instruments whose cases are worth more than my rent.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Lydia by a small Friday habit. Lydia prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I sold the trumpet with a practice mute. The mute is still on my shelf and so is the child's name.”

**What Lydia wants.** To return the practice mute with its original owner's name.

**Family.** [Lionel Wu](../10/02-a-last-ship-together.md#ah-r1368) is Lydia’s stepsibling. A relative let everybody believe a favorite tune was a folk song. Only later did the family find the melody in a book with a relative's real name under it. Lydia is quite capable of keeping a harmless secret for years. Asked about *A last ship together*, Lionel says, “I beat them at dominoes as a teenager. I want one last game with both clocks still ticking on company time.”

**People in this story.** [Frank Hull](#ah-r0191). They compare the player's receipt with the mute still on the pawn counter. Also in the story is [Clyde Darzi](#ah-r0195) (brass repairer).

**Personality.** Lydia finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Lydia has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for salt-and-pepper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a narrow-striped sweater with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Lydia gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands the trumpet stop playing. Lydia asks the player and the neighbor to choose a practice time they can both live with.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Lydia asks for help with a task the neighbor does well, so both people get to contribute something.
- When thanked for a shared success, Lydia points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Talia looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

## Winifred Starr

AH-R0199 · Age 32 · Drug dealer · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Family.** [Melanie Arendt](../04/14-laundry-for-a-stranger.md#ah-r0585) is Winifred’s stepsibling. Winifred remembers the birthdays. Melanie asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *Laundry for a stranger*, Melanie says, “I fitted a pocket for the tablets he won't carry.”

**People in this story.** [Merritt Gomes](#ah-r0193). They find a legal sale behind a rumor that had already attached itself to a dealer.

**Personality.** Winifred always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Winifred is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Winifred has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a washed violet jersey with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Winifred listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands the trumpet stop playing. Winifred asks the player and the neighbor to choose a practice time they can both live with.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Winifred brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Winifred suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

**Under pressure**
