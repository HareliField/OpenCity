## Nolan Costa

AH-R0281 · Age 68 · Retired piano mechanic · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I tuned a piano for a person who paid me in winter groceries.” People around the downtown fountain know Nolan by a small Thursday habit. Nolan keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I memorized which doors stick because I couldn't read the old tags. I can still identify every key by touch.”

**What Nolan wants.** To match the keys to the sticking doors without replacing every lock.

**Family.** [Russell Hooper](../03/01-spin-cycle-choir.md#ah-r0304) is Nolan’s younger relative by marriage. Nolan calls about good news first. Russell is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Spin cycle choir*, Russell says, “My baby sleeps only during the spin cycle. The regulars saved the gentlest machine for us.”

**People in this story.** [Ava Bernal](#ah-r0288) (route scheduler). Touch identifies the keys the coordinator was ready to declare missing.

**Personality.** Nolan is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Nolan is easy to recognize by short white hair and darker eyebrows and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket, Nolan looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant refuses to hand back a spare key. Nolan asks the tenant what access they would be giving up.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Nolan writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Nolan thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Elizabeth Yoon

AH-R0282 · Age 49 · Formerly unhoused day laborer · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I stopped sleeping in doorways when a woman offered me her spare key and a time to return it.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Elizabeth often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I used to hide my key in a shoe. After losing my home I hate the idea somebody else carries it.”

**What Elizabeth wants.** To recover control over access to the home.

**Family.** [Hana Solberg](../01/12-the-last-green.md#ah-r0120) is Elizabeth’s second cousin. Elizabeth sets the time to leave. Hana picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The last green*, Hana says, “The best view is from the passenger side at the crest.”

**People in this story.** [Rae Meyer](#ah-r0290). They agree which doors will open for a volunteer and which owners will open themselves.

**Personality.** Elizabeth loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Elizabeth remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Elizabeth has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Elizabeth counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A volunteer is blamed before the tin is checked. Elizabeth checks the returned tins before letting a name go into the complaint.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Elizabeth keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- When invited to tell the story later, Elizabeth includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Billie Morrow

AH-R0283 · Age 38 · Soup kitchen volunteer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “My first senior route was two dinners and one very heavy holiday cake.” Sunday often includes a stop at Tideline Market, where Billie picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I returned the ring inside a soup tin to keep it dry. Nobody checked the empty containers.”

**What Billie wants.** To tell the route leader where the dry soup tin went.

**Family.** [Eden Diaz](../04/08-the-rescue-hut-mural.md#ah-r0521) is Billie’s second cousin. Billie offers the first invitation. Eden gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The rescue hut mural*, Eden says, “I always thought she was diving. From my wheelchair she's clearly reaching for somebody.”

**People in this story.** [Marc Ramos](#ah-r0287). The soup tin has to travel another route with a rider who now knows where it belongs.

**Personality.** Billie has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Billie has a center part and straight chestnut hair, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Billie gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant refuses to hand back a spare key. Billie asks the tenant what access they would be giving up.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Billie lets the child choose whether to stand by the door or sit beside the window while another adult gets help.
- When somebody trades on a title, Billie asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Paloma Diaz

AH-R0284 · Age 43 · Property caretaker · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I grew up above a shop whose locks changed more often than our address.” On Monday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Paloma takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I had the locks changed before they found the keys. The new key is easier for my mother to turn.”

**What Paloma wants.** To give the mother a spare key she can turn.

**Family.** [Alex Miranda](../10/12-three-minutes-at-the-bridge.md#ah-r1463) is Paloma’s stepsibling. Paloma puts the kettle on. Alex chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *Three minutes at the bridge*, Alex says, “I own the van and want a clean seat for my father on the way home from his appointment.”

**People in this story.** [Marina Carvalho](#ah-r0289). A better-fitting lock goes into a backup list that can leave its keeper's house.

**Personality.** Paloma finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Paloma has a blunt cut just below the ears, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a charcoal zip cardigan. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Paloma rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A volunteer is blamed before the tin is checked. Paloma checks the returned tins before letting a name go into the complaint.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Paloma separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- When thanked for a shared success, Paloma points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Chris Dunn

AH-R0285 · Age 29 · Part-time florist · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “My friend used to ask for violets when she meant she needed company.” People around the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio know Chris by a small Sunday habit. Chris notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I borrowed the ring to water geraniums. I should have told the driver the flowers belonged to my late girlfriend.”

**What Chris wants.** To keep watering a dead friend's geraniums with permission.

**Family.** [Damian Mack](../06/09-a-room-for-a-father.md#ah-r0840) is Chris’s half-sibling. Chris chooses an ordinary story to tell. Damian asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *A room for a father*, Damian says, “I am the one who insisted we call him by his first name.”

**People in this story.** [Franklin Cole](#ah-r0286). A quiet plant-watering visit gives the diner a reason to ask for better arrival times.

**Personality.** Chris keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Chris wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Chris is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Chris listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant refuses to hand back a spare key. Chris asks the tenant what access they would be giving up.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Chris asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Chris offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Franklin Cole

AH-R0286 · Age 77 · Pensioned locksmith · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I have retired with a jar of locks whose keys their owners found too late.” People around Breakwater Way know Franklin by a small Friday habit. Franklin stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I missed lunch waiting for the knock. I want a window of time I can rely on without sitting in my coat.”

**What Franklin wants.** To agree on a delivery window that leaves time for a walk.

**Family.** [Anita Porter](../09/10-a-four-o-clock-kitchen.md#ah-r1293) is Franklin’s younger cousin. In one winter of double shifts family news traveled in notes tucked under the bread bin. Some notes solved practical problems. The favorites never asked anybody to do anything. Franklin checks the back of every folded note. Asked about *A four o'clock kitchen*, Anita says, “I am the cook's older daughter. I can run breakfast, but I'm tired of adults praising how little help I need.”

**People in this story.** [Chris Dunn](#ah-r0285). A quiet plant-watering visit gives the diner a reason to ask for better arrival times.

**Personality.** Franklin always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Franklin is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Franklin is easy to recognize by salt-and-pepper curls and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a cropped bottle-green coat with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Franklin leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A volunteer is blamed before the tin is checked. Franklin checks the returned tins before letting a name go into the complaint.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Franklin asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Franklin suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Marc Ramos

AH-R0287 · Age 15 · Secondary school pupil · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I taught myself to skate braking against our sofa.” Monday often includes a stop at the downtown fountain, where Marc keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I delivered the missing keys on my skates. It's the first time a grown-up thanked me for using the pavement.”

**What Marc wants.** To be trusted with another small delivery on skates.

**Family.** [Nessa Wren](../01/06-window-seven.md#ah-r0052) is Marc’s parent’s cousin. Marc brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Nessa makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Window seven*, Nessa says, “I promised her we'd try. I kept saying try because of the time I promised my father a trip he never got to make.”

**People in this story.** [Billie Morrow](#ah-r0283). The soup tin has to travel another route with a rider who now knows where it belongs.

**Personality.** Marc has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Marc knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Marc has auburn hair cut square at the nape, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Marc starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant refuses to hand back a spare key. Marc asks the tenant what access they would be giving up.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Marc keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Marc asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Rae Meyer

AH-R0290 · Age 70 · Retired tram cleaner · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “In my first pension week I practiced unlocking my door just for the sake of coming home.” On Tuesday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Rae can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I want one key back permanently. Opening my own door is the part of Saturday I look forward to.”

**What Rae wants.** To get one key back and meet the lunch carrier at the door.

**Family.** [Sam Sloan](../03/12-one-unposted-detour.md#ah-r0411) is Rae’s cousin’s child. Rae saves a place. Sam chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *One unposted detour*, Sam says, “I made the map but left out the bakery because it wasn't a registered depot.”

**People in this story.** [Elizabeth Yoon](#ah-r0282). They agree which doors will open for a volunteer and which owners will open themselves.

**Personality.** Rae takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Rae can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Rae has iron-gray waves, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Rae holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A volunteer is blamed before the tin is checked. Rae checks the returned tins before letting a name go into the complaint.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Rae checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Rae turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.
