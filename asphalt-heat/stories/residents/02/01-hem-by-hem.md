## Briar Mann

AH-R0151 · Age 52 · Bridal seamstress · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I learned bias-cut hems unpicking coats for a summer stock company.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Juniper Street, where Briar knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I moved the seam that once hid a pregnancy. The bride wants that seam left visible.”

**What Briar wants.** To leave the old seam where the bride can recognize it.

**Family.** [Anwar Fen](../01/06-window-seven.md#ah-r0058) is Briar’s cousin. Briar can stop the walk at any corner. Anwar chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Window seven*, Anwar says, “I brought breakfast in a cup because her hands shook.”

**People in this story.** [Clyde Ho](#ah-r0153). They compare the hidden seam with the older woman's remembered stitch. Also in the story is [Cian Reese](#ah-r0156) (longtime fitting client).

**Personality.** Briar loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Briar has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Briar raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- The bride wants to keep the old pocket. Briar leaves the pocket in place while the bride tries the dress on.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Briar accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Briar offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Cory Colón

AH-R0152 · Age 27 · Civil wedding celebrant · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “Our first registry office was above a fish stall. We always smelled of pepper.” People around the entrance to the Wash House know Cory by a small Wednesday habit. Cory stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I paid for silk believing it was new. Now I want the old fabric back in our vows.”

**What Cory wants.** To put the dress's real history into the wedding reading.

**Family.** [Remy Carey](../05/07-a-shelf-of-apologies.md#ah-r0662) is Cory’s older cousin. Cory chooses the familiar route. Remy can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *A shelf of apologies*, Remy says, “I want the owner to know I read every line aloud to my dad before he died.”

**People in this story.** [Talia Dai](#ah-r0154). The ceremony words depend on a name recovered from the cutting bill.

**Personality.** Cory enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Cory is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Cory is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Cory looks toward the door whenever a name is called when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to hide the changed seam. Cory asks the bride herself what should show above the hem.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Cory keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Cory keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Clyde Ho

AH-R0153 · Age 55 · Tailoring tutor · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I once taught stitching to a room that owned one needle between them.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Clyde by a small Sunday habit. Clyde checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “My fingers know the aunt's stitch. She always pulled too hard when she was angry.”

**What Clyde wants.** To identify which aunt made the repairs nobody claimed.

**Family.** [Lina Ryan](../07/07-the-first-language.md#ah-r0964) is Clyde’s half-sibling. Clyde brings the paper and a pen. Lina decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The first language*, Lina says, “I read the wrong page aloud to show her adults can survive a mistake.”

**People in this story.** [Briar Mann](#ah-r0151). They compare the hidden seam with the older woman's remembered stitch.

**Personality.** Clyde is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Clyde has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Clyde starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The bride wants to keep the old pocket. Clyde leaves the pocket in place while the bride tries the dress on.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Clyde asks each person to say what they believe is actually owed before anybody proposes a payment.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Clyde helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Talia Dai

AH-R0154 · Age 64 · Family records researcher · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I started tracing relatives after an aunt's death notice gave the wrong birthplace.” On Sunday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Talia can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I kept the original cutting bill through two moves. It names a woman the family stopped mentioning.”

**What Talia wants.** To invite the omitted relative to help date the dress.

**Family.** [Briar Wong](../08/13-the-understudy-sings.md#ah-r1180) is Talia’s second cousin. Talia calls about good news first. Briar is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The understudy sings*, Briar says, “I was at the rehearsal nobody filmed. I will remember the two women laughing halfway through the duet.”

**People in this story.** [Cory Colón](#ah-r0152). The ceremony words depend on a name recovered from the cutting bill.

**Personality.** Talia finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Talia has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Talia touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to hide the changed seam. Talia asks the bride herself what should show above the hem.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Talia keeps the neighbor's place and finds out whether the stranger needs a different kind of help.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Talia waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Gwen Evans

AH-R0155 · Age 67 · Alterations shop owner · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “My first shop had a changing screen made from a striped duvet.” On Wednesday, near Juniper Street, Gwen knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I promised free alterations for life in my first advert. This is the first customer to hold me to it.”

**What Gwen wants.** To honor an old alterations promise without charging the new bride twice.

**Family.** [Tali James](../04/12-the-ladder-crossing.md#ah-r0568) is Gwen’s cousin’s child. Gwen remembers the birthdays. Tali asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The ladder crossing*, Tali says, “I recognized the painter from the night school I quit.”

**People in this story.** [Cian Reese](#ah-r0156). They bring the lifetime fitting promise to the customer's chair.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Gwen has a long silver braid, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Gwen squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- The bride wants to keep the old pocket. Gwen leaves the pocket in place while the bride tries the dress on.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Gwen repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Gwen listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Cian Reese

AH-R0156 · Age 76 · Retired school cook · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I fed schoolchildren who are now old enough to cook for me.” On Wednesday, near the downtown fountain, Cian keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I can't stand long enough for a fitting. The bride carried the mirror to my chair.”

**What Cian wants.** To finish the fitting seated with a mirror she can actually use.

**Family.** [Perry Han](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0027) is Cian’s first cousin once removed. Cian is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Perry calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The small signature*, Perry says, “I was the one who cropped the vendor out of the press picture.”

**People in this story.** [Gwen Evans](#ah-r0155). They bring the lifetime fitting promise to the customer's chair. Also in the story is [Briar Mann](#ah-r0151) (dressmaker).

**Personality.** Cian is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Cian loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Cian has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Look for soft white hair brushed sideways. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is an olive canvas jacket with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Cian draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to hide the changed seam. Cian asks the bride herself what should show above the hem.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Cian asks the cashier quietly which item can be set aside without making the customer explain their finances to the line.
- If somebody calls for applause, Cian gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Jody Baker

AH-R0157 · Age 49 · Women's refuge volunteer · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I first kept emergency bus money under the wrapper in a sewing kit.” People around Ferris Deli know Jody by a small Monday habit. Jody asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I thought the small pocket held a ring. It held bus money in case she wanted to leave.”

**What Jody wants.** To keep a little travel money in the dress for the next wearer.

**Family.** [Robin Kiln](../01/15-the-empty-berth.md#ah-r0149) is Jody’s first cousin once removed. Jody chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Robin brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The empty berth*, Robin says, “My scholarship covers lessons and nothing you wear to them.”

**People in this story.** [Marco Fox](#ah-r0158). They preserve a pocket and a tie out of the same bolts of wedding cloth.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Jody takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Jody has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. In conversation Jody folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- The bride wants to keep the old pocket. Jody leaves the pocket in place while the bride tries the dress on.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Jody tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Jody asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Marco Fox

AH-R0158 · Age 71 · Retired typesetter · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I used to set wedding announcements without being invited to a single reception.” On Friday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Marco knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I was the first groom's witness. I still have the tie made from the offcut.”

**What Marco wants.** To get the wedding party one photograph of the matching tie.

**Family.** [Aidan Hayward](../03/11-the-widow-s-razor.md#ah-r0408) is Marco’s cousin raised in the same household. Marco chooses an ordinary story to tell. Aidan asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The widow's razor*, Aidan says, “I bought my first haircut with fish. The widow remembered the fish before she remembered me.”

**People in this story.** [Jody Baker](#ah-r0157). They preserve a pocket and a tie out of the same bolts of wedding cloth.

**Personality.** Marco can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Marco is easy to recognize by iron-gray waves and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges, Marco turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to hide the changed seam. Marco asks the bride herself what should show above the hem.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Marco repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Marco asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Parker Cho

AH-R0159 · Age 45 · Hotel housekeeper · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “Hotel work taught me that white silk goes yellow fastest where people hold it.” On Thursday, near Maré Coffee, Parker likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I mistook the worn hem for dirt and almost scrubbed away the original chalk marks.”

**What Parker wants.** To save the chalk marks on a piece that can come out before washing.

**Family.** [Tariq Hamilton](../07/09-a-tenant-who-stays.md#ah-r0987) is Parker’s stepsibling. Parker checks the date twice. Tariq brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A tenant who stays*, Tariq says, “I thought my father had promised the house to me.”

**People in this story.** [Nell Graves](#ah-r0160). They photograph the original chalk before the hem is washed.

**Personality.** Parker can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Parker can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Parker is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Parker raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The bride wants to keep the old pocket. Parker leaves the pocket in place while the bride tries the dress on.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Parker offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Parker suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Nell Graves

AH-R0160 · Age 31 · Wedding photographer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “My first wedding assignment was my parents marrying each other a second time.” On Tuesday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Nell usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I've come to photograph the three waistlines. My own wedding pictures show nothing below our shoulders.”

**What Nell wants.** To photograph all three women's changes to the same garment.

**Family.** [Toby Diaz](../05/05-the-clean-ledger.md#ah-r0647) is Nell’s half-sibling. Nell brings a spare shopping bag. Toby chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The clean ledger*, Toby says, “I sell drugs and used to think settling money settled everything.”

**People in this story.** [Parker Cho](#ah-r0159). They photograph the original chalk before the hem is washed.

**Personality.** Nell carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Nell has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Nell looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to hide the changed seam. Nell asks the bride herself what should show above the hem.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Nell offers to help correct the printed name and leaves it to the colleague whether any public explanation is wanted.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Nell offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.
