## Kira Ortiz

AH-R0261 · Age 69 · Retired house painter · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I once painted a storm door the same blue as my daughter's pram.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the bank steps, where Kira takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I used to see my daughter's kitchen from the shelter. Our wave was the only conversation some weeks.”

**What Kira wants.** To keep a small morning ritual with the daughter despite the move.

**Family.** [Jay Montoya](../05/03-the-trust-with-a-typo.md#ah-r0629) is Kira’s younger cousin. Kira offers the first invitation. Jay gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The trust with a typo*, Jay says, “I received this scholarship years ago. I returned the typo letter with one month's pay enclosed.”

**People in this story.** [Jamie Calloway](#ah-r0266). They compare the morning wave lost at one window with the silence gained at another.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Kira takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a striped cotton shirt and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Kira has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with silvery hair gathered at the nape. Kira raises one eyebrow long before saying yes during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A driver goes past the old stop in the rain. Kira stays with the stranded rider and helps check the next usable stop.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Kira asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Kira asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Alicia Nielsen

AH-R0262 · Age 36 · Early hotel porter · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I have opened a hotel before sunrise for fourteen winters.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Alicia by a small Wednesday habit. Alicia looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I stood at the old stop for three days. Each day a different person waited with me so I wouldn't look foolish.”

**What Alicia wants.** To make the old stop a useful meeting point during the change.

**Family.** [Ellen Soto](../10/07-an-empty-dedication.md#ah-r1414) is Alicia’s cousin. Alicia puts the kettle on. Ellen chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *An empty dedication*, Ellen says, “I am a character in the book by accident. I want the dedication to leave my real name alone.”

**People in this story.** [Stella Hull](#ah-r0269). An old-stop regular walks the new wheelchair turn alongside its advocate.

**Personality.** Alicia can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Alicia is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Usually dressed in a washed violet jersey with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges, Alicia folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The route manager declares the move a success. Alicia asks one rider from each corner to walk the new distance.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Alicia asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Alicia asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Agnes Kahn

AH-R0263 · Age 32 · Systems tester · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I tested caption screens because broadcasts got my mother's name wrong.” On Monday, near Breakwater Way, Agnes stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “The new stop faces the sun. I can read the driver's lips for the first time.”

**What Agnes wants.** To show the planner where the light helps passengers read lips.

**Family.** [Geraldine Andrade](../10/14-the-last-home-visit.md#ah-r1489) is Agnes’s older relative by marriage. Agnes chooses an ordinary story to tell. Geraldine asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The last home visit*, Geraldine says, “I hate being called darling and will miss the only person who could get away with it.”

**People in this story.** [Lou Arnett](#ah-r0265). The planner learns why the new morning light matters to a lip reader.

**Personality.** Agnes can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Agnes can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Agnes has short copper curls, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. When somebody else has the floor, Agnes gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- A driver goes past the old stop in the rain. Agnes stays with the stranded rider and helps check the next usable stop.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Agnes checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Agnes suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Nelson Carbone

AH-R0264 · Age 56 · Coffee vendor · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “My first cart belonged to a sailor who made very weak coffee.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Nelson by a small Saturday habit. Nelson watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I sell coffee on the old corner. My first customer is now a pensioner who only comes to talk.”

**What Nelson wants.** To give the cart a new set of regulars without losing the old neighbor.

**Family.** [Samuel Molina](../05/04-one-clean-day.md#ah-r0636) is Nelson’s cousin. Nelson checks the date twice. Samuel brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *One clean day*, Samuel says, “I only knew my partner's working voice. I heard his school nickname and he sounded fourteen.”

**People in this story.** [Teal Holmes](#ah-r0270). The empty coffee corner finally has an errand for the person who found the ring.

**Personality.** Nelson carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Nelson has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for close-cropped dark curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cream pullover with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation Nelson turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- The route manager declares the move a success. Nelson asks one rider from each corner to walk the new distance.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Nelson asks what the friend actually said last time and checks whether anybody is expecting them at home.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Nelson offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Lou Arnett

AH-R0265 · Age 42 · Bus stop planner · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I learned transport planning on the bus I took to two part-time jobs.” Saturday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Lou stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I set the distance in a planning office. My father made me walk it with his groceries.”

**What Lou wants.** To walk the twenty paces with groceries before signing off.

**Family.** [Franklin Brandt](../03/01-spin-cycle-choir.md#ah-r0309) is Lou’s stepsibling. Lou brings a spare shopping bag. Franklin chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Spin cycle choir*, Franklin says, “I wanted the noisy bearing replaced. Hearing the song hasn't changed my mind about my night shift.”

**People in this story.** [Agnes Kahn](#ah-r0263). The planner learns why the new morning light matters to a lip reader. Also in the story is [Stella Hull](#ah-r0269) (access reviewer).

**Personality.** Lou has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a little coil of soft measuring tape. Lou has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Lou looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A driver goes past the old stop in the rain. Lou stays with the stranded rider and helps check the next usable stop.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Lou walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Lou says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Jamie Calloway

AH-R0266 · Age 57 · After-school childminder · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I keep stray mittens sorted by school because the children change stop every term.” People around Ferris Deli know Jamie by a small Friday habit. Jamie asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “Our porch got quieter. I miss knowing school is out by the sudden noise.”

**What Jamie wants.** To keep one ordinary after-school sound in the house.

**Family.** [Benjamin Sun](../09/13-the-school-photograph-that-travels.md#ah-r1328) is Jamie’s younger cousin. Jamie asks before bringing somebody along. Benjamin can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The school photograph that travels*, Benjamin says, “I taught her to swim at Pier Beach. I didn't recognize the quiet uniformed girl with her hair dry.”

**People in this story.** [Kira Ortiz](#ah-r0261). They compare the morning wave lost at one window with the silence gained at another.

**Personality.** Jamie treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Jamie lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Jamie is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Jamie folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The route manager declares the move a success. Jamie asks one rider from each corner to walk the new distance.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Jamie alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Jamie says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Ada Ellis

AH-R0267 · Age 31 · Street merchant · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I sold shoe polish from a crate before the stall had a roof.” On Monday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Ada checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I gave directions using the old stop. A stranger arrived late for a reunion and I want to write to the host.”

**What Ada wants.** To send proper directions to the visitor who missed the reunion.

**Family.** [Jody Thomas](../09/08-the-other-side-of-the-cove.md#ah-r1279) is Ada’s cousin raised in the same household. Ada makes room at the table. Jody brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The other side of the cove*, Jody says, “I am hoping for a quiet place to feed my baby.”

**People in this story.** [Gareth Arrieta](#ah-r0268). They trade better directions for the warning sound that once timed the morning toast.

**Personality.** Ada is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Ada is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a washed violet jersey and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Ada has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with short black hair swept back. Ada folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A driver goes past the old stop in the rain. Ada stays with the stranded rider and helps check the next usable stop.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Ada asks the cashier quietly which item can be set aside without making the customer explain their finances to the line.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Ada agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Gareth Arrieta

AH-R0268 · Age 46 · Overnight nurse · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “Night nursing taught me which breakfast places keep the lights low.” Sunday often includes a stop at Juniper Street, where Gareth knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I listen for the doors before putting toast down. Now I burn it every morning and blame the council.”

**What Gareth wants.** To get toast and shift time back into sensible order.

**Family.** [Daryl Akers](../05/05-the-clean-ledger.md#ah-r0643) is Gareth’s half-sibling. Gareth saves a place. Daryl chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The clean ledger*, Daryl says, “I learned to read in prison. My own name on that page was the first word I didn't want to see.”

**People in this story.** [Ada Ellis](#ah-r0267). They trade better directions for the warning sound that once timed the morning toast.

**Personality.** Gareth tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Gareth will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Gareth has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Out of work clothes, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Gareth looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- The route manager declares the move a success. Gareth asks one rider from each corner to walk the new distance.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Gareth looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Gareth repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Stella Hull

AH-R0269 · Age 51 · Access consultant · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I first measured pavement slopes with a marble and a plumb line.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Stella takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “My wheelchair fits the new turn. I brought the planners cake and asked them to hear the old riders too.”

**What Stella wants.** To preserve the easier wheelchair turn and hear the displaced riders.

**Family.** [Dario Flint](../01/08-a-tide-at-noon.md#ah-r0072) is Stella’s second cousin. A relative kept reporting household news on postcards long after everybody could afford to phone. Reading one takes the family back to an ordinary morning rather than a big event. Stella saves apparently unimportant details. Asked about *A tide at noon*, Dario says, “I let children freeze the turrets in my cart.”

**People in this story.** [Alicia Nielsen](#ah-r0262). An old-stop regular walks the new wheelchair turn alongside its advocate. Also in the story is [Lou Arnett](#ah-r0265) (route planner).

**Personality.** Stella is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Stella asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Stella has brown hair pinned away from the temples, one cheek that creases before the other, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Stella holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A driver goes past the old stop in the rain. Stella stays with the stranded rider and helps check the next usable stop.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Stella asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Stella accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Teal Holmes

AH-R0270 · Age 70 · Retired utility worker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I repaired the original shelter after the wind tore off its timetable.” Saturday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Teal prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I found a ring under the old shelter. The date inside matches a couple who used to catch separate buses.”

**What Teal wants.** To return the found ring to the right couple.

**Family.** [Eden Song](../06/07-reading-the-payslip.md#ah-r0820) is Teal’s younger cousin. Teal keeps the photographs. Eden knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Reading the payslip*, Eden says, “I found a pension contribution and called my father.”

**People in this story.** [Nelson Carbone](#ah-r0264). The empty coffee corner finally has an errand for the person who found the ring.

**Personality.** Teal is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Teal is easy to recognize by silvery hair gathered at the nape and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf, Teal looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The route manager declares the move a success. Teal asks one rider from each corner to walk the new distance.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Teal asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Teal thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.
