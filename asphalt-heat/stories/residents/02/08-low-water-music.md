**Lighthouse Cove.** At Lighthouse Cove an exposed rock produces a hollow note at low tide. A visiting choir asks who first named it.

## Teresa Nielsen

AH-R0221 · Age 38 · Choir singer and receptionist · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I sang my first solo behind a curtain because my shoes were borrowed.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the downtown fountain, where Teresa keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “My mother called it the kettle. I want to hear her recorded laugh alongside whatever name wins.”

**What Teresa wants.** To put the family's kettle name into the choir's programme.

**Family.** [Jasmine Boyd](../06/14-care-with-a-clock.md#ah-r0888) is Teresa’s second cousin. Teresa brings a spare shopping bag. Jasmine chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Care with a clock*, Jasmine says, “I borrowed a stopwatch to prove a point and found our client has a wonderful laugh at minute thirteen.”

**People in this story.** [Jules Moran](#ah-r0230). The child's corrected note can sit beside the singer's memory of a mother's laugh.

**Personality.** Teresa thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Teresa sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. Teresa has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Teresa listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The choir wants to claim the rock's official name. Teresa asks for the neighborhood names to stay with the choir's name.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Teresa repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Teresa asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Percy Andrade

AH-R0222 · Age 68 · Retired factory turner · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “We worked the night the cove froze at its very edge.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Percy by a small Friday habit. Percy looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I scraped my knee there and have called it a much ruder name since. The choir wants to use it.”

**What Percy wants.** To teach the choir the name adults only use in bad weather.

**Family.** [Lola Jeong](../04/01-the-button-card.md#ah-r0460) is Percy’s younger cousin. Percy asks before bringing somebody along. Lola can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The button card*, Lola says, “I came to return a button my wife thought unlucky.”

**People in this story.** [Ruben Keller](#ah-r0225). They decide which decidedly unofficial name can appear on the choir sheet.

**Personality.** Percy gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Percy privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Percy has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for salt-and-pepper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a narrow-striped sweater with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Percy rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A child asks to listen with bare feet. Percy checks where the wet stone is stable and lets the child listen from there.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Percy walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Percy offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Joseph Randall

AH-R0223 · Age 41 · Dance accompanist · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I kept rhythm for dancers for years before wearing hearing aids.” On Sunday, near Juniper Street, Joseph knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I can only hear it through my bare heels. I'm asking the singers to take off their shoes.”

**What Joseph wants.** To let listeners feel the rock's note through bare feet.

**Family.** [Ramon Lloyd](../08/11-the-hair-after-illness.md#ah-r1159) is Joseph’s cousin. Joseph makes room at the table. Ramon brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The hair after illness*, Ramon says, “I am a barber losing my own hair. Today the customer told me she could see the shape of my head too.”

**People in this story.** [Eric Hassan](#ah-r0227). They compare a sound felt through feet with the old story about a fog bell.

**Personality.** Joseph uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Joseph well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Joseph has short copper curls, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, an olive canvas jacket. It usually goes with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. When somebody else has the floor, Joseph turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- The choir wants to claim the rock's official name. Joseph asks for the neighborhood names to stay with the choir's name.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Joseph asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- After a family quarrel, Joseph brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Kathleen Lu

AH-R0224 · Age 27 · Composer and dishwasher · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “The first tune I sold was commissioned to advertise a leaking fountain.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Studio Boulevard, where Kathleen waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I wrote a tune around the note and discovered it changes when the rock is wet. I need two endings.”

**What Kathleen wants.** To write separate endings for wet rock and dry rock.

**Family.** [Cal Esmond](../01/07-an-unfinished-atlas.md#ah-r0069) is Kathleen’s second cousin. Kathleen can stop the walk at any corner. Cal chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *An unfinished atlas*, Cal says, “My funding depends on mapping a vanished street.”

**People in this story.** [Lina Carey](#ah-r0226). The changing wet note gives the marker painter another reason to leave the rock alone. Also in the story is [Jules Moran](#ah-r0230) (choir accompanist).

**Personality.** Kathleen feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a moss-green cardigan and a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. Kathleen has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. Kathleen turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child asks to listen with bare feet. Kathleen checks where the wet stone is stable and lets the child listen from there.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Kathleen offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Kathleen lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Ruben Keller

AH-R0225 · Age 55 · Club secretary · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “Our club borrowed its first ledger from a defunct fishworkers' society.” Monday often includes a stop at the steps at Pier Beach, where Ruben watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “Our boat club wants an official name. I brought nine unofficial ones on a damp receipt.”

**What Ruben wants.** To publish the nine old names beside the club's proposal.

**Family.** [Sasha Crane](../03/10-the-broken-till.md#ah-r0400) is Ruben’s half-sibling. Ruben picks the meeting place. Sasha sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The broken till*, Sasha says, “I wasn't hungry. I joined the queue because it felt like the first real conversation since my divorce.”

**People in this story.** [Percy Andrade](#ah-r0222). They decide which decidedly unofficial name can appear on the choir sheet.

**Personality.** Ruben treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Ruben is easy to recognize by dark brown curls cut close behind the ears and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a washed violet jersey with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Ruben counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The choir wants to claim the rock's official name. Ruben asks for the neighborhood names to stay with the choir's name.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Ruben asks each person to say what they believe is actually owed before anybody proposes a payment.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Ruben changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Lina Carey

AH-R0226 · Age 35 · Coastal volunteer and plasterer · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I learned to follow crabs under the rocks before I knew the tide had a timetable.” On Friday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Lina notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I climbed down to paint a marker and saw a crab with eggs. I carried the paint back unopened.”

**What Lina wants.** To keep paint and visitors away from the crab eggs.

**Family.** [Rae Kelly](../03/15-the-tide-clock.md#ah-r0448) is Lina’s stepsibling. Lina keeps the photographs. Rae knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The tide clock*, Rae says, “I left a note saying see you at high. My old friend understood and the receptionist didn't.”

**People in this story.** [Kathleen Lu](#ah-r0224). The changing wet note gives the marker painter another reason to leave the rock alone.

**Personality.** Lina is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Lina is concerned.

**Appearance.** Lina has a blunt cut just below the ears, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a mustard canvas overshirt. It usually goes with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. When somebody else has the floor, Lina folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A child asks to listen with bare feet. Lina checks where the wet stone is stable and lets the child listen from there.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Lina takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- When someone invents an answer, Lina asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Eric Hassan

AH-R0227 · Age 54 · Garage estimator · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I left garage work for a year to look after my brother's little boys.” Saturday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Eric often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I thought the sound came from the old fog bell. That's what my brother told me when I was small.”

**What Eric wants.** To ask the brother how the fog-bell story started.

**Family.** [Hamish Yeo](../10/07-an-empty-dedication.md#ah-r1415) is Eric’s stepsibling. Eric makes the call. Hamish is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *An empty dedication*, Hamish says, “I paid the electricity through a lean winter.”

**People in this story.** [Joseph Randall](#ah-r0223). They compare a sound felt through feet with the old story about a fog bell.

**Personality.** Eric loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and one cuff folded back twice. Eric has one cheek that creases before the other, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Eric counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The choir wants to claim the rock's official name. Eric asks for the neighborhood names to stay with the choir's name.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Eric separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Eric offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Shay Poole

AH-R0228 · Age 61 · Postcard artist · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “My first coastal postcard was printed with the lighthouse facing the wrong way.” On Sunday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Shay notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I sell postcards of a silent cove. I'm leaving a blank on the next edition for people to write what they heard.”

**What Shay wants.** To leave room on a postcard for a visitor's own sound.

**Family.** [Josephine Fields](../04/07-a-borrowed-name.md#ah-r0512) is Shay’s second cousin. Shay leaves the first voice message. Josephine calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A borrowed name*, Josephine says, “I thought my mother had told the author our story.”

**People in this story.** [Sam Kaur](#ah-r0229). A postcard with an empty line lets the visiting aunt use her own word for the cove.

**Personality.** Shay enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Shay is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Shay has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with silver temples and a dark fringe. Shay listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child asks to listen with bare feet. Shay checks where the wet stone is stable and lets the child listen from there.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Shay moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Shay keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Sam Kaur

AH-R0229 · Age 30 · Bilingual tour guide · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “My aunt taught me the inland word for the low-tide smell.” Monday often includes a stop at the bank steps, where Sam takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I miss my first language most when somebody asks me to name a thing. I've invited my aunt to answer.”

**What Sam wants.** To give the aunt the first word when the name goes public.

**Family.** [Faye Tran](../07/13-a-dance-for-two-fathers.md#ah-r1026) is Sam’s older cousin. Sam chooses the familiar route. Faye can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *A dance for two fathers*, Faye says, “I trained both men on separate nights. Neither wanted the other to know how scared he was.”

**People in this story.** [Shay Poole](#ah-r0228). A postcard with an empty line lets the visiting aunt use her own word for the cove.

**Personality.** Sam is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Sam has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with one cuff folded back twice. When somebody else has the floor, Sam starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- The choir wants to claim the rock's official name. Sam asks for the neighborhood names to stay with the choir's name.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Sam brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Sam helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Jules Moran

AH-R0230 · Age 39 · Children's choir pianist · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I can coax a note from a child who refuses every ordinary singing exercise.” On Wednesday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Jules takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “The choir got the note wrong on purpose so the children would correct them. My shy son sang out first.”

**What Jules wants.** To hear the shy child sing the corrected note again.

**Family.** [Noor Khan](../08/04-first-shift-back.md#ah-r1081) is Jules’s second cousin. Jules brings the paper and a pen. Noor decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *First shift back*, Noor says, “I thought coming back meant picking up exactly where I left.”

**People in this story.** [Teresa Nielsen](#ah-r0221). The child's corrected note can sit beside the singer's memory of a mother's laugh. Also in the story is [Kathleen Lu](#ah-r0224) (composer).

**Personality.** Jules finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Jules has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Jules gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child asks to listen with bare feet. Jules checks where the wet stone is stable and lets the child listen from there.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Jules gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Jules waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.
