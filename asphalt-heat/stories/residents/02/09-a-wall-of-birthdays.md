## Victor Sun

AH-R0231 · Age 71 · Retired laboratory assistant · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I used to check other people's scales using a little weight in my pocket.” On Monday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Victor takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I want to measure myself again. Chemotherapy stole a little of my height and a lot of my nerve.”

**What Victor wants.** To add one present-day height mark after treatment.

**Family.** [Cato Hull](../01/08-a-tide-at-noon.md#ah-r0078) is Victor’s younger relative by marriage. Victor is free to hang up first. Cato makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A tide at noon*, Cato says, “On my break I taught three children how water moves around a pier.”

**People in this story.** [Stefan Tate](#ah-r0235). A new adult height mark fits beside names the former childminder still remembers.

**Personality.** Victor can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a faded indigo overshirt and a narrow brass watch set a little fast. Victor has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with tightly curled gray hair. Victor leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The landlord wants the height marks covered today. Victor offers to help trace the height marks before work continues.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Victor draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Victor asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Grey Good

AH-R0232 · Age 63 · Widowed shift supervisor · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I ran shifts through a winter when the whole family lived in two rooms.” Sunday often includes a stop at the downtown fountain, where Grey keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I papered over the wall because my boy never reached his next mark. His sister wants hers back.”

**What Grey wants.** To give the surviving daughter back the marks she remembers.

**Family.** [Keiko Ruiz](../05/10-midnight-cinnamon.md#ah-r0700) is Grey’s half-sibling. Grey brings a spare shopping bag. Keiko chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Midnight cinnamon*, Keiko says, “I have the recipe nobody else remembers. It calls for leaving one bun on a lonely neighbor's step.”

**People in this story.** [Vera Hardy](#ah-r0238). The decision about uncovering a dead child's mark needs the tenant, as well as the landlord, in the room. Also in the story is [Sam Collins](#ah-r0237) (trace maker).

**Personality.** Grey can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Grey can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Grey has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Look for a neat white crop. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a brown corduroy jacket with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Grey counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks for a tracing to take away. Grey asks which pencil marks the family recognizes as their own.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Grey asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Grey suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Alex Carr

AH-R0233 · Age 48 · Language tutor · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I learned my spouse's language badly enough to make their father laugh.” People around the bank steps know Alex by a small Monday habit. Alex takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I grew up elsewhere and married into this house. There's finally room to write my name at my adult height.”

**What Alex wants.** To add the married-in family member's first pencil mark.

**Family.** [Anton March](../03/02-a-ship-without-postcards.md#ah-r0312) is Alex’s cousin raised in the same household. Alex asks before bringing somebody along. Anton can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A ship without postcards*, Anton says, “I want a picture of the crane shadows. My kid thinks the ships do all the lifting.”

**People in this story.** [Kai Shah](#ah-r0239). A newly married name and an old nickname fit on the same tracing.

**Personality.** Alex carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Alex has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for a high puff tied with plain cord. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Alex leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- The landlord wants the height marks covered today. Alex offers to help trace the height marks before work continues.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Alex offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Alex offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Nora Ford

AH-R0234 · Age 67 · Office cleaner · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I cleaned the place where my sister had won her first prizes.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Nora by a small Friday habit. Nora prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I learned my sister cheated by wearing shoes. Her defense is that they were our mother's heels.”

**What Nora wants.** To reconstruct how high the old shoes made the sister stand.

**Family.** [Adela Oh](../03/15-the-tide-clock.md#ah-r0445) is Nora’s stepsibling. Nora makes room at the table. Adela brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The tide clock*, Adela says, “I painted the moon on it using my wedding ring as a stencil.”

**People in this story.** [Cedric Arrieta](#ah-r0236). A torn date helps settle the argument about wearing high heels to be measured.

**Personality.** Nora has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Nora has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with tightly curled gray hair. Nora touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks for a tracing to take away. Nora asks which pencil marks the family recognizes as their own.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Nora sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Nora says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Stefan Tate

AH-R0235 · Age 77 · Retired childminder · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I minded seven children who pretended not to know me when they grew tall.” On Friday, near Harbour Bakery, Stefan buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I measured every child who lived here while their parents worked. I can identify nine initials nobody else knows.”

**What Stefan wants.** To identify the children behind the remaining initials.

**Family.** [Lionel Walker](../06/03-money-after-marriage.md#ah-r0780) is Stefan’s first cousin once removed. At a family wedding the hired photographer missed the older relatives entirely. They assembled a second album from pictures taken over shoulders at the kitchen door. Stefan looks for the people making the event possible. Asked about *Money after marriage*, Lionel says, “I trained the night manager to cover for family emergencies.”

**People in this story.** [Victor Sun](#ah-r0231). A new adult height mark fits beside names the former childminder still remembers.

**Personality.** Stefan treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Stefan lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Stefan is easy to recognize by steel-colored hair cut square above the collar and full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Stefan rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The landlord wants the height marks covered today. Stefan offers to help trace the height marks before work continues.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Stefan asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Stefan says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Cedric Arrieta

AH-R0236 · Age 39 · Decorator · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “The first wallpaper I ever hung concealed my own school drawing.” People around Maré Coffee know Cedric by a small Sunday habit. Cedric likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I took down one sheet too fast and split a date. I'm looking for the boy born that spring.”

**What Cedric wants.** To restore the damaged date with someone who remembers the spring.

**Family.** [Quinn Avery](../09/13-the-school-photograph-that-travels.md#ah-r1324) is Cedric’s younger cousin. Cedric picks the meeting place. Quinn sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The school photograph that travels*, Quinn says, “I am moving to another coast and the picture is proof there was a time when my whole world fitted on two rows.”

**People in this story.** [Nora Ford](#ah-r0234). A torn date helps settle the argument about wearing high heels to be measured.

**Personality.** Cedric is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Cedric is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Cedric is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Cedric gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks for a tracing to take away. Cedric asks which pencil marks the family recognizes as their own.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Cedric gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Cedric agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Sam Collins

AH-R0237 · Age 29 · Glass engraver · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I engraved the name of somebody not yet born onto my first order.” People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Sam by a small Saturday habit. Sam checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I put the marks on transparent paper so families who rent can take their years with them.”

**What Sam wants.** To make a tracing the renters can take to their next home.

**Family.** [Hugo Paul](../03/14-the-housecoat-commission.md#ah-r0433) is Sam’s parent’s cousin. Sam keeps the photographs. Hugo knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The housecoat commission*, Hugo says, “My partner left me three silk ties. They're becoming the pockets she specifically asked for.”

**People in this story.** [Jaime Holt](#ah-r0240). They design a sheet that can move house on the back of a child's growing board. Also in the story is [Grey Good](#ah-r0232) (wall custodian).

**Personality.** Sam tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Sam will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Sam has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Sam looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The landlord wants the height marks covered today. Sam offers to help trace the height marks before work continues.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Sam moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Sam repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Vera Hardy

AH-R0238 · Age 54 · Tenants' representative · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “Our old landlord sold the building complete with our borrowed curtains.” On Sunday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Vera notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “My landlord says the wall belongs to him. I'd like him to meet the grandmother who owns the pencil.”

**What Vera wants.** To let the landlord hear from the grandmother who owned the pencil.

**Family.** [Tali Allen](../05/07-a-shelf-of-apologies.md#ah-r0661) is Vera’s half-sibling. Vera makes the call. Tali is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A shelf of apologies*, Tali says, “I am giving back a detective novel I stole to impress a girl who hated mysteries.”

**People in this story.** [Grey Good](#ah-r0232). The decision about uncovering a dead child's mark needs the tenant, as well as the landlord, in the room.

**Personality.** Vera is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Vera asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Vera has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation Vera listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks for a tracing to take away. Vera asks which pencil marks the family recognizes as their own.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Vera asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Vera accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Kai Shah

AH-R0239 · Age 47 · Estate appraiser · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I once valued a house my family had been turned away from viewing.” On Friday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Kai can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I came to negotiate a sale and discovered my own childhood nickname behind the radiator.”

**What Kai wants.** To save the nickname before the buyer redecorates.

**Family.** [Ariel Morse](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1304) is Kai’s cousin raised in the same household. Kai leaves the first voice message. Ariel calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Ariel says, “I funded the advert and expected elegant hands.”

**People in this story.** [Alex Carr](#ah-r0233). A newly married name and an old nickname fit on the same tracing.

**Personality.** Kai is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Kai has a blunt cut just below the ears, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a charcoal zip cardigan. It usually goes with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. When somebody else has the floor, Kai looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- The landlord wants the height marks covered today. Kai offers to help trace the height marks before work continues.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Kai offers to cover one concrete expense and leaves the friend room to say the expense isn't the real problem.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Kai thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Jaime Holt

AH-R0240 · Age 37 · Traveling catering worker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “My children remember the colors of old flats better than their addresses.” People around Juniper Street know Jaime by a small Thursday habit. Jaime knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “My kids have moved five times. We're starting their marks on a board we can carry through any door.”

**What Jaime wants.** To build a traveling height board for the children.

**Family.** [Damian Fadel](../08/02-land-without-cranes.md#ah-r1070) is Jaime’s cousin. Jaime chooses the familiar route. Damian can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Land without cranes*, Damian says, “I have the keys and no vote. I have offered every voter an hour inside on a working day.”

**People in this story.** [Sam Collins](#ah-r0237). They design a sheet that can move house on the back of a child's growing board.

**Personality.** Jaime loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Jaime remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Jaime is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Jaime presses the lips together to keep a smile in when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A family asks for a tracing to take away. Jaime asks which pencil marks the family recognizes as their own.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Jaime takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When invited to tell the story later, Jaime includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.
