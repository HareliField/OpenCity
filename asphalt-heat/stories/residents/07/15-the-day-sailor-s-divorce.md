## Gareth Parker

AH-R1041 · Age 47 · Separated bookkeeper · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my son's first good conversation on a boat with no wind.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Gareth by a small Friday habit. Gareth prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I don't want the boat. I want the one time a week my son talks to me without looking down.”

**What Gareth wants.** To keep a weekly conversation with the son afloat.

**Family.** [Jamie Jiang](../03/11-the-widow-s-razor.md#ah-r0401) is Gareth’s older cousin. Gareth asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Jamie chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The widow's razor*, Jamie says, “I was the last customer she shaved. I asked the widow why there was still a little soap in the hinge.”

**People in this story.** [Pablo Roy](#ah-r1042) (former spouse). Former spouses decide whether a Sunday afloat and an hour repairing ashore have equal weight.

**Personality.** Gareth often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Gareth have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Gareth is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a narrow brass watch set a little fast, Gareth gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants both separated adults on the same boat. Gareth asks each adult whether sharing one trip is possible without putting the child in the middle.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Gareth sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Gareth rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Pablo Roy

AH-R1042 · Age 45 · Boat fitter · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember mending sails while my family went out to celebrate.” People around Studio Boulevard know Pablo by a small Wednesday habit. Pablo waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I did every winter repair while my spouse sailed. I think dry-land hours ought to count.”

**What Pablo wants.** To count winter repair time fairly.

**Family.** [Jody Hewitt](../10/01-a-closing-hour.md#ah-r1360) is Pablo’s second cousin. Pablo checks the date twice. Jody brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A closing hour*, Jody says, “I was going to post a complaint. I wrote my week's opening hours on his birthday card because I need a day off too.”

**People in this story.** [Gareth Parker](#ah-r1041) (former spouse). Former spouses decide whether a Sunday afloat and an hour repairing ashore have equal weight.

**Personality.** Pablo thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Pablo sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Pablo is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Pablo listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A spouse asks to count repair hours toward Sunday time. Pablo checks the maintenance log before treating Sundays as the only contribution.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Pablo puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Pablo asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Dana Wong

AH-R1043 · Age 42 · Sailing syndicate member · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember taking messages between adults who shared the same key.” On Tuesday, near Harbour Bakery, Dana buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I co-own the boat and am tired of becoming a messenger between people who know each other's numbers.”

**What Dana wants.** To end the role of go-between.

**Family.** [Dale Lane](../08/09-the-third-bedroom.md#ah-r1132) is Dana’s first cousin once removed. Dana brings a spare shopping bag. Dale chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The third bedroom*, Dale says, “I would like a private room so I can cry without making him think it's his fault.”

**People in this story.** [Kieran Colón](#ah-r1048). A co-owner who refuses to relay messages still has to hear the proposal for a new boat name.

**Personality.** Dana gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Dana privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an olive canvas jacket and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Dana has one cheek that creases before the other, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Dana takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants both separated adults on the same boat. Dana asks each adult whether sharing one trip is possible without putting the child in the middle.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Dana asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Dana offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Jorge Crane

AH-R1044 · Age 69 · Retired sailmaker · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a wedding napkin embroidered with a boat's first name.” On Wednesday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Jorge watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I bought them the sail when they married. I told them dividing it into two napkins was an option.”

**What Jorge wants.** To leave the decision about the old sail to its owners.

**Family.** [Jasmine Laurent](../04/04-station-steps-in-rain.md#ah-r0485) is Jorge’s younger relative by marriage. Jorge is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Jasmine calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Station steps in rain*, Jasmine says, “I used to dread these steps. This time I came to collect my own repaired camera.”

**People in this story.** [Edwin Wilson](#ah-r1050). The wedding sail's donor asks what the children want on the first trip after the papers are signed.

**Personality.** Jorge uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Jorge well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Jorge has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. Back on the street, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Jorge touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A spouse asks to count repair hours toward Sunday time. Jorge checks the maintenance log before treating Sundays as the only contribution.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Jorge asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- After a family quarrel, Jorge brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Malcolm Hayes

AH-R1046 · Age 48 · Part-time home carer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember trying to swap weekends with another tired carer.” Sunday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Malcolm buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I can take Saturdays if somebody swaps my carer's weekend. It's the first practical proposal nobody cried over.”

**What Malcolm wants.** To swap a care weekend for a sailing Saturday.

**Family.** [Maya Abreu](../06/10-one-paid-trial.md#ah-r0841) is Malcolm’s younger relative by marriage. Malcolm brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Maya makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *One paid trial*, Maya says, “I learned with my grandmother's stove and have never worked on a timer.”

**People in this story.** [Quinn Laurent](#ah-r1047). A care rota and a maintenance log set the practical limits on who can sail when.

**Personality.** Malcolm treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a red windbreaker and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Malcolm has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Malcolm holds eye contact until the other person has finished during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A spouse asks to count repair hours toward Sunday time. Malcolm checks the maintenance log before treating Sundays as the only contribution.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Malcolm asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Malcolm changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

**People in this story.** [Jorge Crane](#ah-r1044). The wedding sail's donor asks what the children want on the first trip after the papers are signed.

**Personality.** Edwin is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Edwin has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. In conversation Edwin looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- A spouse asks to count repair hours toward Sunday time. Edwin checks the maintenance log before treating Sundays as the only contribution.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Edwin offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
