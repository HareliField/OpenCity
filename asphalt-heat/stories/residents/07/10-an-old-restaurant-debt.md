# An old restaurant debt

## Devin Mills

AH-R0991 · Age 65 · Family caterer · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember selling the last of my father's good crockery.” On Tuesday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Devin knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I sold the dead man's plates last month and finally had the money. The date still felt like Friday to me.”

**What Devin wants.** To settle the wake debt while the old cooks can hear it.

**Family.** [Opal Finch](../01/04-the-wrong-bicycle.md#ah-r0033) is Devin’s second cousin. Devin knows which ones can be shared. Opal keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The wrong bicycle*, Opal says, “I recognized the child's bell by the second ring it never makes.”

**People in this story.** [Amanda Walker](#ah-r0992) (adult child). Parent and grown child decide what the belated payment can do for another bereaved household.

**Personality.** Devin can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Devin can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Devin has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Look for gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Devin touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A family returns to settle the old wake invoice. Devin brings out the old book and lets the family say how they want the payment recorded.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Devin brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Devin suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Amanda Walker

AH-R0992 · Age 39 · Office receptionist · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cake served on a plate borrowed from our neighbors.” Friday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Amanda stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I was the child eating the cake. I want to pay for the next family whose grown-ups whisper at the till.”

**What Amanda wants.** To fund a meal for another bereaved family.

**Family.** [Martha Ueda](../06/14-care-with-a-clock.md#ah-r0884) is Amanda’s half-sibling. Amanda can always ask for five minutes alone. Martha asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Care with a clock*, Martha says, “I have been buying an extra fifteen minutes for my father without telling my sister I'm in debt.”

**People in this story.** [Devin Mills](#ah-r0991) (parent). Parent and grown child decide what the belated payment can do for another bereaved household.

**Personality.** Amanda carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a charcoal zip cardigan and one cuff folded back twice. Amanda has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Amanda squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The shop owners disagree on what to do with the payment. Amanda asks both owners for figures before anyone promises a fund or a repair.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Amanda offers an ordinary errand that lets the distressed person leave without being followed by an audience.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Amanda offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Jamie Grant

AH-R0993 · Age 68 · Retired deli cook · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember carving onions while grieving over an unfinished quarrel.” On Wednesday, near the downtown fountain, Jamie keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I cooked that wake after a fight with the man who died. I remember the onions better than the apology.”

**What Jamie wants.** To finish the apology the onions once interrupted.

**Family.** [Cynthia Hall](../06/11-a-chair-of-one-s-own.md#ah-r0858) is Jamie’s half-sibling. Jamie can stop the walk at any corner. Cynthia chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *A chair of one's own*, Cynthia says, “I am the mother lending the deposit. I'd like my daughter to stop describing family money as luck.”

**People in this story.** [Scout Saito](#ah-r0998). A cook remembers the quarrel while a debtor's relative remembers the meal that survived it.

**Personality.** Jamie has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Jamie has silver temples and a dark fringe, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a charcoal zip cardigan. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Jamie starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A family returns to settle the old wake invoice. Jamie brings out the old book and lets the family say how they want the payment recorded.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Jamie sits beside them without expecting a performance and asks if they want to go outside.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Jamie says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Annette Marquez

AH-R0994 · Age 37 · Deli bookkeeper · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember entering twenty years of invoices into one blank screen.” On Saturday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Annette notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I deleted the old invoice when we digitized. I'm delighted somebody kept a messier book.”

**What Annette wants.** To restore the invoice as a family record.

**Family.** [Jacqueline Darzi](../06/01-the-upstairs-lease.md#ah-r0753) is Annette’s second cousin. Annette chooses the music. Jacqueline puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The upstairs lease*, Jacqueline says, “I spent our deposit on the dental work that let me return to the counter.”

**People in this story.** [Finn Kahn](#ah-r0999). The recovered invoice gives co-owners figures for a serious disagreement about the money.

**Personality.** Annette treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Annette lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Annette has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Annette listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The shop owners disagree on what to do with the payment. Annette asks both owners for figures before anyone promises a fund or a repair.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Annette walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Annette says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Laura Osborne

AH-R0995 · Age 64 · Recently retired haulage worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember looking at my cousin's collection jar and walking away.” People around the brick passage off the shop row know Laura by a small Sunday habit. Laura takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I am a cousin who contributed nothing then. I want to help today without claiming I was always generous.”

**What Laura wants.** To help with today's payment honestly.

**Family.** [Sadie Liu](../06/13-the-almost-scholarship.md#ah-r0872) is Laura’s younger cousin. Laura chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Sadie brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The almost scholarship*, Sadie says, “I promised to cover books until I saw the price of a single score.”

**People in this story.** [Ethan Wells](#ah-r0996). A late contribution helps the family find the musician at the original wake.

**Personality.** Laura is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Laura is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Laura has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for silver temples and a dark fringe. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. In conversation Laura starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A family returns to settle the old wake invoice. Laura brings out the old book and lets the family say how they want the payment recorded.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Laura tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Laura agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Ethan Wells

AH-R0996 · Age 45 · Community musician · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember playing music nobody in the family had requested.” On Monday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Ethan prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I arrived expecting interest. The owner wants the name of the singer from the service.”

**What Ethan wants.** To identify the service singer.

**Family.** [Ava Boyd](../04/08-the-rescue-hut-mural.md#ah-r0530) is Ethan’s stepsibling. Ethan asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Ava remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The rescue hut mural*, Ava says, “I was going to clean the paint off for free.”

**People in this story.** [Laura Osborne](#ah-r0995). A late contribution helps the family find the musician at the original wake.

**Personality.** Ethan tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Ethan will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Ethan is easy to recognize by black hair twisted into a small knot and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Ethan turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The shop owners disagree on what to do with the payment. Ethan asks both owners for figures before anyone promises a fund or a repair.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Ethan asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Ethan repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Gerard Garza

AH-R0997 · Age 60 · Former hotel steward · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember coming home in somebody else's mourning coat.” Saturday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Gerard usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I lent the family my black coat and got the wrong one back. We recognized the lining over lunch.”

**What Gerard wants.** To return the borrowed mourning coat.

**Family.** [Miriam Lim](../10/07-an-empty-dedication.md#ah-r1416) is Gerard’s cousin. Gerard brings the paper and a pen. Miriam decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *An empty dedication*, Miriam says, “I taught the novelist to read. My favorite thing she wrote is still a vulgar note passed at school.”

**People in this story.** [Lars Tang](#ah-r1000). One exchange returns a coat, another passes down the old knowledge of carving.

**Personality.** Gerard is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Gerard asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a pen clipped inside the neckline. Gerard has fine brows over wide-set eyes, with short white hair and darker eyebrows. Gerard starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A family returns to settle the old wake invoice. Gerard brings out the old book and lets the family say how they want the payment recorded.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Gerard walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Gerard accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

**People in this story.** [Jamie Grant](#ah-r0993). A cook remembers the quarrel while a debtor's relative remembers the meal that survived it.

**Appearance.** Scout has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Scout looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- The shop owners disagree on what to do with the payment. Scout asks both owners for figures before anyone promises a fund or a repair.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Scout offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.

## Lars Tang

AH-R1000 · Age 72 · Retired butcher · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cutting my first ham with an old carpenter directing me.” On Wednesday, near Juniper Street, Lars knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I carved the ham years ago. Now my hands shake and the owner's son asked me to teach him where to start.”

**What Lars wants.** To teach the butcher's son the first proper slice.

**Family.** [Mika Tsai](../10/06-two-birthdays-in-one-ward.md#ah-r1410) is Lars’s younger cousin. Lars offers the first invitation. Mika gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *Two birthdays in one ward*, Mika says, “I was discharged that morning and returned as a visitor in my own coat.”

**People in this story.** [Gerard Garza](#ah-r0997). One exchange returns a coat, another passes down the old knowledge of carving.

**Personality.** Lars has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Lars has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for salt-and-pepper curls. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a gray fisher's sweater with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Lars rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The shop owners disagree on what to do with the payment. Lars asks both owners for figures before anyone promises a fund or a repair.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Lars suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- When somebody trades on a title, Lars asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.
