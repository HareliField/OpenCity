# The expensive summer

[The families who come back](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Pier Beach.** A boardwalk restaurant raises prices. A beach family that has marked every birthday there discovers it can only afford dessert.

## Gerard Ito

AH-R0971 · Age 52 · Family breadwinner and postal worker · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember choosing the special without hearing its price.” On Monday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Gerard notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I suggested pretending we'd already eaten. My mum turned the menu over and told the children the truth.”

**What Gerard wants.** To tell the children the birthday budget truthfully.

**Family.** [Tessa Abreu](../01/05-the-aquarium.md#ah-r0049) is Gerard’s half-sibling. Gerard is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Tessa calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The aquarium*, Tessa says, “My appointment with the glazier ended up on a pavement with our sleeves wet.”

**People in this story.** [Dora Villa](#ah-r0979). One relative wants to admit what supper costs. The other has already covered more than a fair share.

**Personality.** Gerard is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Gerard is concerned.

**Appearance.** Gerard is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a little coil of soft measuring tape, Gerard listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A waiter offers to split one birthday dessert among everybody. Gerard checks that the birthday guest likes the dessert before calculating the portions.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Gerard keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- When someone invents an answer, Gerard asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Eric Romero

AH-R0972 · Age 49 · Restaurant owner · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember replacing old menus by hand on the night of a rent rise.” People around Studio Boulevard know Eric by a small Monday habit. Eric waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I own the restaurant and cannot feed a table at last year's rent. I can still bring out their old candleholder.”

**What Eric wants.** To give the family their candleholder despite the small order.

**Family.** [Remy Rose](../06/10-one-paid-trial.md#ah-r0846) is Eric’s stepsibling. Eric calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Remy leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *One paid trial*, Remy says, “I can take morning deliveries for one week so both bakers get a fair turn at the oven.”

**People in this story.** [Esme Knight](#ah-r0975) (landlord). The restaurant and its landlord have to work out what another expensive summer actually earns.

**Personality.** Eric loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Eric is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it, Eric takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative tries to pay the whole restaurant bill in secret. Eric speaks to the host privately and lets them decide whether the bill is shared.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Eric asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Eric offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Barbara Jackson

AH-R0973 · Age 28 · Restaurant server · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember paying for a birthday dinner in restaurant tips.” Saturday often includes a stop at Lighthouse Cove, where Barbara takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I carry plates and survive on tips. The dessert table stayed two hours and tipped me in four currencies.”

**What Barbara wants.** To keep the dessert table welcome through closing.

**Family.** [Gareth Mercer](../09/12-the-driving-lesson-fund.md#ah-r1316) is Barbara’s half-sibling. Barbara leaves the first voice message. Gareth calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The driving lesson fund*, Gareth says, “I am the student who got the lesson. My employer tried to take the credit and I quietly corrected the newsletter.”

**People in this story.** [Elizabeth Campos](#ah-r0978). Both tips and an affordable birthday cake depend on the dessert table being welcomed.

**Personality.** Barbara enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Barbara is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Barbara has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. When somebody else has the floor, Barbara looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- A waiter offers to split one birthday dessert among everybody. Barbara checks that the birthday guest likes the dessert before calculating the portions.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Barbara clears the dishes, lets the angriest speaker cool off, and resumes the conversation with a smaller audience.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Barbara keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Toby Webb

AH-R0974 · Age 73 · Retired seamstress · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my youngest grandchild copying the way I count coins.” People around the corner by Juniper Tailor know Toby by a small Sunday habit. Toby looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I ordered the cheapest thing and hated realizing my grandson was doing the same calculation.”

**What Toby wants.** To order without making the grandchild conceal the same worry.

**Family.** [John Starr](../08/12-the-high-road-home.md#ah-r1167) is Toby’s second cousin. Toby chooses the familiar route. John can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The high road home*, John says, “I used to drive straight over every night until my companion died.”

**People in this story.** [Teresa Berg](#ah-r0980). A grandparent counting coins and the person celebrating would both like to change the subject.

**Personality.** Toby is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and one cuff folded back twice. Toby has a long face and high cheekbones, with soft white hair brushed sideways. Toby draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A relative tries to pay the whole restaurant bill in secret. Toby speaks to the host privately and lets them decide whether the bill is shared.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Toby tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Toby helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Esme Knight

AH-R0975 · Age 61 · Restaurant landlord · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sitting in my tenant's empty restaurant in good weather.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Esme checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I am the landlord. I counted empty tables during a summer evening for the first time.”

**What Esme wants.** To cost rent against a real summer evening.

**Family.** [Kathleen Miranda](../02/03-the-sandwich-board.md#ah-r0174) is Esme’s cousin raised in the same household. Esme can stop the walk at any corner. Kathleen chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The sandwich board*, Kathleen says, “I used to eat there on credit. I added my unpaid tab to the winning bid.”

**People in this story.** [Eric Romero](#ah-r0972) (restaurant tenant). The restaurant and its landlord have to work out what another expensive summer actually earns.

**Personality.** Esme finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Esme has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Look for two white streaks in otherwise dark hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation Esme listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A waiter offers to split one birthday dessert among everybody. Esme checks that the birthday guest likes the dessert before calculating the portions.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Esme asks each person to say what they believe is actually owed before anybody proposes a payment.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Esme waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Adriana Ingram

AH-R0976 · Age 45 · Cook · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember buying a retired chef the onions she said were essential.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Adriana by a small Saturday habit. Adriana can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I know the former chef's recipe. The family asked for it and I want her blessing before passing it on.”

**What Adriana wants.** To seek the original chef's permission for the recipe.

**Family.** [Rain Malik](../05/09-an-address-in-pencil.md#ah-r0682) is Adriana’s cousin. Adriana chooses whether to use it. Rain saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *An address in pencil*, Rain says, “I delivered flowers to the wrong widow. She walked them over and stayed for dinner.”

**People in this story.** [Cian Ingram](#ah-r0977). The old recipe and the public bench make two possible alternatives to a costly birthday meal.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Adriana is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Usually dressed in a washed violet jersey with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket, Adriana takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A relative tries to pay the whole restaurant bill in secret. Adriana speaks to the host privately and lets them decide whether the bill is shared.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Adriana suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Adriana listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Elizabeth Campos

AH-R0978 · Age 34 · Cake decorator · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cutting a sample cake into eleven sensible pieces.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Elizabeth by a small Monday habit. Elizabeth checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I make fancy cakes. I delivered one small enough to share without pretending it was a feast.”

**What Elizabeth wants.** To deliver a genuinely shareable small cake.

**Family.** [Rosa Vance](../08/15-the-skipper-goes-ashore.md#ah-r1195) is Elizabeth’s cousin raised in the same household. Elizabeth offers the first invitation. Rosa gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The skipper goes ashore*, Rosa says, “I have always been seasick. I told the skipper which bench has the best steady horizon for talking after a trip.”

**People in this story.** [Barbara Jackson](#ah-r0973). Both tips and an affordable birthday cake depend on the dessert table being welcomed.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Elizabeth takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Elizabeth has a dark undercut with a longer wave on top, a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, and a favorite piece of clothing, a washed violet jersey. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Elizabeth gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A relative tries to pay the whole restaurant bill in secret. Elizabeth speaks to the host privately and lets them decide whether the bill is shared.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Elizabeth calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Elizabeth asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Dora Villa

AH-R0979 · Age 47 · Boat surveyor · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember taking the bill from my father when I finally earned more than him.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Studio Boulevard, where Dora waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I used to be the kid with no money at this party. I overpaid the bill before anyone could stop me.”

**What Dora wants.** To take the pressure off the host without embarrassing them.

**Family.** [Cyrus Keller](../08/09-the-third-bedroom.md#ah-r1131) is Dora’s younger cousin. Dora puts the kettle on. Cyrus chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The third bedroom*, Cyrus says, “I have shared a room with my brother ever since the night we left.”

**People in this story.** [Gerard Ito](#ah-r0971). One relative wants to admit what supper costs. The other has already covered more than a fair share.

**Personality.** Dora can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Dora has one cheek that creases before the other, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Dora folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A waiter offers to split one birthday dessert among everybody. Dora checks that the birthday guest likes the dessert before calculating the portions.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Dora stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Dora asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Teresa Berg

AH-R0980 · Age 32 · Birthday celebrant and photographer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a birthday when everybody forgot to ask my favorite flavor.” On Saturday, near Breakwater Way, Teresa stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I want a birthday where the people at the table can argue about movies instead of arithmetic.”

**What Teresa wants.** To celebrate one birthday without counting portions aloud.

**Family.** [Ida Bruno](../05/07-a-shelf-of-apologies.md#ah-r0664) is Teresa’s older cousin. Teresa calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Ida leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A shelf of apologies*, Ida says, “I lent this book so a friend would have to see me again.”

**People in this story.** [Toby Webb](#ah-r0974). A grandparent counting coins and the person celebrating would both like to change the subject.

**Personality.** Teresa can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Teresa can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Teresa has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for close-cropped dark curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Teresa draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A relative tries to pay the whole restaurant bill in secret. Teresa speaks to the host privately and lets them decide whether the bill is shared.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Teresa offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Teresa suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.
