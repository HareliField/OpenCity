# A widow bids again

[The families who come back](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Old Town and Bank of the Coast.** A wealthy widow bids for the Old Town building where her late wife first rented a room. A tenants' group has put in its own offer.

## Priya Vance

AH-R0921 · Age 67 · Property millionaire and former importer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my wife's first room with one cracked window latch.” People around the downtown fountain know Priya by a small Wednesday habit. Priya keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I can outbid them without touching my everyday account. That fact has been keeping me awake.”

**What Priya wants.** To secure a future for the remembered room and its tenants.

**Family.** [Grace Reeves](../09/07-school-after-sixty.md#ah-r1263) is Priya’s younger cousin. Priya calls about good news first. Grace is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *School after sixty*, Grace says, “I am younger than everyone taking my class.”

**People in this story.** [Bianca Ward](#ah-r0924) (property broker). A bidder who can raise the price in seconds asks the broker to read her the actual names.

**Personality.** Priya is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Priya asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Priya has one cheek that creases before the other, with a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. Priya looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The millionaire bidder offers a private deal. Priya gets the offer written down where the tenant group can consider it.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Priya asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Priya accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Sadie Breen

AH-R0922 · Age 39 · Building caretaker · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my grandmother setting rent against a sack of potatoes.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Sadie by a small Sunday habit. Sadie watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I was the original landlady's granddaughter. I know which tenants paid her in groceries.”

**What Sadie wants.** To establish which tenants paid in goods.

**Family.** [Lennon Ahmed](../05/15-a-boat-for-sale-twice.md#ah-r0750) is Sadie’s stepsibling. Sadie knows which ones can be shared. Lennon keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A boat for sale twice*, Lennon says, “I answered both numbers. It was the first time our separated parents had asked me the same question all year.”

**People in this story.** [Morgan Freeman](#ah-r0927). Card leases and rents paid in groceries establish how long the tenants have already shaped the building.

**Personality.** Sadie is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an olive canvas jacket and a pen clipped inside the neckline. Sadie has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, with auburn hair cut square at the nape. Sadie folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant calls attention to damp during the viewing. Sadie allows a proper look at the damp before returning to the argument about price.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Sadie asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Sadie thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Anna Avila

AH-R0923 · Age 58 · Tenant cooperative treasurer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember signing the tenants' first shared repair request.” People around Tideline Market know Anna by a small Wednesday habit. Anna picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I want ownership shared because the widow's next wife might feel differently about us.”

**What Anna wants.** To get protection beyond the current donor's lifetime.

**Family.** [Leon Gao](../02/04-the-second-dog.md#ah-r0190) is Anna’s second cousin. Anna chooses the music. Leon puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The second dog*, Leon says, “I've made two tags with different sounds. You can tell who's coming down the stairs without looking.”

**People in this story.** [Albert Barclay](#ah-r0928). The tenant group adds a usable entry bench to the terms before backing any owner.

**Personality.** Anna loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Anna remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Anna has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Anna takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The millionaire bidder offers a private deal. Anna gets the offer written down where the tenant group can consider it.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Anna asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When invited to tell the story later, Anna includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Bianca Ward

AH-R0924 · Age 45 · Estate broker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember forgetting the name of the woman who cleaned a showing.” On Friday, near Harbour Bakery, Bianca buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I sell luxury property. This is the first bidder who asked me the names on the doorbells.”

**What Bianca wants.** To put the residents' names into the bidder's briefing.

**Family.** [Ida Mercer](../01/01-the-blue-suit.md#ah-r0001) is Bianca’s second cousin. Bianca is free to hang up first. Ida makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The blue suit*, Ida says, “I bought this place with the settlement from my bus accident.”

**People in this story.** [Priya Vance](#ah-r0921) (property bidder). A bidder who can raise the price in seconds asks the broker to read her the actual names.

**Personality.** Bianca has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Bianca has a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape, a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Bianca squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant calls attention to damp during the viewing. Bianca allows a proper look at the damp before returning to the argument about price.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Bianca takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- When somebody trades on a title, Bianca asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Carol Reed

AH-R0925 · Age 61 · Pension trustee · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my husband counting pension money on a school desk.” Saturday often includes a stop at Paper & Pine Books, where Carol starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I can put my pension into the joint offer. My husband says choosing the city over our kids needs both our signatures.”

**What Carol wants.** To decide the pension contribution together at home.

**Family.** [Jacqueline Ueda](../05/15-a-boat-for-sale-twice.md#ah-r0745) is Carol’s younger cousin. Carol chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Jacqueline brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A boat for sale twice*, Jacqueline says, “I put in an offer and withdrew it when my son said he gets seasick every trip.”

**People in this story.** [Wynn Montoya](#ah-r0930). A family pension offer and a deliberately low bid test how seriously small investors are taken.

**Personality.** Carol finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and one cuff folded back twice. Carol has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with salt-and-pepper curls. Carol draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The millionaire bidder offers a private deal. Carol gets the offer written down where the tenant group can consider it.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Carol brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When thanked for a shared success, Carol points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Stella Easton

AH-R0926 · Age 73 · Retired domestic cleaner · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sweeping a room the morning after a proposal.” On Tuesday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Stella pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I used to clean that upstairs room. The famous love story started with me carrying out somebody else's rubbish.”

**What Stella wants.** To tell the unsentimental part of the love story.

**Family.** [Otis Wolff](../01/13-the-borrowed-bass.md#ah-r0126) is Stella’s half-sibling. Stella brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Otis checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The borrowed bass*, Otis says, “I sold that bass to pay for a flight to my sister's wedding.”

**People in this story.** [Nadia Carvalho](#ah-r0929). The cleaner who knew the first room takes the surveyor to the less romantic damp wall.

**Personality.** Stella keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Stella wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Stella has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a pale blue work shirt with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Stella raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant calls attention to damp during the viewing. Stella allows a proper look at the damp before returning to the argument about price.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Stella asks for help with a task the neighbor does well, so both people get to contribute something.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Stella offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Morgan Freeman

AH-R0927 · Age 54 · Lease archivist · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a tenant agreement written across a picture of a gull.” Sunday often includes a stop at Ferris Deli, where Morgan asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**What Morgan wants.** To protect the promises written into the early leases.

**Family.** [Anya Li](../02/10-the-bitter-orange.md#ah-r0242) is Morgan’s cousin. Morgan brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Anya checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The bitter orange*, Anya says, “I squeezed the orange she carried in her pocket.”

**People in this story.** [Sadie Breen](#ah-r0922). Card leases and rents paid in groceries establish how long the tenants have already shaped the building.

**Personality.** Morgan always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Morgan is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Morgan has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Morgan gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Morgan suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Albert Barclay

AH-R0928 · Age 71 · Retired kitchen porter · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember waiting on a staircase for my knees to settle.” On Thursday, near the downtown fountain, Albert keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I want a bench in the entry for those of us who take stairs in stages. Both bidders promised me the roof.”

**What Albert wants.** To win a resting bench in the entry.

**Family.** [Ashley Byrd](../04/11-the-fitting-mirror.md#ah-r0554) is Albert’s first cousin once removed. Albert brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Ashley makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The fitting mirror*, Ashley says, “I fitted a skirt in the salon. The client could sit while I pinned it for the first time in years.”

**People in this story.** [Anna Avila](#ah-r0923). The tenant group adds a usable entry bench to the terms before backing any owner.

**Personality.** Albert has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Albert knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Albert is easy to recognize by gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a brown corduroy jacket with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Albert leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant calls attention to damp during the viewing. Albert allows a proper look at the damp before returning to the argument about price.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Albert agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Albert asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Wynn Montoya

AH-R0930 · Age 49 · Retail bidder · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sitting opposite a rich bidder who let me finish.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Lighthouse Cove, where Wynn takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I put in a deliberately low bid to find out who would laugh. The wealthiest woman in the room took me seriously.”

**What Wynn wants.** To keep even a small bidder's voice in the sale.

**Family.** [Gerard Marquez](../04/13-a-play-in-a-pocket.md#ah-r0572) is Wynn’s younger relative by marriage. Wynn asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Gerard chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *A play in a pocket*, Gerard says, “I played a landlord until my actual landlord sat down.”

**People in this story.** [Carol Reed](#ah-r0925). A family pension offer and a deliberately low bid test how seriously small investors are taken.

**Personality.** Wynn has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Wynn can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Wynn has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with a train token tied to a zipper pull. In conversation Wynn holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant calls attention to damp during the viewing. Wynn allows a proper look at the damp before returning to the argument about price.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Wynn asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- If a friend borrows money, Wynn agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.
