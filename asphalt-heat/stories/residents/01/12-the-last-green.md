## Saul Rosner

AH-R0111 · Age 73 · Retired cabdriver · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** Saul gave up taking fares on stormy evenings long before he gave up driving. His family interpreted each little concession as a promise of a larger one. This time he wants the decision remembered as his own. Wednesday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Saul can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I can name the color of each light with my eyes closed. My daughter says this is exactly the trouble. I want to be the one who hands the keys across.”

**What Saul wants.** To decide where and when to hand the car keys across.

**Family.** [Hamish Baxter](../04/13-a-play-in-a-pocket.md#ah-r0574) is Saul’s younger cousin. Saul remembers the birthdays. Hamish asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A play in a pocket*, Hamish says, “I had ten lines. The listener cried after the first two and asked if we could wait.”

**People in this story.** [Pam Ravel](#ah-r0115) (former driving pupil). They change places in the cab for a final ride over a route both know by smell.

**Personality.** Saul is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Saul loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Saul has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for tightly curled gray hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an olive canvas jacket with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. In conversation Saul squares up pieces of paper while thinking. Saul still wears the soft peaked cap that kept sunlight off the cab mirror.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to keep the driver's keys in secret. Saul asks Saul when he wants to hand over the keys.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Saul draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- If somebody calls for applause, Saul gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Andre Bell

AH-R0112 · Age 42 · Patrol officer with a traffic assignment · Home — Midtown · Department ID ah-pd-102 · Patrol, watch 1, badge 102

**Life in the city.** Andre knew half the city's cabs by their rear lights before he could spell dispatch. On a beat he still catches himself thinking of drivers as a boy grateful for a ride instead of an officer obliged to take their details. On Sunday, near Paper & Pine Books, Andre starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “He once drove my mother to hospital and refused a fare. I am the one who took the report on his last scrape. I offered to stand at the crossing in ordinary clothes when he goes by.”

**What Andre wants.** To give Saul the courtesy of an ordinary goodbye at the crossing.

**Family.** [Noel Gomes](../08/05-the-boy-on-the-corner.md#ah-r1094) is Andre’s second cousin. Andre offers the first invitation. Noel gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The boy on the corner*, Noel says, “I watched the little brother for grocery money.”

**People in this story.** [Hana Solberg](#ah-r0120). One remembers being given a lift in a crisis. The other wants this final lift to include the view at the bridge crest.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Andre takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Andre has a high puff tied with plain cord, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Andre leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- The old driver wants to change the farewell route. Andre takes the changed route seriously and checks who needs to know the new return time.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Andre offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Andre asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Mara Tev

AH-R0113 · Age 53 · Cab dispatcher · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Mara became a dispatcher after losing a thumb joint in a luggage door. She understands the small indignity of a job being taken away piece by piece. Retiring a callsign deserves more ceremony than clearing a screen. Saturday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Mara pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “His callsign has been on my board since I joined. I took it off and put it on the passenger booking. I want the younger drivers to hear how a farewell sounds on the radio.”

**What Mara wants.** To turn the old callsign into a final passenger booking.

**Family.** [Leon White](../07/02-the-absent-signature.md#ah-r0913) is Mara’s cousin. Mara puts the kettle on. Leon chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The absent signature*, Leon says, “I used the berth for pocket money and never asked whose money repaired it.”

**People in this story.** [Carmen Saye](#ah-r0118). The retired callsign gives the payroll worker a way to book a last trip before drawing routes for the bus.

**Personality.** Mara can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Mara has a high puff tied with plain cord, a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Mara touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to keep the driver's keys in secret. Mara asks Saul when he wants to hand over the keys.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Mara tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Mara asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Ole Finchley

AH-R0114 · Age 46 · Occupational therapist · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** Ole learned to ask what driving actually buys a patient. For this cabdriver it bought the pleasure of knowing a route better than the passenger. Choosing a journey is one part of that pleasure he can keep. Wednesday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Ole likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I watched him do everything right until two things happened together. He asked me to explain that to his grandson without using the word failing. We're making a map of journeys he can still choose.”

**What Ole wants.** To draw a useful travel map with the family.

**Family.** [Desmond Baldwin](../05/01-salt-in-the-pockets.md#ah-r0608) is Ole’s half-sibling. Ole chooses an ordinary story to tell. Desmond asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Salt in the pockets*, Desmond says, “I fix linings. Someone had stitched a tiny silk fish beneath the salt line.”

**People in this story.** [Tait Wynn](#ah-r0119). They propose letting the former driver teach one lesson about what a license can and can't measure.

**Personality.** Ole can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Ole can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Out of work clothes, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Ole has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Ole presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The old driver wants to change the farewell route. Ole takes the changed route seriously and checks who needs to know the new return time.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Ole walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Ole suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Pam Ravel

AH-R0115 · Age 44 · Minibus driver · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Pam drove her first bus in work gloves that Saul had cut short for her fingers. She kept them in the spare-tire compartment after upgrading. Now even Saul wants to know why the gloves still ride along. On Sunday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Pam likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “He trained me and never once sat quietly. On the farewell run I made him give me directions right down to the bakery smell at the turn. I want that recording for my first trainee.”

**What Pam wants.** To take a trainee over the route using Saul's recorded directions.

**Family.** [Cassandra Li](../06/02-nine-unpaid-hours.md#ah-r0762) is Pam’s cousin raised in the same household. Pam checks the date twice. Cassandra brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Nine unpaid hours*, Cassandra says, “I was paid because I have a permanent badge.”

**People in this story.** [Saul Rosner](#ah-r0111) (former driving mentor). They change places in the cab for a final ride over a route both know by smell.

**Personality.** Pam carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Pam has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a gray fisher's sweater with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Pam listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to keep the driver's keys in secret. Pam asks Saul when he wants to hand over the keys.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Pam gets the people at the next doorway away from the smoke and asks a bystander to make the call.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Pam offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Len Brune

AH-R0116 · Age 50 · Road painter · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Len painted the crooked arrow before he discovered how a driver's eye reads a bend. The complaints died down, but the driver who teased him became a customer for life. On Sunday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Len pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “His old route passes the crooked arrow I painted in my first week. He has teased me about it for years. I've left it until after Friday so he can get in the last word.”

**What Len wants.** To give the retired cabdriver a last chance to tease about the arrow.

**Family.** [Harley Dias](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1239) is Len’s younger cousin. Len brings a spare shopping bag. Harley chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Harley says, “I called the resident later about paperwork.”

**People in this story.** [Nita Ellisby](#ah-r0117). The road arrow and the seat dip are two marks of the same career that these workers can preserve for a little longer.

**Personality.** Len has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Len has a wide brow and a narrow jaw, with a loose dark braid. Len takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The old driver wants to change the farewell route. Len takes the changed route seriously and checks who needs to know the new return time.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Len asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Len says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Nita Ellisby

AH-R0117 · Age 55 · Auto upholsterer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Nita's upholstery accounts record seats by chassis number. Her own drawings record them by who sat crooked, leaned forward or drove with a child perched beside them in the old days. People around the entrance to the Wash House know Nita by a small Friday habit. Nita stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “The driver's seat has a dip the shape of his hip. He asked me to fix it for the next owner. I kept a square of the old cover to wrap his spare house key.”

**What Nita wants.** To send Saul home with a house key wrapped in his old seat cover.

**Family.** [Billie Abbott](../03/13-the-empty-drum-stool.md#ah-r0423) is Nita’s cousin. Nita asks before bringing somebody along. Billie can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The empty drum stool*, Billie says, “I booked a full band and got an audience member.”

**People in this story.** [Len Brune](#ah-r0116). The road arrow and the seat dip are two marks of the same career that these workers can preserve for a little longer.

**Personality.** Nita treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Nita lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a red windbreaker and a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. Nita has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. Nita gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to keep the driver's keys in secret. Nita asks Saul when he wants to hand over the keys.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Nita asks what the friend actually said last time and checks whether anybody is expecting them at home.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Nita says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Carmen Saye

AH-R0118 · Age 48 · Bus rider and payroll supervisor · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Carmen's father drove everywhere and never learned the bus colors. After he gave up the car she found that being able to read didn't tell him which shelter got a seat first. She draws maps from the knees up. On Thursday, near the market by Market Flats, Carmen often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I used to take his cab after my double shifts. Now he needs to know how to change buses with sore knees. I drew the route with every seat and shelter marked.”

**What Carmen wants.** To test the marked rest stops with Saul on public transport.

**Family.** [Gareth Roth](../03/01-spin-cycle-choir.md#ah-r0301) is Carmen’s cousin. Carmen makes room at the table. Gareth brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Spin cycle choir*, Gareth says, “My father whistled that song through our last argument.”

**People in this story.** [Mara Tev](#ah-r0113). The retired callsign gives the payroll worker a way to book a last trip before drawing routes for the bus.

**Personality.** Carmen is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Carmen is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Carmen has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a long plait tucked inside the collar. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a washed violet jersey with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Carmen holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The old driver wants to change the farewell route. Carmen takes the changed route seriously and checks who needs to know the new return time.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Carmen offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Carmen agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Tait Wynn

AH-R0119 · Age 39 · Driving instructor · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Tait made a good driving pupil and a terrible driving examiner. He left examination work because he kept wanting to teach after the clock stopped. Bringing an old driver into the classroom lets him do exactly that. Tuesday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Tait checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “He told me to get a proper job when I was a kid. I've asked him to speak to my new drivers about stopping in time. The topic includes careers.”

**What Tait wants.** To bring Saul into a beginner class as someone with knowledge to pass on.

**Family.** [Ruth Spencer](../09/14-the-missing-sunday.md#ah-r1339) is Tait’s cousin raised in the same household. Tait saves a place. Ruth chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The missing Sunday*, Ruth says, “I saved his old radio when he moved. It still gets the away matches better than my phone.”

**People in this story.** [Ole Finchley](#ah-r0114). They propose letting the former driver teach one lesson about what a license can and can't measure.

**Personality.** Tait tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Tait will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Tait has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for auburn hair cut square at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. In conversation Tait rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A relative asks to keep the driver's keys in secret. Tait asks Saul when he wants to hand over the keys.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Tait finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Tait repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Hana Solberg

AH-R0120 · Age 41 · Bridge maintenance engineer · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Hana sees the viaduct from ladders, inspection platforms and the driver's seat. She didn't see the lighthouse in the mirror herself until a maintenance passenger showed it to her on a perfectly uneventful crossing. People around Harbour Bakery know Hana by a small Saturday habit. Hana buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “The best view is from the passenger side at the crest. He had crossed it eight thousand times without seeing the lighthouse in the mirror. I asked the driver to give him that seat.”

**What Hana wants.** To show the retiring driver the lighthouse in the passenger mirror.

**Family.** [Elizabeth Yoon](../02/14-saturday-keys.md#ah-r0282) is Hana’s second cousin. Hana picks the meeting place. Elizabeth sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Saturday keys*, Elizabeth says, “I used to hide my key in a shoe. After losing my home I hate the idea somebody else carries it.”

**People in this story.** [Andre Bell](#ah-r0112). One remembers being given a lift in a crisis. The other wants this final lift to include the view at the bridge crest.

**Personality.** Hana is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Hana asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Hana has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Hana looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The old driver wants to change the farewell route. Hana takes the changed route seriously and checks who needs to know the new return time.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Hana suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Hana accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.
