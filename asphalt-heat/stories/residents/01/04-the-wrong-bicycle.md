# The wrong bicycle

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Old Town police counter.** Outside the Old Town police station two nearly identical yellow bicycles are returned to the opposite owners. One has a taped saddle, the other a bell that sticks.

## Marisol Vega

AH-R0031 · Age 39 · Patrol officer · Home — Coast Ridge · Department ID ah-pd-101 · Patrol, watch 1, badge 101

**Life in the city.** Marisol took a paper route as a kid to pay for punctures. The station has three forms for a bicycle and none asks what it feels like under its rider. She wants the return desk to ask that question before asking for a signature. People around Breakwater Way know Marisol by a small Friday habit. Marisol stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “My first bicycle belonged to three cousins before me. I thought recognizing a color was enough. I called both families from my own desk so neither would have to queue at the station again.”

**What Marisol wants.** To give both riders one uninterrupted chance to identify their property.

**Family.** [Cory Walker](../05/03-the-trust-with-a-typo.md#ah-r0624) is Marisol’s parent’s cousin. Marisol saves a place. Cory chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The trust with a typo*, Cory says, “I knew the founder when she couldn't afford the exam.”

**People in this story.** [Hollis Fry](#ah-r0032). They meet a second time at the property counter to compare what the wrong saddle cost.

**Personality.** Marisol treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Marisol is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in a cream pullover with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Marisol steadies a cup with both hands while listening when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A child's account contradicts the adult at the counter. Marisol lets the child finish describing the bicycle and asks to hear the bell.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Marisol agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Marisol changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Hollis Fry

AH-R0032 · Age 28 · Bike courier · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Hollis learned to ride again after a hip operation by delivering groceries for two neighbors. He remembers every doorway where he could set a bag down and lean for a moment. A delivery penalty can still feel like a verdict on his body. People around Lighthouse Cove know Hollis by a small Saturday habit. Hollis takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “The taped saddle tips to the left because my hip does. I made it through one delivery on the wrong bike before I began to limp. I want my lost delivery fee as much as my saddle.”

**What Hollis wants.** To get the correct saddle back and the lost fee credited.

**Family.** [Kate Parra](../03/14-the-housecoat-commission.md#ah-r0438) is Hollis’s half-sibling. Hollis picks the meeting place. Kate sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The housecoat commission*, Kate says, “I borrowed the pattern for my dad. He wants pockets large enough for Sunday's newspaper.”

**People in this story.** [Marisol Vega](#ah-r0031). They meet a second time at the property counter to compare what the wrong saddle cost. Also in the story is [Toby Lin](#ah-r0038) (dispatcher).

**Personality.** Hollis is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Hollis is concerned.

**Appearance.** Hollis has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a charcoal zip cardigan with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Hollis rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The courier asks who will cover the lost delivery. Hollis gets the dispatcher and the courier looking at the same delivery entry.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Hollis asks where they stood and what they could hear, leaving the rest open instead of filling it with guesses.
- When someone invents an answer, Hollis asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Opal Finch

AH-R0033 · Age 58 · School crossing guard · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Opal has watched two generations grow tall enough to cross alone. Little children tell her things adults are too busy to hear. She hopes her replacement will wait for the last syllable before lifting the stop sign. People around Maré Coffee know Opal by a small Friday habit. Opal likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I recognized the child's bell by the second ring it never makes. When the officer handed it back I let the kid explain the fault. People have been speaking over her all term.”

**What Opal wants.** To let the child demonstrate the broken bell herself.

**Family.** [Devin Mills](../07/10-an-old-restaurant-debt.md#ah-r0991) is Opal’s second cousin. Opal keeps the photographs. Devin knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *An old restaurant debt*, Devin says, “I sold the dead man's plates last month and finally had the money.”

**People in this story.** [Jude Wick](#ah-r0035). They trust the child's description after the bell and the spokes offer two independent ways to identify the bike.

**Personality.** Opal loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Opal has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. In conversation Opal presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- A child's account contradicts the adult at the counter. Opal lets the child finish describing the bicycle and asks to hear the bell.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Opal asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Opal offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Priya Tal

AH-R0034 · Age 35 · Public defender · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Priya's first case ended with a client getting his wallet back but missing work to do it. She still counts the cost of taking a morning off whenever an official calls a procedure straightforward. People around Ferris Deli know Priya by a small Thursday habit. Priya asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “My client had a receipt and no photograph. I found myself asking him for proof I'd never ask my neighbors for. I want our property checklist to fit on the back of a library card.”

**What Priya wants.** To get the property checklist down to something a hurried client can carry.

**Family.** [Peyton Mitchell](../07/07-the-first-language.md#ah-r0969) is Priya’s first cousin once removed. Priya makes the call. Peyton is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The first language*, Peyton says, “I want to read to the shop cat first. It has yet to interrupt anybody's pronunciation.”

**People in this story.** [Cleo Penn](#ah-r0036). The new collection slip has to satisfy both the counter and the person who will lose wages collecting it.

**Personality.** Priya enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Priya is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Priya has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for a long plait tucked inside the collar. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an old plum windbreaker with a small enamel bird on the lapel. In conversation Priya takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The courier asks who will cover the lost delivery. Priya gets the dispatcher and the courier looking at the same delivery entry.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Priya asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Priya keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Jude Wick

AH-R0035 · Age 50 · Bicycle repairer · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Jude built wheels for speed until a neighborhood kid asked for one that could survive falling over every day. He has had a box of mismatched practice spokes by the counter ever since. On Saturday, near Juniper Street, Jude knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “Both wheels were built in my shop. I spoke for the wheel I remembered, and the child corrected me. I keep her drawing of the spoke pattern over the truing stand.”

**What Jude wants.** To give the child credit for identifying the wheel.

**Family.** [Samuel Mehta](../10/15-the-last-light-at-the-harbor.md#ah-r1492) is Jude’s half-sibling. Jude leaves the first voice message. Samuel calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The last light at the harbor*, Samuel says, “I turn ours off from below because my hands no longer reach the switch.”

**People in this story.** [Opal Finch](#ah-r0033). They trust the child's description after the bell and the spokes offer two independent ways to identify the bike.

**Personality.** Jude is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Jude is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand, Jude looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A child's account contradicts the adult at the counter. Jude lets the child finish describing the bicycle and asks to hear the bell.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Jude checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Jude helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Cleo Penn

AH-R0036 · Age 34 · Evidence clerk · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Cleo grew up dividing one dresser drawer with two sisters. She assumed everyone could keep track of their things by memory. The station shelves cured her of that notion within a week. On Sunday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Cleo watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I used two identical bags for the keys. I've made a little station where owners can photograph their property with the collection slip. My supervisor wants to know whose idea it was.”

**What Cleo wants.** To leave both owners with photographs of what they collected.

**Family.** [Anita Lund](../05/05-the-clean-ledger.md#ah-r0648) is Cleo’s older relative by marriage. Cleo chooses the familiar route. Anita can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The clean ledger*, Anita says, “I kept the notebook safe through three burglaries.”

**People in this story.** [Priya Tal](#ah-r0034). The new collection slip has to satisfy both the counter and the person who will lose wages collecting it.

**Personality.** Cleo finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Cleo has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Cleo takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The courier asks who will cover the lost delivery. Cleo gets the dispatcher and the courier looking at the same delivery entry.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Cleo asks the person already using it whether they can comfortably stand, rather than presuming who needs it most.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Cleo waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Art Robles

AH-R0037 · Age 68 · Retired detective · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Art was a detective long enough to give a persuasive answer even when a less tidy one was true. Retirement has put him in a family full of children who don't accept his first answer. People around the bank steps know Art by a small Monday habit. Art takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I gave one of those bikes to my grandson and told him it was new. Now the former owner wants to say hello. I have to decide whether a pretty lie is worth another one.”

**What Art wants.** To tell his grandson where the gift actually came from.

**Family.** [Elena Mehta](../05/03-the-trust-with-a-typo.md#ah-r0625) is Art’s half-sibling. Art brings the paper and a pen. Elena decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The trust with a typo*, Elena says, “I endowed the second scholarship only after I heard her laugh at the error.”

**People in this story.** [Alim Ward](#ah-r0040). They both owe a child an honest account of why adults became so invested in a yellow bicycle.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Art is easy to recognize by steel-colored hair cut square above the collar and a round face with a pronounced chin. Usually dressed in a washed violet jersey with a narrow brass watch set a little fast, Art looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A child's account contradicts the adult at the counter. Art lets the child finish describing the bicycle and asks to hear the bell.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Art asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Art listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Toby Lin

AH-R0038 · Age 40 · Delivery dispatcher · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** Toby's drivers measure his decisions against a clock they cannot see. He used to think lateness was the only fair measure because everyone got the same seconds. He is discovering what a second costs a courier with a bad hip. On Monday, near Maré Coffee, Toby likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I docked the courier for arriving late. His customer had watched the entire mix-up. I restored the fee and put a blank line beside the automatic lateness code.”

**What Toby wants.** To restore the courier's pay and hear the next late driver's account.

**Family.** [Cory Walsh](../03/06-the-hospital-radio.md#ah-r0359) is Toby’s half-sibling. Toby calls about good news first. Cory is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The hospital radio*, Cory says, “I brought a pocket radio so two roommates can listen to different matches and still keep score together.”

**People in this story.** [Rosa Marr](#ah-r0039). The screw found in the dustpan helps establish why the delayed delivery really took as long as it did. Also in the story is [Hollis Fry](#ah-r0032) (courier).

**Personality.** Toby is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Toby loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Toby has thick, tightly coiled hair, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Toby grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- The courier asks who will cover the lost delivery. Toby gets the dispatcher and the courier looking at the same delivery entry.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Toby keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- If somebody calls for applause, Toby gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Rosa Marr

AH-R0039 · Age 56 · Station cleaner · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Rosa works nights so she and her wife can share a late breakfast. The little screw delayed that breakfast for fifteen minutes, the same number they used to spend helping their own children search for lost things. On Thursday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Rosa usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I found the missing bell screw in my dustpan. At home my wife can fix anything small enough to fit in a jam jar. We walked it back before the repair shop closed.”

**What Rosa wants.** To return the screw before somebody buys a whole new bell.

**Family.** [Noel Aubin](../03/14-the-housecoat-commission.md#ah-r0432) is Rosa’s cousin raised in the same household. Rosa remembers the birthdays. Noel asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The housecoat commission*, Noel says, “I measured seated and standing because dignity shouldn't disappear when she leans forward.”

**People in this story.** [Toby Lin](#ah-r0038). The screw found in the dustpan helps establish why the delayed delivery really took as long as it did.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Rosa takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a pale blue work shirt and one cuff folded back twice. Rosa has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Rosa looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation. A wedding ring hangs on a cord while Rosa cleans. Her wife made the little knot at the back of her neck.

**Under pressure**

- A child's account contradicts the adult at the counter. Rosa lets the child finish describing the bicycle and asks to hear the bell.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Rosa writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Rosa asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Alim Ward

AH-R0040 · Age 45 · Community mediator · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Alim became a mediator after a neighborhood football league split over a painted line. His best meetings start with an object on the table and enough time for its owners to contradict each other. Monday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Alim takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “The adults wanted separate appointments. The two riders wanted to compare yellow paint. I booked one long table and made everyone leave room at the ends for the bikes.”

**What Alim wants.** To get both bicycles and both riders to one table.

**Family.** [Franklin Davila](../08/13-the-understudy-sings.md#ah-r1172) is Alim’s half-sibling. A cousin asked for help with the rent and got six different lectures. The family now tries to agree on the amount it can offer before composing any advice. Alim will say exactly how much help is possible. Asked about *The understudy sings*, Franklin says, “I am the lead. I want the audience to know why I am in the front row and not to spend the whole song looking at me.”

**People in this story.** [Art Robles](#ah-r0037). They both owe a child an honest account of why adults became so invested in a yellow bicycle.

**Personality.** Alim can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Alim has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a washed violet jersey with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. In conversation Alim counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- The courier asks who will cover the lost delivery. Alim gets the dispatcher and the courier looking at the same delivery entry.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Alim asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Alim asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.
