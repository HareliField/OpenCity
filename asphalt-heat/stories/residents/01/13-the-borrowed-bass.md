## Cory Halevi

AH-R0121 · Age 17 · Teenage bassist · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Cory borrows instruments because the family's rent changes faster than anyone can save. Learning the sound of a loose screw gave him an accidental education in listening to a thing other people have used. People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Cory by a small Monday habit. Cory knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I heard that rattle every time I played the low E. I thought it was my timing. I'm missing the audition to walk the instrument back unless the owner lets me tell the band the whole story.”

**What Cory wants.** To return the bass honestly and still make the audition if possible.

**Family.** [Gareth Rivera](../09/05-a-clean-coat-for-court.md#ah-r1241) is Cory’s first cousin once removed. Cory calls about good news first. Gareth is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *A clean coat for court*, Gareth says, “I cheated him. Borrowing his coat feels worse than borrowing money because I'm wearing his good opinion.”

**People in this story.** [Stevie Dain](#ah-r0125) (bandmate). The audition only stays open if the drummer can persuade the other musicians to change their order. Also in the story is [Iris Cade](#ah-r0122) (music teacher).

**Personality.** Cory finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Outside school, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Cory has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. Cory squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Somebody says the pawn ticket proves the bass was stolen. Cory asks to see whether the ticket had already been settled.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Cory stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- When thanked for a shared success, Cory points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Iris Cade

AH-R0122 · Age 44 · Music teacher · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** Iris teaches music to children who rarely control which room they can practice in. Her lending system depends on knowing when an overdue instrument signals a family emergency and when it signals a reluctant pupil. People around Harbour Bakery know Iris by a small Tuesday habit. Iris buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I lent the bass because I remembered what it was like to be the kid with empty hands. I hadn't asked where the donor got it. I have to make that call in front of the student.”

**What Iris wants.** To identify the lender in front of the pupil who trusted the loan.

**Family.** [Dana Harris](../08/06-two-names-on-the-chart.md#ah-r1102) is Iris’s older cousin. The family spent one holiday with the electricity off. They remember who found the candles, who kept the food cold and who cracked the joke that let the children stop being brave. Iris remembers the joke word for word. Asked about *Two names on the chart*, Dana says, “I am the divorced spouse whose surname still turns up.”

**People in this story.** [Dev Ferris](#ah-r0123). The teacher and the pawnbroker make the call that establishes whether the old ticket was settled. Also in the story is [Cory Halevi](#ah-r0121) (music student).

**Personality.** Iris keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Iris wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Iris is easy to recognize by a high puff tied with plain cord and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a black overshirt with pearl buttons with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread, Iris steadies a cup with both hands while listening when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The band is called early for its audition. Iris finds out how many minutes the band actually has before treating the slot as lost.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Iris alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Iris offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Dev Ferris

AH-R0123 · Age 58 · Pawnbroker · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Dev ran the pawn counter through years when half his customers didn't want to be recognized. Releasing a paid ticket is often easy. Getting someone to believe the account is settled can take another decade. People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Dev by a small Friday habit. Dev knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “Somebody paid the ticket years ago and never removed it. I can tell from the tear. I want the old owner to find that out from me because we parted on ugly terms.”

**What Dev wants.** To show the former owner when the ticket was actually paid.

**Family.** [Edmund Porter](../05/02-the-crane-shadow.md#ah-r0616) is Dev’s second cousin. Dev offers the first invitation. Edmund gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The crane shadow*, Edmund says, “I can no longer climb the cab ladder. I stood inside its shadow and taught a child the controls from memory.”

**People in this story.** [Iris Cade](#ah-r0122). The teacher and the pawnbroker make the call that establishes whether the old ticket was settled.

**Personality.** Dev always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Dev is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Dev has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Dev grins on one side while the eyes stay serious during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Somebody says the pawn ticket proves the bass was stolen. Dev asks to see whether the ticket had already been settled.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Dev asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Dev suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Pax Orr

AH-R0124 · Age 32 · Sound technician · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Pax started doing sound work in order to learn what his absent father knew. The more successful he became, the less sure he was that a repaired instrument could answer any of his original questions. People around Maré Coffee know Pax by a small Tuesday habit. Pax likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I recognized the wiring by a burned thumbprint in the solder. My father repaired it the week he left us. I want a photo of the wiring and no part of the ownership argument.”

**What Pax wants.** To take a wiring photograph without claiming the instrument.

**Family.** [Rae Nelson](../03/08-the-painted-footprint.md#ah-r0380) is Pax’s cousin raised in the same household. Pax puts the kettle on. Rae chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The painted footprint*, Rae says, “I wear an artificial foot. I want to trace mine beside it on paper and take the pair home.”

**People in this story.** [Sid Idris](#ah-r0127). They discuss how a photograph of a repair could stay with the family while the instrument goes on earning its keep.

**Personality.** Pax has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Pax knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a dark green knitted vest and a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. Pax has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with auburn hair cut square at the nape. Pax steadies a cup with both hands while listening during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The band is called early for its audition. Pax finds out how many minutes the band actually has before treating the slot as lost.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Pax offers to help correct the printed name and leaves it to the colleague whether any public explanation is wanted.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Pax asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

**Life in the city.** Stevie plays loud enough to feel brave. The quiet measure while the group waits for a call is the one she has practiced least. She wants the other acts to know her for something beyond volume. On Friday, near Lighthouse Cove, Stevie takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “My friend thinks an audition happens once. I got the other acts to agree to go first. Now I have to play quietly enough for the phone to ring.”

**What Stevie wants.** To secure a later time in the audition order for the full band.

**Appearance.** Stevie is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. **Under pressure**

## Otis Wolff

AH-R0126 · Age 72 · Pensioned touring musician · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Otis toured on other people's buses. He can summon a hundred venues from the smell of varnish and only one wedding he did not attend. A teenager getting an ordinary first audition matters to him for that reason. On Wednesday, near the bank steps, Otis takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I sold that bass to pay for a flight to my sister's wedding. She died before I got there. Hearing a teenager play it is the first ending to that story I've liked.”

**What Otis wants.** To hear a new player take the old bass through a first audition.

**Family.** [Stella Easton](../07/03-a-widow-bids-again.md#ah-r0926) is Otis’s half-sibling. Otis checks the date twice. Stella brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A widow bids again*, Stella says, “I used to clean that upstairs room. The famous love story started with me carrying out somebody else's rubbish.”

**People in this story.** [Rami Bose](#ah-r0130). They are the only two who know why the wrong note in the welcome tune is an act of kindness.

**Personality.** Otis has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Otis can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Otis has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for tightly curled gray hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. In conversation Otis looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The band is called early for its audition. Otis finds out how many minutes the band actually has before treating the slot as lost.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Otis writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- If a friend borrows money, Otis agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Sid Idris

His sister plays oboe and still sends him a repair estimate after every tour as an argument for what his policies leave out. People around Lighthouse Cove know Sid by a small Tuesday habit. Sid takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “The paperwork values the wood and hardware. The two hours everybody spent trying to return it count for nothing on my form. I've started another page for the lender to keep.”

**Personality.** Sid takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Sid can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an olive canvas jacket and a narrow brass watch set a little fast. Sid has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Sid takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

**Life in the city.** Nia took park work to spend more time outdoors and spends most of it negotiating who gets to make noise. Her private map of the fountain is divided into places where conversation will travel and places where it won't. On Tuesday, near Juniper Street, Nia knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I usually have to tell bands to turn down. I loaned this one the quiet side of the fountain for the phone call. Their drummer came back afterwards and asked how loud the ducks like it.”

**What Nia wants.** To keep a quiet place free beside the fountain for the call.

**Family.** [Elise Cotton](../03/12-one-unposted-detour.md#ah-r0417) is Nia’s stepsibling. Nia asks before bringing somebody along. Elise can always ask for five minutes alone. **Appearance.** Nia is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Nia counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

## Rami Bose

AH-R0130 · Age 38 · Busking cellist · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Rami's street performances have taught him how a crowd can be drawn by one recognizable mistake in a familiar tune. Giving the old musician his wrong note back seems to him a more personal invitation than a formal letter. On Thursday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Rami looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I know the old owner's favorite tune. I wrote the bass line on a grocery bag so these kids can play it when he walks into the park. There's a wrong note he always insisted on.”

**What Rami wants.** To teach the old musician's favorite bass line before he arrives.

**Family.** [Jay Arendt](../09/06-a-doctor-moves-away.md#ah-r1258) is Rami’s second cousin. Rami saves a place. Jay chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *A doctor moves away*, Jay says, “I translated her visits for sixteen years. We know each other's pauses better than some married people.”

**People in this story.** [Otis Wolff](#ah-r0126). They are the only two who know why the wrong note in the welcome tune is an act of kindness.

**Personality.** Rami reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Rami trusts most.

**Appearance.** Rami has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. In conversation Rami looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- The band is called early for its audition. Rami finds out how many minutes the band actually has before treating the slot as lost.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Rami puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- If told to back off, Rami does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.
