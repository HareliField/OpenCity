## Mae Linford

AH-R0131 · Age 49 · Meal coordinator · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Mae can shop for twenty people in less time than her brother takes buying for one. Her father taught her that skill and most of her best recipes. He also taught her how easily efficient shopping can obscure who will share the meal. Sunday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Mae can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I nearly canceled the extra dinner. Then I remembered the winter my father fed our neighbor off his own plate. I want our form to have a box for someone you'd like to eat with.”

**What Mae wants.** To keep the unregistered diner on the food round.

**Family.** [Miles Price](../02/07-the-margin-reader.md#ah-r0215) is Mae’s cousin. Mae brings the paper and a pen. Miles decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The margin reader*, Miles says, “I donated the books during a divorce. My ex kept replying after I'd already moved out.”

**People in this story.** [Mel Graydon](#ah-r0133). The coordinator eats a visit's worth of lunch alongside the aide before deciding what goes on the register.

**Personality.** Mae prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Mae has short black hair swept back, a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Mae squares up pieces of paper while thinking. A stub of green shopping pencil lives in the hem of Mae’s apron.

**Under pressure**

- A visiting supervisor wants the second dinner withdrawn. Mae asks for a meal to be held while the people who eat it explain the second portion.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Mae finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Mae clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Essie Good

AH-R0132 · Age 36 · Unpaid family carer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Essie's paid job starts two hours after her caring day begins. She owns a work badge for one and has no name at all in the other service's register. A second plate would recognize something she already does every day. Monday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Essie checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “The spare portion is mine. I go home before the register calls me a resident and come back before breakfast. I'd like to eat sitting down without justifying my address to anybody.”

**What Essie wants.** To sit down to a whole meal without having to prove residence.

**Family.** [Bianca Gallagher](../08/02-land-without-cranes.md#ah-r1065) is Essie’s older cousin. Essie calls about good news first. Bianca is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Land without cranes*, Bianca says, “I work the rail siding. Moving the warehouse shifts half my day onto the road.”

**People in this story.** [Eamon Tor](#ah-r0134) (relative receiving care). They are the two people the argument about a second portion is actually about.

**Personality.** Essie never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Essie can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Essie has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Look for a long plait tucked inside the collar. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. In conversation Essie rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The diner asks to have company without being asked about care. Essie lets the diner start an ordinary conversation and leaves the care questions for another time.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Essie asks the cashier quietly which item can be set aside without making the customer explain their finances to the line.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Essie may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Mel Graydon

AH-R0133 · Age 45 · Home-care aide · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Mel learned home care in flats with elevators and had to learn her job a second time on western porches. People sitting in their own doorway are often waiting to be invited further into their own life. People around Paper & Pine Books know Mel by a small Saturday habit. Mel starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I knew about the second plate. I was afraid putting it in my notes would make someone take it away. I've invited the coordinator to join us and bring her own lunch.”

**What Mel wants.** To get the coordinator and both diners around one table.

**Family.** [Jo Graves](../09/15-the-missing-name-on-the-stern.md#ah-r1343) is Mel’s stepsibling. Mel remembers the birthdays. Jo asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The missing name on the stern*, Jo says, “I bought the boat and have no connection to either spelling.”

**People in this story.** [Mae Linford](#ah-r0131). The coordinator eats a visit's worth of lunch alongside the aide before deciding what goes on the register.

**Personality.** Mel often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Mel have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a dark green knitted vest and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Mel has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with short copper curls. Mel squares up pieces of paper while thinking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A visiting supervisor wants the second dinner withdrawn. Mel asks for a meal to be held while the people who eat it explain the second portion.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Mel asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Mel rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Eamon Tor

AH-R0134 · Age 77 · Tenant and retired machinist · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Eamon measures time in the old shop's unit of a tea break. The whole argument about gravy lasts six such breaks. He gets just as much nourishment out of being contradicted as he ever got out of roast beef. On Monday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Eamon looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I put two because a meal eaten alone lasts nine minutes and an argument over gravy lasts an hour. I'll pay for the gravy myself if they leave the hour alone.”

**What Eamon wants.** To keep an hour of company on the meal schedule.

**Family.** [Javier Turner](../07/01-the-uniform-money.md#ah-r0906) is Eamon’s younger relative by marriage. Eamon offers the first invitation. Javier gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The uniform money*, Javier says, “I found the supplier who will sell singles without a bulk order.”

**People in this story.** [Essie Good](#ah-r0132) (family carer). They are the two people the argument about a second portion is actually about. Also in the story is [Pia Mercerly](#ah-r0140) (community cook).

**Personality.** Eamon thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Eamon sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Eamon is easy to recognize by a thin scattering of white hair over the crown and a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Usually dressed in a narrow-striped sweater with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Eamon looks toward the door whenever a name is called when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The diner asks to have company without being asked about care. Eamon lets the diner start an ordinary conversation and leaves the care questions for another time.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Eamon asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Eamon asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Tori Gran

AH-R0135 · Age 43 · Grocer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Tori took over the shop after her mum retired and discovered how much of the firm's charitable reputation rested on giving away things nobody wanted. She has started pricing donations at what a customer would actually choose. Sunday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Tori prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “Our shop donated tins nobody likes. I was embarrassed hearing what the cook did to make them edible. Next week I'm letting the diners write the shopping list.”

**What Tori wants.** To let the diners choose the next useful donation.

**Family.** [Karim Byrd](../07/11-the-salon-on-strike.md#ah-r1001) is Tori’s cousin. Tori puts the kettle on. Karim chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The salon on strike*, Karim says, “I volunteered because I need new clients. I forgot my coworkers need the hours paid today.”

**People in this story.** [Pia Mercerly](#ah-r0140). They exchange a blank shopping list for some of the diner's most opinionated recipes.

**Personality.** Tori gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Tori privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Tori is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Tori takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A visiting supervisor wants the second dinner withdrawn. Tori asks for a meal to be held while the people who eat it explain the second portion.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Tori draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Tori offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Amin Bach

AH-R0136 · Age 31 · Dietary assistant · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Amin can recognize an allergy-safe tray at twenty paces. He is trying to get equally good at recognizing all the little tastes that make a person something more than an allergy on a card. On Monday, near the market by Market Flats, Amin often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “The carer likes spice and the tenant doesn't. I packed one little paper twist of pepper. Now three addresses are asking who gets to decide what tastes like home.”

**What Amin wants.** To pack seasoning so two diners can choose differently.

**Family.** [Ben Ortiz](../06/04-the-saturday-swap.md#ah-r0783) is Amin’s second cousin. Amin chooses an ordinary story to tell. Ben asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The Saturday swap*, Ben says, “I have a language exam that morning. I studied for it in the parked car because the house thinks I'm fluent already.”

**People in this story.** [Sybil Rey](#ah-r0139). The cost of a twist of pepper is the auditor's smallest example of the difference between meals and people.

**Personality.** Amin uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Amin well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Amin has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a pale blue work shirt with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Amin gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- The diner asks to have company without being asked about care. Amin lets the diner start an ordinary conversation and leaves the care questions for another time.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Amin asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- After a family quarrel, Amin brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Jae Alder

AH-R0137 · Age 38 · School attendance clerk · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** Jae's school records say mother absent beside almost every parents' night she remembers. She saw her mother in three homes doing indispensable work on each of those evenings. That still determines which phone calls she makes first. On Friday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Jae usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “My mother missed parents' nights because she was doing this kind of care. I'm holding the next one over two evenings so carers can swap. I need one family to test the arrangement.”

**What Jae wants.** To offer a usable parents' night to a family on care shifts.

**Family.** [Lena Crane](../04/07-a-borrowed-name.md#ah-r0513) is Jae’s older cousin. Jae checks the date twice. Lena brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A borrowed name*, Lena says, “I published the book and missed the echo because I never go home to that street.”

**People in this story.** [Dane Aubin](#ah-r0138). The school clerk and the food driver compare the times when carers can actually answer a door.

**Personality.** Jae feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Jae has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. When somebody else has the floor, Jae holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A visiting supervisor wants the second dinner withdrawn. Jae asks for a meal to be held while the people who eat it explain the second portion.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Jae repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Jae lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Dane Aubin

AH-R0138 · Age 57 · Food bank driver · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** Dane drives his food bank van by a map in which knocks, bells, side entrances and impatient dogs are more important than road numbers. He worries that management will call his handwritten music symbols unnecessary detail. Monday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Dane can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I take the stairs because the bell is louder than the tenant can bear. The new driver needs to know who can hear which knock. I drew a music note beside the address.”

**What Dane wants.** To pass each household's preferred knock to the next driver.

**Family.** [Albert Daniels](../10/11-an-ordinary-mirror.md#ah-r1457) is Dane’s cousin. Dane brings a spare shopping bag. Albert chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *An ordinary mirror*, Albert says, “I am the client waiting behind her. I heard myself offer a story I have never told our own tailor.”

**People in this story.** [Jae Alder](#ah-r0137). The school clerk and the food driver compare the times when carers can actually answer a door.

**Personality.** Dane treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Dane has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. In conversation Dane squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- The diner asks to have company without being asked about care. Dane lets the diner start an ordinary conversation and leaves the care questions for another time.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Dane asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Dane changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Sybil Rey

AH-R0139 · Age 46 · Social services auditor · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Sybil was once praised for spotting a small overpayment while the service was losing a fortune in mileage. She has never forgotten what the praise taught her about which details rise through an office. People around the corner outside Coastal Barber know Sybil by a small Wednesday habit. Sybil usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “The second portion costs less than my mileage for investigating it. I can't erase the charge, but I can put both numbers on the same sheet for the person reviewing it.”

**What Sybil wants.** To show both the food cost and the investigation cost on the review.

**Family.** [Elsa Allen](../05/10-midnight-cinnamon.md#ah-r0696) is Sybil’s cousin. Sybil asks before bringing somebody along. Elsa can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Midnight cinnamon*, Elsa says, “I was fasting when the first tray came out.”

**People in this story.** [Amin Bach](#ah-r0136). The cost of a twist of pepper is the auditor's smallest example of the difference between meals and people.

**Personality.** Sybil is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Sybil is concerned.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a boxy denim jacket and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Sybil has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, with brown hair pinned away from the temples. Sybil folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A visiting supervisor wants the second dinner withdrawn. Sybil asks for a meal to be held while the people who eat it explain the second portion.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Sybil stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- When someone invents an answer, Sybil asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Pia Mercerly

AH-R0140 · Age 52 · Community cook · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Pia has prepared enough comfort food to know that a gentle cook isn't always what a person wants. The retired machinist at the western house enjoys explaining exactly how she has got his partner's recipe wrong. Saturday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Pia stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “The man gives me his late partner's recipes and corrects every batch. I want him to sit in my kitchen one afternoon while I'm still making the mistakes.”

**What Pia wants.** To give the retired cook a say in one whole afternoon of preparation.

**Family.** [Devin Jeong](../05/02-the-crane-shadow.md#ah-r0617) is Pia’s cousin. Pia makes room at the table. Devin brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The crane shadow*, Devin says, “I thought the bird was a stain and scrubbed one wing away.”

**People in this story.** [Tori Gran](#ah-r0135). They exchange a blank shopping list for some of the diner's most opinionated recipes. Also in the story is [Eamon Tor](#ah-r0134) (home diner).

**Personality.** Pia loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Pia is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Pia raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The diner asks to have company without being asked about care. Pia lets the diner start an ordinary conversation and leaves the care questions for another time.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Pia separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Pia offers one about their own embarrassment instead.
