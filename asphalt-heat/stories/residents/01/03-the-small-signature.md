# The small signature

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Bank of the Coast and the downtown fountain park.** Bank of the Coast displays a ceremonial check for a park endowment. Its smallest signature belongs to a snack vendor whose contribution was eleven dollars.

## Vera Rios

AH-R0021 · Age 62 · Self-made millionaire and cold-store owner · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Vera built her fortune keeping food cold for businesses that couldn't buy their own storage. During the first summer she slept beside a faulty alarm. Today plenty of people call her generous. Very few remember that she used to owe the snack seller lunch money. Friday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Vera buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “For years I bought my crews soup from that cart. I gave the big check because I wanted my late wife's name on a bench. Now the vendor is asking who can actually afford to sit there at lunchtime.”

**What Vera wants.** To put a memorial where her wife would actually have spent a lunch break.

**Family.** [Emma Davis](../09/01-a-room-above-the-dryers.md#ah-r1206) is Vera’s second cousin. Vera picks the meeting place. Emma sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *A room above the dryers*, Emma says, “I live across the landing. I asked for free sittings for the old people nobody photographs unless they're blowing candles out.”

**People in this story.** [Gil Atwood](#ah-r0022) (former lunch vendor). They knew each other over a cart long before anybody put their names on the same check.

**Personality.** Vera admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Vera learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Vera has a long silver braid, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Vera turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- The paper wants a photograph with only the biggest donor. Vera asks the vendor where they would like to stand and tells the photographer who paid the eleven dollars.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Vera writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Vera describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Gil Atwood

AH-R0022 · Age 55 · Park snack vendor · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Gil fixes the park cart's smallest wheel with a piece of the chess set the children broke. Repairing the table would let him retire the makeshift axle. He is tired of donors assuming that gratitude pays for spare parts. People around Ferris Deli know Gil by a small Thursday habit. Gil asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “My eleven dollars were the change regulars left in a jar after a boy broke the chess table. Somebody called me a philanthropist. I still owe those regulars a chess table.”

**What Gil wants.** To replace the children's chess table before the ceremony.

**Family.** [Daniel Song](../05/01-salt-in-the-pockets.md#ah-r0606) is Gil’s second cousin. Gil keeps the photographs. Daniel knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Salt in the pockets*, Daniel says, “I need the big coat to keep my knees warm on the early ferry.”

**People in this story.** [Vera Rios](#ah-r0021) (former lunch customer). They knew each other over a cart long before anybody put their names on the same check.

**Personality.** Gil has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Gil has short copper curls, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a moss-green cardigan. It usually goes with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. When somebody else has the floor, Gil squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- The new chess table arrives too high for one of its players. Gil puts one of the actual playing pieces into the affected player's hand before accepting delivery.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Gil offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- If accused of favoritism, Gil explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Luz Aranda

AH-R0023 · Age 27 · Bank teller · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** Luz counts tips separately from wages because her mother once lost benefits over money somebody only thought she'd earned. She thinks collecting coins deserves as much paper trail as accepting a six-figure transfer. Monday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Luz prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I counted the jar twice and kept the bent coin. It's in my drawer because I liked knowing exactly which part of that giant number came from us. I've offered to count the next jar in public.”

**What Luz wants.** To have each little contribution visible in the endowment record.

**Family.** [Sonia Neal](../10/10-the-kitchen-without-a-boss.md#ah-r1444) is Luz’s parent’s cousin. Luz makes the call. Sonia is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The kitchen without a boss*, Sonia says, “I supply eggs and normally ask for the boss.”

**People in this story.** [Min Calder](#ah-r0029). They count the little donations against the costs the formal accounts left out.

**Personality.** Luz reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Luz trusts most.

**Appearance.** Luz has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. When somebody else has the floor, Luz looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- The paper wants a photograph with only the biggest donor. Luz asks the vendor where they would like to stand and tells the photographer who paid the eleven dollars.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Luz gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- If told to back off, Luz does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Silas Pike

AH-R0024 · Age 47 · Estate lawyer · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** Silas went into estate law after his mother signed a paper she could not read. He earns most of his money from clients who like difficult clauses. His most interesting work begins when those two facts collide. On Friday, near Harbour Bakery, Silas buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I wrote the gift restriction in language the vendor couldn't read. My own mother left school at twelve. I owe them a version she could read aloud without stopping.”

**What Silas wants.** To rewrite the gift terms in words a street seller can challenge.

**Family.** [Dana Randall](../10/01-a-closing-hour.md#ah-r1351) is Silas’s stepsibling. Silas leaves the first voice message. Dana calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A closing hour*, Dana says, “I advertised always open before I had a daughter.”

**People in this story.** [Etta Webb](#ah-r0026). One drafts the words of the gift. The other has to find out if a child can actually use what those words buy.

**Personality.** Silas has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Silas's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Silas has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a loose dark braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cream pullover with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Silas holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The new chess table arrives too high for one of its players. Silas puts one of the actual playing pieces into the affected player's hand before accepting delivery.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Silas moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If the joke lands badly, Silas seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Joan Morley

AH-R0025 · Age 58 · Park gardener · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Joan looks after park soil on a contractor's budget that changes every spring. She distrusts gifts for things that need watering because no donor has ever wanted their name on the water bill. On Monday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Joan pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “The proposed bench faces straight into the sprinkler. I put a folding chair there and invited the donor out at six in the morning. I want one decision made with wet shoes.”

**What Joan wants.** To move the bench out of the sprinkler's reach.

**Family.** [Martin Rahman](../05/02-the-crane-shadow.md#ah-r0611) is Joan’s cousin raised in the same household. Joan chooses the familiar route. Martin can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The crane shadow*, Martin says, “I painted the gull where the shadow touched on the day I started.”

**People in this story.** [Sol Lane](#ah-r0030). They are walking the proposed bench site at the hour when a violin and a sprinkler can both be heard.

**Personality.** Joan listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Joan has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a small enamel bird on the lapel. In conversation Joan leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- The paper wants a photograph with only the biggest donor. Joan asks the vendor where they would like to stand and tells the photographer who paid the eleven dollars.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Joan waits at the lit stop with them and checks whether the next service is one they can actually board.
- When a second witness disagrees, Joan keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Etta Webb

AH-R0026 · Age 36 · Chess coach · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Etta started the chess club so kids could lose at something that wasn't school. She refuses to post rankings. She can, however, recite the first move of every child who has ever beaten her. On Tuesday, near the blocks below Foundry Lofts, Etta checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “Our best player can't grip the heavy replacement pieces. I brought along the wooden set my father whittled. The donor played her and lost both rooks.”

**What Etta wants.** To find pieces the club's strongest player can grip.

**Family.** [Elise Cho](../06/05-the-first-straight-paycheck.md#ah-r0796) is Etta’s older cousin. Etta brings the paper and a pen. Elise decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The first straight paycheck*, Elise says, “I am the mother who always took his gifts. I need to learn to accept a cheaper birthday present without asking why.”

**People in this story.** [Silas Pike](#ah-r0024). One drafts the words of the gift. The other has to find out if a child can actually use what those words buy.

**Personality.** Etta talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Etta's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Etta has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Etta raises one eyebrow long before saying yes during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The new chess table arrives too high for one of its players. Etta puts one of the actual playing pieces into the affected player's hand before accepting delivery.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Etta helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Etta says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Perry Han

AH-R0027 · Age 41 · Wealth manager · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Perry grew up being moved to the edge of school pictures whenever somebody taller arrived. At work he took the flattering crop before he thought about the person on its edge. He has asked the vendor to choose the replacement. On Friday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Perry likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I was the one who cropped the vendor out of the press picture. I used to sell school photos and hated making children stand at the edge. I called the editor myself.”

**What Perry wants.** To restore the vendor's place in the newspaper photograph.

**Family.** [Cian Reese](../02/01-hem-by-hem.md#ah-r0156) is Perry’s first cousin once removed. Perry calls about good news first. Cian is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Hem by hem*, Cian says, “I can't stand long enough for a fitting. The bride carried the mirror to my chair.”

**People in this story.** [Ava Baptiste](#ah-r0028). They both worked on the press coverage and have different mistakes to repair.

**Personality.** Perry prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. Perry has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. Perry starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation. For a press event Perry brings a square of lens cloth in the same pocket as a carefully folded tie.

**Under pressure**

- The paper wants a photograph with only the biggest donor. Perry asks the vendor where they would like to stand and tells the photographer who paid the eleven dollars.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Perry draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Perry clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Ava Baptiste

AH-R0028 · Age 32 · Community reporter · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Ava can produce seven hundred words about any ribbon cutting in twenty minutes. She secretly misses the old district desk where every misspelled name brought an angry relative to the office. Sunday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Ava stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “The big check made my deadline easy. Then the vendor asked me to name the eleven people behind the eleven dollars. I gave up my afternoon off to find the first four.”

**What Ava wants.** To identify the regulars who filled the eleven-dollar jar.

**Family.** [Thomas Sun](../10/08-no-lifeguard-at-dusk.md#ah-r1427) is Ava’s second cousin. Ava remembers the birthdays. Thomas asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *No lifeguard at dusk*, Thomas says, “I thought we had budgeted for coverage until dusk.”

**People in this story.** [Perry Han](#ah-r0027). They both worked on the press coverage and have different mistakes to repair.

**Personality.** Ava never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Ava can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Ava has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Ava presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- The new chess table arrives too high for one of its players. Ava puts one of the actual playing pieces into the affected player's hand before accepting delivery.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Ava stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Ava may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Min Calder

AH-R0029 · Age 39 · Foundation bookkeeper · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Min used to balance the books for a dance hall that ran out of money with six weddings still to host. Public accounts make her nervous when they only record what happened to the cash. Monday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Min often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “The bank charged more to print the display than the cart raised. I separated those lines on the public statement. It will make our winter appeal awkward, and it ought to.”

**What Min wants.** To publish the cost of promoting the donation alongside the donation.

**Family.** [Jesse Arnett](../06/02-nine-unpaid-hours.md#ah-r0766) is Min’s cousin raised in the same household. Min offers the first invitation. Jesse gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *Nine unpaid hours*, Jesse says, “I took another job at sea before the meeting.”

**People in this story.** [Luz Aranda](#ah-r0023). They count the little donations against the costs the formal accounts left out.

**Personality.** Min often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Min have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Min is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Min looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The paper wants a photograph with only the biggest donor. Min asks the vendor where they would like to stand and tells the photographer who paid the eleven dollars.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Min checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Min rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Sol Lane

AH-R0030 · Age 67 · Street violinist · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Sol's wife played the stronger opening and Sol played the better ending. They passed an entire courtship arguing about the middle game. Playing at a dedication is easy. Hearing chess clocks afterward is the hard part. People around Lighthouse Cove know Sol by a small Thursday habit. Sol takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “My wife and I used to play chess with bottle caps here. I'm giving the opening concert, but I want the first minute quiet enough to hear the clocks on the tables.”

**What Sol wants.** To play something that will leave the first chess game audible.

**Family.** [Beatrice Ko](../05/13-the-fountain-score.md#ah-r0721) is Sol’s cousin’s child. Sol puts the kettle on. Beatrice chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The fountain score*, Beatrice says, “I chose a saucepan with a dent from my first flat.”

**People in this story.** [Joan Morley](#ah-r0025). They are walking the proposed bench site at the hour when a violin and a sprinkler can both be heard.

**Personality.** Sol thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Sol sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Sol has two white streaks in otherwise dark hair, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Sol leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- The new chess table arrives too high for one of its players. Sol puts one of the actual playing pieces into the affected player's hand before accepting delivery.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Sol suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Sol asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.
