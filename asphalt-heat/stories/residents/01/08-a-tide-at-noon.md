# A tide at noon

[The people you meet first](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Pier Beach.** At Pier Beach a spring tide erases the line painted for the annual sandcastle judging. Half the entrants have built on the far side.

## Paz Harrow

AH-R0071 · Age 31 · Lifeguard · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Paz saves a little sand from the first day of each season in old film canisters. Her younger brother considers the collection proof of excessive seriousness. She privately suspects he's right. Sunday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Paz stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “My little brother still thinks his castle was disqualified because I didn't like it. I moved the flags to dry ground and asked every builder to walk me to their front door.”

**What Paz wants.** To let every young builder be judged from a reachable patch of sand.

**Family.** [Selena Roberts](../03/02-a-ship-without-postcards.md#ah-r0319) is Paz’s stepsibling. Paz checks the date twice. Selena brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A ship without postcards*, Selena says, “I work nights and never see a blue sky here.”

**People in this story.** [Lia Banks](#ah-r0076) (beach colleague). They move the judging line together and discover there are two different meanings of a reachable castle.

**Personality.** Paz often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Paz have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Paz has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a plum-colored cardigan with a watch on a cracked leather band. In conversation Paz steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- The water reaches a child's castle before judging. Paz gives the builder time to show the judges what the water took.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Paz writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Paz rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Dario Flint

AH-R0072 · Age 45 · Ice cream vendor · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Dario's cart is the second business he has owned. The first failed because he never charged friends. He has devised elaborate small jobs children can do in return for treats, a system nobody mistakes for sound accounting. On Sunday, near the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, Dario checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I let children freeze the turrets in my cart. They kept opening the lid and my stock softened. I sold the soft ones cheap and put their names on the receipts as architects.”

**What Dario wants.** To replace the softened stock without charging the children for their turrets.

**Family.** [Stella Hull](../02/12-the-sheltered-corner.md#ah-r0269) is Dario’s second cousin. A relative kept reporting household news on postcards long after everybody could afford to phone. Reading one takes the family back to an ordinary morning rather than a big event. Dario prefers an ordinary request to a grand declaration of affection. Asked about *The sheltered corner*, Stella says, “My wheelchair fits the new turn. I brought the planners cake and asked them to hear the old riders too.”

**People in this story.** [Ada Firth](#ah-r0079). Turrets from the ice cart and flags from the old competition meet in the families' photograph.

**Personality.** Dario thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Dario sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Dario has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. When somebody else has the floor, Dario leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- The judges ask the fastest builder to speak for everyone. Dario asks each child how they would enter their own castle.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Dario takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Dario asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

## Aster Loy

AH-R0073 · Age 70 · Retired surveyor · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** Aster can calculate a slope in her head and can't make herself throw out an old calendar. This combination was harmless until a tide line involved children who had worked all morning. On Monday, near Tideline Market, Aster picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I painted the line with a tide table from the wrong year. I told the smallest builder first. She asked for my ruler and put me to work on drainage.”

**What Aster wants.** To correct the tide mark in front of the builders affected.

**Family.** [Jeff Jones](../06/09-a-room-for-a-father.md#ah-r0839) is Aster’s younger cousin. Aster asks before bringing somebody along. Jeff can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A room for a father*, Jeff says, “I paid for the train fare and secretly hoped he'd miss the train.”

**People in this story.** [Cato Hull](#ah-r0078). One knows where the line should have gone. The other knows how the water reached it anyway.

**Personality.** Aster gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Aster privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Aster is easy to recognize by closely cropped salt-and-pepper hair and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Aster holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The water reaches a child's castle before judging. Aster gives the builder time to show the judges what the water took.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Aster asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Aster offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Nico Salas

AH-R0074 · Age 36 · Sand sculptor · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Nico makes perfect castles for hotels that photograph them before admitting guests. His nephew's crooked castle with a sink has made him think about the difference between a castle that sells rooms and one a child imagines entering. People around the steps at Pier Beach know Nico by a small Wednesday habit. Nico watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I earn money making flawless hotel displays. My nephew wanted the ugly castle with a kitchen sink to win. I made him show me the pipes before we went back to judging.”

**What Nico wants.** To judge one castle by how someone imagines living there.

**Family.** [Frankie Palmer](../05/10-midnight-cinnamon.md#ah-r0698) is Nico’s half-sibling. Nico makes room at the table. Frankie brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Midnight cinnamon*, Frankie says, “I used to pour my wages into scratch cards on this street.”

**People in this story.** [Pip Arlen](#ah-r0080). They decide what an award ought to recognize after a professional sculptor notices the child's kitchen.

**Personality.** Nico uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Nico well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Nico has a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Nico gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- The judges ask the fastest builder to speak for everyone. Nico asks each child how they would enter their own castle.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Nico says what they know in their own name and refuses to treat a guess as something the child has to disprove.
- After a family quarrel, Nico brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Ely Kern

AH-R0075 · Age 43 · Hotel concierge · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Ely's concierge notebook contains the quickest remedies for a hundred small disappointments. He is trying to fill the last pages with things that only become possible after a plan goes slightly wrong. On Saturday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Ely takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I sent guests down in polished shoes. One guest took hers off and stayed for hours. I want our welcome card to say when wet sand starts and who has towels.”

**What Ely wants.** To add practical tide and towel information to the hotel card.

**Family.** [Joel Arendt](../10/02-a-last-ship-together.md#ah-r1366) is Ely’s older cousin. Ely saves a place. Joel chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *A last ship together*, Joel says, “I spent forty years packing those two lunches.”

**People in this story.** [Mona Neri](#ah-r0077). They help small visitors find both a dry towel and an unthreatening way to collect their prize.

**Personality.** Ely feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a moss-green cardigan and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Ely has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. Ely leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The water reaches a child's castle before judging. Ely gives the builder time to show the judges what the water took.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Ely asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Ely lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Lia Banks

AH-R0076 · Age 32 · Beach wheelchair attendant · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Lia learned to read sand density pushing her father's chair. A photograph of an accessible beach usually shows the clean path. She wants people to photograph what happens when the path ends. Sunday often includes a stop at Lighthouse Cove, where Lia takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “The judging route crossed a soft patch I knew wouldn't hold a wheel. I borrowed three boards from the marina and made the judges take the long route with everyone else.”

**What Lia wants.** To keep the wheelchair route open through the entire judging session.

**Family.** [Clyde Darzi](../02/05-the-pawned-trumpet.md#ah-r0195) is Lia’s cousin raised in the same household. Lia picks the meeting place. Clyde sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The pawned trumpet*, Clyde says, “I repair brass at night. I can give the player a different instrument but I can't transfer those teeth marks.”

**People in this story.** [Paz Harrow](#ah-r0071) (beach colleague). They move the judging line together and discover there are two different meanings of a reachable castle.

**Personality.** Lia treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Lia has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Lia folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The judges ask the fastest builder to speak for everyone. Lia asks each child how they would enter their own castle.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Lia asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Lia changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Mona Neri

AH-R0077 · Age 54 · Pediatric dentist · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** Mona works with children who are often frightened before they reach her room. On the beach she thought she had left that white-shirt authority behind and was startled to discover how easily it followed her outdoors. On Saturday, near Paper & Pine Books, Mona starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I donated the prize bucket. The winning child was afraid to come near my white shirt. I sat on the sand with my back to her until she came and filled it herself.”

**What Mona wants.** To let the winning child choose when to collect the bucket.

**Family.** [Jo Allard](../10/09-a-house-divided-kindly.md#ah-r1438) is Mona’s cousin’s child. Mona keeps the photographs. Jo knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A house divided kindly*, Jo says, “I am the grandchild who expected to inherit this house.”

**People in this story.** [Ely Kern](#ah-r0075). They help small visitors find both a dry towel and an unthreatening way to collect their prize.

**Personality.** Mona is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Mona is concerned.

**Appearance.** Mona has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Mona rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The water reaches a child's castle before judging. Mona gives the builder time to show the judges what the water took.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Mona tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When someone invents an answer, Mona asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Cato Hull

AH-R0078 · Age 41 · Ferry deckhand · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Cato worked boats before learning to swim properly. He dislikes stories that make seafarers sound fearless. Changing currents, failed castles and children's honest questions are more interesting to him than brave poses. Sunday often includes a stop at the steps at Pier Beach, where Cato watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “On my break I taught three children how water moves around a pier. Their castle vanished first. They want me back next year to show them how to build the same thing again.”

**What Cato wants.** To return next summer to try a new water channel with the children.

**Family.** [Victor Sun](../02/09-a-wall-of-birthdays.md#ah-r0231) is Cato’s older relative by marriage. Cato makes the call. Victor is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A wall of birthdays*, Victor says, “I want to measure myself again. Chemotherapy stole a little of my height and a lot of my nerve.”

**People in this story.** [Aster Loy](#ah-r0073). One knows where the line should have gone. The other knows how the water reached it anyway.

**Personality.** Cato loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Cato has a blunt cut just below the ears, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. When somebody else has the floor, Cato holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The judges ask the fastest builder to speak for everyone. Cato asks each child how they would enter their own castle.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Cato walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Cato offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Ada Firth

AH-R0079 · Age 68 · Beachcomber and pensioner · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** Ada associates every beach flag with the summer somebody mended it. She cannot remember who won most years. She can remember whose child was learning to knot a thread when the wind turned. Tuesday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Ada pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I found the old finish flag under the new tide. My husband sewed the sleeve on it the year our twins competed. I loaned it for the photo and brought it home.”

**What Ada wants.** To get the family flag home after the closing picture.

**Family.** [Briar Henry](../05/14-the-split-visit.md#ah-r0737) is Ada’s younger relative by marriage. Ada leaves the first voice message. Briar calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The split visit*, Briar says, “I was coming to fix the porch rail and ended up assembling a folding lunch table for three.”

**People in this story.** [Dario Flint](#ah-r0072). Turrets from the ice cart and flags from the old competition meet in the families' photograph.

**Personality.** Ada enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Ada is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Ada has silver temples and a dark fringe, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. When somebody else has the floor, Ada raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- The water reaches a child's castle before judging. Ada gives the builder time to show the judges what the water took.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Ada brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Ada keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Pip Arlen

AH-R0080 · Age 29 · Event accountant · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** Pip studied accountancy to leave the family souvenir shop and keeps finding ways to spend public budgets on things that fit inside a child's pocket. It is the family habit that proved hardest to leave. Friday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Pip checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I budgeted for one trophy and found myself facing thirty-two mayors of imaginary towns. I paid the photographer for contact sheets and let every mayor sign their own municipal record.”

**What Pip wants.** To give every young mayor a signed copy of a town photograph.

**Family.** [Taylor Sethi](../07/01-the-uniform-money.md#ah-r0908) is Pip’s older relative by marriage. Pip chooses the familiar route. Taylor can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The uniform money*, Taylor says, “I wanted to give anonymous money. My niece asked why I was so embarrassed to help someone I knew.”

**People in this story.** [Nico Salas](#ah-r0074). They decide what an award ought to recognize after a professional sculptor notices the child's kitchen.

**Personality.** Pip is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Pip has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for a loose dark braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a pale blue work shirt with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. In conversation Pip counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- The judges ask the fastest builder to speak for everyone. Pip asks each child how they would enter their own castle.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Pip writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Pip helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.
