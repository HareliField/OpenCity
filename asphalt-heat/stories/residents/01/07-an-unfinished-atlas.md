## Mavis Yeo

AH-R0061 · Age 46 · Bookseller · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Mavis bought the bookshop's old stock, including four boxes nobody had opened in ten years. She pays the rent selling new books and keeps the boxes under her desk for days when rent feels like the entire point. On Wednesday, near Harbour Bakery, Mavis buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I priced the atlas at four dollars before I saw the paste. My parents used to disagree about the name of that street. I pulled the price off and invited them separately to identify it.”

**What Mavis wants.** To hear both parents identify the street before committing a label.

**Family.** [Audrey Mason](../02/06-a-red-mitten.md#ah-r0204) is Mavis’s cousin. Mavis brings a spare shopping bag. Audrey chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A red mitten*, Audrey says, “I used the mitten to warm the metal armrest for a man in a thin shirt.”

**People in this story.** [Carl Reeve](#ah-r0066). The clearing worker brought the atlas to the shop, and the bookseller must now decide what a fair return means. Also in the story is [Cal Esmond](#ah-r0069) (research visitor).

**Personality.** Mavis feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and a train token tied to a zipper pull. Mavis has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Mavis draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A collector offers cash for the unexamined atlas. Mavis asks the bookseller to put the atlas somewhere safe while its ownership is checked.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Mavis asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Mavis lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Rene Mott

AH-R0062 · Age 52 · Map conservator · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** Rene spent a year repairing flood-damaged school papers, most of which nobody famous had touched. He developed a tenderness for cheap glue. It is often the only signature a child leaves on a map. People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Rene by a small Monday habit. Rene checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “My hand shook the day I ruined a school register with too much water. I can lift this page, but the paste itself tells us when it was made. I want photographs before anyone reaches for a brush.”

**What Rene wants.** To document both sides of the handmade page before lifting anything.

**Family.** [Charlie Starr](../06/09-a-room-for-a-father.md#ah-r0834) is Rene’s cousin. Rene asks before bringing somebody along. Charlie can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A room for a father*, Charlie says, “I painted the spare room blue. He called it a color for a nursery and I nearly sent him packing.”

**People in this story.** [Ona Voss](#ah-r0067). They compare the age of the paste with the direction of a child's hand stitches.

**Personality.** Rene treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Rene is easy to recognize by short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Rene takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A family member disputes the penciled street. Rene marks the disputed piece of street in pencil and keeps the alternative name beside it.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Rene gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Rene changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Elsa Venn

AH-R0063 · Age 63 · Taxi driver · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Elsa knows the town by what a passenger might smell with the window open. Street names have changed twice on her old taxi route. The woman with the coffee always stood in the same breeze. People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Elsa by a small Saturday habit. Elsa checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I drove that lane on my first night shift. It dead-ended at a woman selling coffee from a thermos. I'm trying to remember her name before the map gets a label.”

**What Elsa wants.** To recover the coffee seller's name while the old route can still be walked.

**Family.** [Patricia Ryan](../08/03-the-guarantee.md#ah-r1076) is Elsa’s younger cousin. Elsa makes room at the table. Patricia brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The guarantee*, Patricia says, “I can pay the interest for six months if the family stops calling it a rescue every time we eat.”

**People in this story.** [Melle Juric](#ah-r0068). They walk the likely street with somebody who remembers a face more easily than a name.

**Personality.** Elsa is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Elsa is concerned.

**Appearance.** Elsa is easy to recognize by a long silver braid and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a brown corduroy jacket with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges, Elsa leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A collector offers cash for the unexamined atlas. Elsa asks the bookseller to put the atlas somewhere safe while its ownership is checked.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Elsa draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- When someone invents an answer, Elsa asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Noam Booth

AH-R0064 · Age 57 · Printmaker · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Noam ran a print studio on discarded industrial ink. A well-funded art school once attributed his distinctive blue to taste. He wants the port cleaners who saved the leftovers to see their part in his work. On Sunday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Noam looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “The blue isn't sea. We used that ink for drains because it was free from the shipyard. I want the caption to say who paid for the original plate.”

**What Noam wants.** To credit the crew that supplied the salvaged blue ink.

**Family.** [Daria Singh](../03/11-the-widow-s-razor.md#ah-r0405) is Noam’s cousin. Noam saves a place. Daria chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The widow's razor*, Daria says, “I keep the spare handle. It belonged to a matched pair we bought the day we opened separate shops.”

**People in this story.** [Fara Bloom](#ah-r0070). One recognizes the industrial ink. The other has a cupboard where the class can see it.

**Personality.** Noam loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a dark green knitted vest and a little coil of soft measuring tape. Noam has a round face with a pronounced chin, with short copper curls. Noam rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A family member disputes the penciled street. Noam marks the disputed piece of street in pencil and keeps the alternative name beside it.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Noam tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Noam offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Una Vida

AH-R0065 · Age 44 · Adult literacy tutor · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Una learned to read at the same table where she now tutors. She deliberately leaves the teacher's chair empty on Thursday evenings so a student with another kind of knowledge can use it first. Monday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Una pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “One of my students can read the tiny block letters upside down. He learned setting type. I asked him to teach Thursday's class and chose a seat at the back.”

**What Una wants.** To give the former typesetter an evening at the front of the class.

**Family.** [Dina Hooper](../09/15-the-missing-name-on-the-stern.md#ah-r1344) is Una’s half-sibling. Una picks the meeting place. Dina sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The missing name on the stern*, Dina says, “I kept the original stencil on the back of a cupboard door.”

**People in this story.** [Cal Esmond](#ah-r0069). A reading class and a thesis both depend on letting a former typesetter correct the map.

**Personality.** Una enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Una is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a boxy denim jacket and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Una has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, with short black hair swept back. Una steadies a cup with both hands while listening during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A collector offers cash for the unexamined atlas. Una asks the bookseller to put the atlas somewhere safe while its ownership is checked.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Una asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Una keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Carl Reeve

AH-R0066 · Age 35 · Estate clearance worker · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Carl clears estates for relatives who are sometimes four countries away. He dislikes deciding which ordinary things count as effects. A book with a folded leaf is more difficult than a cabinet of costly china. On Tuesday, near Tideline Market, Carl picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I saved the atlas from the paper skip because its spine matched my coat. Now the family wants it back. I'll tell them where it is once the shop agrees to keep the tracing.”

**What Carl wants.** To return the family's atlas and keep a lawful tracing at the shop.

**Family.** [Lina Barrett](../08/14-the-new-language-at-dinner.md#ah-r1187) is Carl’s cousin raised in the same household. Carl keeps the photographs. Lina knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The new language at dinner*, Lina says, “I was ashamed my language sounded provincial when I moved here.”

**People in this story.** [Mavis Yeo](#ah-r0061). The clearing worker brought the atlas to the shop, and the bookseller must now decide what a fair return means.

**Personality.** Carl is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Carl is easy to recognize by a high puff tied with plain cord and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Carl squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A family member disputes the penciled street. Carl marks the disputed piece of street in pencil and keeps the alternative name beside it.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Carl asks what the friend actually said last time and checks whether anybody is expecting them at home.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Carl helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Ona Voss

AH-R0067 · Age 51 · Bookbinder · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** Ona repaired hotel menus before anybody entrusted her with books. She earns more rebinding legal registers, but a child's uneven stitches still hold her attention longer than a perfect office volume. People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Ona by a small Wednesday habit. Ona knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I can tell a right-handed child did the sewing. The knots point away from the working hand. I brought thread for visitors to try it before they believe me.”

**What Ona wants.** To teach pupils to read the direction of the stitches.

**Family.** [Drew Silva](../08/04-first-shift-back.md#ah-r1084) is Ona’s stepsibling. Ona makes the call. Drew is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *First shift back*, Drew says, “I trained under the returning worker. Now we have to admit I'm the one who knows the new forms.”

**People in this story.** [Rene Mott](#ah-r0062). They compare the age of the paste with the direction of a child's hand stitches.

**Personality.** Ona finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Ona has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Ona gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- A collector offers cash for the unexamined atlas. Ona asks the bookseller to put the atlas somewhere safe while its ownership is checked.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Ona suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Ona waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Melle Juric

AH-R0068 · Age 38 · Speech therapist · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** Melle became a speech therapist after learning that her father could name a place if his feet remembered the approach. She buys comfortable shoes before buying a new set of picture cards. On Saturday, near the blocks below Foundry Lofts, Melle checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “My father remembers the coffee seller's face but loses names halfway through a sentence. We walked the old route with no questions until he named her on his own.”

**What Melle wants.** To give her father a walk on which he can find the name himself.

**Family.** [Dominic Alvarez](../04/05-the-dead-man-s-tab.md#ah-r0500) is Melle’s parent’s cousin. Melle leaves the first voice message. Dominic calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The dead man's tab*, Dominic says, “I'm his old victim's sister. I offered to cover only the meals that went to people still alive.”

**People in this story.** [Elsa Venn](#ah-r0063). They walk the likely street with somebody who remembers a face more easily than a name.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Melle has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. Out of work clothes, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. In conversation Melle turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A family member disputes the penciled street. Melle marks the disputed piece of street in pencil and keeps the alternative name beside it.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Melle finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Melle listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Cal Esmond

AH-R0069 · Age 28 · Graduate cartographer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** Cal came here on a small research grant and is privately terrified the city will prove too ordinary for a thesis. The bookseller has begun showing him exactly how many ordinary streets need their history checked. On Thursday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Cal stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “My funding depends on mapping a vanished street. It would be convenient if this were it. I wrote down three reasons it might be somewhere else before opening my laptop.”

**What Cal wants.** To test whether the thesis street and the atlas street are actually the same.

**Family.** [Kathleen Lu](../02/08-low-water-music.md#ah-r0224) is Cal’s second cousin. Cal chooses the familiar route. Kathleen can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Low water music*, Kathleen says, “I wrote a tune around the note and discovered it changes when the rock is wet.”

**People in this story.** [Una Vida](#ah-r0065). A reading class and a thesis both depend on letting a former typesetter correct the map. Also in the story is [Mavis Yeo](#ah-r0061) (bookseller).

**Personality.** Cal is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Cal loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Cal has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Cal folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A collector offers cash for the unexamined atlas. Cal asks the bookseller to put the atlas somewhere safe while its ownership is checked.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Cal asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- If somebody calls for applause, Cal gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

## Fara Bloom

AH-R0070 · Age 60 · School caretaker · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** Fara has the keys to every school cupboard and uses that power sparingly. She wants the pupils to see the old city in the paintwork without letting visitors turn a working school into a personal museum. Wednesday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Fara buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “There is a copy of that blue ink under the third coat of paint in our map cupboard. I'm asking the head for permission to uncover a square the size of a stamp.”

**What Fara wants.** To secure permission to expose one small square of old paint.

**Family.** [Charlotte Sawyer](../06/12-the-late-crossing.md#ah-r0865) is Fara’s first cousin once removed. Fara brings the paper and a pen. Charlotte decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The late crossing*, Charlotte says, “I sit in the back because being driven after dark reminds me of my parents driving when they fought.”

**People in this story.** [Noam Booth](#ah-r0064). One recognizes the industrial ink. The other has a cupboard where the class can see it.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Fara takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Fara has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for closely cropped salt-and-pepper hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. In conversation Fara leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- A family member disputes the penciled street. Fara marks the disputed piece of street in pencil and keeps the alternative name beside it.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Fara tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Fara asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.
