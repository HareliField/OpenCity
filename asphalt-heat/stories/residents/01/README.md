# The people you meet first

[All residents](../README.md) · [Alphabetical directory](../directory.md)

Residents 1–150. Each episode follows ten people who are affected differently by the same event.

| Episode | People | Opening situation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [The blue suit](01-the-blue-suit.md) | 1–10 | A blue suit comes out of a washer at the Wash House streaked white on the morning of a citizenship ceremony. The owner has ninety minutes and exactly one change of clothes. |
| [A chair on the quay](02-a-chair-on-the-quay.md) | 11–20 | A wooden kitchen chair turns up beside the rail sidings at the Port of Asphalt. A retiring crane operator recognizes the height marks cut into its back. |
| [The small signature](03-the-small-signature.md) | 21–30 | Bank of the Coast displays a ceremonial check for a park endowment. Its smallest signature belongs to a snack vendor whose contribution was eleven dollars. |
| [The wrong bicycle](04-the-wrong-bicycle.md) | 31–40 | Outside the Old Town police station two nearly identical yellow bicycles are returned to the opposite owners. One has a taped saddle, the other a bell that sticks. |
| [The aquarium](05-the-aquarium.md) | 41–50 | A landlord empties a ground-floor flat off the brick passage. The tenant has gone to stay with family. A hundred-litre aquarium with two living fish remains on the pavement. |
| [Window seven](06-window-seven.md) | 51–60 | A patient in St. Mary Hospital asks to see the sunrise from window seven one last time. A delivery cage, a curtain rail and two institutional timetables stand in the way. |
| [An unfinished atlas](07-an-unfinished-atlas.md) | 61–70 | Paper & Pine Books receives an atlas with a handmade map pasted across the coastal page. A penciled street disappears under the spine. |
| [A tide at noon](08-a-tide-at-noon.md) | 71–80 | At Pier Beach a spring tide erases the line painted for the annual sandcastle judging. Half the entrants have built on the far side. |
| [The porch light](09-the-porch-light.md) | 81–90 | On a western residential street a porch lamp has burned every night for thirty years. It goes dark the week the owner puts the house up for sale. |
| [The thirteenth doughnut](10-the-thirteenth-doughnut.md) | 91–100 | Night Shift Donuts sells a dozen in a box designed for thirteen. Someone starts leaving a thirteenth doughnut for the person who opens the shop next door. |
| [Ten fingers](11-ten-fingers.md) | 101–110 | Verve Nail Studio's oldest regular asks ten people to choose one color each for her birthday appointment and forbids anyone to coordinate. |
| [The last green](12-the-last-green.md) | 111–120 | A driver who used Harbor Viaduct every morning for twenty years has to surrender his license. He asks to ride his usual route once as a passenger. |
| [The borrowed bass](13-the-borrowed-bass.md) | 121–130 | A bass borrowed for a fountain-park rehearsal has a pawn ticket tucked under its pickguard. The printed redemption date is tomorrow. |
| [Two portions](14-two-portions.md) | 131–140 | A volunteer's meal list on the western residential streets has two portions beside an address where only one person is registered. |
| [The empty berth](15-the-empty-berth.md) | 141–150 | One of South Shore Harbor's four boats is temporarily lifted out for repairs. A rectangle of clean water makes the marina's private waiting list a subject of public curiosity. |
