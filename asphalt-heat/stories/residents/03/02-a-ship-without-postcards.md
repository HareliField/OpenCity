# A ship without postcards

## Edgar Joseph

AH-R0311 · Age 28 · Ship electrician · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember painting shadow cats on my first locker.” Saturday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Edgar pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I draw the alley bins because my mother knows cities by their cats.”

**What Edgar wants.** To draw the city's working cats for family.

**Family.** [Rain Yang](../08/14-the-new-language-at-dinner.md#ah-r1183) is Edgar’s cousin raised in the same household. Edgar leaves the first voice message. Rain calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The new language at dinner*, Rain says, “I speak his language but the agency only knows I clean offices.”

**People in this story.** [Hank Fry](#ah-r0314). An onboard drawing gives the souvenir seller an alternative to the hotel view.

**Personality.** Edgar is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Edgar asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Edgar has black hair twisted into a small knot, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a plum-colored cardigan. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Edgar turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A family ashore asks for a picture of the crew. Edgar asks the crew who wants to be visible in the family picture.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Edgar finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Edgar accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Anton March

AH-R0312 · Age 43 · Rail porter · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember waiting through unloading with a blanket around two shoulders.” On Thursday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Anton looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I want a picture of the crane shadows. My kid thinks the ships do all the lifting.”

**What Anton wants.** To send the child a crane-shadow photograph.

**Family.** [Alex Carr](../02/09-a-wall-of-birthdays.md#ah-r0233) is Anton’s cousin raised in the same household. Anton can always ask for five minutes alone. Alex asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A wall of birthdays*, Alex says, “I grew up elsewhere and married into this house.”

**People in this story.** [Iris Vidal](#ah-r0315). The crane shadows and the crooked rail fit into one unmistakable postcard.

**Personality.** Anton is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Anton is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Anton counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A souvenir shop offers to print the cards as an advert. Anton checks whether the families want their messages used outside the envelopes.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Anton asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Anton thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Ida Henry

AH-R0313 · Age 36 · Crew steward · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember orange rind kept dry in my sea bag.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Ida buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I bought a blank card to send my aunt the smell of her old soap.”

**What Ida wants.** To send the familiar scent without damaging the card.

**Family.** [Idris Yeo](../05/08-the-misplaced-umbrella.md#ah-r0671) is Ida’s cousin raised in the same household. Ida brings the paper and a pen. Idris decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The misplaced umbrella*, Idris says, “I stitched the patch from a dress my aunt wore on her only holiday.”

**People in this story.** [Nora Ray](#ah-r0317). They compare how little room a parcel needs to carry the idea of home.

**Personality.** Ida loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Ida remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Ida is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Ida gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A family ashore asks for a picture of the crew. Ida asks the crew who wants to be visible in the family picture.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Ida offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- When invited to tell the story later, Ida includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Hank Fry

AH-R0314 · Age 49 · Souvenir seller · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cards my mother displayed facing the wall.” People around Breakwater Way know Hank by a small Saturday habit. Hank stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I sell lighthouse cards printed inland. I asked the crew to show me their view from the bunk.”

**What Hank wants.** To stock a view from the crew bunks.

**Family.** [Merritt Hart](../09/15-the-missing-name-on-the-stern.md#ah-r1349) is Hank’s second cousin. Hank calls about good news first. Merritt is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The missing name on the stern*, Merritt says, “I thought I was hiring a painter. The apprentice taught me to say the owner's name without flattening the last sound.”

**People in this story.** [Edgar Joseph](#ah-r0311). An onboard drawing gives the souvenir seller an alternative to the hotel view.

**Personality.** Hank has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Hank is easy to recognize by short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Hank holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A souvenir shop offers to print the cards as an advert. Hank checks whether the families want their messages used outside the envelopes.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Hank offers to cover one concrete expense and leaves the friend room to say the expense isn't the real problem.
- When somebody trades on a title, Hank asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Iris Vidal

AH-R0315 · Age 32 · Industrial photographer · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first roll of film exposed through a torn pocket.” People around the corner outside Coastal Barber know Iris by a small Tuesday habit. Iris usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I am saving the crooked rail that proves my photograph was taken on the early shift.”

**What Iris wants.** To save an unmistakable print of the early rail shift.

**Family.** [Luis Soto](../07/04-a-civilian-apology.md#ah-r0938) is Iris’s stepsibling. Iris remembers the birthdays. Luis asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A civilian apology*, Luis says, “I was a probationer then. I kept quiet because the man signing the note was my mentor.”

**People in this story.** [Anton March](#ah-r0312). The crane shadows and the crooked rail fit into one unmistakable postcard. Also in the story is [Selena Roberts](#ah-r0319) (night shift subject).

**Personality.** Iris finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a moss-green cardigan and a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. Iris has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, with short black hair swept back. Iris presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A family ashore asks for a picture of the crew. Iris asks the crew who wants to be visible in the family picture.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Iris asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- When thanked for a shared success, Iris points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## August Jackson

AH-R0316 · Age 57 · Deck cook · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a letter arriving on the wrong continent.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know August by a small Saturday habit. August pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I fold the cards into boats. My granddaughter can open them only by reading flags.”

**What August wants.** To teach the grandchild to unfold the flagged card.

**Family.** [Patricia Ramos](../06/12-the-late-crossing.md#ah-r0864) is August’s younger relative by marriage. August offers the first invitation. Patricia gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The late crossing*, Patricia says, “I used to get lifts from my boss until I felt I couldn't refuse overtime.”

**People in this story.** [Lee Murphy](#ah-r0318). A folded boat reaches one grandchild while a dance-hall print finds an old partner.

**Personality.** August keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. August wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** August has short copper curls, a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, an olive canvas jacket. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, August takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A souvenir shop offers to print the cards as an advert. August checks whether the families want their messages used outside the envelopes.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. August asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- When somebody asks who said it first, August offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Nora Ray

AH-R0317 · Age 38 · Ship mechanic · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember moving with every possession in a flimsy bag.” Friday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Nora checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I wrote my address on the picture side because the picture looks more like home than my address does.”

**What Nora wants.** To get the home address on the preferred side of the picture.

**Family.** [Bailey Solis](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1306) is Nora’s stepsibling. Nora puts the kettle on. Bailey chooses the music. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Bailey says, “I can retouch it away. I offered a print with no edits so the client could make the first choice.”

**People in this story.** [Ida Henry](#ah-r0313). They compare how little room a parcel needs to carry the idea of home.

**Personality.** Nora always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Nora is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Nora has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a faded indigo overshirt with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. In conversation Nora touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A family ashore asks for a picture of the crew. Nora asks the crew who wants to be visible in the family picture.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Nora tells the stranger when the next opening is, then stays with the neighbor until their turn comes.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Nora suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Lee Murphy

AH-R0318 · Age 66 · Former ballroom hostess · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a ballroom door too narrow for our scenery.” People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Lee by a small Friday habit. Lee checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I found our old dance hall in the background of a harbor print and sent it to my ex.”

**What Lee wants.** To mail the dance-hall scene to a former partner.

**Family.** [Conrad Mann](../06/11-a-chair-of-one-s-own.md#ah-r0852) is Lee’s first cousin once removed. Lee chooses an ordinary story to tell. Conrad asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *A chair of one's own*, Conrad says, “I rent my chair and earn well. I never tell newcomers my partner pays our mortgage.”

**People in this story.** [August Jackson](#ah-r0316). A folded boat reaches one grandchild while a dance-hall print finds an old partner.

**Personality.** Lee has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Lee knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Lee has two white streaks in otherwise dark hair, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a plum-colored cardigan. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Lee raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- A souvenir shop offers to print the cards as an advert. Lee checks whether the families want their messages used outside the envelopes.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Lee takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Lee asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Selena Roberts

AH-R0319 · Age 43 · Night freight worker · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the silence before the first floodlight ignites.” On Tuesday, near the bank steps, Selena takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I work nights and never see a blue sky here. I drew the floodlights instead.”

**What Selena wants.** To draw the actual night shift lights.

**Family.** [Paz Harrow](../01/08-a-tide-at-noon.md#ah-r0071) is Selena’s stepsibling. Selena brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Paz checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A tide at noon*, Paz says, “My little brother still thinks his castle was disqualified because I didn't like it.”

**People in this story.** [Harold Byrd](#ah-r0320). They send the night lights and the lunch receipt as two honest views from one shift. Also in the story is [Iris Vidal](#ah-r0315) (harbor photographer).

**Personality.** Selena enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Selena has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with black hair twisted into a small knot. Selena looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A family ashore asks for a picture of the crew. Selena asks the crew who wants to be visible in the family picture.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Selena looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Selena puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Harold Byrd

AH-R0320 · Age 25 · Assistant navigator · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a packet of foreign coins taped to the pantry door.” On Monday, near the window at Night Shift Donuts, Harold knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I mailed a receipt for lunch. My family has been asking what an ordinary day costs.”

**What Harold wants.** To explain the cost of an ordinary crew lunch at home.

**Family.** [Isabel Doyle](../08/08-a-summer-marriage.md#ah-r1127) is Harold’s cousin raised in the same household. Harold brings a spare shopping bag. Isabel chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A summer marriage*, Isabel says, “I have been saving for a huge wedding. Hearing these two haggle over chairs has made me question mine.”

**People in this story.** [Selena Roberts](#ah-r0319). They send the night lights and the lunch receipt as two honest views from one shift.

**Personality.** Harold has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Harold can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Harold has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for close-cropped dark curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Harold rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- A souvenir shop offers to print the cards as an advert. Harold checks whether the families want their messages used outside the envelopes.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Harold keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If a friend borrows money, Harold agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.
