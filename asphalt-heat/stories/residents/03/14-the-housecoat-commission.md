## Oakley Han

AH-R0431 · Age 74 · Retired restaurateur · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a waiter ironing my sleeves before our first opening.” On Saturday, near Harbour Bakery, Oakley buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I want it lined in cold blue like the restaurant I used to run.”

**What Oakley wants.** To host a Sunday dinner in the blue-lined coat.

**Family.** [Silas Voss](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1309) is Oakley’s younger cousin. Oakley remembers the birthdays. Silas asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Silas says, “I wrote a manipulative slogan about survival and took it out before the presentation.”

**People in this story.** [Noel Aubin](#ah-r0432) (housecoat maker). They measure a garment for a whole Sunday, including sitting at the head of the table.

**Personality.** Oakley loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Oakley remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Oakley has a gray streak running back from the forehead, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Oakley starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor asks where the housecoat was bought. Oakley gives the maker the chance to be credited without revealing what the garment cost.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Oakley offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- When invited to tell the story later, Oakley includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Noel Aubin

AH-R0432 · Age 49 · Seated-fashion tailor · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sewing a coat around a seated client's favorite chair.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Noel by a small Sunday habit. Noel knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I measured seated and standing because dignity shouldn't disappear when she leans forward.”

**What Noel wants.** To make the wearer comfortable seated and standing.

**Family.** [Rosa Marr](../01/04-the-wrong-bicycle.md#ah-r0039) is Noel’s cousin raised in the same household. Noel asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Rosa remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The wrong bicycle*, Rosa says, “I found the missing bell screw in my dustpan.”

**People in this story.** [Oakley Han](#ah-r0431) (housecoat client). They measure a garment for a whole Sunday, including sitting at the head of the table.

**Personality.** Noel has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Noel has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. In conversation Noel looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The diner wants to fasten the new cuff unaided. Noel leaves the cuff in reach and lets the diner set the pace.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Noel asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- When somebody trades on a title, Noel asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Hugo Paul

AH-R0433 · Age 53 · Widowed textile worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my widowed aunt refusing to throw away neckties.” On Wednesday, near the bank steps, Hugo takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “My partner left me three silk ties. They're becoming the pockets she specifically asked for.”

**What Hugo wants.** To turn the late partner's ties into real pockets.

**Family.** [Sam Collins](../02/09-a-wall-of-birthdays.md#ah-r0237) is Hugo’s cousin’s child. Hugo knows which ones can be shared. Sam keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A wall of birthdays*, Sam says, “I put the marks on transparent paper so families who rent can take their years with them.”

**People in this story.** [Rowan Reyes](#ah-r0440). A visitor straight from work notices the ties inside the pockets.

**Personality.** Hugo finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Hugo has a loose dark braid, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a gray fisher's sweater. It usually goes with one cuff folded back twice. When somebody else has the floor, Hugo gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor asks where the housecoat was bought. Hugo gives the maker the chance to be credited without revealing what the garment cost.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Hugo asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- When thanked for a shared success, Hugo points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Max Clay

AH-R0434 · Age 41 · Fabric buyer · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first expensive silk ruined by cheap soap.” On Wednesday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Max prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I bought washable cloth and lied about the price. She made me bring back the receipt.”

**What Max wants.** To agree the actual cloth price with the client.

**Family.** [Grant Tran](../10/13-one-last-dress-rehearsal.md#ah-r1479) is Max’s half-sibling. Max chooses an ordinary story to tell. Grant asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *One last dress rehearsal*, Grant says, “I usually photograph every performance. I put the camera away because the mother wanted her face to be private.”

**People in this story.** [Alice Brooks](#ah-r0435). They choose a color the daytime receipt and salon lamp can both explain.

**Personality.** Max keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Max wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Max is easy to recognize by a long plait tucked inside the collar and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Max looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The diner wants to fasten the new cuff unaided. Max leaves the cuff in reach and lets the diner set the pace.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Max offers to cover one concrete expense and leaves the friend room to say the expense isn't the real problem.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Max offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Alice Brooks

AH-R0435 · Age 26 · Nail artist · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember filing a chipped nail in a bus shelter.” People around Lighthouse Cove know Alice by a small Saturday habit. Alice takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I painted her nails to match before the cloth came. Now we have to laugh about two different reds.”

**What Alice wants.** To find a nail shade that works with the different red.

**Family.** [Javier Rahman](../06/08-summer-staff.md#ah-r0823) is Alice’s first cousin once removed. Alice checks the date twice. Javier brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *Summer staff*, Javier says, “I sold my van to afford the cart permit. I'm asking the council whether the permit can follow me inland.”

**People in this story.** [Max Clay](#ah-r0434). They choose a color the daytime receipt and salon lamp can both explain.

**Personality.** Alice always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Alice is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Alice has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a mustard canvas overshirt. It usually goes with one cuff folded back twice. When somebody else has the floor, Alice holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor asks where the housecoat was bought. Alice gives the maker the chance to be credited without revealing what the garment cost.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Alice moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Alice suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Felix Rubio

AH-R0436 · Age 38 · Home cook · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember getting invited into the dining room after washing up.” Thursday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Felix looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I cook her Sunday lunch. She's invited me to eat my own food with the guests this time.”

**What Felix wants.** To eat a whole meal with the client this Sunday.

**Family.** [Scout Marin](../06/15-winter-afloat.md#ah-r0892) is Felix’s older cousin. Felix brings a spare shopping bag. Scout chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *Winter afloat*, Scout says, “I can offer a sofa but my son needs a place to come back to when he's sober.”

**People in this story.** [Dennis Perry](#ah-r0439). The home cook gets to sit beside the person who ironed the collar.

**Personality.** Felix has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Felix knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Felix has an aquiline nose and a square chin. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Felix takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The diner wants to fasten the new cuff unaided. Felix leaves the cuff in reach and lets the diner set the pace.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Felix tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Felix asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Judith Hong

AH-R0437 · Age 44 · Adaptive clothing fitter · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember making trouser seams disappear beneath a brace.” On Wednesday, near Ferris Deli, Judith asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “My first outfit after surgery tied in back. I'm designing fastenings she can reach by herself.”

**What Judith wants.** To install a fastening the wearer can reach.

**Family.** [Jules Sun](../02/04-the-second-dog.md#ah-r0187) is Judith’s second cousin. Judith is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Jules calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The second dog*, Jules says, “The first owner got angry at me for smiling.”

**People in this story.** [Kate Parra](#ah-r0438). They compare the fastenings needed by two different seated wearers.

**Personality.** Judith enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Judith is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Judith leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor asks where the housecoat was bought. Judith gives the maker the chance to be credited without revealing what the garment cost.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Judith gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Judith puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Kate Parra

AH-R0438 · Age 35 · Shop clerk · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember carrying a pattern in my father's hat.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Kate usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I borrowed the pattern for my dad. He wants pockets large enough for Sunday's newspaper.”

**What Kate wants.** To make a newspaper pocket for the father's coat.

**Family.** [Hollis Fry](../01/04-the-wrong-bicycle.md#ah-r0032) is Kate’s half-sibling. Kate sets the time to leave. Hollis picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The wrong bicycle*, Hollis says, “The taped saddle tips to the left because my hip does.”

**People in this story.** [Judith Hong](#ah-r0437). They compare the fastenings needed by two different seated wearers.

**Personality.** Kate has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Kate can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Kate is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Usually dressed in a brown corduroy jacket with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Kate folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The diner wants to fasten the new cuff unaided. Kate leaves the cuff in reach and lets the diner set the pace.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Kate asks the person already using it whether they can comfortably stand, rather than presuming who needs it most.
- If a friend borrows money, Kate agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Dennis Perry

AH-R0439 · Age 51 · Home seamster · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember our kitchen iron warming the bed in winter.” Thursday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Dennis likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I iron on a child's old desk. The collar turned out square because the desk was, and she liked it.”

**What Dennis wants.** To keep the square collar as the client's own choice.

**Family.** [Alexei Gibbs](../05/03-the-trust-with-a-typo.md#ah-r0623) is Dennis’s cousin. At home the birthday guest used to get first choice of song. One quiet relative chose silence and got ten wonderful minutes in which everybody could hear the sea. Dennis knows that someone quiet can still have a strong preference. Asked about *The trust with a typo*, Alexei says, “I made the typo from a penciled instruction the founder wrote herself.”

**People in this story.** [Felix Rubio](#ah-r0436). The home cook gets to sit beside the person who ironed the collar.

**Personality.** Dennis takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Dennis can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Dennis is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Dennis turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor asks where the housecoat was bought. Dennis gives the maker the chance to be credited without revealing what the garment cost.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Dennis stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Dennis turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

**Appearance.** Rowan has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a boxy denim jacket with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. In conversation Rowan folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- The diner wants to fasten the new cuff unaided. Rowan leaves the cuff in reach and lets the diner set the pace.
