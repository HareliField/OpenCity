## Jorge Dias

AH-R0361 · Age 55 · Retired scaffolder · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being laughed at for reading with my finger.” Saturday often includes a stop at Harbour Bakery, where Jorge buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I borrowed it after failing a reading test. This is the first time I've finished the last page.”

**What Jorge wants.** To finish the library reading in front of the original lender.

**Family.** [Audrey Kahn](../05/08-the-misplaced-umbrella.md#ah-r0674) is Jorge’s first cousin once removed. Jorge makes room at the table. Audrey brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The misplaced umbrella*, Audrey says, “I rented the parasol by the hour. Its whole day's income is now sitting over a street chess game.”

**People in this story.** [Byron Carbone](#ah-r0363). They turn the unpaid fine into a reading the former pupil actually wants to finish.

**Personality.** Jorge enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Jorge is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Jorge rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The former pupil offers to pay forty years of fines. Jorge asks the library what it actually needs from the returning reader.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Jorge accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Jorge puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Emilia Dean

AH-R0362 · Age 50 · Pharmacy clerk · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the careful slant of my mum's newly married signature.” On Thursday, near Paper & Pine Books, Emilia starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “My mother signed the slip. It's the earliest sample I've seen of the married name she later gave up.”

**What Emilia wants.** To keep a copy of the mother's old signature.

**Family.** [Billie Hunt](../07/06-the-empty-chair-on-nights.md#ah-r0952) is Emilia’s cousin. Emilia saves a place. Billie chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The empty chair on nights*, Billie says, “I know why she left. It's her marriage and her choice to tell the ward if she wants.”

**People in this story.** [Martin Arrieta](#ah-r0364) (school conservator). The repair paste helps date a signature in a newly adopted surname.

**Personality.** Emilia has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Emilia can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Emilia has a loose dark braid, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, Emilia draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- The school asks to display the borrower's name. Emilia asks the former pupil to choose how their name appears.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Emilia asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- If a friend borrows money, Emilia agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Byron Carbone

AH-R0363 · Age 63 · Retired supermarket buyer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember paying ten pennies a month into a tea tin.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Byron likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I kept the fine in coins for years. The library wants a story hour instead.”

**What Byron wants.** To give the library a useful story hour.

**Family.** [Mateo Park](../05/14-the-split-visit.md#ah-r0735) is Byron’s cousin. Byron picks the meeting place. Mateo sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The split visit*, Mateo says, “I baked one cake to thank two people. They finally got to cut it while we were all in the same room.”

**People in this story.** [Jorge Dias](#ah-r0361). They turn the unpaid fine into a reading the former pupil actually wants to finish.

**Personality.** Byron takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Byron can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Byron has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for iron-gray waves. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a boxy denim jacket with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. In conversation Byron looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- The former pupil offers to pay forty years of fines. Byron asks the library what it actually needs from the returning reader.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Byron asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Byron turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Martin Arrieta

AH-R0364 · Age 58 · School conservator · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember book paste that smelled exactly like supper.” On Thursday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Martin usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I can tell which classroom it came from by the paste we used in winter.”

**What Martin wants.** To identify the classroom from its repaired binding.

**Family.** [Ronan Vidal](../06/08-summer-staff.md#ah-r0828) is Martin’s stepsibling. A relative came back from a stay in hospital to a house rearranged for convenience and walked straight into the new chair. The family learned to ask before making helpful changes. Martin gets the person affected to choose where things go. Asked about *Summer staff*, Ronan says, “I sew flag repairs by the piece. All the flags came in at once and I won't have enough for autumn.”

**People in this story.** [Emilia Dean](#ah-r0362) (manuscript donor). The repair paste helps date a signature in a newly adopted surname.

**Personality.** Martin admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Martin learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Martin is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a washed violet jersey with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Martin looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The school asks to display the borrower's name. Martin asks the former pupil to choose how their name appears.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Martin asks the driver to come back on foot and look at the damaged goods before discussing cost.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Martin describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Val Evans

AH-R0365 · Age 57 · Retired theater technician · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember wearing a cloak held shut with washing pegs.” On Monday, near the blocks below Foundry Lofts, Val checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I played the villain in the school production. I want a rematch in front of my own grandchildren.”

**What Val wants.** To perform the villain again for the grandchildren.

**Family.** [Jody Gomez](../06/14-care-with-a-clock.md#ah-r0889) is Val’s stepsibling. Val makes the call. Jody is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Care with a clock*, Jody says, “I care for my uncle free. I'm learning what calling my labor free has cost my own pension.”

**People in this story.** [Orla Jain](#ah-r0368). Sorting the box unearths the old school production remembered by its villain.

**Personality.** Val has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Val has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for short copper curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a moss-green cardigan with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Val draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- The former pupil offers to pay forty years of fines. Val asks the library what it actually needs from the returning reader.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Val keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- If accused of favoritism, Val explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Damian Neal

AH-R0366 · Age 43 · Counseling receptionist · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the old classroom violin missing a string.” On Tuesday, near Tideline Market, Damian picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I wrote my crush's name under the flap. They're coming to the reading with their spouse.”

**What Damian wants.** To meet the old crush without embarrassing anyone.

**Family.** [Ada Flynn](../07/04-a-civilian-apology.md#ah-r0932) is Damian’s half-sibling. Damian leaves the first voice message. Ada calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *A civilian apology*, Ada says, “I read the wrong name to my son for years. I want him beside me when the right one goes up.”

**People in this story.** [Alma Pike](#ah-r0369). A child's declaration of love gives the teacher a difficult margin to read aloud.

**Personality.** Damian reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Damian trusts most.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a dark green knitted vest and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Damian has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Damian turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The school asks to display the borrower's name. Damian asks the former pupil to choose how their name appears.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Damian takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- If told to back off, Damian does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Liam Roberts

AH-R0367 · Age 59 · Letterpress printer · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember climbing onto a desk to reach a word.” On Tuesday, near the bank steps, Liam takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I translated the difficult words for my desk mate. He left them all in the margins for me.”

**What Liam wants.** To let the desk mate read the old translations.

**Family.** [Basil Levi](../06/05-the-first-straight-paycheck.md#ah-r0793) is Liam’s stepsibling. Liam chooses the familiar route. Basil can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The first straight paycheck*, Basil says, “I taught him to lift boxes. When someone recognized him on the floor he had to decide who told the foreman first.”

**People in this story.** [Devin Rhodes](#ah-r0370). They debate the ending with the knowledge of the missing words.

**Personality.** Liam has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Liam's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Liam is easy to recognize by a long silver braid and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with one cuff folded back twice, Liam listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The former pupil offers to pay forty years of fines. Liam asks the library what it actually needs from the returning reader.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Liam asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- If the joke lands badly, Liam seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Orla Jain

AH-R0368 · Age 32 · Estate sorter · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember fishing one wet page out of the paper bin.” On Friday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Orla can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I bought the whole box for the cookbook on top. I'd like the first reader to choose where the others go.”

**What Orla wants.** To give the reader a choice of destination for the boxes.

**Family.** [Keiko Cross](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1310) is Orla’s older cousin. Orla brings the paper and a pen. Keiko decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Keiko says, “I bought a plain postcard of the hand. I want the client to choose what to write on the back.”

**People in this story.** [Val Evans](#ah-r0365). Sorting the box unearths the old school production remembered by its villain.

**Personality.** Orla listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Orla has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a gray fisher's sweater. It usually goes with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. When somebody else has the floor, Orla looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- The school asks to display the borrower's name. Orla asks the former pupil to choose how their name appears.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Orla stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- When a second witness disagrees, Orla keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Alma Pike

AH-R0369 · Age 53 · Classroom teacher · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a pupil reading aloud to an empty chair.” People around the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio know Alma by a small Sunday habit. Alma notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “The head said I wasn't ready for this book. I've read it to my pupils thirty-seven times.”

**What Alma wants.** To read the difficult chapter to the former teacher.

**Family.** [Hollis Wick](../06/01-the-upstairs-lease.md#ah-r0755) is Alma’s half-sibling. Alma calls about good news first. Hollis is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The upstairs lease*, Hollis says, “I want to swap homes with the upstairs family because my mother can't climb my stairs anymore.”

**People in this story.** [Damian Neal](#ah-r0366). A child's declaration of love gives the teacher a difficult margin to read aloud.

**Personality.** Alma talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Alma's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Alma has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Look for short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. In conversation Alma holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The former pupil offers to pay forty years of fines. Alma asks the library what it actually needs from the returning reader.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Alma calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Alma says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Devin Rhodes

AH-R0370 · Age 55 · Bookshop event host · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember arguing with an ending until my bus went by.” On Wednesday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Devin looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I hated that ending at ten. At fifty I still hate it and finally have a room in which to say why.”

**What Devin wants.** To defend a different ending in the adult reading group.

**Family.** [Nell Rios](../06/14-care-with-a-clock.md#ah-r0887) is Devin’s half-sibling. Devin remembers the birthdays. Nell asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *Care with a clock*, Nell says, “I keep our local accounts. Four households together can afford a floating quarter hour if everybody agrees.”

**People in this story.** [Liam Roberts](#ah-r0367). They debate the ending with the knowledge of the missing words.

**Personality.** Devin prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. Devin has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, with close-cropped dark curls. Devin draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The school asks to display the borrower's name. Devin asks the former pupil to choose how their name appears.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Devin stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Devin clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.
