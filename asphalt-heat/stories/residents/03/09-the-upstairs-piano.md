# The upstairs piano

[Small things that travel](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Midtown.** A secondhand piano will fit through the front door of a Midtown walk-up and nowhere beyond it.

## Lena Osborne

AH-R0381 · Age 47 · Parent and security guard · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the house where scales replaced the supper bell.” On Saturday, near the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, Lena can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I bought it so my son would come home to practice. I didn't measure the stairs or ask him.”

**What Lena wants.** To invite the son to choose whether to come back and play.

**Family.** [Keir Pierce](../07/01-the-uniform-money.md#ah-r0904) is Lena’s half-sibling. Lena brings a spare shopping bag. Keir chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The uniform money*, Keir says, “I caused the leak moving a hose to mop the floor.”

**People in this story.** [Alexei Taylor](#ah-r0387) (ground-floor host). The owner and the ground-floor tenant have to agree on lessons as well as rent.

**Personality.** Lena can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Lena can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Lena has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a gray fisher's sweater with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Lena leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands that the piano be taken off the landing. Lena asks how much clear passage is needed today before arranging another move.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Lena asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Lena suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Penelope Hong

AH-R0382 · Age 38 · Professional pianist · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember carrying a cello in a very small postal van.” On Tuesday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Penelope notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I trained as a mover before I played professionally. The owner wants me to choose which skill matters.”

**What Penelope wants.** To move the instrument without sacrificing the painted banister.

**Family.** [Cameron Tsai](../10/07-an-empty-dedication.md#ah-r1420) is Penelope’s cousin raised in the same household. Penelope asks before bringing somebody along. Cameron can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *An empty dedication*, Cameron says, “I own Paper & Pine. We are selling a few copies with the dedication still blank for readers to give away.”

**People in this story.** [Remy Morris](#ah-r0386). They measure a carved obstacle before moving the piano again.

**Personality.** Penelope carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Penelope has thick, tightly coiled hair, two dimples that appear only with a full smile, and a favorite piece of clothing, a charcoal zip cardigan. It usually goes with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. When somebody else has the floor, Penelope rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The owner asks a child to play first. Penelope finds a steady seat for the child and waits for the opening notes.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Penelope brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Penelope offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Amanda Hart

AH-R0383 · Age 41 · Upstairs tenant and cleaner · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember leaning over a stair rail to hear a concert.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Amanda can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I live above the landing. I offered my breakfast hour for a concert while we figure it out.”

**What Amanda wants.** To give the stranded piano a breakfast audience.

**Family.** [Rafe Costa](../01/15-the-empty-berth.md#ah-r0147) is Amanda’s half-sibling. Amanda asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Rafe chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The empty berth*, Rafe says, “My father tied up here before there was a gate.”

**People in this story.** [Kit Porter](#ah-r0388). A breakfast listener gives the child an unhurried first audience.

**Personality.** Amanda has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Amanda is easy to recognize by a loose dark braid and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in an olive canvas jacket with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole, Amanda rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands that the piano be taken off the landing. Amanda asks how much clear passage is needed today before arranging another move.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Amanda offers to walk to the next staffed place together, at the stranger's pace, to ask about another ride.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Amanda says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Ivy Knight

AH-R0384 · Age 50 · Record shop clerk · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember hearing my first record with one ear covered.” Thursday often includes a stop at Paper & Pine Books, where Ivy starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I'm deaf in one ear. The landing is the only place in this building where I hear the bass clearly.”

**What Ivy wants.** To reserve a listening spot for the working bass register.

**Family.** [Nora Nolasco](../04/04-station-steps-in-rain.md#ah-r0483) is Ivy’s second cousin. Ivy saves a place. Nora chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *Station steps in rain*, Nora says, “I brought a petition and the names ran together.”

**People in this story.** [John Collins](#ah-r0390). A street recording lets the neighbor compare how the bass sounds outside.

**Personality.** Ivy treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Ivy lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a red windbreaker and a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. Ivy has a long face and high cheekbones, with short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat. Ivy presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The owner asks a child to play first. Ivy finds a steady seat for the child and waits for the opening notes.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Ivy draws the route by its turns and entrances and has the person show which turn comes first.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Ivy says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Terry Acosta

AH-R0385 · Age 45 · Piano teacher · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a cabinet with its maker's name sanded off.” On Friday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Terry watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “My father scratched the piano name off after the creditors came. I recognized his chisel work.”

**What Terry wants.** To identify the removed makers' plate.

**Family.** [Marc Ward](../04/05-the-dead-man-s-tab.md#ah-r0492) is Terry’s half-sibling. Terry picks the meeting place. Marc sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The dead man's tab*, Marc says, “I shared his flat after prison. The tab includes meals for men whose first paycheck hadn't come.”

**People in this story.** [Silas Watts](#ah-r0389). They weigh the damaged maker's name against the cost of a new door.

**Personality.** Terry is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Terry is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Terry has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Terry turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands that the piano be taken off the landing. Terry asks how much clear passage is needed today before arranging another move.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Terry keeps the neighbor's place and finds out whether the stranger needs a different kind of help.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Terry agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Remy Morris

AH-R0386 · Age 37 · Cabinet fitter · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember measuring a doorway twice and a fresco once.” Monday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Remy stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I calculated the turn perfectly and forgot the banister finial my grandmother carved.”

**What Remy wants.** To measure the carved finial with the family present.

**Family.** [Sylvia Kang](../10/12-three-minutes-at-the-bridge.md#ah-r1469) is Remy’s cousin raised in the same household. Remy keeps the photographs. Sylvia knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *Three minutes at the bridge*, Sylvia says, “I manage payroll and discovered our time clock rounds those exact three minutes away in one department.”

**People in this story.** [Penelope Hong](#ah-r0382). They measure a carved obstacle before moving the piano again.

**Personality.** Remy tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Remy will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Remy has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a brown corduroy jacket with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Remy draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- The owner asks a child to play first. Remy finds a steady seat for the child and waits for the opening notes.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Remy asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Remy repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Alexei Taylor

AH-R0387 · Age 21 · Student and shop cashier · Home — the ground floor of a Midtown walk-up

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my landlady teaching me the loud pedal.” People around the market by Market Flats know Alexei by a small Sunday habit. Alexei often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I rent the ground floor. I asked for lessons in exchange for living with somebody else's piano.”

**What Alexei wants.** To exchange room in the flat for lessons.

**Family.** [Anthony Bell](../04/08-the-rescue-hut-mural.md#ah-r0524) is Alexei’s older cousin. Alexei makes the call. Anthony is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The rescue hut mural*, Anthony says, “I sell postcards from the wrong angle and people prefer them.”

**People in this story.** [Lena Osborne](#ah-r0381) (piano owner). The owner and the ground-floor tenant have to agree on lessons as well as rent.

**Personality.** Alexei is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Alexei asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Alexei has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. In conversation Alexei steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands that the piano be taken off the landing. Alexei asks how much clear passage is needed today before arranging another move.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Alexei offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Alexei accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Kit Porter

AH-R0388 · Age 11 · Primary school pupil · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember drumming on every tall step before school.” People around the downtown fountain know Kit by a small Sunday habit. Kit keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I played scales while the adults argued. By the time they stopped, I could play with both hands.”

**What Kit wants.** To learn the landing tune with both hands.

**Family.** [Rowan Armas](../05/08-the-misplaced-umbrella.md#ah-r0677) is Kit’s stepsibling. A lost family paycheck once turned up in the lining of a coat. Their relatives still describe that week by the three people who quietly extended credit. Kit would rather forgive a debt than make a friend cross the street. Asked about *The misplaced umbrella*, Rowan says, “I wanted an elegant beach photograph. The patch in the background is the one thing my mother recognized.”

**People in this story.** [Amanda Hart](#ah-r0383). A breakfast listener gives the child an unhurried first audience.

**Personality.** Kit is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Kit is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a charcoal zip cardigan with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip, Kit draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The owner asks a child to play first. Kit finds a steady seat for the child and waits for the opening notes.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Kit suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Kit thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Silas Watts

AH-R0389 · Age 57 · Landlord and plumber · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember fitting a window for a neighbor's parrot.” On Saturday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Silas usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I wanted to cut a new door. The downstairs tenant offered the piano a room of its own.”

**What Silas wants.** To agree a ground-floor home for the piano.

**Family.** [Selena Lin](../10/10-the-kitchen-without-a-boss.md#ah-r1445) is Silas’s cousin raised in the same household. Silas chooses the familiar route. Selena can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The kitchen without a boss*, Selena says, “I am the ill owner. I rang to find out what burned and for once three people refused to tell me.”

**People in this story.** [Terry Acosta](#ah-r0385). They weigh the damaged maker's name against the cost of a new door.

**Personality.** Silas loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Silas remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Silas has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with short copper curls. Silas turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor demands that the piano be taken off the landing. Silas asks how much clear passage is needed today before arranging another move.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Silas repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- When invited to tell the story later, Silas includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## John Collins

AH-R0390 · Age 29 · Field recordist · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember hearing someone whistle my first composition.” On Monday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, John prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I heard the landing concert from the street. I've given the household the recording of its reluctant orchestra.”

**What John wants.** To deliver the impromptu concert to the upstairs family.

**Family.** [Teal Ruiz](../10/14-the-last-home-visit.md#ah-r1485) is John’s first cousin once removed. John brings the paper and a pen. Teal decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The last home visit*, Teal says, “I always asked for the window shut because my partner felt the cold.”

**People in this story.** [Ivy Knight](#ah-r0384). A street recording lets the neighbor compare how the bass sounds outside.

**Personality.** John has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** John has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation John leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- The owner asks a child to play first. John finds a steady seat for the child and waits for the opening notes.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. John asks the cashier to hold it and lets a claimant identify the writing and amount privately.
- When somebody trades on a title, John asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.
