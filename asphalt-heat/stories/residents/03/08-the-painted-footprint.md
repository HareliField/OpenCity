# The painted footprint

[Small things that travel](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Pier Beach.** Somebody traces a single bare footprint on the Pier Beach boardwalk and dates it to a summer twenty years past.

## Ivy Scott

AH-R0371 · Age 67 · Retired sprint coach · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the high arch I once tried to flatten.” People around the brick passage off the shop row know Ivy by a small Thursday habit. Ivy takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I came to see whether it was my foot before my toes began to curl.”

**What Ivy wants.** To compare the old footprint to the changed adult foot.

**Family.** [Devin Bruno](../05/07-a-shelf-of-apologies.md#ah-r0669) is Ivy’s younger relative by marriage. Ivy asks before bringing somebody along. Devin can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A shelf of apologies*, Devin says, “I don't know who loaned it to me. I remember their dog's name and hope somebody recognizes it.”

**People in this story.** [Eli Mason](#ah-r0372). They check whether the print came from the foot or the painter's brush.

**Personality.** Ivy gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Ivy privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Ivy has a gray streak running back from the forehead, a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a black overshirt with pearl buttons. It usually goes with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. When somebody else has the floor, Ivy leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor insists the footprint must belong to a famous runner. Ivy checks for a family photograph before accepting the famous name.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Ivy helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Ivy offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

## Eli Mason

AH-R0372 · Age 53 · Boardwalk painter · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember painting carnival numbers on bare shoulders.” People around the downtown fountain know Eli by a small Sunday habit. Eli keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I drew it with house paint because the lifeguard had used up all our chalk.”

**What Eli wants.** To preserve the paint recipe and its first date.

**Family.** [Eva Moss](../08/04-first-shift-back.md#ah-r1083) is Eli’s half-sibling. Eli makes room at the table. Eva brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *First shift back*, Eva says, “I visited the family off duty. I am learning which parts of that visit never belong in the break room.”

**People in this story.** [Ivy Scott](#ah-r0371). They check whether the print came from the foot or the painter's brush.

**Personality.** Eli uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Eli well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a gray fisher's sweater and one cuff folded back twice. Eli has a straight nose and deep-set eyes, with short copper curls. Eli starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants to trace a different sort of foot. Eli gives the child paper and room for a footprint that goes home with them.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Eli asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- After a family quarrel, Eli brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Hannah Hewitt

AH-R0373 · Age 47 · Former summer attendant · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an orange sandal caught against the rocks.” People around Juniper Street know Hannah by a small Wednesday habit. Hannah knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I remember the girl standing on one foot. She was waiting for her other sandal to wash back.”

**What Hannah wants.** To trace the missing sandal's owner.

**Family.** [Terry Perry](../09/09-the-returned-daughter.md#ah-r1289) is Hannah’s stepsibling. Hannah saves a place. Terry chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The returned daughter*, Terry says, “I am her partner. We picked one word she can say when she's ready to get back in the car.”

**People in this story.** [Charlotte Bruno](#ah-r0374). They tell a child why a bare foot stayed still on that particular day.

**Personality.** Hannah feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Hannah has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, an olive canvas jacket. It usually goes with a little coil of soft measuring tape. When somebody else has the floor, Hannah looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor insists the footprint must belong to a famous runner. Hannah checks for a family photograph before accepting the famous name.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Hannah brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Hannah lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Charlotte Bruno

AH-R0374 · Age 38 · Primary teacher · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember learning what frightened my son by what he drew.” On Wednesday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Charlotte prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “My son thought the print marked a buried person. I fetched the painter so he could ask.”

**What Charlotte wants.** To introduce the frightened child to the painter.

**Family.** [Scout Saito](../07/10-an-old-restaurant-debt.md#ah-r0998) is Charlotte’s cousin raised in the same household. Charlotte picks the meeting place. Scout sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *An old restaurant debt*, Scout says, “I heard my father left us debt. Seeing one debt turn into a family dinner has softened that memory.”

**People in this story.** [Hannah Hewitt](#ah-r0373). They tell a child why a bare foot stayed still on that particular day. Also in the story is [Darcy Baran](#ah-r0378) (volunteer survey interviewer).

**Personality.** Charlotte treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Charlotte has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a boxy denim jacket. It usually goes with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. When somebody else has the floor, Charlotte gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants to trace a different sort of foot. Charlotte gives the child paper and room for a footprint that goes home with them.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Charlotte finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Charlotte changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Emery Holland

AH-R0375 · Age 55 · Boardwalk carpenter · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember repairing the boardwalk under the runners' feet.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Emery by a small Tuesday habit. Emery knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I covered it during repairs and felt awful. There's a tracing under my toolbox lid.”

**What Emery wants.** To put the saved tracing in the beach records.

**Family.** [Greta Ray](../04/13-a-play-in-a-pocket.md#ah-r0579) is Emery’s younger cousin. Emery keeps the photographs. Greta knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A play in a pocket*, Greta says, “I was filming until I understood the invitation was for one person.”

**People in this story.** [Ellen Scott](#ah-r0376). A saved tracing gives the runner somewhere to look for the buried starting mark.

**Personality.** Emery is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Emery is concerned.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a plum-colored cardigan and a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. Emery has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling, with a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. Emery looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor insists the footprint must belong to a famous runner. Emery checks for a family photograph before accepting the famous name.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Emery takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- When someone invents an answer, Emery asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Ellen Scott

AH-R0376 · Age 69 · Retired athletics judge · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an old football shoe that touched only three toes.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Ellen by a small Friday habit. Ellen checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I ran barefoot races here. I want to know whether the old starting mark lies under that plank.”

**What Ellen wants.** To locate the old racing start line.

**Family.** [Yvette Weber](../10/14-the-last-home-visit.md#ah-r1482) is Ellen’s cousin. Ellen makes the call. Yvette is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The last home visit*, Yvette says, “I want the new worker to knock twice. A single knock still sounds like a debt collector at my old front door.”

**People in this story.** [Emery Holland](#ah-r0375). A saved tracing gives the runner somewhere to look for the buried starting mark.

**Personality.** Ellen loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Ellen has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for closely cropped salt-and-pepper hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Ellen folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants to trace a different sort of foot. Ellen gives the child paper and room for a footprint that goes home with them.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Ellen stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Ellen offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Logan Dinh

AH-R0377 · Age 39 · Podiatry technician · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember wet plaster setting around a gull's wing.” Tuesday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Logan stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I brought the plaster cast my father took. It belongs to a gull hunter with enormous feet.”

**What Logan wants.** To match the plaster cast to a real former swimmer.

**Family.** [Zachary Abbott](../08/13-the-understudy-sings.md#ah-r1171) is Logan’s second cousin. Logan leaves the first voice message. Zachary calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The understudy sings*, Zachary says, “I want the part and hate the thought that I might be happy about someone else's bad news.”

**People in this story.** [Darcy Baran](#ah-r0378). A real plaster cast gives the class a comparison that can remain anonymous.

**Personality.** Logan enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Logan is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Logan has a high puff tied with plain cord, a straight nose and deep-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Logan turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor insists the footprint must belong to a famous runner. Logan checks for a family photograph before accepting the famous name.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Logan says what they know in their own name and refuses to treat a guess as something the child has to disprove.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Logan keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Darcy Baran

AH-R0378 · Age 33 · Statistics tutor · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a teacher letting us count surfers for credit.” People around the approach to Seabright Court know Darcy by a small Friday habit. Darcy likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I'm measuring everyone's guesses for a statistics class. The owner can remain unnamed.”

**What Darcy wants.** To let the owner opt out of the class survey.

**Family.** [Lee Ingram](../07/12-the-crash-anniversary.md#ah-r1011) is Darcy’s stepsibling. Darcy chooses the familiar route. Lee can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The crash anniversary*, Lee says, “I survived it. I need one route to work where nobody recognizes me from the newspaper photograph.”

**People in this story.** [Logan Dinh](#ah-r0377). A real plaster cast gives the class a comparison that can remain anonymous. Also in the story is [Charlotte Bruno](#ah-r0374) (beach visitor).

**Personality.** Darcy is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Darcy is easy to recognize by a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a striped cotton shirt with a small enamel bird on the lapel, Darcy draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants to trace a different sort of foot. Darcy gives the child paper and room for a footprint that goes home with them.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Darcy keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Darcy helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Keiko Ames

AH-R0379 · Age 51 · Wedding celebrant · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember two people throwing rice straight into the wind.” Sunday often includes a stop at Paper & Pine Books, where Keiko starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I saw that foot in my wedding photograph. I'm meeting a stranger to compare summers.”

**What Keiko wants.** To compare the wedding print with a stranger's family album.

**Family.** [Eileen Teo](../06/05-the-first-straight-paycheck.md#ah-r0794) is Keiko’s younger cousin. Keiko brings the paper and a pen. Eileen decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The first straight paycheck*, Eileen says, “I sold alongside him. Hearing how little he makes honestly has not convinced me to follow yet.”

**People in this story.** [Rae Nelson](#ah-r0380). Two new photographs connect a long-ago wedding with a present-day footprint.

**Personality.** Keiko finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Keiko has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a narrow-striped sweater with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. In conversation Keiko raises one eyebrow long before saying yes.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor insists the footprint must belong to a famous runner. Keiko checks for a family photograph before accepting the famous name.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Keiko takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Keiko waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

## Rae Nelson

AH-R0380 · Age 43 · Prosthetics workshop coordinator · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first shoe worn for walking instead of posing.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Rae likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I wear an artificial foot. I want to trace mine beside it on paper and take the pair home.”

**What Rae wants.** To take home a tracing of both feet.

**Family.** [Pax Orr](../01/13-the-borrowed-bass.md#ah-r0124) is Rae’s cousin raised in the same household. Rae chooses the music. Pax puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The borrowed bass*, Pax says, “I recognized the wiring by a burned thumbprint in the solder.”

**People in this story.** [Keiko Ames](#ah-r0379). Two new photographs connect a long-ago wedding with a present-day footprint.

**Personality.** Is a careful borrower and a reckless lender. Things leave this person's house with elaborate instructions and very little expectation of ever coming back.

**Appearance.** Rae is easy to recognize by short black hair swept back and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a striped cotton shirt with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Rae gives a little nod before asking a hard question when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A child wants to trace a different sort of foot. Rae gives the child paper and room for a footprint that goes home with them.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Rae asks for help with a task the neighbor does well, so both people get to contribute something.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Rae listens first to the account of how it was actually used.
