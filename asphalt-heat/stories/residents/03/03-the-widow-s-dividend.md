# The widow's dividend

[Small things that travel](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Bank of the Coast.** A small property company declares a dividend for the first time since one founder died. His widow arrives at the bank carrying the old ledger.

[Taylor Beck](#ah-r0321) · [Rain Salcedo](#ah-r0322) · [Samuel Choi](#ah-r0323) · [Iva Allard](#ah-r0324) · [Daryl Trent](#ah-r0325) · [Frances Brady](#ah-r0326) · [Grey Garza](#ah-r0327) · [Yasmin Arrieta](#ah-r0328) · [Lydia Macrae](#ah-r0329) · [Reuben Sawyer](#ah-r0330)

## Taylor Beck

AH-R0321 · Age 63 · Property company cofounder · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember borrowing a refrigerator to cool the founders' lunch.” On Saturday, near Tideline Market, Taylor picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I want the first dividend in cash to tip the woman who cleaned our first office.”

**What Taylor wants.** To give the first dividend to the original cleaner.

**Family.** [Elaine Alcázar](../07/13-a-dance-for-two-fathers.md#ah-r1025) is Taylor’s younger relative by marriage. Taylor makes the call. Elaine is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A dance for two fathers*, Elaine says, “I want my little girl to see grown men making room without one of them having to disappear.”

**People in this story.** [Samuel Choi](#ah-r0323). They trace the first cleaner's tip back to an unpaid ledger line.

**Personality.** Taylor reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Taylor trusts most.

**Appearance.** Taylor is easy to recognize by a neat white crop and a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Usually dressed in a gray fisher's sweater with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole, Taylor raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A family member wants the ledger locked away. Taylor asks whether the heirs can look at copies without parting with the old ledger.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Taylor takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- If told to back off, Taylor does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Rain Salcedo

AH-R0322 · Age 70 · Retired silent investor · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember an office key stamped with my maiden initials.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Rain by a small Saturday habit. Rain starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I had a silent share. My children still think my wealth came from my second husband.”

**What Rain wants.** To let the children hear about their mother's share.

**Family.** [Nell Marks](../02/07-the-margin-reader.md#ah-r0218) is Rain’s younger cousin. Rain can stop the walk at any corner. Nell chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The margin reader*, Nell says, “I'm translating the swear words first. They tell me where two polite people finally trusted each other.”

**People in this story.** [Reuben Sawyer](#ah-r0330). A silent shareholder discusses what a child would gain from one small voting share. Also in the story is [Samuel Choi](#ah-r0323) (ledger reader).

**Personality.** Rain has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Rain's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a boxy denim jacket and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Rain has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones, with a gray streak running back from the forehead. Rain counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An unexpected dividend arrives in the wrong envelope. Rain checks the payee's name with the teller before opening the envelope.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Rain asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- If the joke lands badly, Rain seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Samuel Choi

AH-R0323 · Age 41 · Shop accountant · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the ledger balanced on an ironing board.” People around Juniper Street know Samuel by a small Thursday habit. Samuel knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I found a pencil debt to my mother between two lines about roofing nails.”

**What Samuel wants.** To settle the penciled debt to the mother.

**Family.** [Bianca Sethi](../02/13-the-quiet-verse.md#ah-r0271) is Samuel’s younger relative by marriage. Samuel asks what the birthday guest would like this year. Bianca remembers the birthdays. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The quiet verse*, Bianca says, “I wrote the verse about my dad and then decided nobody gets to hear it yet.”

**People in this story.** [Taylor Beck](#ah-r0321). They trace the first cleaner's tip back to an unpaid ledger line. Also in the story is [Rain Salcedo](#ah-r0322) (founding shareholder).

**Personality.** Samuel listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Samuel has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Samuel looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- A family member wants the ledger locked away. Samuel asks whether the heirs can look at copies without parting with the old ledger.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Samuel alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- When a second witness disagrees, Samuel keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Iva Allard

AH-R0324 · Age 56 · Former building partner · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember practicing a signature in the back of a cab.” Monday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Iva knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I countersigned every page. The one missing signature is from the day I walked out.”

**What Iva wants.** To date the one unsigned ledger page.

**Family.** [Maria Tanner](../09/07-school-after-sixty.md#ah-r1269) is Iva’s second cousin. Iva brings the paper and a pen. Maria decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *School after sixty*, Maria says, “I am the old class bully. The classmate I frightened has chosen the chair opposite me and asked me to keep quiet.”

**People in this story.** [Daryl Trent](#ah-r0325). The missing signature makes the trustee ask whose stake the ledger didn't show.

**Personality.** Iva talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Iva's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Iva has a wide brow and a narrow jaw, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Iva listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An unexpected dividend arrives in the wrong envelope. Iva checks the payee's name with the teller before opening the envelope.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Iva asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Iva says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Daryl Trent

AH-R0325 · Age 55 · Pension trustee · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first pension notice arriving on a postcard.” People around Tideline Market know Daryl by a small Saturday habit. Daryl picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “My pension owns a sliver of this firm. I want the tenants to know we're tied together.”

**What Daryl wants.** To introduce the tenant association to the pension trustee.

**Family.** [Mateo Alston](../02/15-a-sail-cut-small.md#ah-r0298) is Daryl’s cousin. Daryl chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Mateo brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *A sail cut small*, Mateo says, “I know where my old racing rival patched the sail.”

**People in this story.** [Iva Allard](#ah-r0324). The missing signature makes the trustee ask whose stake the ledger didn't show.

**Personality.** Daryl prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Daryl has a blunt cut just below the ears, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a charcoal zip cardigan. It usually goes with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. When somebody else has the floor, Daryl squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- A family member wants the ledger locked away. Daryl asks whether the heirs can look at copies without parting with the old ledger.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Daryl takes a small plate to the quieter end of the table and saves the seat beside it.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Daryl clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Grey Garza

AH-R0327 · Age 37 · Delivery coordinator · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember confusing our birthday tape with a board meeting.” Saturday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Grey takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I forecast a loss and delayed moving home. One unexpected payment could bring my wife back to the coast.”

**What Grey wants.** To play the founding cassette to the shareholders.

**Family.** [Lane Bose](../08/07-a-prize-nobody-entered.md#ah-r1112) is Grey’s stepsibling. Grey offers the first invitation. Lane gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *A prize nobody entered*, Lane says, “I posted it because the author never took chances.”

**People in this story.** [Frances Brady](#ah-r0326). The seller's old pen helps date the cassette of the founding meeting.

**Personality.** Grey often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Grey have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. Grey has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Grey gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A family member wants the ledger locked away. Grey asks whether the heirs can look at copies without parting with the old ledger.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Grey writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Grey rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

## Reuben Sawyer

AH-R0330 · Age 69 · Retired letting clerk · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the smell of a new envelope containing an old promise.” People around the corner outside Coastal Barber know Reuben by a small Thursday habit. Reuben usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I want one share for my grandson so somebody younger will keep asking how the rent is set.”

**What Reuben wants.** To buy a share the grandson can responsibly vote.

**Family.** [Gus Lark](../01/09-the-porch-light.md#ah-r0086) is Reuben’s cousin. Reuben chooses whether to use it. Gus saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The porch light*, Gus says, “I sold the owner two bulbs every winter. He always wrote my name in a little book beside the price.”

**People in this story.** [Rain Salcedo](#ah-r0322). A silent shareholder discusses what a child would gain from one small voting share.

**Personality.** Reuben uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Reuben well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an old plum windbreaker and a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. Reuben has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with a neat white crop. Reuben turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- An unexpected dividend arrives in the wrong envelope. Reuben checks the payee's name with the teller before opening the envelope.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Reuben asks each person to say what they believe is actually owed before anybody proposes a payment.
- After a family quarrel, Reuben brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.
