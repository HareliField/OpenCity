## Jamie Jiang

AH-R0401 · Age 70 · Retired ship cook · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a barber warming the handle before passing it over.” Saturday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Jamie looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I was the last customer she shaved. I asked the widow why there was still a little soap in the hinge.”

**What Jamie wants.** To ask the widow about the last trace of soap.

**Family.** [Gareth Parker](../07/15-the-day-sailor-s-divorce.md#ah-r1041) is Jamie’s younger cousin. Jamie chooses an ordinary story to tell. Gareth asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The day sailor's divorce*, Gareth says, “I don't want the boat. I want the one time a week my son talks to me without looking down.”

**People in this story.** [Jo March](#ah-r0404). A last shave and a first chance to barber belong to the widow's account of the razor.

**Personality.** Jamie thinks best while walking. Sitting beneath someone's office clock makes Jamie sound more certain than they feel, an effect close friends have learned to distrust.

**Appearance.** Jamie has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for a gray streak running back from the forehead. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Jamie gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A buyer makes a high offer for the old razor. Jamie lets the widow hear the price privately and keep the box in hand.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Jamie gives up their own space if possible and asks the passengers who arrived first to choose what happens next.
- When family demands an immediate answer, Jamie asks them to walk to the corner together and listen until the corner comes.

Asked about *Care with a clock*, Alex says, “I can dress in sixteen minutes. I will not practice being ashamed of the sixteenth.”

**People in this story.** [Patricia Lang](#ah-r0406) (display assistant). They work out where to polish the blade and where to keep the thumbprint bright.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. Albert has an aquiline nose and a square chin, with a center part and straight chestnut hair. Albert presses the lips together to keep a smile in during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

## Jo March

AH-R0404 · Age 68 · Retired barber · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember learning to shave a balloon without breaking it.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Jo by a small Tuesday habit. Jo prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I was forbidden to cut girls' hair here. That woman lent me the razor for my first paying customer.”

**What Jo wants.** To show the widow what her wife's loan made possible.

**Family.** [Jody Clay](../09/14-the-missing-sunday.md#ah-r1334) is Jo’s cousin’s child. Jo asks before bringing somebody along. Jody can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The missing Sunday*, Jody says, “I love his company but I also need Sundays for my own marriage.”

**People in this story.** [Jamie Jiang](#ah-r0401). A last shave and a first chance to barber belong to the widow's account of the razor.

**Personality.** Jo feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Jo has gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. When somebody else has the floor, Jo looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- The widow wants to tell the apprentice who held it first. Jo makes space for the widow and the apprentice to talk without buyers listening.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Jo asks for help with a task the neighbor does well, so both people get to contribute something.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Jo lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

**Under pressure**
