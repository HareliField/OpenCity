## Magdalena Macrae

AH-R0771 · Age 62 · Cold-chain company founder · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first freezer our family bought at auction.” People around the market by Market Flats know Magdalena by a small Friday habit. Magdalena often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I founded it but my ex brought every first customer. I can't teach a valuation spreadsheet their names.”

**What Magdalena wants.** To agree a valuation that recognizes the customer relationships.

**Family.** [Avery Williams](../09/14-the-missing-sunday.md#ah-r1331) is Magdalena’s second cousin. Magdalena brings the paper and a pen. Avery decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The missing Sunday*, Avery says, “I know when my pills are due. I want somebody who will argue about the football on a Sunday.”

**People in this story.** [Terry Lin](#ah-r0778). They put the value of established customers beside the company's formal valuations. Also in the story is [Ariel Jain](#ah-r0774) (adult child).

**Personality.** Magdalena tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Magdalena will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Magdalena has a round face with a pronounced chin, with silver temples and a dark fringe. Magdalena looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The divorcing founders ask staff to pick a side. Magdalena asks when the next wages will arrive before giving either founder a public endorsement.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Magdalena calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Magdalena repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Marlowe Nord

AH-R0772 · Age 48 · Employee shareholder · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a share certificate handed over in a flour envelope.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Marlowe by a small Sunday habit. Marlowe pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I own a small share from the year we couldn't pay wages. This divorce could make me a millionaire or put me out of work.”

**What Marlowe wants.** To protect both the back wages and the future post.

**Family.** [Miguel Tsai](../04/09-the-porch-auction.md#ah-r0540) is Marlowe’s stepsibling. Marlowe is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Miguel calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The porch auction*, Miguel says, “I left without a chair. I got the new address and a Sunday invitation instead.”

**People in this story.** [Rosemary Bhandari](#ah-r0777). The early lender and the employee shareholder remember exactly when wages stopped arriving.

**Personality.** Marlowe is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Marlowe asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Marlowe is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Marlowe looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A driver says payday matters more than the settlement. Marlowe checks what money is ring-fenced for the drivers already on the rota.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Marlowe offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Marlowe accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

**Personality.** Charles is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Charles has iron-gray waves, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a plum-colored cardigan. It usually goes with one cuff folded back twice. When somebody else has the floor, Charles counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- The divorcing founders ask staff to pick a side. Charles asks when the next wages will arrive before giving either founder a public endorsement.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Charles asks where they stood and what they could hear, leaving the rest open instead of filling it with guesses.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Charles thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.
