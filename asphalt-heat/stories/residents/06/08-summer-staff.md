## Scout Hull

AH-R0821 · Age 28 · Seasonal lifeguard · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember getting my first room over a surf shop.” People around Juniper Street know Scout by a small Thursday habit. Scout knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I rented a room through September and my final lifeguard check comes in August.”

**What Scout wants.** To cover the last three weeks of the room rental.

**Family.** [Ruben King](../08/13-the-understudy-sings.md#ah-r1177) is Scout’s cousin. Scout picks the meeting place. Ruben sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The understudy sings*, Ruben says, “I spent my student loan on a ticket to hear the original singer.”

**People in this story.** [Levi Boyd](#ah-r0825) (hotel employer). An early last paycheck has the hotel manager reconsidering where a retention bonus belongs.

**Personality.** Scout treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Scout has a loose dark braid, a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Scout listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor demands a refund after the season ends early. Scout checks the actual dates printed on the booking before discussing its refund.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Scout offers an ordinary errand that lets the distressed person leave without being followed by an audience.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Scout changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Ida Gupta

AH-R0822 · Age 20 · Summer hut attendant · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember taking my little brother out before the beach opened.” On Wednesday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Ida stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I promised my little brother he could work his first shift beside me. I am training him for a day I can't pay him for.”

**What Ida wants.** To train the younger brother for useful future work.

**Family.** [Jules Rice](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1238) is Ida’s older cousin. Ida keeps the photographs. Jules knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Jules says, “I want the young staff to see that a good handover can sound like an ordinary conversation.”

**People in this story.** [Tali Peters](#ah-r0830). The younger workers get a direct explanation about the huts closing before school starts.

**Personality.** Ida is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Ida is concerned.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a narrow-striped sweater and a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. Ida has a round face with a pronounced chin, with a center part and straight chestnut hair. Ida holds eye contact until the other person has finished during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Two workers are offered one extra paid week. Ida asks both workers what commitments they made on the strength of the old end date.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Ida asks what the friend actually said last time and checks whether anybody is expecting them at home.
- When someone invents an answer, Ida asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Javier Rahman

AH-R0823 · Age 49 · Licensed ice cart vendor · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember buying a cart whose canopy came down when it rained.” On Saturday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Javier pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I sold my van to afford the cart permit. I'm asking the council whether the permit can follow me inland.”

**What Javier wants.** To transfer the cart permit to a working site.

**Family.** [Alice Brooks](../03/14-the-housecoat-commission.md#ah-r0435) is Javier’s first cousin once removed. Javier brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Alice checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The housecoat commission*, Alice says, “I painted her nails to match before the cloth came.”

**People in this story.** [Clement Laurent](#ah-r0826). The cart permit and the public showers are the two usable things that may outlast the season.

**Personality.** Javier loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Javier has dark brown curls cut close behind the ears, a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, and a favorite piece of clothing, a pale blue work shirt. It usually goes with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. When somebody else has the floor, Javier draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor demands a refund after the season ends early. Javier checks the actual dates printed on the booking before discussing its refund.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Javier agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Javier offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Jesse Ngo

**What Jesse wants.** To post the real closing date for visitors.

**Family.** [Eden Fisher](../05/15-a-boat-for-sale-twice.md#ah-r0742) is Jesse’s stepsibling. Jesse chooses the music. Eden puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *A boat for sale twice*, Eden says, “I can afford half a boat. I asked another bidder what half a Saturday looks like.”

**People in this story.** [Terry Fong](#ah-r0829). A server putting the date on a board helps a teacher stop selling lessons after that date.

**Personality.** Jesse enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Jesse is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Jesse is easy to recognize by close-cropped dark curls and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Jesse counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two workers are offered one extra paid week. Jesse asks both workers what commitments they made on the strength of the old end date.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Jesse asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Jesse keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Levi Boyd

AH-R0825 · Age 57 · Seafront hotel owner · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a cook who lost a bonus because winter came early.” On Saturday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Levi notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I have money for one retention bonus. I'm asking every worker what a useful final week would look like.”

**What Levi wants.** To spend the final bonus where it buys a useful week.

**Family.** [Benjamin Fox](../04/05-the-dead-man-s-tab.md#ah-r0491) is Levi’s stepsibling. Levi knows which ones can be shared. Benjamin keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The dead man's tab*, Benjamin says, “He conned my dad and paid for my lunch. Both facts belong on the same page.”

**People in this story.** [Scout Hull](#ah-r0821) (seasonal worker). An early last paycheck has the hotel manager reconsidering where a retention bonus belongs.

**Personality.** Levi is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** Levi is easy to recognize by a high puff tied with plain cord and an aquiline nose and a square chin. Usually dressed in a mustard canvas overshirt with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it, Levi looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor demands a refund after the season ends early. Levi checks the actual dates printed on the booking before discussing its refund.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Levi asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Levi helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Clement Laurent

AH-R0826 · Age 46 · Lifelong local and tiler · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember washing my aunt's feet in the public showers.” Sunday often includes a stop at Juniper Street, where Clement knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I am an off-season local. I want the beach showers left open for those of us who still live here.”

**What Clement wants.** To keep the showers available to residents.

**Family.** [Stella Rizzo](../07/09-a-tenant-who-stays.md#ah-r0985) is Clement’s second cousin. Clement brings the paper and a pen. Stella decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *A tenant who stays*, Stella says, “I carried the saplings here for my cousin. I can identify which ones can travel.”

**People in this story.** [Javier Rahman](#ah-r0823). The cart permit and the public showers are the two usable things that may outlast the season.

**Personality.** Clement finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Clement has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for auburn hair cut square at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a brown corduroy jacket with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. In conversation Clement grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- Two workers are offered one extra paid week. Clement asks both workers what commitments they made on the strength of the old end date.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Clement agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Clement waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

Asked about *Level with the window*, Pablo says, “I was angry about the noise until I saw the pensioners on the upper deck counting our flowerpots.”

On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an old plum windbreaker with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Catherine counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

## Ronan Vidal

AH-R0828 · Age 54 · Flag repairer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the last flag I mended by candlelight.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Ronan by a small Tuesday habit. Ronan starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I sew flag repairs by the piece. All the flags came in at once and I won't have enough for autumn.”

**What Ronan wants.** To spread flag repairs far enough to bridge autumn.

**Family.** [Martin Arrieta](../03/07-overdue-by-a-lifetime.md#ah-r0364) is Ronan’s stepsibling. A relative came back from a stay in hospital to a house rearranged for convenience and walked straight into the new chair. The family learned to ask before making helpful changes. Ronan will change a whole arrangement back without arguing. Asked about *Overdue by a lifetime*, Martin says, “I can tell which classroom it came from by the paste we used in winter.”

**People in this story.** [Catherine Easton](#ah-r0827). A traveling summer contract looks different from the viewpoint of the worker who mends its flags.

**Personality.** Ronan is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Ronan loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** Ronan has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for a high puff tied with plain cord. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a charcoal zip cardigan with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Ronan counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- Two workers are offered one extra paid week. Ronan asks both workers what commitments they made on the strength of the old end date.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Ronan walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- If somebody calls for applause, Ronan gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.

They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The return of a witness*, Winifred says, “I am her daughter. **People in this story.** [Jesse Ngo](#ah-r0824). A server putting the date on a board helps a teacher stop selling lessons after that date.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Terry takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Terry has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Terry takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor demands a refund after the season ends early. Terry checks the actual dates printed on the booking before discussing its refund.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Terry gets the people at the next doorway away from the smoke and asks a bystander to make the call.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Terry asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

AH-R0830 · Age 36 · Beach activity coach · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember packing equipment while my child counted the cork floats.” On Saturday, near Harbour Bakery, Tali buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**Appearance.** Tali has fine brows over wide-set eyes. Look for close-cropped dark curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Tali takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Two workers are offered one extra paid week. Tali asks both workers what commitments they made on the strength of the old end date.

- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Tali asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.
