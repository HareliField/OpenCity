## Vera Gallagher

AH-R0811 · Age 54 · Late literacy learner and cook · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a novel finished on my fiftieth birthday.” On Tuesday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Vera stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I learned to read novels at fifty. Numbers with brackets still make me sweat.”

**What Vera wants.** To read a full wage slip without hiding the hard line.

**Family.** [Nelson Gallagher](../04/01-the-button-card.md#ah-r0456) is Vera’s half-sibling. Vera asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Nelson chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The button card*, Nelson says, “I left cufflinks on the card and came back to find someone had paired them with shoelaces.”

**People in this story.** [Anthony Penn](#ah-r0813). A former pupil teaches a former teacher a deduction neither understood in school.

**Personality.** Vera admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Vera learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Vera has thick, tightly coiled hair, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a black overshirt with pearl buttons. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Vera presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- A classmate asks for help reading a real wage slip. Vera sits beside the classmate and covers personal figures that aren't part of the question.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Vera asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Vera describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Olivia Teo

AH-R0812 · Age 44 · Parcel sorter · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the looping signature I borrowed from my spouse.” On Sunday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Olivia likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I sign my wife's name for deliveries. Tonight I want to learn to write mine on a wage dispute.”

**What Olivia wants.** To sign a pay query unaided.

**Family.** [Gabriel Han](../05/08-the-misplaced-umbrella.md#ah-r0680) is Olivia’s half-sibling. Olivia gets to change the plan. Gabriel offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The misplaced umbrella*, Gabriel says, “I promised never to leave things on the beach.”

**People in this story.** [Eli Fong](#ah-r0815). A signed wage query has to make sense in both languages before it travels.

**Personality.** Olivia has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Olivia has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. In conversation Olivia counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- An employer asks who is attending the class. Olivia protects the attendance list and offers general information about the class.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Olivia asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- If accused of favoritism, Olivia explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Anthony Penn

AH-R0813 · Age 68 · Retired school secretary · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a former pupil turning over my question sheet kindly.” Sunday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Anthony pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I was too proud to sit with my former student. He knows every deduction I kept looking past.”

**What Anthony wants.** To take a lesson from the former pupil.

**Family.** [Jacqueline Clarke](../04/15-the-engine-lullaby.md#ah-r0593) is Anthony’s stepsibling. Anthony chooses the music. Jacqueline puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The engine lullaby*, Jacqueline says, “I complained about it last year. Now I need to tell the owner what I missed when it stopped.”

**People in this story.** [Vera Gallagher](#ah-r0811). A former pupil teaches a former teacher a deduction neither understood in school. Also in the story is [Jean Miranda](#ah-r0816) (classmate).

**Personality.** Anthony reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Anthony trusts most.

**Appearance.** Anthony is easy to recognize by tightly curled gray hair and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a striped cotton shirt with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Anthony steadies a cup with both hands while listening when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A classmate asks for help reading a real wage slip. Anthony sits beside the classmate and covers personal figures that aren't part of the question.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Anthony asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- If told to back off, Anthony does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

**Appearance.** Taylor has a long plait tucked inside the collar, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. When somebody else has the floor, Taylor squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- If the joke lands badly, Taylor seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Eli Fong

AH-R0815 · Age 39 · Payroll translator · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember trying to translate a deduction with no ordinary name.” On Tuesday, near the market by Market Flats, Eli often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I translate payroll terms into two languages. Both have a better word for money you never see.”

**What Eli wants.** To translate deductions into ordinary language.

**Family.** [Laura Bhandari](../08/14-the-new-language-at-dinner.md#ah-r1182) is Eli’s second cousin. Eli brings the paper and a pen. Laura decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The new language at dinner*, Laura says, “I am his daughter and never learned it. I want lessons even if he gets better faster than I do.”

**People in this story.** [Olivia Teo](#ah-r0812). A signed wage query has to make sense in both languages before it travels.

**Personality.** Eli listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Eli is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Eli counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A classmate asks for help reading a real wage slip. Eli sits beside the classmate and covers personal figures that aren't part of the question.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Eli helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- When a second witness disagrees, Eli keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Jean Miranda

AH-R0816 · Age 51 · Shoe repairer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sharpening my little sister's pencils after she went to bed.” On Friday, near the corner by Juniper Tailor, Jean looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I sharpen pencils for class because it gives my shaking hands a job.”

**What Jean wants.** To keep the class stocked and take one practice sheet home.

**Family.** [Quinn Fong](../03/11-the-widow-s-razor.md#ah-r0407) is Jean’s parent’s cousin. Jean sets the time to leave. Quinn picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The widow's razor*, Quinn says, “I can't see the maker's mark. My granddaughter read it out and found her own middle name.”

**People in this story.** [Taylor Lucas](#ah-r0814). Tips create an arithmetic problem worth staying to solve beside the sharpened pencils. Also in the story is [Anthony Penn](#ah-r0813) (classmate).

**Personality.** Jean talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Jean's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Jean has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a little coil of soft measuring tape. When somebody else has the floor, Jean looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry.

**Under pressure**

- An employer asks who is attending the class. Jean protects the attendance list and offers general information about the class.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Jean asks the person already using it whether they can comfortably stand, rather than presuming who needs it most.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Jean says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Camille looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**
