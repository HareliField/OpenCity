# Nine unpaid hours

[What a working week costs](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Port of Asphalt.** A Port of Asphalt contractor closes its office on payday. Last week's hours survive on a paper tally in the break room.

## Simon Hunter

AH-R0761 · Age 40 · Contract loader · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a week's overtime written on a damp matchbook.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the entrance to the Wash House, where Simon stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I have nine hours on that sheet and a refrigerator full of food I bought on credit.”

**What Simon wants.** To recover the nine hours before the grocery tab is due.

**Family.** [Melanie Nelson](../05/05-the-clean-ledger.md#ah-r0644) is Simon’s half-sibling. Simon decides what stays off the paper. Melanie brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The clean ledger*, Melanie says, “I am the creditor everyone thought dead. I'm asking for an afternoon of help moving house.”

**People in this story.** [Cassandra Li](#ah-r0762). The permanent worker's payslip corroborates the contractor's nine unpaid hours. Also in the story is [Bruna Morse](#ah-r0764) (payroll contact).

**Personality.** Simon has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Simon has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Simon listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The contractor offers selected workers a partial payment. Simon asks that the offer be written down with the remaining unpaid hours.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Simon tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- When somebody trades on a title, Simon asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Cassandra Li

AH-R0762 · Age 58 · Permanent freight handler · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my temporary badge finally being replaced.” On Saturday, near the blocks below Foundry Lofts, Cassandra checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I was paid because I have a permanent badge. I brought my payslip to show exactly where our shifts overlapped.”

**What Cassandra wants.** To prove where temporary and permanent shifts overlapped.

**Family.** [Pam Ravel](../01/12-the-last-green.md#ah-r0115) is Cassandra’s cousin raised in the same household. Cassandra brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Pam checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The last green*, Pam says, “He trained me and never once sat quietly. On the farewell run I made him give me directions right down to the bakery smell at the turn.”

**People in this story.** [Simon Hunter](#ah-r0761). The permanent worker's payslip corroborates the contractor's nine unpaid hours.

**Personality.** Cassandra finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an olive canvas jacket and one cuff folded back twice. Cassandra has fine brows over wide-set eyes, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Cassandra starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A worker asks to give their account privately. Cassandra arranges a one-to-one account and checks which facts the worker permits to be shared.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Cassandra asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- When thanked for a shared success, Cassandra points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Taylor Vargas

AH-R0763 · Age 36 · Dock rigger · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a long talk held beside a motionless crane.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Taylor checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I worked through a break nobody signed for. Three people remember the conversation we had instead of eating.”

**What Taylor wants.** To get the worked break acknowledged.

**Family.** [Dale Zhou](../03/06-the-hospital-radio.md#ah-r0355) is Taylor’s cousin. Taylor calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Dale leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The hospital radio*, Dale says, “I found a folded list of songs behind the battery cover.”

**People in this story.** [Amanda Wong](#ah-r0770). A missed break can be added to the union tally before the sheet leaves the room.

**Personality.** Taylor keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Taylor wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Taylor has fine brows over wide-set eyes, with short copper curls. Taylor draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The contractor offers selected workers a partial payment. Taylor asks that the offer be written down with the remaining unpaid hours.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Taylor agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Taylor offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Bruna Morse

AH-R0764 · Age 43 · Payroll administrator · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the morning a payroll transfer stopped halfway through.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Bruna by a small Saturday habit. Bruna knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I ran payroll. I warned management on Wednesday and told the crews on Friday. I can't get Thursday back.”

**What Bruna wants.** To tell the crew exactly when management got the warning.

**Family.** [Anton Dove](../01/06-window-seven.md#ah-r0051) is Bruna’s half-sibling. Bruna can always ask for five minutes alone. Anton asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Window seven*, Anton says, “Window seven is the first place I saw the sea after I moved here.”

**People in this story.** [Penelope Jeong](#ah-r0767). The manager's daughter hears which day the payroll worker knew about the shortfall. Also in the story is [Simon Hunter](#ah-r0761) (freight worker).

**Personality.** Bruna always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Bruna is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Bruna is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a striped cotton shirt with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges, Bruna rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A worker asks to give their account privately. Bruna arranges a one-to-one account and checks which facts the worker permits to be shared.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Bruna says what they know in their own name and refuses to treat a guess as something the child has to disprove.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Bruna suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Ramona Penn

AH-R0765 · Age 46 · Former contractor and ferryman · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a goodbye drink poured before the final pay arrived.” On Saturday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Ramona stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I left the contractor over favoritism. They still owe me two goodbye shifts and a borrowed radio.”

**What Ramona wants.** To recover the goodbye shifts and the borrowed radio.

**Family.** [Eli Carr](../01/10-the-thirteenth-doughnut.md#ah-r0098) is Ramona’s second cousin. Ramona sets the time to leave. Eli picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The thirteenth doughnut*, Eli says, “My father's flour route paid for my first drum kit.”

**People in this story.** [Jesse Arnett](#ah-r0766). A borrowed radio and a statement from a seafarer travel back to the break room.

**Personality.** Ramona has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Ramona knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Ramona has short black hair swept back, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, an unlined tan jacket. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Ramona starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- The contractor offers selected workers a partial payment. Ramona asks that the offer be written down with the remaining unpaid hours.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Ramona asks the cashier quietly which item can be set aside without making the customer explain their finances to the line.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Ramona asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Jesse Arnett

AH-R0766 · Age 34 · Merchant seafarer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember missing a hearing while my ship was in another harbor.” On Wednesday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Jesse notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I took another job at sea before the meeting. I asked the quietest porter to read my words for me.”

**What Jesse wants.** To have the overseas statement heard at the meeting.

**Family.** [Min Calder](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0029) is Jesse’s cousin raised in the same household. Jesse gets to change the plan. Min offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The small signature*, Min says, “The bank charged more to print the display than the cart raised.”

**People in this story.** [Ramona Penn](#ah-r0765). A borrowed radio and a statement from a seafarer travel back to the break room.

**Personality.** Jesse enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** Jesse has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. Back on the street, the familiar outfit is a boxy denim jacket with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. In conversation Jesse takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A worker asks to give their account privately. Jesse arranges a one-to-one account and checks which facts the worker permits to be shared.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Jesse asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, Jesse puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Penelope Jeong

AH-R0767 · Age 29 · Contract manager's daughter and sign maker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my father serving supper to the men he employed.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Penelope by a small Friday habit. Penelope starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I am the boss's daughter. The lads asked for my signature and didn't ask me to defend him.”

**What Penelope wants.** To sign what actually happened without defending the father.

**Family.** [Francis Lim](../09/10-a-four-o-clock-kitchen.md#ah-r1296) is Penelope’s cousin. A relative always brought two cakes, the good one and the one that had collapsed. People at home learned to ask for the collapsed one first so the baker could sit down. Penelope thinks it tasted better with the icing in the middle. Asked about *A four o'clock kitchen*, Francis says, “I was asking for cheaper coffee before I learned the cook was paying someone to wake her youngest.”

**People in this story.** [Bruna Morse](#ah-r0764). The manager's daughter hears which day the payroll worker knew about the shortfall.

**Personality.** Penelope has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Penelope can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Penelope has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for a blunt cut just below the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a mustard canvas overshirt with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Penelope rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The contractor offers selected workers a partial payment. Penelope asks that the offer be written down with the remaining unpaid hours.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Penelope helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- If a friend borrows money, Penelope agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

## Wynn Kapoor

AH-R0768 · Age 35 · Shift loader · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember trying to explain a late payment to an impatient child.” People around the market by Market Flats know Wynn by a small Monday habit. Wynn often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I missed child support because the transfer didn't come. I want a letter my former partner can actually verify.”

**What Wynn wants.** To obtain a wage-delay letter the other parent can verify.

**Family.** [Lara Carey](../10/11-an-ordinary-mirror.md#ah-r1451) is Wynn’s parent’s cousin. Wynn asks before bringing somebody along. Lara can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *An ordinary mirror*, Lara says, “I want it for the mornings when I can't imagine another version of myself getting dressed.”

**People in this story.** [Carmen Kapoor](#ah-r0769). Both need usable wage records, one for family support and one for work done unofficially.

**Personality.** Wynn takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Wynn can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Wynn has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Wynn touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A worker asks to give their account privately. Wynn arranges a one-to-one account and checks which facts the worker permits to be shared.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Wynn asks the neighbor privately what they want, without treating their place as something for sale.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Wynn turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Carmen Kapoor

AH-R0769 · Age 50 · Casual freight worker · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being passed over because my old letters were in another language.” People around Breakwater Way know Carmen by a small Saturday habit. Carmen stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I used to accept cash so nobody asked about my papers. I want the union to hear me without promising miracles.”

**What Carmen wants.** To get a protected hearing from the union.

**Family.** [Sofia Valdez](../08/12-the-high-road-home.md#ah-r1169) is Carmen’s cousin. Carmen makes room at the table. Sofia brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The high road home*, Sofia says, “I can tell when the driver is scared because the radio gets louder.”

**People in this story.** [Wynn Kapoor](#ah-r0768). Both need usable wage records, one for family support and one for work done unofficially.

**Personality.** Carmen admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Carmen learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Carmen has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a striped cotton shirt. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Carmen folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**

- The contractor offers selected workers a partial payment. Carmen asks that the offer be written down with the remaining unpaid hours.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Carmen offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Carmen describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Amanda Wong

AH-R0770 · Age 52 · Union timekeeper · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my wife circling a missing hour in red.” On Friday, near Breakwater Way, Amanda stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I counted overtime for everyone except myself. My wife sat down with the sheet and found my name.”

**What Amanda wants.** To include the overlooked personal overtime in the claim.

**Family.** [Amir Jain](../07/14-the-carer-s-afternoon.md#ah-r1037) is Amanda’s half-sibling. Amanda saves a place. Amir chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The carer's afternoon*, Amir says, “I learned the meal routine because I wanted to flirt with the neighbor and ended up loving Wednesdays.”

**People in this story.** [Taylor Vargas](#ah-r0763). A missed break can be added to the union tally before the sheet leaves the room.

**Personality.** Amanda has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Amanda is easy to recognize by a smooth side part with one rebellious curl and fine brows over wide-set eyes. Usually dressed in a narrow-striped sweater with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve, Amanda folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A worker asks to give their account privately. Amanda arranges a one-to-one account and checks which facts the worker permits to be shared.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Amanda keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- If accused of favoritism, Amanda explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.
