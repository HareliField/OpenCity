## Rowan Kelly

AH-R0891 · Age 47 · Liveaboard and window fitter · Home — a boat at South Shore Harbor

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first night at sea without a shore address.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Rowan by a small Monday habit. Rowan pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I budgeted for timber and forgot the cost of a bed. I haven't rented a room since my marriage broke up.”

**What Rowan wants.** To find a bed for the dry-dock weeks.

**Family.** [Darcy Reyes](../05/03-the-trust-with-a-typo.md#ah-r0626) is Rowan’s younger cousin. Rowan chooses the music. Darcy puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The trust with a typo*, Darcy says, “I asked the school to use my own nickname on the shortlist.”

**People in this story.** [Grey Clement](#ah-r0897). The old berth lease may give the displaced resident a real key ashore. Also in the story is [Drew Hayward](#ah-r0898) (shore room coordinator).

**Personality.** Rowan never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Rowan can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Rowan is easy to recognize by a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears and a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Usually dressed in a pale blue work shirt with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread, Rowan draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A yard manager asks the liveaboard to leave by Friday. Rowan asks where the resident can sleep on the first night ashore.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Rowan asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Rowan may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Scout Marin

AH-R0892 · Age 56 · Overnight baker · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a sofa whose cushion stayed marked by a visitor's elbow.” Friday often includes a stop at Breakwater Way, where Scout stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I can offer a sofa but my son needs a place to come back to when he's sober.”

**What Scout wants.** To offer a sofa without closing the door on the son.

**Family.** [Felix Rubio](../03/14-the-housecoat-commission.md#ah-r0436) is Scout’s younger cousin. Scout chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Felix brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The housecoat commission*, Felix says, “I cook her Sunday lunch. She's invited me to eat my own food with the guests this time.”

**People in this story.** [Jo Wong](#ah-r0896). Two people offering space compare the limits of a sofa and a winter flat.

**Personality.** Scout often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Scout have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Scout has a loose dark braid, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a charcoal zip cardigan. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Scout leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- A friend offers a sofa for an uncertain number of weeks. Scout agrees a first review date for the sofa without pretending it settles the winter.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Scout asks what evidence supports the accusation and declines to supply a name on gossip alone.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Scout rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.

**In their own words.** “I want him to keep feeding the gull with the broken foot. I didn't think to ask who would feed him.”

**What Sam wants.** To arrange food for the person who looks after the gull.

**Family.** [Bruna Andrade](../05/09-an-address-in-pencil.md#ah-r0681) is Sam’s first cousin once removed. Sam brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Bruna makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *An address in pencil*, Bruna says, “I wrote the figure the way Dad did and nobody under thirty could read it.”

**People in this story.** [Sasha Gil](#ah-r0899). A departing berth holder is still somebody a dock neighbor is counting on to feed a gull.

**Personality.** Sam gets protective of anybody who comes into a meeting unprepared. Sam privately wishes people would notice the amount of preparation behind this apparent ease.

**Appearance.** Sam has a dark undercut with a longer wave on top, heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, an olive canvas jacket. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Sam leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations.

**Under pressure**

- A friend offers a sofa for an uncertain number of weeks. Sam agrees a first review date for the sofa without pretending it settles the winter.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Sam writes down their exact words with room left for uncertainty and gives them back to check.
- If a colleague lies to save face, Sam offers one private chance to correct it before the next conversation.

**In their own words.** “I loaned him the key to the workshop shower. He returned it because he wanted to pay for an ordinary membership.”

**Family.** [Louise Hooper](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1305) is Kit’s younger cousin. Kit asks before bringing somebody along. Louise can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Louise says, “I am the junior worker who polished the scarred hand.”

**People in this story.** [Fiona Salas](#ah-r0900). The spare shower key means something different to somebody who once spent a winter as a supposed visitor.

**Personality.** Kit uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Kit well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Kit is easy to recognize by a center part and straight chestnut hair and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket, Kit listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A yard manager asks the liveaboard to leave by Friday. Kit asks where the resident can sleep on the first night ashore.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Kit puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- After a family quarrel, Kit brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

**Appearance.** Jo is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Usually dressed in a narrow-striped sweater with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair, Jo looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

**Appearance.** Grey has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Back on the street, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a pen clipped inside the neckline. In conversation Grey rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

**Appearance.** Drew has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an old plum windbreaker with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Drew folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing.

**Under pressure**
