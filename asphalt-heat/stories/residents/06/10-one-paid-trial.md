# One paid trial

[What a working week costs](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Night Shift Donuts.** Night Shift Donuts offers a paid trial to a talented baker who has never held a formal job. The existing apprentice was promised the next opening.

## Maya Abreu

AH-R0841 · Age 24 · Family-trained baker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember setting pastries with the clock taken off our kitchen wall.” On Tuesday, near the steps at Pier Beach, Maya watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I learned with my grandmother's stove and have never worked on a timer. I brought her wristwatch.”

**What Maya wants.** To get a real chance to learn the timed oven.

**Family.** [Malcolm Hayes](../07/15-the-day-sailor-s-divorce.md#ah-r1046) is Maya’s older relative by marriage. Maya makes room at the table. Malcolm brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The day sailor's divorce*, Malcolm says, “I can take Saturdays if somebody swaps my carer's weekend.”

**People in this story.** [Remy Rose](#ah-r0846). Extra delivery cover creates a real oven opportunity for the self-taught applicant.

**Personality.** Maya can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Maya is easy to recognize by brown hair pinned away from the temples and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Maya squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The new baker gets the first oven slot. Maya makes sure the other applicant gets a comparable chance with the same oven.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Maya agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Maya asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Ida Boyd

AH-R0842 · Age 27 · Bakery apprentice · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember washing an oven on my first day with no gloves left.” People around Juniper Street know Ida by a small Sunday habit. Ida knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I have done the unglamorous closing shift for eleven months. I want the promise written where I can see it.”

**What Ida wants.** To put the old promise of promotion on paper.

**Family.** [Sonia Arnett](../08/03-the-guarantee.md#ah-r1071) is Ida’s cousin raised in the same household. Ida saves a place. Sonia chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The guarantee*, Sonia says, “I assumed Mum put down savings. I found out when she wouldn't lend me the money for a replacement boiler.”

**People in this story.** [Devin Bernal](#ah-r0847). The closer helps the apprentice list the unpaid work behind the old promotion promise. Also in the story is [Harley Kelly](#ah-r0843) (shift manager).

**Personality.** Ida can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Ida can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Ida has a center part and straight chestnut hair, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Ida rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The apprentice asks for an old promise in writing. Ida writes down the remembered promise including who was there to hear it.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Ida keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Ida suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Harley Kelly

AH-R0843 · Age 46 · Bakery shift manager · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember offering a promise beside a sink that cost more than I thought.” On Tuesday, near Studio Boulevard, Harley waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I posted the job without asking the apprentice what I once said across a sink.”

**What Harley wants.** To account for both candidates' prior labor.

**Family.** [Ava Nichols](../04/05-the-dead-man-s-tab.md#ah-r0499) is Harley’s second cousin. Harley chooses the music. Ava puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The dead man's tab*, Ava says, “I made the soup. He said it tasted like school dinners, which I discovered was a compliment.”

**People in this story.** [Harriet Ho](#ah-r0850). The shift manager has to cost the proposal that upends a simple hiring contest. Also in the story is [Ida Boyd](#ah-r0842) (apprentice).

**Personality.** Harley carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Harley has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for a loose dark braid. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a gray fisher's sweater with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Harley gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- The new baker gets the first oven slot. Harley makes sure the other applicant gets a comparable chance with the same oven.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Harley asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Harley offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Edward Pike

AH-R0844 · Age 38 · Pastry teacher · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember tasting food served by somebody I had just broken up with.” On Thursday, near Harbour Bakery, Edward buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I tasted both loaves blind and chose my ex's. I'm standing by it even though nobody will believe me.”

**What Edward wants.** To defend a blind tasting despite the old relationship.

**Family.** [Dora Garza](../08/07-a-prize-nobody-entered.md#ah-r1119) is Edward’s cousin raised in the same household. Edward keeps the photographs. Dora knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A prize nobody entered*, Dora says, “I own the bookshop. I have given the author the keys after closing so the decision doesn't happen in front of a queue.”

**People in this story.** [Frances Acosta](#ah-r0848). The certificate holder and the blind taster disagree about where expertise is visible.

**Personality.** Edward has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Edward has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a long navy raincoat with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Edward counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- The apprentice asks for an old promise in writing. Edward writes down the remembered promise including who was there to hear it.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Edward brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Edward says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Tara Dean

AH-R0845 · Age 59 · Food stall investor · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember funding a chance I never got at twenty.” On Saturday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Tara takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I sponsored the trial wage. My name can stay off the winner's contract.”

**What Tara wants.** To fund the trial with no obligation to the sponsor.

**Family.** [Martha Laurent](../10/09-a-house-divided-kindly.md#ah-r1439) is Tara’s second cousin. Tara makes the call. Martha is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A house divided kindly*, Martha says, “I am a tenant on the same street. I can't move a fence but I can share the bench I built from packing wood.”

**People in this story.** [Emery Colón](#ah-r0849). Sponsor and bread customer both care whether the new job leaves a loaf ordinary workers can buy.

**Personality.** Tara treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Tara lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a rust-colored canvas coat and a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. Tara has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with two white streaks in otherwise dark hair. Tara gives a little nod before asking a hard question during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The new baker gets the first oven slot. Tara makes sure the other applicant gets a comparable chance with the same oven.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Tara asks the nearest shop worker to stay too, then talks about ordinary things until the child can explain who is late.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Tara says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Remy Rose

AH-R0846 · Age 42 · Bread courier · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember leaving bread on a doorstep before the customer's illness became public.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Remy by a small Friday habit. Remy checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I can take morning deliveries for one week so both bakers get a fair turn at the oven.”

**What Remy wants.** To give each baker equal oven access.

**Family.** [Eric Romero](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0972) is Remy’s stepsibling. Remy leaves the first voice message. Eric calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Eric says, “I own the restaurant and cannot feed a table at last year's rent.”

**People in this story.** [Maya Abreu](#ah-r0841). Extra delivery cover creates a real oven opportunity for the self-taught applicant.

**Personality.** Remy is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Remy is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Remy has two dimples that appear only with a full smile. Look for a cropped cut that shows the shape of the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a red windbreaker with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. In conversation Remy grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- The apprentice asks for an old promise in writing. Remy writes down the remembered promise including who was there to hear it.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Remy asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Remy agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Devin Bernal

AH-R0847 · Age 32 · Closing cleaner · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a cleaning shift nobody could remember putting on the board.” Monday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Devin checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I cleaned with the apprentice for a year. I'm helping her write down every extra thing she learned unpaid.”

**What Devin wants.** To document the apprentice's actual skills.

**Family.** [Talia Drew](../04/06-the-waiting-room-plant.md#ah-r0504) is Devin’s first cousin once removed. Devin calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Talia leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The waiting-room plant*, Talia says, “I built the rolling stand from my own discarded hospital tray.”

**People in this story.** [Ida Boyd](#ah-r0842). The closer helps the apprentice list the unpaid work behind the old promotion promise.

**Personality.** Devin tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Devin will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a dark green knitted vest and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Devin has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Devin listens with the head tilted a little to the left during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Devin accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Devin repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Frances Acosta

AH-R0848 · Age 55 · Hotel baker · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember earning a certificate while the best cook at home had none.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Frances by a small Tuesday habit. Frances checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I only hire qualified bakers. Watching this trial has made me wonder what my certificate never tested.”

**What Frances wants.** To let a capable unqualified baker into the next hiring round.

**Family.** [Remy Cruz](../10/04-names-on-the-night-wall.md#ah-r1388) is Frances’s second cousin. Frances brings the paper and a pen. Remy decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Names on the night wall*, Remy says, “I chose myself first. After a year of treatment, showing up each week really has been a shared decision between past and present me.”

**People in this story.** [Edward Pike](#ah-r0844). The certificate holder and the blind taster disagree about where expertise is visible.

**Personality.** Frances is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Frances asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Frances has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, and a favorite piece of clothing, a boxy denim jacket. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Frances holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The apprentice asks for an old promise in writing. Frances writes down the remembered promise including who was there to hear it.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Frances asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Frances accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

AH-R0850 · Age 49 · Bakery partner · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first time two employees offered one job a better shape.” Monday often includes a stop at the public end of the South Shore boardwalk, where Harriet checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**What Harriet wants.** To get the two-shift proposal costed honestly.

**Family.** [Frances Ortiz](../03/15-the-tide-clock.md#ah-r0449) is Harriet’s cousin. Harriet brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Frances makes room at the table. **Personality.** Harriet loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Harriet remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Harriet has a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a washed violet jersey with a little coil of soft measuring tape. **Under pressure**

- The apprentice asks for an old promise in writing. Harriet writes down the remembered promise including who was there to hear it.
