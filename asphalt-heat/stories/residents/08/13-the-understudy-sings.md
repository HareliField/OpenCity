## Zachary Abbott

AH-R1171 · Age 24 · Chorus understudy · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember learning somebody else's song while they were still in the room.” On Friday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Zachary usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I want the part and hate the thought that I might be happy about someone else's bad news.”

**What Zachary wants.** To take the lead without hiding complicated feelings.

**Family.** [Logan Dinh](../03/08-the-painted-footprint.md#ah-r0377) is Zachary’s second cousin. Zachary calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Logan leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The painted footprint*, Logan says, “I brought the plaster cast my father took. It belongs to a gull hunter with enormous feet.”

**People in this story.** [Franklin Davila](#ah-r1172). The two singers discuss who gets to announce the change and who gets to hear the new voice first.

**Personality.** Zachary can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Zachary can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** Zachary has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, fine brows over wide-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a brown corduroy jacket. It usually goes with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. When somebody else has the floor, Zachary holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The understudy is offered the lead before the announcement. Zachary asks that the lead singer hear the change directly before it is advertised.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Zachary brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Zachary suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Franklin Davila

AH-R1172 · Age 34 · Lead singer · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember watching the subtitles at my own show.” People around Tideline Market know Franklin by a small Saturday habit. Franklin picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I am the lead. I want the audience to know why I am in the front row and not to spend the whole song looking at me.”

**What Franklin wants.** To choose what the audience knows and where to sit.

**Family.** [Alim Ward](../01/04-the-wrong-bicycle.md#ah-r0040) is Franklin’s half-sibling. A cousin asked for help with the rent and got six different lectures. The family now tries to agree on the amount it can offer before composing any advice. Franklin is good at asking what kind of help was actually requested. Asked about *The wrong bicycle*, Alim says, “The adults wanted separate appointments. The two riders wanted to compare yellow paint.”

**People in this story.** [Zachary Abbott](#ah-r1171). The two singers discuss who gets to announce the change and who gets to hear the new voice first. Also in the story is [Aria Miller](#ah-r1176) (sibling).

**Personality.** Franklin carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a soft blue checked shirt and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Franklin has fine brows over wide-set eyes, with a blunt cut just below the ears. Franklin starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The original singer asks to choose a seat in the audience. Franklin lets the singer choose a seat without alerting the whole audience.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Franklin separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Franklin offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Chloe Briggs

AH-R1173 · Age 42 · Publicity manager · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember printing an understudy's name on the last poster of a season.” Thursday often includes a stop at the corner outside Coastal Barber, where Chloe usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I promoted the show around one voice. I put the understudy's full name on every remaining poster.”

**What Chloe wants.** To give the understudy proper billing.

**Family.** [Carol Dunn](../06/12-the-late-crossing.md#ah-r0870) is Chloe’s stepsibling. Chloe decides what stays off the paper. Carol brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The late crossing*, Carol says, “I thought all six of us wanted a bus. One wants his license back and another wants work nearer her dad.”

**People in this story.** [Briar Wong](#ah-r1180). A replacement poster and a borrowed library score can carry the understudy's full name.

**Personality.** Chloe has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** Chloe has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for auburn hair cut square at the nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with a train token tied to a zipper pull. In conversation Chloe presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- The understudy is offered the lead before the announcement. Chloe asks that the lead singer hear the change directly before it is advertised.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Chloe takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Chloe says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Gareth Jiang

**Life in the city.** “I still remember realizing I read familiar lips before hearing the sound.” On Friday, near Tideline Market, Gareth picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I read lips in rehearsals. I offered to teach both singers without asking either for a diagnosis.”

**What Gareth wants.** To share accessible rehearsal habits without prying.

It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *No lifeguard at dusk*, Harold says, “I am her doctor and emphatically don't get to decide how she spends an ordinary evening.”

**People in this story.** [Agnes Colón](#ah-r1178). A caption worker and monitor technician help two performers share the same rehearsal.

**Personality.** Gareth treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Gareth lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Gareth has a high puff tied with plain cord, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a long navy raincoat. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Gareth draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- The original singer asks to choose a seat in the audience. Gareth lets the singer choose a seat without alerting the whole audience.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Gareth offers to help correct the printed name and leaves it to the colleague whether any public explanation is wanted.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Gareth says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Amos Ferraro

AH-R1175 · Age 48 · Show director · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the rehearsal that revealed nobody could cover the solo.” On Thursday, near Lighthouse Cove, Amos takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I am the director who waited too long to rehearse a second cast. We lost a whole good afternoon to my vanity.”

**What Amos wants.** To give the second cast real rehearsal time.

**Family.** [Morgan Lim](../10/07-an-empty-dedication.md#ah-r1417) is Amos’s half-sibling. Amos keeps the photographs. Morgan knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *An empty dedication*, Morgan says, “I translated the title. I want readers to understand that the word home in our language has a plural.”

**People in this story.** [Ana Crane](#ah-r1179). The director discovers practical help the family would rather have than another performance.

**Personality.** Amos is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Amos is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Amos has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Look for a dark undercut with a longer wave on top. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. In conversation Amos draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- The understudy is offered the lead before the announcement. Amos asks that the lead singer hear the change directly before it is advertised.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Amos repeats the correct name quietly, making the correction easy to hear without turning it into a performance.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Amos agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

**Family.** [Emery Rahman](../03/12-one-unposted-detour.md#ah-r0412) is Aria’s second cousin. Aria asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Emery chooses an ordinary story to tell. Asked about *One unposted detour*, Emery says, “I took the long road and discovered where my daughter has breakfast before school.”

**Appearance.** Aria has brown hair pinned away from the temples, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, an olive canvas jacket. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Aria squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

## Briar Wong

AH-R1180 · Age 50 · Music librarian · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember filing a score that still had two people's laughter in the margin.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Briar by a small Saturday habit. Briar pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**What Briar wants.** To save the duet as something the singers shared.

**Family.** [Talia Dai](../02/01-hem-by-hem.md#ah-r0154) is Briar’s second cousin. Briar is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Talia calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Hem by hem*, Talia says, “I kept the original cutting bill through two moves.”

**People in this story.** [Chloe Briggs](#ah-r1173). A replacement poster and a borrowed library score can carry the understudy's full name.

**Personality.** Briar has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Briar has auburn hair cut square at the nape, a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Briar counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices.

**Under pressure**

- The original singer asks to choose a seat in the audience. Briar lets the singer choose a seat without alerting the whole audience.
- A traveler asks for the last seat at the bus stop. Briar looks for a second safe place to sit and stays long enough for the waiting people to settle.
- When somebody trades on a title, Briar asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.
