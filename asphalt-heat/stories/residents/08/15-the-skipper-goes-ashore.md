## Ignacio Schmidt

AH-R1191 · Age 48 · Former skipper and new marine office worker · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first clear horizon I couldn't make myself look at.” On Tuesday, near the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, Ignacio notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I can face a hard crossing and can't face my crew seeing me wait for the nausea to settle.”

**What Ignacio wants.** To take a shore job with clear daily duties.

**Family.** [Riley Evans](../09/11-a-scar-at-the-salon.md#ah-r1307) is Ignacio’s second cousin. Ignacio checks the date twice. Riley brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A scar at the salon*, Riley says, “I used to hide my fingers after a factory injury.”

**People in this story.** [Adrian Andrade](#ah-r1198) (spouse). The shore job's real wage belongs in the family discussion about keeping the house. Also in the story is [Mika Lam](#ah-r1194) (successor).

**Personality.** Ignacio has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Ignacio has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. Back on the street, the familiar outfit is a rust-colored canvas coat with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Ignacio rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- An owner offers the former skipper a title with no clear duties. Ignacio asks what actual work, hours and wage go with the shore title.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Ignacio moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- When somebody trades on a title, Ignacio asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Irene Baines

AH-R1192 · Age 40 · Deckhand · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember holding a helm after another person's bad news.” On Sunday, near the bank steps, Irene takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I served under the skipper. She kept steering with a broken heart after her wife's death, so stopping now is hers to choose.”

**What Irene wants.** To give the former skipper room to choose.

**Family.** [Frankie Reyes](../05/04-one-clean-day.md#ah-r0637) is Irene’s cousin. Irene chooses the music. Frankie puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *One clean day*, Frankie says, “I took a call during the photograph. My friends left a space and took another after I came back.”

**People in this story.** [Mika Lam](#ah-r1194). The outgoing skipper's deckhand and the successor compare how to honor the old routine.

**Personality.** Irene finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Back on the street, look for a gray fisher's sweater and one cuff folded back twice. Irene has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with a center part and straight chestnut hair. Irene touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The new skipper changes the departure routine. Irene gives the new skipper space to explain the change before measuring it against the former routine.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Irene clears the dishes, lets the angriest speaker cool off, and resumes the conversation with a smaller audience.
- When thanked for a shared success, Irene points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Scout Ames

AH-R1193 · Age 60 · Yacht owner and investor · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember asking a retired pilot to lend a boat his good name.” Thursday often includes a stop at the downtown fountain, where Scout keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I own the boat. I want her to keep a title because the buyers know her, and she wants a real desk with work on it.”

**What Scout wants.** To agree a real office assignment.

**Family.** [Willa Rizzo](../09/09-the-returned-daughter.md#ah-r1285) is Scout’s cousin raised in the same household. Scout asks before bringing somebody along. Willa can always ask for five minutes alone. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The returned daughter*, Willa says, “I was the neighbor who opened a door for her.”

**People in this story.** [Emery Fields](#ah-r1196). The boat's owner gets a real office assignment costed by the marine accountant.

**Personality.** Scout keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Scout wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Scout has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek. Look for silver temples and a dark fringe. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a plum-colored cardigan with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. In conversation Scout presses the lips together to keep a smile in.

**Under pressure**

- An owner offers the former skipper a title with no clear duties. Scout asks what actual work, hours and wage go with the shore title.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Scout asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Scout offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Rosa Vance

AH-R1195 · Age 43 · Seafront café worker · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember learning where the harbor looks most level from a bench.” On Saturday, near Ferris Deli, Rosa asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I have always been seasick. I told the skipper which bench has the best steady horizon for talking after a trip.”

**What Rosa wants.** To provide a steady place to meet on land.

**Family.** [Elizabeth Campos](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0978) is Rosa’s cousin raised in the same household. Rosa gets to change the plan. Elizabeth offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Elizabeth says, “I make fancy cakes. I delivered one small enough to share without pretending it was a feast.”

**People in this story.** [Cyrus Berg](#ah-r1200). A steady patch of horizon by the café makes a place for the first shore payday lunch.

**Personality.** Rosa has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Rosa knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Rosa has a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a black overshirt with pearl buttons. It usually goes with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. When somebody else has the floor, Rosa steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- An owner offers the former skipper a title with no clear duties. Rosa asks what actual work, hours and wage go with the shore title.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Rosa suggests one straightforward message without accusations and gives the friend time to answer.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Rosa asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## Oakley Carbone

AH-R1197 · Age 38 · Former sailing pupil and teacher · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my first skipper letting me board before my nerves settled.” Monday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Oakley likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I was her first sailing student. I asked her to teach my child from the pier without pretending she was still on board.”

**What Oakley wants.** To arrange a lesson on the pier for the child.

**Family.** [Adriana Diaz](../06/09-a-room-for-a-father.md#ah-r0835) is Oakley’s younger cousin. When the family split between two addresses, the good frying pan traveled with whoever cooked Sunday dinner. The pan has outlasted the quarrel and both kitchens. Oakley likes a clear rule for sharing a disputed thing. Asked about *A room for a father*, Adriana says, “I want my grandfather to teach me one thing with his hands before anybody asks him to explain.”

**People in this story.** [Diana Baldwin](#ah-r1199). A docking tune gives a child a first pier lesson they can remember at home.

**Personality.** Oakley has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Oakley can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** Oakley has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for close-cropped dark curls. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a replacement button a shade darker than the others. In conversation Oakley squares up pieces of paper while thinking.

**Under pressure**

- An owner offers the former skipper a title with no clear duties. Oakley asks what actual work, hours and wage go with the shore title.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Oakley takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- If a friend borrows money, Oakley agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

**Appearance.** Adrian has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a copper-colored clasp that has been repaired by hand. In conversation Adrian gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a brown corduroy jacket with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. **Under pressure**

- The new skipper changes the departure routine. Cyrus gives the new skipper space to explain the change before measuring it against the former routine.
