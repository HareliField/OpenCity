# The third bedroom

## Cyrus Keller

AH-R1131 · Age 15 · Foster child and secondary pupil · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember holding my brother's wrist whenever the bedroom door shut.” On Saturday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Cyrus likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I have shared a room with my brother ever since the night we left. A closed door still feels like an alarm.”

**What Cyrus wants.** To choose when the new room's door closes.

**Family.** [Dora Villa](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0979) is Cyrus’s older cousin. Cyrus chooses the music. Dora puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Dora says, “I used to be the kid with no money at this party.”

**People in this story.** [Dale Lane](#ah-r1132) (foster sibling). The siblings want different things from the same closed bedroom door. Also in the story is [Ivy Burke](#ah-r1133) (foster parent).

**Personality.** Cyrus uses precise names for tools and very approximate names for feelings. People who know Cyrus well listen to what gets repaired after a difficult conversation.

**Appearance.** Cyrus is easy to recognize by thick, tightly coiled hair and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a plum-colored cardigan with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges, Cyrus looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A caseworker asks the foster siblings to describe sharing. Cyrus gives each sibling room to describe sharing separately.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Cyrus asks everybody to retrace their own last ordinary actions before asking the young worker a question.
- After a family quarrel, Cyrus brings back the borrowed thing in good condition and tries the conversation again without an audience.

## Dale Lane

AH-R1132 · Age 14 · Foster child and secondary pupil · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first room I could sit in with the light turned off.” People around Harbour Bakery know Dale by a small Monday habit. Dale buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I would like a private room so I can cry without making him think it's his fault.”

**What Dale wants.** To get room to cry without alarming the brother.

**Family.** [Dana Wong](../07/15-the-day-sailor-s-divorce.md#ah-r1043) is Dale’s first cousin once removed. Dale chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Dana brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *The day sailor's divorce*, Dana says, “I co-own the boat and am tired of becoming a messenger between people who know each other's numbers.”

**People in this story.** [Cyrus Keller](#ah-r1131) (foster sibling). The siblings want different things from the same closed bedroom door. Also in the story is [Ivy Burke](#ah-r1133) (foster parent).

**Personality.** Dale feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Dale has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip. Look for short black hair swept back. Outside school, the familiar outfit is a dark green knitted vest with a narrow brass watch set a little fast. In conversation Dale holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The lodger is asked to leave on short notice. Dale checks where the lodger can go before treating an empty room as a settled fact.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Dale tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Dale lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Ivy Burke

AH-R1133 · Age 46 · Foster carer and receptionist · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember saving two places at a crowded supper table.” People around the downtown fountain know Ivy by a small Saturday habit. Ivy keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**In their own words.** “I want to stay their carer. Our spare room pays the mortgage with a lodger in it.”

**What Ivy wants.** To keep both foster placements financially possible.

**Family.** [Ken Calloway](../04/10-closing-soup.md#ah-r0544) is Ivy’s cousin raised in the same household. Ivy chooses whether to use it. Ken saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *Closing soup*, Ken says, “I was ashamed my family lived on leftovers.”

**People in this story.** [Patrick Osborne](#ah-r1134). The carer and the lodger negotiate two families' housing at once. Other connections include [Cyrus Keller](#ah-r1131) (foster child), [Dale Lane](#ah-r1132) (foster child).

**Personality.** Ivy treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Ivy is easy to recognize by a high puff tied with plain cord and a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a replacement button a shade darker than the others, Ivy starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A caseworker asks the foster siblings to describe sharing. Ivy gives each sibling room to describe sharing separately.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Ivy asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Ivy changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

## Patrick Osborne

AH-R1134 · Age 33 · Lodger and warehouse worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember finding a room through a note in a newsagent's window.” On Wednesday, near Ferris Deli, Patrick asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I am the lodger who offered to leave. I need six weeks and a guarantor, which nobody remembered to ask about.”

**What Patrick wants.** To secure another room and guarantor before moving.

**Family.** [Diego Mitchell](../04/13-a-play-in-a-pocket.md#ah-r0580) is Patrick’s first cousin once removed. Patrick is free to hang up first. Diego makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A play in a pocket*, Diego says, “I did the same scene six times and only the oldest listener guessed where the missing brother had gone.”

**People in this story.** [Ivy Burke](#ah-r1133). The carer and the lodger negotiate two families' housing at once.

**Personality.** Patrick is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Patrick is concerned.

**Appearance.** Patrick has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a gray fisher's sweater. It usually goes with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric. When somebody else has the floor, Patrick grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- The lodger is asked to leave on short notice. Patrick checks where the lodger can go before treating an empty room as a settled fact.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Patrick asks who can manage the stairs and who would rather wait by the landing, without attempting a move on their behalf.
- When someone invents an answer, Patrick asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Allen Mora

AH-R1135 · Age 44 · Foster placement assessor · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember measuring a bedroom while somebody tried to tell me their history.” Monday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Allen looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I do the assessment. I brought a tape measure and ended up drawing two alternate plans on the kitchen floor.”

**What Allen wants.** To assess both floor plans with the children present.

**Family.** [Ren Berry](../04/15-the-engine-lullaby.md#ah-r0592) is Allen’s older cousin. Allen gets to change the plan. Ren offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The engine lullaby*, Ren says, “I tuned that engine solely by ear. I can hear which part was replaced while I was away.”

**People in this story.** [Shay Valdez](#ah-r1136). The assessor's second plan depends on a partition the fitter can build without losing the window.

**Personality.** Allen loves an audience until a private hurt enters the room. Then the performer disappears and someone very good at getting other people through a door takes over.

**Appearance.** Allen has a long plait tucked inside the collar, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a narrow-striped sweater. It usually goes with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. When somebody else has the floor, Allen looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- A caseworker asks the foster siblings to describe sharing. Allen gives each sibling room to describe sharing separately.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Allen walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- If a stranger asks to hear a private story, Allen offers one about their own embarrassment instead.

## Shay Valdez

AH-R1136 · Age 51 · Cabinet fitter · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember building a wardrobe around a precious patch of sunlight.” People around Breakwater Way know Shay by a small Thursday habit. Shay stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I build wardrobes. A proper partition would eat the window, so I'm pricing a different door.”

**What Shay wants.** To design a division that preserves daylight.

**Family.** [Rain Scott](../09/15-the-missing-name-on-the-stern.md#ah-r1348) is Shay’s cousin raised in the same household. Shay calls about good news first. Rain is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The missing name on the stern*, Rain says, “I was embarrassed by the old name growing up.”

**People in this story.** [Allen Mora](#ah-r1135). The assessor's second plan depends on a partition the fitter can build without losing the window.

**Personality.** Shay enjoys routines and will defend the right of someone else to break one. As a result, Shay is often everyone's favorite cover for a missed morning.

**Appearance.** Shay has a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Look for a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a black overshirt with pearl buttons with one cuff folded back twice. In conversation Shay steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- The lodger is asked to leave on short notice. Shay checks where the lodger can go before treating an empty room as a settled fact.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Shay alerts the nearest occupied shop, stays outside and writes down when the smoke first appeared.
- If a family plan changes at the door, Shay keeps a place for the late person without making the others postpone their meal.

## Katherine Mack

AH-R1137 · Age 31 · Former foster child and pharmacy clerk · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sharing a room that everybody else called cozy.” People around the blocks below Foundry Lofts know Katherine by a small Saturday habit. Katherine checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I was fostered on this street and hated sharing. I'm resisting the urge to decide for these particular boys.”

**What Katherine wants.** To help without projecting a separate childhood onto the boys.

**Family.** [Sage Nielsen](../04/02-the-soft-wharf-chalk.md#ah-r0470) is Katherine’s first cousin once removed. Katherine can always ask for five minutes alone. Sage asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The soft wharf chalk*, Sage says, “I was the one they were waiting for. I missed the ship and built my whole life here.”

**People in this story.** [Walter Colón](#ah-r1139). The former foster child warns the teacher what happens when two children's voices get confused.

**Personality.** Katherine is cheerful on a bad morning and unexpectedly melancholy after a success. Finishing a hard thing means losing the daily company of the people who worked on it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a long navy raincoat and a little coil of soft measuring tape. Katherine has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape. Katherine counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A caseworker asks the foster siblings to describe sharing. Katherine gives each sibling room to describe sharing separately.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Katherine asks the cashier quietly which item can be set aside without making the customer explain their finances to the line.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Katherine helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.

## Maria Dias

AH-R1138 · Age 39 · Night shift silk printer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a homework session interrupted by the night neighbor coming home.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Maria by a small Saturday habit. Maria knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I can rent a room to the lodger but I have a noisy night shift. We are trying a weekend first.”

**What Maria wants.** To test the offered room through a night shift.

**Family.** [Malcolm Garrett](../09/12-the-driving-lesson-fund.md#ah-r1311) is Maria’s second cousin. Maria offers the first invitation. Malcolm gets to change the plan. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The driving lesson fund*, Malcolm says, “I paid for my license by cleaning the instructor's flat.”

**People in this story.** [Roland Bloom](#ah-r1140). Trying a noisy room and keeping Thursday supper give the move an ordinary weekly rhythm.

**Personality.** Maria finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Maria is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial, Maria turns fully toward the person speaking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The lodger is asked to leave on short notice. Maria checks where the lodger can go before treating an empty room as a settled fact.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Maria stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Maria waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a pen clipped inside the neckline. Walter has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw, with short black hair swept back. Walter looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

## Roland Bloom

AH-R1140 · Age 46 · Family meal volunteer · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember stretching a Sunday across two different households.” On Sunday, near Ferris Deli, Roland asks for the end slice and listens to the names the counter workers use for regulars.

**In their own words.** “I want the family to keep eating at one table after the doors go up. I have promised to come on Thursdays.”

**What Roland wants.** To keep Thursday supper together after the move.

**Family.** [Barbara Frost](../09/02-the-worker-who-couldn-t-read-the-joke.md#ah-r1213) is Roland’s second cousin. Roland chooses an ordinary story to tell. Barbara asks the question the rest of the family avoided. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The worker who couldn't read the joke*, Barbara says, “I took the picture to show my brother the crew.”

**People in this story.** [Maria Dias](#ah-r1138). Trying a noisy room and keeping Thursday supper give the move an ordinary weekly rhythm.

**Personality.** Roland is kind in very practical ways and prickly about being thanked in public. Roland loves being recognized by the people who notice what happens after the guests have left.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it. Roland has a round face with a pronounced chin, with a long plait tucked inside the collar. Roland gives a little nod before asking a hard question during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The lodger is asked to leave on short notice. Roland checks where the lodger can go before treating an empty room as a settled fact.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Roland keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- If somebody calls for applause, Roland gives the credit to a specific helper and slips out before the photograph.
