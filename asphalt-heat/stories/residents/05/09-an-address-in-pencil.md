## Bruna Andrade

AH-R0681 · Age 70 · Retired tile setter · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember our family painting its first house number together.” On Wednesday, near the bank steps, Bruna takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I wrote the figure the way Dad did and nobody under thirty could read it.”

**What Bruna wants.** To write an address visitors can read.

**Family.** [Sam Ochoa](../06/15-winter-afloat.md#ah-r0894) is Bruna’s first cousin once removed. Bruna makes room at the table. Sam brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *Winter afloat*, Sam says, “I want him to keep feeding the gull with the broken foot.”

**People in this story.** [Robin Cao](#ah-r0683) (sign maker). A metal cutter reads the penciled number from the far side of the street.

**Personality.** Bruna has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cream pullover and a replacement button a shade darker than the others. Bruna has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with silver temples and a dark fringe. Bruna looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A delivery goes to the newly renumbered neighbor. Bruna checks both doorways before moving the delivery.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Bruna offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Bruna says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Rain Malik

AH-R0682 · Age 33 · Flower courier · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember delivering birthday flowers a street too far south.” On Saturday, near Lighthouse Cove, Rain takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I delivered flowers to the wrong widow. She walked them over and stayed for dinner.”

**What Rain wants.** To deliver the birthday flowers to both doorsteps gracefully.

**Family.** [Adriana Ingram](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0976) is Rain’s cousin. Rain saves a place. Adriana chooses whether to use it. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Adriana says, “I know the former chef's recipe. The family asked for it and I want her blessing before passing it on.”

**People in this story.** [Clara Ngo](#ah-r0684). A delayed letter and the misdelivered flowers travel to neighboring porches.

**Personality.** Rain treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Rain lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Rain has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a washed violet jersey with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Rain steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- A resident asks for the old metal number back. Rain asks who has already taken the metal down and where it was left.
- A stranger offers money to cut ahead of an exhausted neighbor. Rain keeps the neighbor's place and finds out whether the stranger needs a different kind of help.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Rain says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Robin Cao

AH-R0683 · Age 51 · Metal number cutter · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember cutting number ones until my wrist got steady.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the public road outside the Port of Asphalt, where Robin can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I make numbers for office buildings. I cut a new one big enough to see without crossing the road.”

**What Robin wants.** To cut a permanent number visible across the road.

**Family.** [Nico Ibrahim](../04/10-closing-soup.md#ah-r0547) is Robin’s stepsibling. Robin is free to hang up first. Nico makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Closing soup*, Nico says, “I brought a thermos for my ex. We still buy food for each other even though we cannot share a table.”

**People in this story.** [Bruna Andrade](#ah-r0681) (homeowner). A metal cutter reads the penciled number from the far side of the street.

**Personality.** Robin is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Robin is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Robin has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, an aquiline nose and a square chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. When somebody else has the floor, Robin draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- A delivery goes to the newly renumbered neighbor. Robin checks both doorways before moving the delivery.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Robin keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Robin agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Clara Ngo

AH-R0684 · Age 49 · Letterpress assistant · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the envelope I carried past a neighbor's gate twice.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Clara by a small Friday habit. Clara starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I used to live opposite. The penciled number reminded me that I had a letter I never sent.”

**What Clara wants.** To hand the unsent letter over.

**Family.** [Levi Lau](../06/11-a-chair-of-one-s-own.md#ah-r0856) is Clara’s younger cousin. A grown relative showed up at the family table in work clothes one holiday because taking extra time to change would mean missing the meal. The dusty chair is now part of the telling. Clara gets people inside before commenting on how they look. Asked about *A chair of one's own*, Levi says, “I gave her a lift to view another shop. I hope she'll stay but I want it to be a choice.”

**People in this story.** [Rain Malik](#ah-r0682). A delayed letter and the misdelivered flowers travel to neighboring porches.

**Personality.** Clara tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Clara will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Clara has a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Look for thick, tightly coiled hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a cropped bottle-green coat with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. In conversation Clara draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- A resident asks for the old metal number back. Clara asks who has already taken the metal down and where it was left.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Clara takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Clara repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Carla Molina

AH-R0685 · Age 28 · Substitute postal worker · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the porch with the permanently crooked curtain.” On Monday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Carla stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I am the postal substitute. I know the houses by curtains until everybody changes them.”

**What Carla wants.** To draw a route that works after the curtains change.

**Family.** [Faye Boone](../08/01-reopening-at-six.md#ah-r1057) is Carla’s cousin. Carla makes the call. Faye is free to hang up first. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Reopening at six*, Faye says, “I live over the machines. I want one night when I can hear my husband's voice in bed.”

**People in this story.** [Darcy Bay](#ah-r0687). A substitute carrier draws the porch on a card for the pensioner driver.

**Personality.** Carla is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Carla asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a cropped bottle-green coat and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Carla has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. Carla takes off the watch before a long conversation during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A delivery goes to the newly renumbered neighbor. Carla checks both doorways before moving the delivery.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Carla brings the worker into the conversation and asks what they remember without feeding them a story.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Carla accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Valentina Carey

AH-R0686 · Age 59 · Neighborhood archivist · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a family street known by its old name after the sign changed.” On Wednesday, near Maré Coffee, Valentina likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I didn't know we had two numbering systems before the viaduct opened. I want both in my photo album.”

**What Valentina wants.** To put both numbering schemes in the old album.

**Family.** [Petra Noel](../01/15-the-empty-berth.md#ah-r0150) is Valentina’s second cousin. Somebody once took a family child's drawing to be framed and trimmed away the signature. The trimming hurt worse than spoiling the picture. At home they still ask where the signature goes. Valentina remembers exactly who made the thing. Asked about *The empty berth*, Petra says, “From offshore you can see straight through the gap to the porch where I grew up.”

**People in this story.** [Bianca Trent](#ah-r0690). Both families keep their old numbering on the door and in the album.

**Personality.** Valentina is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Valentina is easy to recognize by closely cropped salt-and-pepper hair and a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth. Usually dressed in a narrow-striped sweater with a pen clipped inside the neckline, Valentina touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A resident asks for the old metal number back. Valentina asks who has already taken the metal down and where it was left.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Valentina writes down the shift time accurately, then helps them explain the circumstances without having to tell the whole room.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Valentina thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Darcy Bay

AH-R0687 · Age 42 · Community driver · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember waiting outside the wrong building with two heavy cases.” On Saturday, near Paper & Pine Books, Darcy starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I missed the correct door collecting my pensioner fares. The family made me a card with the porch drawn on it.”

**What Darcy wants.** To find the right porch for every fare.

**Family.** [Drew Hayward](../06/15-winter-afloat.md#ah-r0898) is Darcy’s cousin. Darcy chooses the familiar route. Drew can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Winter afloat*, Drew says, “I am the person who has to empty that room.”

**People in this story.** [Carla Molina](#ah-r0685). A substitute carrier draws the porch on a card for the pensioner driver.

**Personality.** Darcy loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Darcy remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Darcy is easy to recognize by a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow and a softly rounded chin under sharp cheekbones. Usually dressed in an old plum windbreaker with a pocket notebook shaped by years of sitting on it, Darcy raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A delivery goes to the newly renumbered neighbor. Darcy checks both doorways before moving the delivery.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Darcy keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- When invited to tell the story later, Darcy includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Marcel Lai

AH-R0688 · Age 54 · Scrap sorter · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember finding my old scooter's registration in a scrap bin.” People around Studio Boulevard know Marcel by a small Tuesday habit. Marcel waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I saved the old numeral for scrap and realized it matched the dent on my first motorcycle.”

**What Marcel wants.** To keep a scrap of metal from the old number.

**Family.** [David Soto](../08/12-the-high-road-home.md#ah-r1163) is Marcel’s stepsibling. Marcel brings the paper and a pen. David decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The high road home*, David says, “I trust my daughter and distrust the road. I had to say both things without making her fear bigger.”

**People in this story.** [Jasmine Miranda](#ah-r0689). Scrap metal and an engraved address have two very different futures in the same workshop.

**Personality.** Marcel has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Marcel has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for black hair twisted into a small knot. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a brown corduroy jacket with a little coil of soft measuring tape. In conversation Marcel gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A resident asks for the old metal number back. Marcel asks who has already taken the metal down and where it was left.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Marcel offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When somebody trades on a title, Marcel asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Bianca Trent

AH-R0690 · Age 38 · Home-care aide · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember two alphabets on the door of our old flat.” People around the public road outside the Port of Asphalt know Bianca by a small Wednesday habit. Bianca can recognize shift change by the lull between two groups of walkers.

**In their own words.** “I offered my grandmother's spare number tile. Now there are two grandparents' handwritings over our front door.”

**What Bianca wants.** To mount the two handwritings above the family door.

**Family.** [Sasha Baldwin](../09/06-a-doctor-moves-away.md#ah-r1254) is Bianca’s cousin. Bianca remembers the birthdays. Sasha asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *A doctor moves away*, Sasha says, “I clean her office. She always notices the first orange I bring from my tree and nobody else at work does.”

**People in this story.** [Valentina Carey](#ah-r0686). Both families keep their old numbering on the door and in the album.

**Personality.** Bianca keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Bianca wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Bianca has a deep crease beside the mouth when smiling. Look for a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. In conversation Bianca gathers both sleeves neatly before settling in.

**Under pressure**

- A resident asks for the old metal number back. Bianca asks who has already taken the metal down and where it was left.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Bianca asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Bianca offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.
