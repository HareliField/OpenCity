## Tali Allen

AH-R0661 · Age 49 · Plumbing estimator · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember pretending to enjoy a mystery to impress somebody.” On Thursday, near the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, Tali prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I am giving back a detective novel I stole to impress a girl who hated mysteries.”

**What Tali wants.** To return the detective novel in person.

**Family.** [Vera Hardy](../02/09-a-wall-of-birthdays.md#ah-r0238) is Tali’s half-sibling. Tali is free to hang up first. Vera makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *A wall of birthdays*, Vera says, “My landlord says the wall belongs to him. I'd like him to meet the grandmother who owns the pencil.”

**People in this story.** [Devin Bruno](#ah-r0669). They look through remembered nicknames for the first owner of a borrowed mystery. Also in the story is [Elaine Aguilar](#ah-r0667) (fellow borrower).

**Personality.** Tali takes a rejection personally for a day and analytically on the second morning. Tali can be generous with a rival once the first night's sleep has intervened.

**Appearance.** Tali is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in an unlined tan jacket with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Tali looks toward the door whenever a name is called when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The book's original owner asks to keep the apology private. Tali lets the book go back with the note folded inside.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Tali offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- If someone else wins the opportunity, Tali turns up the next morning with the one piece of advice that would have helped either of them.

## Remy Carey

AH-R0662 · Age 52 · Family reader and florist · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the first book I read to my frightened father.” On Saturday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Remy stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I want the owner to know I read every line aloud to my dad before he died.”

**What Remy wants.** To tell the owner how the book helped the father.

**Family.** [Cory Colón](../02/01-hem-by-hem.md#ah-r0152) is Remy’s younger cousin. Remy can stop the walk at any corner. Cory chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Hem by hem*, Cory says, “I paid for silk believing it was new. Now I want the old fabric back in our vows.”

**People in this story.** [Priya Solis](#ah-r0665). The recorded reading and the sea route both belong inside the owner's returned book.

**Personality.** Remy admires skill and distrusts the idea that skill gives anyone permission to be rude. Remy learns fastest from somebody willing to show how they made a mistake.

**Appearance.** Remy is easy to recognize by short copper curls and a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case, Remy rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A bookseller wants to display the most surprising note. Remy asks the writer who was supposed to receive the note.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Remy keeps the customer's place while they check the amount and gets the impatient people talking about something else.
- When a beginner is embarrassed, Remy describes a first attempt of their own in enough detail to make the comparison fair.

## Anton Long

AH-R0663 · Age 58 · Coffee seller · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember copying an illustration ruined by hot chocolate.” Saturday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Anton looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I spilled coffee on page six and copied the page by hand. Thirty years later my hand looks like my mum's.”

**What Anton wants.** To show the copied page to the original lender.

**Family.** [Zara Kang](../04/12-the-ladder-crossing.md#ah-r0567) is Anton’s cousin raised in the same household. Anton sets the time to leave. Zara picks the meeting place. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *The ladder crossing*, Zara says, “My wife stepped between the rungs laughing.”

**People in this story.** [Elaine Aguilar](#ah-r0667). They compare an exact replacement with the original damaged page.

**Personality.** Anton has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** Anton has one cheek that creases before the other. Look for a loose dark braid. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. In conversation Anton draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first.

**Under pressure**

- The book's original owner asks to keep the apology private. Anton lets the book go back with the note folded inside.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Anton takes one immediate task off their hands and asks when they want to tell the supervisor.
- If accused of favoritism, Anton explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.

## Ida Bruno

AH-R0664 · Age 72 · Retired reading tutor · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a borrowed book returned by the person I married.” People around the steps at Pier Beach know Ida by a small Sunday habit. Ida watches the working part of the waterfront before turning toward the open water.

**In their own words.** “I lent this book so a friend would have to see me again. The note finally explained why she didn't.”

**What Ida wants.** To hear why the old friend stayed away.

**Family.** [Teresa Berg](../07/08-the-expensive-summer.md#ah-r0980) is Ida’s younger cousin. Ida leaves the first voice message. Teresa calls back when there is finally a little privacy. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The expensive summer*, Teresa says, “I want a birthday where the people at the table can argue about movies instead of arithmetic.”

**People in this story.** [Logan Gupta](#ah-r0666). A loan meant to prolong a friendship helped another borrower out of a marriage.

**Personality.** Ida reads little signals of discomfort very accurately and has an unfortunate habit of overruling people for their own good. The friends who say stop are the ones Ida trusts most.

**Appearance.** Ida has a long face and high cheekbones. Look for a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a sand-colored linen shirt with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. In conversation Ida takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A bookseller wants to display the most surprising note. Ida asks the writer who was supposed to receive the note.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Ida stays where both neighbors can hear a familiar voice and gets news from the caretaker.
- If told to back off, Ida does so immediately and stays available at a distance the other person chooses.

## Priya Solis

AH-R0665 · Age 50 · Seafarer · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a map drawn inside an ocean-crossing novel.” People around the window at Night Shift Donuts know Priya by a small Friday habit. Priya knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I took mine on a ship and carried it through three oceans. I drew the route on the apology.”

**What Priya wants.** To give the owner the ocean route map.

**Family.** [Roman Wade](../08/02-land-without-cranes.md#ah-r1064) is Priya’s half-sibling. Priya chooses the familiar route. Roman can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *Land without cranes*, Roman says, “I got my first contract sweeping that floor after a conviction.”

**People in this story.** [Remy Carey](#ah-r0662). The recorded reading and the sea route both belong inside the owner's returned book.

**Personality.** Priya has a quick wit and an equally quick sense of shame. A joke is often Priya's way of finding out whether a room is friendly enough for a serious question.

**Appearance.** Priya is easy to recognize by a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a long navy raincoat with one loose pocket corner secured with green thread, Priya holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The book's original owner asks to keep the apology private. Priya lets the book go back with the note folded inside.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Priya stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- If the joke lands badly, Priya seeks out the person it hurt before telling anybody why it was supposed to be funny.

## Logan Gupta

AH-R0666 · Age 41 · Separated office administrator · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the library card I kept after leaving home.” Sunday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Logan often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I am not sorry I kept it. It got me out of the marriage described in chapter twelve.”

**What Logan wants.** To keep the marked chapter that made leaving possible.

**Family.** [Amalia Keller](../07/02-the-absent-signature.md#ah-r0912) is Logan’s second cousin. The family gave a child a coin for every question asked at an appointment. By the third visit the child was paying the others to keep quiet and let the professional answer. Logan will wait through an awkward pause for a real answer. Asked about *The absent signature*, Amalia says, “I want to sell so my wife can retire. My missing sister thinks I want my childhood erased.”

**People in this story.** [Ida Bruno](#ah-r0664). A loan meant to prolong a friendship helped another borrower out of a marriage.

**Personality.** Logan listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Logan is easy to recognize by a swept fringe and a closely clipped nape and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light, Logan looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A bookseller wants to display the most surprising note. Logan asks the writer who was supposed to receive the note.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Logan takes it to a staffed counter with a second person present and leaves contact details for the owner.
- When a second witness disagrees, Logan keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Elaine Aguilar

AH-R0667 · Age 33 · Day-care worker · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a child's bite mark on a dictionary.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Elaine by a small Thursday habit. Elaine prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I donated the clean replacement. The owner chose the dirty original because our toddler bit the corner.”

**What Elaine wants.** To return both copies and let the owner choose.

**Family.** [Neal Heller](../06/06-home-with-stairs.md#ah-r0807) is Elaine’s half-sibling. Elaine calls about good news first. Neal is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *Home with stairs*, Neal says, “I could give her a piggyback ride. **People in this story.** [Anton Long](#ah-r0663). They compare an exact replacement with the original damaged page. Also in the story is [Tali Allen](#ah-r0661) (fellow borrower).

**Appearance.** Elaine has a smooth side part with one rebellious curl, a round face with a pronounced chin, and a favorite piece of clothing, a plum-colored cardigan. It usually goes with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. When somebody else has the floor, Elaine looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The book's original owner asks to keep the apology private. Elaine lets the book go back with the note folded inside.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Elaine offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Elaine says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Dominic Cho

AH-R0668 · Age 45 · Library shelf clerk · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember knocking at the old school after the desks were sold.” Saturday often includes a stop at Lighthouse Cove, where Dominic takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I am returning a book to a closed classroom. The retired teacher offered to meet me at the till.”

**What Dominic wants.** To meet the retired teacher at the bookshop.

**Family.** [Jonas Osborne](../08/10-the-first-delivery-fee.md#ah-r1142) is Dominic’s cousin raised in the same household. Dominic remembers the birthdays. Jonas asks what the birthday guest would like this year. A present that was perfect at sixteen may be completely wrong at forty. They take the trouble of finding out. Asked about *The first delivery fee*, Jonas says, “I am the regular who complained first. I believed the owner paid for delivery all these years.”

**People in this story.** [Silas Murphy](#ah-r0670). A retired teacher gets a visit and an apology drafted under the writer's own name.

**Personality.** Dominic prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a small enamel bird on the lapel. Dominic has a wide brow and a narrow jaw, with short sandy hair and a marked widow's peak. Dominic grins on one side while the eyes stay serious during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A bookseller wants to display the most surprising note. Dominic asks the writer who was supposed to receive the note.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Dominic starts a quiet conversation with the most impatient onlookers so the musician can recover their own time.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Dominic clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.

## Devin Bruno

AH-R0669 · Age 38 · Pet sitter · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a dog with a finer name than anyone on our street.” People around Tideline Market know Devin by a small Tuesday habit. Devin picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I don't know who loaned it to me. I remember their dog's name and hope somebody recognizes it.”

**What Devin wants.** To identify the lender through the old dog's name.

**Family.** [Ivy Scott](../03/08-the-painted-footprint.md#ah-r0371) is Devin’s older relative by marriage. Devin can always ask for five minutes alone. Ivy asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The painted footprint*, Ivy says, “I came to see whether it was my foot before my toes began to curl.”

**People in this story.** [Tali Allen](#ah-r0661). They look through remembered nicknames for the first owner of a borrowed mystery.

**Personality.** Devin never tires of being shown how somebody makes a living. Devin can ask an expert question of a craftsperson and still mishandle a simple emotional cue from a friend.

**Appearance.** Devin has a wide brow and a narrow jaw. Look for short black hair swept back. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a pale blue work shirt with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. In conversation Devin gives a little nod before asking a hard question.

**Under pressure**

- The book's original owner asks to keep the apology private. Devin lets the book go back with the note folded inside.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Devin asks the stranger who will be expecting them, then helps find a way to tell that person.
- When a relative hints at needing company, Devin may initially offer a repair, but sits down as soon as the request becomes clear.

## Silas Murphy

AH-R0670 · Age 56 · Executive secretary · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember writing a customer's apology for damaged stage curtains.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Silas by a small Thursday habit. Silas prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I used to write apologies for my boss. This is the first one I signed with my own name.”

**What Silas wants.** To sign the apology personally this time.

**Family.** [Adele Gomez](../03/15-the-tide-clock.md#ah-r0450) is Silas’s half-sibling. Silas chooses whether to use it. Adele saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The tide clock*, Adele says, “I collect clocks that measure something worth waiting for.”

**People in this story.** [Dominic Cho](#ah-r0668). A retired teacher gets a visit and an apology drafted under the writer's own name.

**Personality.** Silas often says yes too quickly out of pride in being useful. The people closest to Silas have learned to ask again the next morning, when the answer is more likely to account for sleep.

**Appearance.** Silas is easy to recognize by a high puff tied with plain cord and a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Usually dressed in a soft blue checked shirt with a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead, Silas squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A bookseller wants to display the most surprising note. Silas asks the writer who was supposed to receive the note.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Silas stays with the rider after the people who saw the fall start drifting away.
- When two people ask for the same hour, Silas rings both of them before quietly counting on somebody else to fill in.
