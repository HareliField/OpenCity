## Rowan Katz

AH-R0621 · Age 72 · Scholarship founder and retired accountant · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the classroom where my nickname hurt most.” People around the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio know Rowan by a small Thursday habit. Rowan notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I chose that nickname to survive school. Seeing it on bank stationery made me feel sixteen again.”

**What Rowan wants.** To let the scholarship use the old nickname with permission.

**Family.** [Catherine Leung](../04/09-the-porch-auction.md#ah-r0533) is Rowan’s second cousin. Rowan chooses whether to use it. Catherine saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The porch auction*, Catherine says, “I proposed to my second husband in the low chair.”

**People in this story.** [Howard Poole](#ah-r0627) (trust secretary). Founder and secretary can now meet under the easier childhood name.

**Personality.** Rowan listens for the familiar version of a story and then for the one detail that changes it. Friends bring problems here when they want to be heard twice.

**Appearance.** Rowan has closely cropped salt-and-pepper hair, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a brown corduroy jacket. It usually goes with a small enamel bird on the lapel. When somebody else has the floor, Rowan turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A recipient thinks the nickname makes the letter a joke. Rowan confirms the letter with the trust before asking the recipient to respond.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Rowan keeps the money and the rumor out of the conversation and shares only firsthand observations with the person taking the report.
- When a second witness disagrees, Rowan keeps the accounts separate until the witnesses have had a chance to meet.

## Clara Baines

AH-R0622 · Age 19 · Scholarship applicant · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember waiting at a mailbox for my examination date.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the window at Night Shift Donuts, where Clara knows when the going-home crowd and the coming-to-work crowd will both be there.

**In their own words.** “I feared the nickname would disqualify my form and missed the deadline trying to replace it.”

**What Clara wants.** To get the delayed application accepted.

**Family.** [Emil Lund](../06/11-a-chair-of-one-s-own.md#ah-r0857) is Clara’s second cousin. Clara brings the paper and a pen. Emil decides what stays off the paper. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *A chair of one's own*, Emil says, “I have always wanted my own salon. I sat in that chair after closing and costed the dream down to the last bottle.”

**People in this story.** [Rae Villa](#ah-r0628). A discarded envelope is proof that a missed deadline began with a plausible mistake.

**Personality.** Clara talks very little about private fear and recognizes it quickly in strangers. Offering a choice of seats is one of Clara's ways of saying that it is all right to leave.

**Appearance.** Clara has a center part and straight chestnut hair, one cheek that creases before the other, and a favorite piece of clothing, a boxy denim jacket. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Clara holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- The founder is asked to use a formal name. Clara leaves the founder to decide what her own name on a letter should be.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Clara accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- When pressed to explain someone else's silence, Clara says only what happened and lets the absent person keep their reasons.

## Alexei Gibbs

AH-R0623 · Age 39 · Typesetter · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember printing the wrong accent on a teacher's name.” On Saturday, near Juniper Street, Alexei knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I made the typo from a penciled instruction the founder wrote herself. I kept the instruction.”

**What Alexei wants.** To keep the penciled instruction with the print proof.

**Family.** [Dennis Perry](../03/14-the-housecoat-commission.md#ah-r0439) is Alexei’s cousin. At home the birthday guest used to get first choice of song. One quiet relative chose silence and got ten wonderful minutes in which everybody could hear the sea. Alexei can keep a good gathering from becoming an obligation to entertain. Asked about *The housecoat commission*, Dennis says, “I iron on a child's old desk. The collar turned out square because the desk was, and she liked it.”

**People in this story.** [Lane Marsh](#ah-r0630). The instruction preserved by the printer determines which letters the founder will sign.

**Personality.** Alexei prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** Alexei has close-cropped dark curls, a wide brow and a narrow jaw, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket. When somebody else has the floor, Alexei draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting.

**Under pressure**

- A recipient thinks the nickname makes the letter a joke. Alexei confirms the letter with the trust before asking the recipient to respond.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Alexei helps shift the damp stock and makes sure the worker gets to describe the loss without interruption.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Alexei clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.
