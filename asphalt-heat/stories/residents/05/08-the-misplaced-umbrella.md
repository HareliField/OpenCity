## Idris Yeo

AH-R0671 · Age 45 · Garment repairer · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a wedding dress that became several practical patches.” Wednesday often includes a stop at the foot of Harbor Viaduct, where Idris pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I stitched the patch from a dress my aunt wore on her only holiday. I don't care what the parasol cost.”

**What Idris wants.** To recover the aunt's patched fabric.

**Family.** [Ida Henry](../03/02-a-ship-without-postcards.md#ah-r0313) is Idris’s cousin raised in the same household. Idris decides what stays off the paper. Ida brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *A ship without postcards*, Ida says, “I bought a blank card to send my aunt the smell of her old soap.”

**People in this story.** [Denise Hamm](#ah-r0678). The dress patch comes with credit for its seamstress in the mending class.

**Personality.** Idris feels a room through sound. A door slammed in high spirits can produce the same instant startle as an angry one, and it takes a little while to hear what actually follows.

**Appearance.** Idris is easy to recognize by auburn hair cut square at the nape and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining, Idris squares up pieces of paper while thinking when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Both umbrella owners turn up at the beach. Idris gets the two families together in the shade to identify the items.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Idris stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- If an argument gets loud at home, Idris lowers their own voice and asks for one thought at a time.

## Aubrey Yates

AH-R0672 · Age 39 · Hotel operations manager · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember stripes sewn slightly differently for each hotel balcony.” On Friday, near Juniper Street, Aubrey knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I manage the hotel that owns the stripes. I took the patched one to lunch and liked the shade better.”

**What Aubrey wants.** To return the hotel's stripes to the hire stand.

**Family.** [Ellen Boone](../08/02-land-without-cranes.md#ah-r1068) is Aubrey’s stepsibling. Aubrey picks the meeting place. Ellen sets the time to leave. It lets them stay close without turning a family visit into the loss of an entire day. They have both had stretches when that distinction mattered. Asked about *Land without cranes*, Ellen says, “I am taking an office course in secret. I want the conversion to include a room where I could actually learn.”

**People in this story.** [Audrey Kahn](#ah-r0674) (boardwalk supplier). They trace a striped hotel parasol from the hire stand to the chess game.

**Personality.** Aubrey treats keeping somebody waiting as a serious discourtesy. This can turn a helpful person into an impatient one when everybody else needs a slower conversation.

**Appearance.** Aubrey has short copper curls, a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cream pullover. It usually goes with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. When somebody else has the floor, Aubrey holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A hotel worker asks to throw the patched umbrella away. Aubrey puts the patched umbrella aside until the owner can explain the repair.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Aubrey keeps listening through the pause and lets the musician choose the next note.
- If a frightened person needs longer, Aubrey changes the plan openly and takes responsibility for the delay.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a child's first important use of the word yours.” People around the entrance to the Wash House know Paul by a small Friday habit. Paul stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I saw the switch but the toddler holding both had just learned mine and yours.”

**What Paul wants.** To let the child explain the swap.

**Family.** [Ana West](../09/04-a-call-heard-twice.md#ah-r1236) is Paul’s half-sibling. Paul keeps the photographs. Ana knows which ones can be shared. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A call heard twice*, Ana says, “I walked over with a cup of water. I am grateful the letter didn't mention me because I cried afterward.”

**People in this story.** [June Kent](#ah-r0679). A child's confusion about ownership leads the secondhand buyer to the aunt who made the first dress.

**Personality.** Paul is patient with a person learning and fiercely impatient with somebody pretending to know. A half-finished job left where a beginner can see it is an invitation, as far as Paul is concerned.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a boxy denim jacket and a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. Paul has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with auburn hair cut square at the nape. Paul grins on one side while the eyes stay serious during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Both umbrella owners turn up at the beach. Paul gets the two families together in the shade to identify the items.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Paul agrees on a sum and a date with the friend in private, keeping the arrangement out of family gossip.
- When someone invents an answer, Paul asks them to try it in front of the person who will have to live with it.

## Carlo Leung

AH-R0676 · Age 47 · Delivery cyclist · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sharing one umbrella along a street with six awnings.” Friday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Carlo takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I walked an elderly man home under the wrong umbrella. That's where we found the right owner.”

**What Carlo wants.** To walk the umbrellas to their correct owners.

**Family.** [Kai Kohli](../09/12-the-driving-lesson-fund.md#ah-r1319) is Carlo’s older cousin. Carlo chooses the familiar route. Kai can stop the walk at any corner. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The driving lesson fund*, Kai says, “I used to be a cabdriver. I am teaching map reading in the park while the fund grows.”

**People in this story.** [Rowan Armas](#ah-r0677). The rescued man and a family photograph give the patched umbrella two independent witnesses.

**Appearance.** Carlo has a small notch in the left eyebrow. Look for dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a boxy denim jacket with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. In conversation Carlo holds eye contact until the other person has finished.

**Under pressure**

- A hotel worker asks to throw the patched umbrella away. Carlo puts the patched umbrella aside until the owner can explain the repair.
- A witness is embarrassed about remembering only a little. Carlo tells them which ordinary detail proved helpful on a previous occasion, then listens without suggesting an answer.
- When everyone else wants to celebrate immediately, Carlo helps set the table, then takes a short turn outside alone.
