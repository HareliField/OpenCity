# The crane shadow

[People behind the counters](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Port of Asphalt.** A port apprentice notices that a crane's shadow reaches a painted gull on the quay at the same hour each spring.

## Martin Rahman

AH-R0611 · Age 67 · Retired port painter · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a crane shadow hitting my first steel-toed shoe.” Tuesday often includes a stop at the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms, where Martin prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I painted the gull where the shadow touched on the day I started. I've been timing my career by it.”

**What Martin wants.** To photograph one more spring shadow.

**Family.** [Joan Morley](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0025) is Martin’s cousin raised in the same household. Martin can stop the walk at any corner. Joan chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The small signature*, Joan says, “The proposed bench faces straight into the sprinkler.”

**People in this story.** [Tom Fox](#ah-r0613). The painter's first date goes into a shift log alongside the shadow. Also in the story is [Zachary Teo](#ah-r0614) (design student).

**Personality.** Martin loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Martin remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Martin has gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a soft blue checked shirt. It usually goes with one cuff folded back twice. When somebody else has the floor, Martin grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- A supervisor asks the apprentice to work through the crane-shadow moment. Martin asks the foreman whether the minutes can be traded rather than simply lost.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Martin asks for help with a task the neighbor does well, so both people get to contribute something.
- When invited to tell the story later, Martin includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Edmund Porter

AH-R0616 · Age 70 · Retired crane operator · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the last ladder I climbed without stopping.” People around the public end of the South Shore boardwalk know Edmund by a small Thursday habit. Edmund checks the shade on the benches before deciding how long to stay.

**In their own words.** “I can no longer climb the cab ladder. I stood inside its shadow and taught a child the controls from memory.”

**What Edmund wants.** To teach one child from the quay level.

**Family.** [Dev Ferris](../01/13-the-borrowed-bass.md#ah-r0123) is Edmund’s second cousin. Edmund gets to change the plan. Dev offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The borrowed bass*, Dev says, “Somebody paid the ticket years ago and never removed it.”

**People in this story.** [William Marks](#ah-r0619). A father's dated picture gives a retired crane operator the time of the gull's next meeting.

**Personality.** Edmund has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Edmund knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Edmund has a round face with a pronounced chin. Look for a thin scattering of white hair over the crown. Back on the street, the familiar outfit is an olive canvas jacket with a stitched row of blue crosses along an old repair. In conversation Edmund steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- A visitor reaches the quay too late to see it. Edmund shows the visitor where the shadow fell and when the next chance comes.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Edmund asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Edmund asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.
