# An empty dedication

[When the lights go out](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

**Paper & Pine Books.** A local novelist leaves an empty dedication page and invites the printers, caregivers and relatives who made the book possible to decide what it says.

## Eva Bay

AH-R1411 · Age 51 · Novelist · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my husband reading pages aloud over a cooking pot.” On Friday, near the brick passage off the shop row, Eva takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I wrote the book while my husband cooked. He hates public thanks and likes a hot meal waiting for him.”

**What Eva wants.** To thank the husband in a form he actually likes.

**Family.** [Gloria Batista](../09/03-the-million-dollar-flat.md#ah-r1222) is Eva’s stepsibling. Eva chooses the music. Gloria puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The million-dollar flat*, Gloria says, “I valued the view and overlooked the years she carried her shopping up without a lift.”

**People in this story.** [Sofia Brady](#ah-r1413). The novelist hears the difference between giving a caregiver wages and giving them public credit. Also in the story is [Blake Liu](#ah-r1419) (former spouse).

**Personality.** Eva is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Eva is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an unlined tan jacket and a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip. Eva has a round face with a pronounced chin, with thick, tightly coiled hair. Eva counts on the side of one finger where hardly anybody notices during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The author offers the dedication to someone who dislikes public praise. Eva asks the intended recipient which kind of thanks would actually feel welcome.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Eva puts distance between the frightened person and the argument and asks nearby staff for help.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Eva agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Tobias Starr

AH-R1412 · Age 44 · Print worker · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember getting ink under my sleeve while printing a friend's draft.” Sunday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Tobias likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I printed the first draft on my lunch break. I want the other print workers credited for covering for me.”

**What Tobias wants.** To credit the workers who covered the printing.

**Family.** [Yasmin Arrieta](../03/03-the-widow-s-dividend.md#ah-r0328) is Tobias’s cousin. Tobias chooses the music. Yasmin puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The widow's dividend*, Yasmin says, “I recorded the founding meeting on a birthday cassette.”

**People in this story.** [Morgan Lim](#ah-r1417). A translation and a lunch-break proof both owe part of their existence to uncredited coworkers.

**Personality.** Tobias tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Tobias will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** Tobias has short copper curls, a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a red windbreaker. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Tobias turns fully toward the person speaking.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks for a copy with the page still blank. Tobias keeps a clean, blank copy aside for the reader to dedicate.
- Somebody says a missing friend must be avoiding their calls. Tobias offers to accompany the caller to a familiar public meeting place without turning speculation into neighborhood news.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Tobias repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Sofia Brady

AH-R1413 · Age 49 · Paid childminder · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

Gloria chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *Station steps in rain*, Gloria says, “I took the photo everyone liked. The man in the front doesn't want his face online and I'm collecting the copies.”

**People in this story.** [Eva Bay](#ah-r1411). The novelist hears the difference between giving a caregiver wages and giving them public credit.

**Personality.** Sofia is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Sofia asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Sofia has a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a dark green knitted vest. It usually goes with a little coil of soft measuring tape. When somebody else has the floor, Sofia touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- The author offers the dedication to someone who dislikes public praise. Sofia asks the intended recipient which kind of thanks would actually feel welcome.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Sofia suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Sofia accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Hamish Yeo

AH-R1415 · Age 53 · Family wage earner and tiler · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember paying a writer's light bill with my winter bonus.” People around the foot of Harbor Viaduct know Hamish by a small Sunday habit. Hamish pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I paid the electricity through a lean winter. My price was one honest conversation after the launch.”

**What Hamish wants.** To have one honest post-launch conversation.

**Family.** [Eric Hassan](../02/08-low-water-music.md#ah-r0227) is Hamish’s stepsibling. Hamish is free to hang up first. Eric makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *Low water music*, Eric says, “I thought the sound came from the old fog bell.”

**People in this story.** [Jacqueline Glass](#ah-r1418). One supporter paid to keep the lights on while another kept the writer's own voice audible.

**Personality.** Hamish loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Hamish remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Hamish is easy to recognize by a blunt cut just below the ears and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a faded indigo overshirt with a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve, Hamish looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The author offers the dedication to someone who dislikes public praise. Hamish asks the intended recipient which kind of thanks would actually feel welcome.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Hamish stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- When invited to tell the story later, Hamish includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Miriam Lim

AH-R1416 · Age 72 · Retired teacher · Home — Old Town

**Life in the city.** “I still remember confiscating a school note too funny to throw away.” People around the brick passage off the shop row know Miriam by a small Monday habit. Miriam takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I taught the novelist to read. My favorite thing she wrote is still a vulgar note passed at school.”

**What Miriam wants.** To let the novelist keep the embarrassing school note private.

**Family.** [Gerard Garza](../07/10-an-old-restaurant-debt.md#ah-r0997) is Miriam’s cousin. Miriam decides what stays off the paper. Gerard brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *An old restaurant debt*, Gerard says, “I lent the family my black coat and got the wrong one back.”

**People in this story.** [Cameron Tsai](#ah-r1420). The bookseller can leave copies blank for teachers who have a story of their own to dedicate.

**Personality.** Miriam has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Miriam has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing. Look for two white streaks in otherwise dark hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is an unlined tan jacket with a small enamel bird on the lapel. In conversation Miriam touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks for a copy with the page still blank. Miriam keeps a clean, blank copy aside for the reader to dedicate.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Miriam moves the onlookers' bags out of the way, asks the rider what hurts, and asks one named bystander to call for help.
- When somebody trades on a title, Miriam asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Morgan Lim

AH-R1417 · Age 37 · Literary translator · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember translating a family word that needed an entire sentence.” People around Tideline Market know Morgan by a small Monday habit. Morgan picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**Family.** [Amos Ferraro](../08/13-the-understudy-sings.md#ah-r1175) is Morgan’s half-sibling. Morgan knows which ones can be shared. Amos keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The understudy sings*, Amos says, “I am the director who waited too long to rehearse a second cast.”

**People in this story.** [Tobias Starr](#ah-r1412). A translation and a lunch-break proof both owe part of their existence to uncredited coworkers.

**Personality.** Morgan finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Morgan has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a straight nose and deep-set eyes, and a favorite piece of clothing, a faded indigo overshirt. It usually goes with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring. When somebody else has the floor, Morgan grins on one side while the eyes stay serious.

**Under pressure**

- The author offers the dedication to someone who dislikes public praise. Morgan asks the intended recipient which kind of thanks would actually feel welcome.
- The last bus leaves while an elderly stranger is still crossing toward it. Morgan waits at the lit stop with them and checks whether the next service is one they can actually board.
- When thanked for a shared success, Morgan points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Jacqueline Glass

AH-R1418 · Age 48 · Book editor · Home — the neighborhood around Seabright Court

**Life in the city.** “I still remember returning a paragraph with just its finest line intact.” On Sunday, near the approach to Seabright Court, Jacqueline likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**People in this story.** [Hamish Yeo](#ah-r1415). One supporter paid to keep the lights on while another kept the writer's own voice audible.

**Personality.** Jacqueline keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Jacqueline wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a black overshirt with pearl buttons and one cuff folded back twice. Jacqueline has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow, with brown hair pinned away from the temples. Jacqueline looks toward the door whenever a name is called during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- When somebody asks who said it first, Jacqueline offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Cameron Tsai

AH-R1420 · Age 51 · Bookshop owner · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember selling two identical books for entirely different family reasons.” Friday often includes a stop at the market by Market Flats, where Cameron often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I own Paper & Pine. We are selling a few copies with the dedication still blank for readers to give away.”

**What Cameron wants.** To offer a few copies readers can dedicate themselves.

**Family.** [Penelope Hong](../03/09-the-upstairs-piano.md#ah-r0382) is Cameron’s cousin raised in the same household. Cameron can always ask for five minutes alone. Penelope asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *The upstairs piano*, Penelope says, “I trained as a mover before I played professionally.”

**People in this story.** [Miriam Lim](#ah-r1416). The bookseller can leave copies blank for teachers who have a story of their own to dedicate.

**Personality.** Cameron has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Cameron knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Cameron is easy to recognize by short copper curls and full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side. Usually dressed in a red windbreaker with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole, Cameron looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A reader asks for a copy with the page still blank. Cameron keeps a clean, blank copy aside for the reader to dedicate.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Cameron brings the envelope to the counter unopened and stays long enough to make a note of who accepted it.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Cameron asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.
