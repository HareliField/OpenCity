## Joseph Moran

AH-R1371 · Age 67 · Property developer and millionaire · Home — Coast Ridge

**Life in the city.** “I still remember selling my first strip of land to a family with a van.” Friday often includes a stop at Studio Boulevard, where Joseph waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I made money selling buildings. I thought the finest gift I could give her was never having to ask a landlord for anything.”

**What Joseph wants.** To offer the daughter security on terms she can decline.

**Family.** [Jo Bloom](../04/02-the-soft-wharf-chalk.md#ah-r0463) is Joseph’s younger cousin. Joseph is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Jo calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The soft wharf chalk*, Jo says, “I thought it was a safety warning and stopped a delivery.”

**People in this story.** [Cassandra Collins](#ah-r1372) (daughter). A wealthy father and a young tailor disagree about whether owning a whole building constitutes freedom.

**Personality.** Joseph has excellent recall for voices and a poor memory for official titles. Someone who waited patiently at a counter years ago is more likely to be remembered than the person in charge.

**Appearance.** Joseph is easy to recognize by gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears and a straight nose and deep-set eyes. Usually dressed in a boxy denim jacket with a thin silver ring worn on a cord, Joseph raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The daughter declines the property gift. Joseph takes the daughter's preferred shop seriously before proposing another gift.
- A friend asks for a loan due back before payday. Joseph asks which other bill repayment would displace, then offers only what the household can afford to give.
- When somebody trades on a title, Joseph asks for the name of the person who will still be around tomorrow.

## Cassandra Collins

AH-R1372 · Age 32 · Junior tailor · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember pinning cloth in a shop barely wider than the mirror.” Monday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Cassandra looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I am the daughter. Dad has offered me a valuable building with his office on the top floor.”

**What Cassandra wants.** To get a modest tailoring space under personal control.

**Family.** [Freya King](../02/10-the-bitter-orange.md#ah-r0250) is Cassandra’s older cousin. Cassandra calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Freya leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The bitter orange*, Freya says, “I brought her son in expecting tears. He ordered something completely different and made everyone laugh.”

**People in this story.** [Joseph Moran](#ah-r1371) (father). A wealthy father and a young tailor disagree about whether owning a whole building constitutes freedom. Also in the story is [Marin Brady](#ah-r1377) (master tailor).

**Personality.** Cassandra finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a brown corduroy jacket and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Cassandra has fine brows over wide-set eyes, with a soft fringe that falls across one eyebrow. Cassandra folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant hears about the building sale secondhand. Cassandra gives the tenant a reliable contact for the sale information and a real date to expect an answer.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Cassandra asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When thanked for a shared success, Cassandra points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Cassandra Knight

AH-R1373 · Age 49 · Valuer · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember pricing a view the prospective owner never wanted.” On Sunday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Cassandra usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I was hired to value the gift. I asked whether the young woman wanted to be a landlord at all.”

**What Cassandra wants.** To ask the intended owner what responsibilities are welcome.

**Family.** [Quinn Pereira](../02/11-one-sleeve-short.md#ah-r0254) is Cassandra’s younger cousin. Cassandra chooses whether they walk home or sit a while. Quinn brings a spare shopping bag. That small arrangement gives both a reason to see each other on weeks when asking for company outright feels difficult. Asked about *One sleeve short*, Quinn says, “I practiced my handshake with the unaltered arm.”

**People in this story.** [Tom Crane](#ah-r1379). The valuation and the tenants' explanation give the daughter a realistic picture of ownership.

**Personality.** Cassandra keeps other people's confidences so well that friends sometimes mistake discretion for taking sides. Cassandra wants to be trusted and resents being asked for proof of it.

**Appearance.** Cassandra has a dark undercut with a longer wave on top, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a rust-colored canvas coat. It usually goes with a square of clean cloth for polishing smudges. When somebody else has the floor, Cassandra listens with the head tilted a little to the left.

**Under pressure**

- The daughter declines the property gift. Cassandra takes the daughter's preferred shop seriously before proposing another gift.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Cassandra stays beside the bystander in a public place and reports only what happened in sight and earshot.
- When somebody asks who said it first, Cassandra offers to introduce them to the person willing to put a name to it.

## Grey Black

AH-R1374 · Age 45 · Downstairs shop tenant · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember renewing a lease before my family knew we could stay.” On Saturday, near Juniper Street, Grey knows which of the west-side porches has a seat in the shade.

**In their own words.** “I rent the shop below. The family's generosity could decide where my family eats next year.”

**What Grey wants.** To obtain the proposed building's actual tenant terms.

**Family.** [Mo Gill](../01/05-the-aquarium.md#ah-r0048) is Grey’s half-sibling. Grey decides what stays off the paper. Mo brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The aquarium*, Mo says, “I gave them an old pump marked display only.”

**People in this story.** [Marin Brady](#ah-r1377). A tenant's actual upstairs neighbor is one of the people measuring how little room a tailor needs.

**Personality.** Grey always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Grey is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Grey has a flat bridge to the nose and a mobile brow. Look for a center part and straight chestnut hair. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a fabric zip pull cut from a school scarf. In conversation Grey starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant hears about the building sale secondhand. Grey gives the tenant a reliable contact for the sale information and a real date to expect an answer.
- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Grey asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Grey suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.

## Ivan Ruiz

AH-R1375 · Age 38 · School cleaner · Home — the western blocks near Ferry Street Rooms

**Life in the city.** “I still remember meeting an heir who needed permission to disappoint a parent.” People around the market by Market Flats know Ivan by a small Friday habit. Ivan often waits until the delivery baskets are stacked before going in.

**In their own words.** “I would give my right arm to inherit a place. Listening to the daughter made me reconsider what a gift with no exit costs.”

**What Ivan wants.** To listen to the heir without comparing family fortunes.

**Family.** [Cynthia Barrett](../07/07-the-first-language.md#ah-r0967) is Ivan’s second cousin. Ivan gets to change the plan. Cynthia offers the first invitation. They learned that a promise to visit is easier to keep when changing a time does not count as letting down the whole family. Asked about *The first language*, Cynthia says, “I wrote an alphabet on the inside of my lunch tin so I could practice without answering questions.”

**People in this story.** [Keir Rios](#ah-r1378). A donor's dinner gets easier once two people can explain why it is possible to refuse a gift.

**Personality.** Ivan has a talent for getting adversaries to laugh at exactly the same thing. Ivan knows better than to expect the laugh to settle what they are arguing about.

**Appearance.** Ivan has black hair twisted into a small knot, a long face and high cheekbones, and a favorite piece of clothing, a sand-colored linen shirt. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Ivan looks down for a moment before saying something difficult.

**Under pressure**

- The daughter declines the property gift. Ivan takes the daughter's preferred shop seriously before proposing another gift.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. Ivan asks the counter staff for a receipt so its return can be traced even after their shift changes.
- When people declare an argument over too soon, Ivan asks them to describe one concrete thing that will be different tomorrow.

## John Machado

AH-R1376 · Age 70 · Retired developer · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember watching my partner finally move out of his father's office.” People around Paper & Pine Books know John by a small Thursday habit. John starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I am the father's old partner and I remember what he sacrificed to get out from under his own father.”

**What John wants.** To remind the wealthy father why he wanted independence.

**Family.** [Isaac Mendez](../09/01-a-room-above-the-dryers.md#ah-r1208) is John’s younger relative by marriage. John knows which ones can be shared. Isaac keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *A room above the dryers*, Isaac says, “I want to draw somebody who dislikes me. The worker offered to sit after I explained the complaint I made.”

**People in this story.** [Allen Clay](#ah-r1380). An early rent receipt substantiates a business partner's account of the father's own struggle for independence.

**Personality.** John enjoys bargaining, including over things already settled. It is partly a joke and partly a test of whether the other person feels able to say no.

**Appearance.** John is easy to recognize by silver temples and a dark fringe and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a striped cotton shirt with a folded shopping list secured by a miniature clip, John listens with the head tilted a little to the left when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant hears about the building sale secondhand. John gives the tenant a reliable contact for the sale information and a real date to expect an answer.
- Somebody finds a lost wallet outside the bank. John keeps the documents out of view and lets the person claiming it identify it before hearing any details.
- When a friend starts agreeing too quickly, John puts the deal away and suggests a walk.

## Marin Brady

AH-R1377 · Age 55 · Master tailor · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember teaching a young tailor to measure a room before a suit.” Sunday often includes a stop at the bank steps, where Marin takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I trained the daughter in tailoring. She told me exactly how little space her first shop needs.”

**What Marin wants.** To price a suitably small first tailoring shop.

**Family.** [Anton Roman](../08/06-two-names-on-the-chart.md#ah-r1110) is Marin’s younger relative by marriage. Marin can always ask for five minutes alone. Anton asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *Two names on the chart*, Anton says, “I had to tell a waiting patient why the counter was busy.”

**People in this story.** [Grey Black](#ah-r1374). A tenant's actual upstairs neighbor is one of the people measuring how little room a tailor needs. Also in the story is [Cassandra Collins](#ah-r1372) (former tailoring pupil).

**Personality.** Marin has a long memory for unfair prices and a short one for small insults. Marin can get along perfectly well with an enemy provided the account between them is clear.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a mustard canvas overshirt and a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. Marin has a crooked front tooth visible when laughing, with a smooth side part with one rebellious curl. Marin rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The daughter declines the property gift. Marin takes the daughter's preferred shop seriously before proposing another gift.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Marin offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- If a friend borrows money, Marin agrees on an amount both can bear losing before any cash changes hands.

**Under pressure**

## Allen Clay

AH-R1380 · Age 52 · Family archivist · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**What Allen wants.** To bring the father's framed rent receipt to the conversation.

**Family.** [Fiona Nord](../02/07-the-margin-reader.md#ah-r0212) is Allen’s younger cousin. Allen brings whatever was actually growing or affordable that week. Fiona makes room at the table. The meal sometimes produces a solution. Even when it does not, everyone goes away knowing what the other household is up against. Asked about *The margin reader*, Fiona says, “I recognized my old teacher by the way she corrects a question mark.”

**People in this story.** [John Machado](#ah-r1376). An early rent receipt substantiates a business partner's account of the father's own struggle for independence.

**Personality.** Allen has a strong competitive streak and a very soft spot for the person finishing last. A prize tastes better when everybody had enough time to enter.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a moss-green cardigan and a pair of reading glasses in a cloth sleeve. Allen has a heart-shaped face and prominent ears, with a loose dark braid. Allen looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A tenant hears about the building sale secondhand. Allen gives the tenant a reliable contact for the sale information and a real date to expect an answer.
- A frightened child is waiting outside a shuttered shop. Allen stays where the child can see the shop door and asks the neighboring counter to help find the adult expected there.
- If accused of favoritism, Allen explains exactly what help was offered and offers the same practical help to the accuser.
