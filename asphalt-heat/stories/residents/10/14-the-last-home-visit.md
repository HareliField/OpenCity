## Ariel Powell

AH-R1481 · Age 66 · Retiring home-care worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember thinking a polite client had enjoyed every cup.” On Friday, near Lighthouse Cove, Ariel takes a few minutes by the low rocks, where conversation sounds different as the tide turns.

**In their own words.** “I brought sweet tea for years. My oldest client said she'd waited for my last day to admit she liked it bitter.”

**What Ariel wants.** To learn the real tea preference before retirement.

**Family.** [Ruth Eade](../01/09-the-porch-light.md#ah-r0082) is Ariel’s cousin’s child. Ariel brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Ruth checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The porch light*, Ruth says, “I described it as original hardware. When the owner started to cry I crossed the phrase out.”

**People in this story.** [Philip Moreau](#ah-r1483) (successor). The retiring visitor and the new one share a round without expecting the new worker to inherit all the old preferences.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Ariel takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Ariel has steel-colored hair cut square above the collar, a strong profile and a little space between the front teeth, and a favorite piece of clothing, a mustard canvas overshirt. It usually goes with a pen clipped inside the neckline. When somebody else has the floor, Ariel touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word.

**Under pressure**

- A client tells the retiring carer what they disliked. Ariel lets the client finish the uncomfortable answer before proposing a fix.
- An envelope of wages is found under a café chair. Ariel finds a second person to witness its return so an honest mistake won't cost a worker their pay.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Ariel asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.

## Yvette Weber

AH-R1482 · Age 73 · Retired hotel porter · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember waiting beside a door that echoed like my old tenement.” On Wednesday, near the entrance to the Wash House, Yvette stops for local notices even on a day without a load to wash.

**In their own words.** “I want the new worker to knock twice. A single knock still sounds like a debt collector at my old front door.”

**What Yvette wants.** To have the new worker knock twice.

**Family.** [Ellen Scott](../03/08-the-painted-footprint.md#ah-r0376) is Yvette’s cousin. Yvette is free to hang up first. Ellen makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The painted footprint*, Ellen says, “I ran barefoot races here. I want to know whether the old starting mark lies under that plank.”

**People in this story.** [Harry Rhodes](#ah-r1488). A private introduction lets a client ask both for a particular knock and for medicine on a reachable shelf.

**Personality.** Yvette can be wonderfully decisive about an emergency and maddeningly undecided about a gift. This is because a person can say precisely what hurts and rarely says exactly what they want.

**Appearance.** Yvette has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose. Look for short white hair and darker eyebrows. On an ordinary day, the familiar outfit is a striped cotton shirt with a pressed flower kept in the clear back of a phone case. In conversation Yvette rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- The successor asks for a private introduction at the door. Yvette introduces the successor quietly and gives the client a chance to set the routine.
- Smoke appears under a shop door after closing. Yvette calls for emergency assistance from outside and waits to give the street number.
- If a neighbor offers a costly present, Yvette asks for an afternoon to work out whether accepting it would put someone else in debt.

## Philip Moreau

AH-R1483 · Age 31 · New home-care worker · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember receiving a retiring worker's perfectly illegible notebook.” Saturday often includes a stop at the brick passage off the shop row, where Philip takes the longer way when the smell from the ovens reaches the pavement.

**In their own words.** “I am the successor. I would like to make one mistake that hasn't already been made by someone everyone adores.”

**What Philip wants.** To make a relationship that belongs to the new worker.

**Family.** [Quinn Navarro](../04/04-station-steps-in-rain.md#ah-r0484) is Philip’s second cousin. Philip decides what stays off the paper. Quinn brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *Station steps in rain*, Quinn says, “I kept the wheelchair space clear and missed the speech my daughter wrote.”

**People in this story.** [Ariel Powell](#ah-r1481) (retiring carer). The retiring visitor and the new one share a round without expecting the new worker to inherit all the old preferences.

**Personality.** Philip can be lavish with time and quite careful with cash. A free afternoon is sometimes the biggest gift Philip can afford to give, and family knows to take it seriously.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a sand-colored linen shirt and a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. Philip has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, with a loose dark braid. Philip leans a shoulder against the wall during long conversations during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A client tells the retiring carer what they disliked. Philip lets the client finish the uncomfortable answer before proposing a fix.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Philip accepts a small agreed amount if paying lets the neighbor keep their pride.
- If invited to an expensive outing, Philip suggests meeting afterward and explains why without asking the others to abandon their plans.

## Alba Nolasco

AH-R1484 · Age 69 · Retired bookkeeper · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember my good cups coming out for everybody except me.” Sunday often includes a stop at the corner by Juniper Tailor, where Alba looks at the things people actually bring in for repair before looking in the display window.

**In their own words.** “I was too proud to ask for the good cup. I told the new worker which shelf my visitors use.”

**What Alba wants.** To get the good cup down at ordinary visits.

**Family.** [Alan Chapman](../05/06-two-left-slippers.md#ah-r0659) is Alba’s first cousin once removed. Alba chooses the music. Alan puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *Two left slippers*, Alan says, “I sew nonmatching shoes for a dance act. My costume box solved one ordinary morning.”

**People in this story.** [Geraldine Andrade](#ah-r1489). A pensioner who wants the good cup and a pensioner who dislikes pet names insist on two different kinds of respect.

**Personality.** Alba carries anger into a conversation very slowly. Once it arrives, a quiet statement of facts can be considerably harder to sidestep than a shout.

**Appearance.** Alba is easy to recognize by a neat white crop and one cheek that creases before the other. Usually dressed in a cropped bottle-green coat with a watch on a cracked leather band, Alba touches a knuckle to the chin when searching for a word when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The successor asks for a private introduction at the door. Alba introduces the successor quietly and gives the client a chance to set the routine.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Alba asks for help with a task the neighbor does well, so both people get to contribute something.
- If a friend asks for a false alibi for an ordinary missed appointment, Alba offers to accompany them while they tell the truth.

## Teal Ruiz

AH-R1485 · Age 77 · Widowed retired grocer · Home — the streets inland from Pier Beach

**Life in the city.** “I still remember opening a window for the first time after my husband died.” People around Breakwater Way know Teal by a small Thursday habit. Teal stops on the landward side of the cycle track to watch the first boats turn for home.

**In their own words.** “I always asked for the window shut because my partner felt the cold. He died last year and I'd like the breeze now.”

**What Teal wants.** To reopen the window now the cold is welcome.

**Family.** [John Collins](../03/09-the-upstairs-piano.md#ah-r0390) is Teal’s first cousin once removed. Teal decides what stays off the paper. John brings the paper and a pen. They have helped each other make decisions that were far easier to discuss once the figures could be moved around. The decisions themselves still belong to the person asking. Asked about *The upstairs piano*, John says, “I heard the landing concert from the street.”

**People in this story.** [Willa Bishop](#ah-r1490). An open window and a loose end of knitting make two invitations to come back in ordinary clothes.

**Personality.** Teal has an appetite for a good argument about little things and works hard to keep a serious disagreement from being turned into entertainment.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a moss-green cardigan and a comb in a sleeve sewn into the lining. Teal has a broad nose and a deep dimple on one cheek, with a gray streak running back from the forehead. Teal looks at people's hands before looking at what they carry during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A client tells the retiring carer what they disliked. Teal lets the client finish the uncomfortable answer before proposing a fix.
- An argument on the pavement makes a bystander feel unsafe. Teal walks with the bystander to a staffed doorway and checks whether they want someone called.
- When a private quarrel becomes a public joke, Teal says the plainest truthful thing possible and changes the subject.

## Teal Starr

AH-R1486 · Age 44 · Care agency payroll clerk · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember signing expenses a worker felt guilty submitting.” On Tuesday, near Tideline Market, Teal picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I did her payroll. The route took an extra fifteen minutes every Friday and she never asked me to find them.”

**What Teal wants.** To account for the Friday quarter-hours.

**Family.** [Elinor Stein](../05/15-a-boat-for-sale-twice.md#ah-r0744) is Teal’s second cousin. Teal brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Elinor checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *A boat for sale twice*, Elinor says, “I only wanted the dinghy we learned to row in.”

**People in this story.** [Jody Schmidt](#ah-r1487). A payroll worker records the lost quarter hours and a neighbor supplies the first dark-morning lift.

**Personality.** Teal treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Teal lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a faded indigo overshirt and a thin silver ring worn on a cord. Teal has a small notch in the left eyebrow, with short copper curls. Teal starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The successor asks for a private introduction at the door. Teal introduces the successor quietly and gives the client a chance to set the routine.
- A customer is short at the till while a queue forms. Teal offers the shortfall once and accepts the answer, including a refusal.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Teal says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Jody Schmidt

AH-R1487 · Age 38 · Van driver · Home — the western residential streets

**Life in the city.** “I still remember sharing a car through a week of family hospital visits.” People around Studio Boulevard know Jody by a small Thursday habit. Jody waits by the window where the whole crossing can be seen without leaning into the road.

**In their own words.** “I borrowed her car when my mother was dying. I am giving the successor a lift on the first dark morning.”

**What Jody wants.** To give the new worker a lift through the first dark morning.

**Family.** [Desmond Green](../09/05-a-clean-coat-for-court.md#ah-r1244) is Jody’s younger cousin. Jody chooses the music. Desmond puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *A clean coat for court*, Desmond says, “I want the court artist to draw my father in the shirt underneath.”

**People in this story.** [Teal Starr](#ah-r1486). A payroll worker records the lost quarter hours and a neighbor supplies the first dark-morning lift.

**Under pressure**

- When asked to wait without intervening, Jody agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Harry Rhodes

AH-R1488 · Age 75 · Retired geography teacher · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember standing on my own to fetch my first full pill box.” Thursday often includes a stop at the approach to Seabright Court, where Harry likes to watch the beach traffic thin enough for workers going the other way.

**In their own words.** “I can reach my own medicine from a lower shelf. I wanted to tell the old worker before she stopped seeing me.”

**What Harry wants.** To lower the medicine shelf and fetch it personally.

**Family.** [Terry Aranda](../06/13-the-almost-scholarship.md#ah-r0879) is Harry’s younger cousin. When an older relative began losing nouns, they drew a map of places where familiar words came back. The map still gets folded correctly every time it comes out. Harry marks the places where asking a question helped. Asked about *The almost scholarship*, Terry says, “My own scholarship fell apart over an unpaid deposit.”

**People in this story.** [Yvette Weber](#ah-r1482). A private introduction lets a client ask both for a particular knock and for medicine on a reachable shelf.

**Personality.** Harry tells a better story in a kitchen than at a microphone. Family members sometimes underestimate how far Harry will go to back a quiet opinion once it is actually challenged.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for an old plum windbreaker and a partly rubbed-away embroidered initial. Harry has full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, with two white streaks in otherwise dark hair. Harry draws breath as if to speak and lets someone else go first during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The successor asks for a private introduction at the door. Harry introduces the successor quietly and gives the client a chance to set the routine.
- A neighbor offers to pay for a favor that was meant kindly. Harry suggests breakfast together and lets the offer rest until another day.
- If a relative makes a joke at a child's expense, Harry repeats the child's actual point and waits for a reply to that.

## Geraldine Andrade

AH-R1489 · Age 72 · Retired neighbor and switchboard worker · Home — the streets around Foundry Lofts

**Life in the city.** “I still remember being called by a pet name I refused to answer to.” On Friday, near the corner outside Coastal Barber, Geraldine usually checks the board before the regulars give a more colorful account of the news.

**In their own words.** “I hate being called darling and will miss the only person who could get away with it.”

**What Geraldine wants.** To say goodbye to the one permitted pet name.

**Family.** [Agnes Kahn](../02/12-the-sheltered-corner.md#ah-r0263) is Geraldine’s younger relative by marriage. Geraldine asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Agnes chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The sheltered corner*, Agnes says, “The new stop faces the sun. I can read the driver's lips for the first time.”

**People in this story.** [Alba Nolasco](#ah-r1484). A pensioner who wants the good cup and a pensioner who dislikes pet names insist on two different kinds of respect.

**Personality.** Geraldine is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Geraldine asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Geraldine has steel-colored hair cut square above the collar, one cheek that creases before the other, and a favorite piece of clothing, a gray fisher's sweater. It usually goes with a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. When somebody else has the floor, Geraldine looks toward the door whenever a name is called.

**Under pressure**

- A client tells the retiring carer what they disliked. Geraldine lets the client finish the uncomfortable answer before proposing a fix.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Geraldine asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Geraldine accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Willa Bishop

AH-R1490 · Age 80 · Retired knitter · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember unraveling the last row of a very neat scarf.” People around Paper & Pine Books know Willa by a small Sunday habit. Willa starts with the handwritten shelf cards and often leaves with a suggestion for somebody else.

**In their own words.** “I knitted her a retirement scarf with a loose end deliberately sticking out. I want her to come back and complain about my finishing.”

**What Willa wants.** To give the retiree a reason to come back and tease.

**Family.** [Harriet Chapman](../06/06-home-with-stairs.md#ah-r0802) is Willa’s younger relative by marriage. Willa calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Harriet leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *Home with stairs*, Harriet says, “I want my mother at home but can't lift her.”

**People in this story.** [Teal Ruiz](#ah-r1485). An open window and a loose end of knitting make two invitations to come back in ordinary clothes.

**Personality.** Willa is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Willa is easy to recognize by gray hair trimmed very close behind the ears and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a black overshirt with pearl buttons with a knotted piece of sail thread on a key ring, Willa holds eye contact until the other person has finished when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The successor asks for a private introduction at the door. Willa introduces the successor quietly and gives the client a chance to set the routine.
- A family supper turns into an argument about old debt. Willa separates the money question from the broken promise and asks which one the family wants to discuss first.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Willa thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.
