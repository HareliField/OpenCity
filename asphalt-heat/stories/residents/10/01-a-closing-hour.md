## Dana Randall

AH-R1351 · Age 48 · Laundry operator · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember turning the laundromat key while my daughter slept upstairs.” Wednesday often includes a stop at Maré Coffee, where Dana likes the table where people coming off shift stop checking the time.

**In their own words.** “I advertised always open before I had a daughter. She asked which of those two promises I intended to keep.”

**What Dana wants.** To make the birthday a real day off.

**Family.** [Silas Pike](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0024) is Dana’s stepsibling. Dana calls back when there is finally a little privacy. Silas leaves the first voice message. In their family a missed ring can mean a bus, a customer, a sleeping child or a bad hour. Neither treats it as an answer in itself. Asked about *The small signature*, Silas says, “I wrote the gift restriction in language the vendor couldn't read.”

**People in this story.** [Idris Marsh](#ah-r1357). The family birthday only remains a real commitment if the owner and organizer set the same hour. Also in the story is [Jonathan Lund](#ah-r1353) (laundry employee).

**Personality.** Dana prefers exact instructions at work and pleasant surprises at home. The two standards occasionally collide, especially when a relative thinks dropping by counts as notice.

**Appearance.** On an ordinary day, look for a pale blue work shirt and a braided bracelet with a single wooden bead. Dana has a scattering of freckles across the bridge of the nose, with a high puff tied with plain cord. Dana turns fully toward the person speaking during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The operator closes the doors for a family birthday. Dana looks for a neighboring wash service and leaves the family birthday intact.
- A coworker arrives late after a night at the hospital. Dana asks them privately what can be shared about the delay and respects the answer.
- When a visitor arrives unexpectedly, Dana clears one chair and says honestly how long the visit can last.
