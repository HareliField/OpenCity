## Sylvia Reeves

AH-R1391 · Age 46 · Returning witness and postal clerk · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember walking past the brick passage without looking up.” On Saturday, near Tideline Market, Sylvia picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I want to see my aunt without strangers assuming my visit is another statement.”

**What Sylvia wants.** To visit the aunt without giving a fresh statement.

**Family.** [Karina Heller](../08/12-the-high-road-home.md#ah-r1164) is Sylvia’s cousin raised in the same household. Sylvia knows which ones can be shared. Karina keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The high road home*, Karina says, “I have vertigo in an ordinary lift. I will ride along if I can look out at the horizon.”

**People in this story.** [Walter Easton](#ah-r1398). A person returning for a visit and a gardener with enormous plants have to decide what can actually go home. Also in the story is [Winifred Bell](#ah-r1396) (adult daughter).

**Personality.** Sylvia finds names easy and faces hard on a crowded street. People who assume they have been snubbed usually get a warmer greeting once they speak.

**Appearance.** Sylvia is easy to recognize by a dark undercut with a longer wave on top and a small notch in the left eyebrow. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a printed handkerchief folded into the pocket, Sylvia looks down for a moment before saying something difficult when listening.

**Under pressure**

- The witness runs into a convicted neighbor's family. Sylvia gives both parties the space to pass or speak without an audience.
- A diner goes silent in the middle of a noisy family celebration. Sylvia asks one close relative to check in privately instead of stopping the party for a public inquiry.
- When somebody waves from across traffic, Sylvia waits at the corner to hear their voice and give them a proper answer.

**Life in the city.** “I still remember seeing the person who got hurt weeks after the gossip died down.” People around the downtown fountain know Linden by a small Tuesday habit. Linden keeps a little time free to hear who is playing at the far end of the park.

**Family.** [Jasper Baker](../08/10-the-first-delivery-fee.md#ah-r1150) is Linden’s first cousin once removed. Linden chooses the music. Jasper puts the kettle on. The cups come out before either brings up the reason for the visit. They trust a difficult conversation more if it has had an ordinary beginning. Asked about *The first delivery fee*, Jasper says, “I agreed to collect three neighbors' lunches on one run.”

**People in this story.** [Alexei Walsh](#ah-r1393). A former dealer and the convicted man's brother have different reasons to let an encounter stay ordinary.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a cream pullover and a tiny red thread tied through the lowest buttonhole. Linden has a narrow face with an expressive upper lip, with dark brown curls cut close behind the ears. Linden looks down for a moment before saying something difficult during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- A colleague says somebody used the wrong name in public. Linden asks which name they want to hear next time and uses it in the next ordinary introduction.
- If a stranger returns something damaged, Linden listens first to the account of how it was actually used.

## Parker Parker

AH-R1394 · Age 62 · Family host and home help · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember resetting a dinner table after explaining who was invited.” On Friday, near the foot of Harbor Viaduct, Parker pauses where the machinery overhead makes two people stand close enough to hear each other.

**In their own words.** “I invited her for lunch and didn't tell her who would be at the table. I called back to give her the names.”

**What Parker wants.** To give each lunch guest the names before they arrive.

**Family.** [Wynn Martin](../07/07-the-first-language.md#ah-r0970) is Parker’s cousin’s child. Parker brings something that can still be enjoyed tomorrow. Wynn checks the date twice. Arrangements do change in their family. Taking a delay seriously without treating it as an insult took both of them years. Asked about *The first language*, Wynn says, “I am the classmate she fears. I thought she was showing off until I tried one of those lines myself.”

**People in this story.** [Gabriel Ibrahim](#ah-r1400). The host and the aunt's carer agree on both the guest list and the hour lunch will end.

**Personality.** Likes beautiful, unnecessary things and gets cross with people who mistake poverty for a lack of taste. Parker takes longer choosing a cheap birthday card than an expensive coat.

**Appearance.** Parker is easy to recognize by iron-gray waves and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a dark green knitted vest with a seam let out by a narrow strip of contrasting fabric, Parker starts to lift a hand, then waits to be invited to speak when listening.

**Under pressure**

- A reporter wants to attend the visit. Parker asks the family whether a reporter was invited before sharing a time or an address.
- A power cut leaves two neighbors waiting on the stairs. Parker asks which apartment is expecting someone home and makes sure that household knows about the delay.
- When a relative dismisses a little luxury, Parker asks which ordinary pleasure the relative would give up first.
