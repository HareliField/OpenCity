# The kitchen without a boss

[When the lights go out](README.md) · [City directory](../directory.md)

## Ren Ruiz

AH-R1441 · Age 46 · Bakery supervisor · Home — Midtown

**Life in the city.** “I still remember answering the first bakery call in my pajamas.” People around the bank steps know Ren by a small Wednesday habit. Ren takes a few minutes to read the public notices, especially the ones written in a hurry.

**In their own words.** “I have wanted this job for years. I learned on day two that the deliveries call before the children wake.”

**What Ren wants.** To run the month without losing early family commitments.

**Family.** [Jasmine Moreau](../09/13-the-school-photograph-that-travels.md#ah-r1322) is Ren’s second cousin. Ren chooses whether to use it. Jasmine saves a place. The invitation means something because declining it has never ended the friendship between their households. Asked about *The school photograph that travels*, Jasmine says, “I am the cleaner who opened the library early.”

**People in this story.** [Selena Lin](#ah-r1445) (bakery owner). A worker who wanted responsibility gets a whole month in which the owner has to let the decisions stand.

**Personality.** Ren treats a small promise with the gravity others reserve for a contract. This makes Ren lovely to rely on and surprisingly difficult to invite on a whim.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a red windbreaker and a watch on a cracked leather band. Ren has one cheek that creases before the other, with short copper curls. Ren folds a paper napkin into quarters without noticing during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The absent owner calls to ask which pastry burned. Ren gives the owner a truthful account without handing back all three workers' decisions.
- A street musician can't finish a familiar song. Ren offers a little space from the crowd and waits for the player to indicate whether company is wanted.
- When offered a favor with ambiguous terms, Ren says what can be returned and asks whether that will do.

## Lee Roche

AH-R1442 · Age 41 · Night baker · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember the night loaf I learned to make without looking at a clock.” Sunday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Lee checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I bake the quiet-shift bread nobody photographs. I want that work counted before we raise the flashy pastry prices.”

**What Lee wants.** To keep the unphotographed night loaves affordable.

**Family.** [Yasmin Vega](../06/11-a-chair-of-one-s-own.md#ah-r0851) is Lee’s stepsibling. Lee can always ask for five minutes alone. Yasmin asks before bringing somebody along. It keeps a conversation between two people from unexpectedly turning into a family committee. Asked about *A chair of one's own*, Yasmin says, “I have enough clients until one of them goes on maternity leave.”

**People in this story.** [Nina Khan](#ah-r1447). The quiet loaf specialist meets a regular who counts breakfast in coins.

**Personality.** Lee is good at starting things, dreadful at waiting for them to take effect. Close family can tell how worried Lee is by the number of tiny errands suddenly being done.

**Appearance.** Lee is easy to recognize by a long plait tucked inside the collar and a long face and high cheekbones. Usually dressed in a sand-colored linen shirt with a narrow brass watch set a little fast, Lee takes off the watch before a long conversation when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two kitchen workers disagree on the new shift rota. Lee puts both proposed rotas beside the actual delivery and childcare times.
- Someone offers a cash reward for naming a rumored culprit. Lee checks privately on the person being talked about and offers to accompany a real witness who wants to speak.
- When asked to wait without intervening, Lee agrees on a check-in time and keeps busy somewhere out of sight.

## Sonia Neal

AH-R1444 · Age 54 · Egg supplier · Home — Eastside

**Life in the city.** “I still remember driving eggs over a ridge without breaking one.” Sunday often includes a stop at the crossing outside Verve Nail Studio, where Sonia notices whose chair is empty and who has stayed talking after an appointment.

**In their own words.** “I supply eggs and normally ask for the boss. I learned all three new names when they sent back a cracked delivery.”

**What Sonia wants.** To deal directly with the three acting managers.

**Family.** [Luz Aranda](../01/03-the-small-signature.md#ah-r0023) is Sonia’s cousin’s child. Sonia is free to hang up first. Luz makes the call. Some of their best calls last less than a minute. A change of tone is sometimes all the news either one needs. Asked about *The small signature*, Luz says, “I counted the jar twice and kept the bent coin.”

**People in this story.** [Dolores Silva](#ah-r1449). The egg supplier and rival baker each offer equipment or stock on straightforward terms.

**Personality.** Sonia is fond of ambitious ideas and deeply suspicious of grand openings. The question Sonia asks at any launch is who has the keys for cleaning up a month later.

**Appearance.** Sonia has short, uneven waves that refuse to lie flat, a small notch in the left eyebrow, and a favorite piece of clothing, a black overshirt with pearl buttons. It usually goes with a train token tied to a zipper pull. When somebody else has the floor, Sonia steadies a cup with both hands while listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two kitchen workers disagree on the new shift rota. Sonia puts both proposed rotas beside the actual delivery and childcare times.
- A stranger waves a phone camera in a crying person's face. Sonia asks the filmer to lower the phone and gets another familiar face to stand by the person crying.
- If offered a ceremonial role, Sonia accepts only after finding out whether the person doing the regular work can come too.

## Selena Lin

AH-R1445 · Age 61 · Bakery owner · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember phoning my shop from a room where I was supposed to rest.” On Saturday, near Harbour Bakery, Selena buys one familiar thing and asks who chose the unfamiliar thing in the window.

**In their own words.** “I am the ill owner. I rang to find out what burned and for once three people refused to tell me.”

**What Selena wants.** To let the workers finish the trial without remote interference.

**Family.** [Silas Watts](../03/09-the-upstairs-piano.md#ah-r0389) is Selena’s cousin raised in the same household. Selena can stop the walk at any corner. Silas chooses the familiar route. Walking side by side makes it easier to disagree without turning each disagreement into a contest. Asked about *The upstairs piano*, Silas says, “I wanted to cut a new door. The downstairs tenant offered the piano a room of its own.”

**People in this story.** [Ren Ruiz](#ah-r1441) (temporary bakery manager). A worker who wanted responsibility gets a whole month in which the owner has to let the decisions stand.

**Personality.** Selena is firm about boundaries and unusually forgiving of somebody who admits to crossing one. Demands to act grateful for unwanted help can end a friendship.

**Appearance.** Away from the heat of the kitchen, look for a moss-green cardigan and one loose pocket corner secured with green thread. Selena has two dimples that appear only with a full smile, with steel-colored hair cut square above the collar. Selena draws small circles on a tabletop while waiting during a conversation.

**Under pressure**

- The absent owner calls to ask which pastry burned. Selena gives the owner a truthful account without handing back all three workers' decisions.
- A cyclist goes down in a crowded crossing. Selena tells the first helper what they actually saw and makes room for the rider to answer for themself.
- If a relative arrives with a solution nobody requested, Selena thanks them for coming and explains the part that still belongs to the household.

## Francis Parra

AH-R1446 · Age 59 · Bakery shareholder · Home — the blocks north of the port

**Life in the city.** “I still remember trying to put on an apron over my shareholder's coat.” People around the courtyard by Ferry Street Rooms know Francis by a small Saturday habit. Francis prefers the bench where the sound of the shop door reaches before the visitor does.

**In their own words.** “I have shares and no apron. I offered to carry flour for one afternoon before looking at the daily numbers.”

**What Francis wants.** To put real flour-handling time behind a shareholder opinion.

**Family.** [Talia Valdez](../02/05-the-pawned-trumpet.md#ah-r0198) is Francis’s stepsibling. Francis is allowed to ask about the bad news afterward. Talia calls about good news first. Neither wants every family conversation to begin with a bill, a diagnosis or an apology. Asked about *The pawned trumpet*, Talia says, “I taught two pupils on that horn. Neither ever knew I owned it first.”

**People in this story.** [Teal Aranda](#ah-r1450). Carrying flour changes what a shareholder is prepared to say about the workers' rota.

**Personality.** Francis loves a well-told anecdote and distrusts the neat ending. The parts Francis remembers are the interruptions, unfinished jobs and people who had to go back to work halfway through.

**Appearance.** Francis has heavy brows and an unusually delicate jaw. Look for steel-colored hair cut square above the collar. Away from the heat of the kitchen, the familiar outfit is a soft blue checked shirt with a cloth patch so neatly matched it only shows in strong light. In conversation Francis takes off the watch before a long conversation.

**Under pressure**

- Two kitchen workers disagree on the new shift rota. Francis puts both proposed rotas beside the actual delivery and childcare times.
- A driver splashes a market worker and tries to laugh it off. Francis offers the worker something dry first and leaves it to them whether to pursue the apology.
- When invited to tell the story later, Francis includes the name of the person who arrived after the photograph.

## Marlowe Gross

AH-R1448 · Age 32 · Local food reviewer · Home — the neighborhood around Market Flats

**Life in the city.** “I still remember publishing an unkind review written before my tea cooled.” People around Tideline Market know Marlowe by a small Wednesday habit. Marlowe picks out the bruised fruit first and asks the seller what will ripen by supper.

**In their own words.** “I wrote an unfair review on their first rushed morning. I came back for twelve breakfasts before writing a correction.”

**What Marlowe wants.** To publish a correction informed by several visits.

**Family.** [Lennon Heller](../09/15-the-missing-name-on-the-stern.md#ah-r1346) is Marlowe’s cousin. Marlowe knows which ones can be shared. Lennon keeps the photographs. They enjoy remembering people in public and also know what it feels like to have a private afternoon turned into an anecdote for strangers. Asked about *The missing name on the stern*, Lennon says, “I quoted one afternoon's work. I budgeted an extra morning after meeting the grandmother who wanted to watch.”

**People in this story.** [Dale Garrett](#ah-r1443). A worker washing plates tells the reviewer what was missed in the rushed opening morning.

**Personality.** Marlowe finds it easier to own up to a mistake than to accept praise. The worse the mistake, the more precise the account of exactly when it happened.

**Appearance.** Marlowe is easy to recognize by a long plait tucked inside the collar and a heart-shaped face and prominent ears. Usually dressed in a charcoal zip cardigan with a train token tied to a zipper pull, Marlowe raises one eyebrow long before saying yes when listening.

**Under pressure**

- Two kitchen workers disagree on the new shift rota. Marlowe puts both proposed rotas beside the actual delivery and childcare times.
- Someone begins blaming the youngest worker for a missing item. Marlowe says what they know in their own name and refuses to treat a guess as something the child has to disprove.
- When thanked for a shared success, Marlowe points to the person who noticed the original mistake.

## Teal Aranda

AH-R1450 · Age 45 · Shop accountant · Home — the neighborhood above Lighthouse Cove

**Life in the city.** “I still remember a calendar where everyone else's birthday came before mine.” Friday often includes a stop at the blocks below Foundry Lofts, where Teal checks the little hand-lettered notices around Harbour Supply.

**In their own words.** “I want to keep the rota the workers designed even after the owner returns. They put their own birthdays on it first.”

**What Teal wants.** To get the workers' birthday rota kept after the owner returns.

**Family.** [Michael Gallagher](../07/02-the-absent-signature.md#ah-r0914) is Teal’s half-sibling. Teal asks the question the rest of the family avoided. Michael chooses an ordinary story to tell. They tend to get further with an important subject when it arrives in the middle of talk about dinner or work. Asked about *The absent signature*, Michael says, “I forwarded her mail through two countries.”

**People in this story.** [Francis Parra](#ah-r1446). Carrying flour changes what a shareholder is prepared to say about the workers' rota.

**Personality.** Teal always notices who paid for the last round. In a group that can be a kindness or a small tyranny, and Teal is trying to tell the difference before the bill arrives.

**Appearance.** Teal has a high puff tied with plain cord, full cheeks and a mouth that pulls slightly to one side, and a favorite piece of clothing, a cropped bottle-green coat. It usually goes with a thin silver ring worn on a cord. When somebody else has the floor, Teal rubs a thumb along the edge of the nearest pocket.

**Under pressure**

- Two kitchen workers disagree on the new shift rota. Teal puts both proposed rotas beside the actual delivery and childcare times.
- An unfamiliar person asks for directions but cannot read the street signs. Teal walks with them as far as the first landmark and points to the next one from there.
- If somebody tries to repay an old favor all at once, Teal suggests a smaller gesture they can repeat without hardship.
